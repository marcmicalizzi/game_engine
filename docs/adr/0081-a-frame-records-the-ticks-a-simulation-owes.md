# ADR-0081: A simulation that steps on the card is recorded at the top of every frame, through a registration point of its own, for the ticks the scheduler says it owes

- **Date:** 2026-11-26

## Context

ADR-0080 (accepted 2026-11-26) gives the water a state, stepped on the card by `water_live` (W175) and held to the reference on the CPU, `water_state` (W184). Its tenth decision says the state "advances by the simulation's ticks, not by frames: a frame submits the ticks it owes (none, one or two), each with the sub-steps its domain's stability limit asks", its seventh that an event is "applied between steps in a fixed order" at its tick, and its twelfth that `water_live` owns "the scheduler's entry that advances them" and "the step's compute pass (in the render graph only while a tile is awake)". W191 is the row that puts the state in a running scene. A read of the code on 2026-11-26 found that it cannot start, for two reasons, and a third that the first two hide.

**No stage of a frame can carry a step.** The renderer's one registration point for a capability's work in a frame is the frame pass (`systems/renderer/include/systems/renderer/frame_pass.h`, `SceneRenderer::add_frame_pass`; [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#frame-passes)). Its three stages, `AfterResolve`, `Exposure` and `Reconstruction`, all run after the material resolve. `SceneRenderer::record_frame` calls them only when `frame_passes_on` holds: a pass is registered or an HDR frame carries an overlay (`ui_layer_on`), the path is not `--raster direct`, the linear image exists, and the view mode is shaded — so a debug view, the ramp and the direct path call none. And the first `add_frame_pass` waits for the device and makes the linear colour image (`R16G16B16A16Sfloat`), its resolve pipeline and the encode, so registering any pass changes how every shaded frame is resolved and encoded. A step put there would stop in every debug view and under `--raster direct`, would charge a scene with a live domain the linear image even with no tile awake, and would run after the cull, the deformed-vertex pool and the resolve that W192's surface must feed. The table is also nearly full: `k_max_frame_passes` is 4, and the renderer's own exposure, texture feedback and accumulation (or a reconstruction provider), the medium (`systems/volumetrics`) and the collision drawing (`systems/scene_collision`) already compete for it. The one recording in the frame that runs outside the stages and without the linear image is the renderer's own texture-residency feedback (`SceneRenderer::record_texture_residency`), in every view mode but not under `--raster direct`; it is not a registration point.

**A frame is recorded more often than it is asked for.** `SceneRenderer::submit_frame` records, submits and waits up to three times while the runs of the visible list are unmeasured (a tail's first bound, W156-2); `SceneRenderer::render_offscreen` calls `submit_frame` up to three times when the traced chain or a run did not fit; `SceneRenderer::capture` calls `render_offscreen` once for each phase of an accumulation cycle under the accumulation's `mean`, once for the frame, and once more for a normals frame when normals are asked for. So one `FrameDesc` handed to the renderer is several recordings, a number the host does not bound, each a full `record_frame`. Anything that steps once per recording steps a state an unpredictable number of times per frame. engine-view's animated world already follows the rule that answers this — "a frame drawn twice from the same clock draws the same pose" (`advance_animation` in `apps/engine_view/animation.h`) — but it does so outside the renderer, in the host.

**The viewer has no scheduler, and its tick is not the world's.** Only engine-host's runtime world owns a `sim::SimScheduler` (`apps/engine_host/ops_methods.cpp`, at `k_hz` = 60, "the rate every other fixed step in the engine runs at"), and that world opens no device; engine-host's `render.*` methods open a device and own no scheduler. engine-view's ticks are its `view::FlySession`'s, fed from a `FixedStepClock` at the session's rate — **240 Hz by default** in `run_view`'s loop, capped at a quarter second of ticks a frame past which a stall is dropped — and what runs on a tick is a chain of tick observers (the game runner, the player's body, the sound host, the animated world). The animated world steps at **60 Hz** (`k_animation_hz`), step k falling due at session tick k·tick_hz/60 (`AnimationTicks`); without a session it steps on the frame index, one step a frame (`k_frame_index_hz` = 60); a timed flight (`renderer::fly_camera_path`) puts path frame f at `world_rate · f / 60` world seconds and draws its characters at rest, and its warm-up loop runs `w < warmup` or until `warmup_seconds` have passed, so which warm-up frame is the last is known only once it is drawn. ADR-0080's "none, one or two" ticks a frame is therefore true of the 60 Hz world step and false of the 240 Hz session tick, which a 60 Hz window runs four of a frame; W175 measured its step per 1/60 s tick, and W173's row records that the design says 1/60 s. `domain/sim` is **not optional** (its `CMakeLists.txt`: "a registration point that can be switched off is one nobody can register with") and links no flecs: `sim::SimScheduler` runs its own table with no executor at all (`TickExecutor`'s functions are null by default and `step()` skips them), so a host may own one without `domain/ecs`. engine-view links `sim` today only where `scene_collision` or `world` is configured.

What `water_live` offers today is the step without a world: `DeviceStep::record(graph, count, max_dt, timer)` puts a run of steps into a graph the caller owns; `DeviceStep::advance` and every upload (`set_bed`, `set_source`, `write_state`) submit and wait; the step's length is chosen on the card by a one-workgroup pass, and `advance` refuses a source on a coarser clock (`set_source(..., every > 1)`) because that clock counts steps on the host ([water_live](../subsystems/water_live.md)). `gfx::CommandList::dispatch_indirect` exists. The reading of the reference's results for the step rule — one sub-step a 60 Hz tick for a stream and a pool, one or two for a surf zone — is on [water_state](../subsystems/water_state.md#the-clocks-and-what-a-tick-costs).

What was on the table, **for where the step is recorded**:

1. **A fourth frame-pass stage**, `FramePassStage::Compute`, recorded before the cull (the first proposal, made on 2026-11-25 in a reading of the code for W191). It reuses one table and one registration call, but every rule of that table is a picture's: the linear image made at the first registration, the shaded-only and visibility-buffer-only call, a context built of views, images, clocks and the sky, and a shared cap of four. Each would need an exception keyed on the stage, which is the table branching on what a capability registered.
2. **A registration point of its own**, called at the top of every recorded frame's graph, with a context that holds no part of the picture.
3. **A submission of the capability's own**, outside the frame: the capability records its steps into a graph and a command buffer of its own and submits them on the same queue before the frame. The renderer is untouched and a redraw steps nothing, by construction. But its GPU time is outside the frame's timer, so the benchmark's per-pass record and the frame budgets do not see it; it needs fences and slots of its own that must agree with the renderer's frames in flight; and the readers in the frame (W192's surface in the pool's stage, the cull's bounds from a tile's range of levels) reach state written by another submission, which the frame's graph cannot see and order.
4. **The step handed in each `FrameDesc`** (a callback per frame). The renderer copies a `FrameDesc` into every redraw and every frame of a capture, so the step would ride every recording; and every host would have to set it on every frame.

**For who decides how many ticks a frame owes**:

1. **The frame**, from `FrameDesc::dt_s` or the change of `world_time_s`. A replay would then depend on the frame rate, and the renderer would count time, which no part of it does.
2. **Two paths over one function**: a `SystemDesc` in engine-host and a tick observer in engine-view (the same reading's proposal, which left unifying them to ADR-0038).
3. **The engine's scheduler in both hosts**, the viewer gaining one whenever a capability installs a system in it.

**And for how a tick's sub-steps are recorded**, given that the card chooses each sub-step's length and the host must fix a frame's commands before it has: *(a)* a fixed run of sub-step slots per tick, a tick that runs out carrying its remaining time into the next; *(b)* every tick recorded with the domain's worst case of slots; *(c)* the card walking a queue of ticks across a frame's shared slots. (a) shortens a tick and moves its events' boundary, which ADR-0080 decision 7 does not allow, and makes the bytes depend on how many slots a frame recorded; (b) keeps the bytes but pays the worst case every tick and needs a worst case nobody can state for water that may deepen and speed up; (c) is decision 6.

## Decision

1. **The tick a simulation steps on is the world's 60 Hz step**, in every host: engine-host's `k_hz`, engine-view's animated world, the step ADR-0080's "none, one or two a frame" and W175's measurements were stated at. Never a frame, and never engine-view's 240 Hz session tick, which would multiply the step's fixed passes by four for nothing the step rule asks. A domain on a coarser cadence of its own is a shortcut of ADR-0080 decision 3's kind and is under "Revisit when".

2. **A step that a simulation records on the card is a frame step: a registration point of the renderer's, of its own, not a frame-pass stage.** `systems/renderer/include/systems/renderer/frame_step.h` declares `FrameStepFn` (records the step's passes; false with an error fails the frame, as a frame pass does), `FrameStepCommitFn` (decision 4), `FrameStepContext` and `k_max_frame_steps` = 4; `SceneRenderer::add_frame_step(fn, commit, user, zone)` and `remove_frame_step(fn, user)` register and remove one, never between `begin_frame` and `submit_frame`, removal waiting for the device so the frames in flight have finished with the step's buffers. `record_frame` calls every registered step, in the order registered, **before the first pass of the frame's graph** — the reset and the uploads — and so before the cull, the deformed-vertex pool, the rasterizers, the traced chain and the resolve, any of which may read the state from W192 on (the cull a tile's range of levels, the pool its heights, the resolve its bed and level). It calls them **in every recorded frame whatever it draws**: every rasterizer, the ray path, `--raster direct`, every debug view and the ramp, a capture's every frame, a flight's warm-ups and its drain. Nothing in the renderer names a capability; the step is a function, a user pointer and a timer zone. A frame step is for any simulation that advances a state on the card by ticks — the water first; a cloth, a smoke or a sand on the same terms. A step registers with one renderer; registered with two, it would step twice, and a host does not do that.

3. **A step touches its own buffers and nothing of the picture.** `FrameStepContext` holds the graph, the GPU timer, the device, the bindless set (for a step whose shader reads through an address the set holds), the frame slot and `frames_in_flight`, and the frame index; it holds no image, no visibility buffer, no view, no camera, no eye, no clock and no `dt_s`. A step's passes declare buffers it imported or made, through the graph, which derives every barrier (gfx.md, "Render graph v0": passes are recorded in declaration order, so being called first is being first). **Registering a step makes no linear image, changes no resolve pipeline, leaves `frame_passes_on` as it was and adds no branch to the resolve**: a frame step is not a picture's stage, and the reasons the frame-pass table makes the linear image (a medium composites light onto light, the encode quantizes once) are a picture's reasons. A step with nothing to do this frame adds no pass, no import and no barrier.

4. **A frame's step is recorded once for each frame the host hands over, whatever the renderer records it for.** The host calls the capability's `prepare` once in its turn before `begin_frame`, which arms one recording; the armed step records its work into the next recording, and `FrameStepCommitFn` — which `SceneRenderer::submit_recorded` calls with the timeline value after the queue has accepted a recording, and never for one that failed — disarms it and marks what that recording staged as on the card. So a redraw of the measured runs, a recording `render_offscreen` grows and draws again, the later frames of a capture's accumulation cycle and its normals frame record no step and draw the state the first recording left; a recording that failed stages and steps again in the next.

5. **The scheduler counts the ticks; the card is handed every tick whole.** The capability installs, with `sim::SimScheduler::add_system`, a `Derived` system at `TickPhase::PostPhysics`, `water_live.ticks`, which on each tick decides which tiles wake at that tick — from the events that touch a tile and from the simulation's observers by distance with hysteresis (ADR-0080 decision 4, [ADR-0010](0010-deterministic-sim-and-lod-contract.md)), never from the render camera and never from a read-back. What a tick may honestly take as an observer differs by host: in engine-view a walking session with the walker's physics has the walker's body (the observer `walk.cpp` gives the collision's tile ring); a flying session has only the views' eyes (`build_observers` in `anim_lod.h`), which are the camera and so not one; a flight and an offscreen run have none; engine-host's runtime world has its streamed ring's observers and a bound player, and its `render.*` session none. Where there is none, only events wake tiles — a source, an edit, and the scene's own authored wakes at tick 0, which are scene data like its sources (ADR-0080 decision 7) — and water that flows towards an asleep tile meets that tile's edge, closed, until the rows that give engine-view the simulation's observers and the wake by need are built. The system then appends a **tick record** to the host's list of owed ticks: the tick, its length in the state's time (1/60 s times the world rate the host published for that tick, [ADR-0056](0056-one-world-clock-and-the-days-length.md)), its wakes and its events in their fixed order (tick, the kind's declared order, the source's index in its domain, the order of submission). **engine-view gains a `sim::SimScheduler`** at 60 Hz, made only when a capability installs a system in it, so a scene with no such capability makes none. In a session it is stepped from a tick observer that chains whichever observer was set before it (the animated world's, or with `ENGINE_WITH_ECS=OFF` the game runner's or none), step k at session tick k·tick_hz/60, the world rate sampled where step k falls due; with no session it is stepped once for each new frame index and not for a frame drawn again; in a flight, from the flight's hook, once a path frame (decision 7). It has no executor and needs no ECS, so `ENGINE_WITH_ECS=OFF` and the no-ECS presets keep it, and the minimal build, where every capability that would install a system is off, never makes one. **Every host that steps a state on a device owns the scheduler that runs the tick system**: engine-view as above; engine-host's `render.*` session, which opens a device and owns no scheduler today, makes one the same way when it loads a scene with a live domain, stepped from its flights' `before_frame` hook (it already passes one, `ground_before_frame`, where a scene's ground asks, which the new hook chains); engine-host's runtime world (`session.run_headless`), which opens no device, installs only decision 9's coarse system. One function and one table serve every host.

6. **The card walks the queue of ticks itself, so its bytes never depend on how a frame was recorded.** Tick records and their events live on the card across frames:
   - **Staging.** The armed step copies the owed records it has room for, whole and in order, from the frame slot's own host-visible block into **the card's tick queue** (64 records of 16 bytes: the tick's low 32 bits — `SimTick` is wider, and the card compares ticks only within its own queue — a length, the first and count of its entries) and their events and wakes into **the card's event arena** (sized at attach for the domain's largest event, at least one tile's patch of bed heights, 16 KiB at 64² cells, and for a quarter second of small events beside it). A record goes onto the card only once all of its events are there, so a large event — a changed bed's patch — that the slot's block cannot carry in one frame is staged over several, and its tick and every tick after it wait on the host's list: the lag grows, the bytes do not change. What a patch costs is its bytes once over the bus and an apply pass over its cells at its tick.
   - **The walk.** The step then records a run of **sub-step slots, shared by every tick on the queue**, each six passes in this order, so that no sub-step's length is chosen from speeds older than the state it steps:
     1. **begin**, one workgroup: reads the card's cursor and, if the current tick has landed and another is queued, begins it, writing the indirect arguments of the next two passes — the apply's groups for that tick's events and wakes, and `prime`'s groups for the tiles the apply changes — or zero for both when no tick began, or when it began with no event and no wake;
     2. **apply**, indirect: writes the tick's events and wakes into the inputs, the tile table and the woken tiles' starting state, at the tick's own boundary;
     3. **prime**, indirect: the workgroups' records (`prime_main`'s, today) of what the apply changed, so the speeds the next pass reads are the new state's;
     4. **length and ledger**, one workgroup (`dt_main`'s): chooses the sub-step's length by the reference's rule from those records, lands the last one on the tick's end, folds the ledger, and writes the next passes' indirect arguments — the domain's groups, or zero when nothing is left to step;
     5. **faces**, indirect, where faces are stored;
     6. **update**, indirect.

     A tick that has not landed when a frame's slots run out continues in the next frame's first slots, from where the card stands. Which copy of the state is current is a word on the card that every reader reads, so the host-side copy flag `DeviceStep::record` keeps today, and the copies' addresses it puts in the push constants, go: every pass declares both copies, read and write, and the shaders choose by the word.
   - **What this buys.** Each tick is stepped as a unit, from the state the tick before left, its events applied at its own boundary and primed before its first sub-step, and every sub-step chosen by the card from the state alone. How many slots a frame recorded, and when a read-back arrived, change only **how far the drawn state lags** its ticks, never the state the ticks produce (decision 8 names the two things that can). The host never waits.
   - **How many slots.** Twice the sub-steps the frames needed over the last second, by the read-backs, and at least twice the ticks newly staged, up to `water_live.frame.max_slots` (a tunable read once at attach, 16 to begin: four ticks of four sub-steps). A frame records none only when the last read-back showed nothing queued **and** no commit since that read-back's frame staged a tick; otherwise it records at least what that work needs by the same rule, whether or not it stages anything itself. Since slots change only the lag, taking their count from a read-back is allowed.
   - **What holds the records, and what happens when it is full.** The card's queue holds 64 records: a size chosen so that a domain keeping up never waits for room, not a bound anything correct rests on. A record is reclaimed only when the read-back of a frame F — folded when frame F + `frames_in_flight` is recorded — shows the cursor past it, so the records held are the lag at F plus what `frames_in_flight` frames have staged since; with a lag just under the owner's threshold (15 ticks) and a hitch frame staging 15 on top, that is about 60 with the renderer's default two frames in flight and about 75 with three. A record the queue has no room for waits on the host's list, at the cost of lag only. The host's list is made at attach with **128 records**: the queue's 64 and about a second of ticks beside it, more than `frames_in_flight` frames of hitches stage before the read-back that triggers the owner's decision arrives. If it fills before then, the owner's decision starts there: the newest ticks are dropped and counted from that tick. While ticks are dropped, their events are carried in order by one events-only record a frame, so a frozen domain adds at most one record a frame; events that find neither the arena nor the list with room are dropped too, counted, and said once.
   - **What bounds the lag.** The queue, the list, the arena and `max_slots` bound how much is held; what bounds the lag is the owner's decision A, past a quarter second of ticks, acting when the read-back of the frame whose lag passed it is folded, `frames_in_flight` frames later — or sooner, if the host's list fills first.
   - **What it costs when nothing lags.** Every recorded slot past the work is two one-workgroup passes (begin, and length and ledger) and three empty indirect dispatches (apply, prime and update; four where faces are stored), with their barriers (not measured; W191 measures it); with twice the work recorded, about as many idle slots as working ones. No awake tile, nothing queued at the last read-back and nothing staged since its frame: no slot, no pass.

7. **What a frame owes, case by case.**
   - **The remainder of real time below one tick** stays in the host's clock, as for every reader of a tick. A frame that kept up draws the last tick it stepped; a frame that lags draws the state where the card stood, which may be inside a tick. Nothing interpolates or extrapolates a state by the clock's fraction (a surface between two ticks' levels is W192's question).
   - **At a hitch** the host's clock runs at most a quarter second of ticks and drops the rest of the stall, for the state as for everything else on the clock: a stall the clock dropped is not owed. The frame after a hitch stages every owed tick the queue has room for, up to 15; its slots carry what they can and the rest is lag.
   - **Paused** (a session that runs no ticks) a frame owes no new tick; it still records slots while the card may hold queued work — what the last read-back showed queued, and what was staged in the frames since, which the host knows from its commits — and records nothing once both are empty. **At a world rate of 0** the ticks run and have no length: a record carries its events and wakes and no sub-step, and one with neither is not appended.
   - **Past the fastest world rate a live state follows** (ADR-0080's seventh decision for the owner: four times, to start) records carry their events with no length, so the inputs stay current while the state steps nothing, and the domain runs baked for as long as the rate lasts; when the rate comes back its tiles start again as a waking tile does. **A domain whose far field is stale is frozen as it stands instead**, and steps again from where it stood (that decision's second half; staleness arrives with W194's edits the bake does not know, so W191 has no stale domain).
   - **A clock set by hand is not a tick.** A `render.*` request's clock, `--time-of-day`, a capture of a frame drawn again, an editor's scrub: each moves the picture's clocks and steps nothing; the state stands at the tick its ticks left it, and the run's summary says which tick that is. Bringing a state to a time it has not stepped through is ADR-0080 decision 9's (a tile starts again from the steady answer), never a fast-forward of the step inside a frame.
   - **A flight is repeatable.** Path frame f owes the tick of frame f's own clock (one 60 Hz tick of `world_rate / 60` seconds); warm-up frames and the drain after the last path frame owe none; the state starts again from its load at warm-up frame 0 of every repeat, or at path frame 0 of a repeat with no warm-up, which the summary says. **A restart clears everything a lagging repeat could leave**: the host's list, the card's queue, its arena, its cursor and its ledger, and a restart count each read-back carries, so a read-back from before the restart is folded into nothing but statistics; otherwise a repeat that lagged would leave its unlanded ticks queued in front of the next. So repeat r's frame f steps the same ticks from the same state, and a benchmark of a live scene measures the same water every repeat.

8. **The same bytes at any frame rate, while no tick was dropped and no wake was refused.** On one device, a window at 40 frames a second, one at 144, an offscreen run and a replay of the same ticks give the same state, tick for tick, **so long as no tick was dropped** (the owner's decision A) **and no wake was refused** (decision 12: whether a tile's pages can be grown depends on the device's memory at that moment, not on the ticks), with a flight's repeats each started from a cleared queue (decision 7). A dropped tick and a refused wake are each counted and reported; the card's state is `derived` either way (ADR-0080 decision 11); and every case that holds the card to the reference, or one run to another, asserts that neither happened.

9. **The CPU state inside the tick shares the count of ticks and never reads the card.** ADR-0080's first decision, as accepted, has gameplay read the baked answer and, where a game asks for live water it can feel, a coarse state stepped inside the tick by the reference's arithmetic. That state steps in a `Hashed` system of the same capability at `TickPhase::Physics` (`water_live.coarse`, W194's to build), on the same tick, from the same events, which it applies to its own inputs; `water_live.ticks` runs after it at `PostPhysics`, so a presentation offset (ADR-0080 decision 1's "drawn with a presentation-only offset onto the card's surface") may read both of one tick. Nothing `Hashed` reads the host's list, the card's queue, a read-back or the card. The card's state and the coarse one are two functions of one sequence of ticks, held together by a bound on the cell-averaged level that a case checks and nothing nudges. **A host with no device** installs `water_live.coarse` where a game asks for it and nothing else: no list, no frame step, no `water_live.ticks`, so it runs baked or the coarse state.

10. **Read-backs are one slot late at the earliest, never waited for, and never an input to anything hashed or to the state: the texture feedback's shape.** The step's last pass copies the cursor (the last tick landed and how far into the current one), for each tick landed this frame its number, its sub-steps, its shortest and longest sub-step, the ledger's sums and the domain's wet cells, and on a case's request a state hash or a window of cells, into a host-visible block of the frame slot's own. The block is folded when the slot is recorded again, after `begin_frame` has waited for it (`TextureFeedback::fold`'s place), and only if the recording that wrote it was committed. Every record carries **the tick it describes**, because the latency is fixed in frames (`frames_in_flight`) and a frame steps none, one or several ticks. The fold feeds the capability's statistics, the reclaiming of the queue and the arena, the next frames' slot counts, the lag that decision A reads, and the cases — none of which changes a byte of the state. **No read-back decides which tiles are awake**: waking is decision 5's, and whether a tile has settled and may retire is ADR-0080 decision 11's hand-off, taken from a `hashed` source at a fixed tick or declared `derived`, to be designed with the tiers (W194) and the tiles by distance (W195).

11. **A steady frame allocates nothing, compiles nothing and is timed under its own name.** A step's record and its commit make no engine allocation, no device buffer and no pipeline: the host's list, the slots' blocks, the card's queue and arena, the read-back blocks and every pipeline and its specialization are made at attach. A tile's pages, made when it first wakes (ADR-0080 decision 10), are grown by the capability in `prepare`, between frames on the frame's thread, counted like any growth and not steady state; the record of the tick that wakes it is staged only once its pages exist, or once their growth was refused (decision 12), so a growth that succeeds can lag the state and never change it. A frame after the woken set stops changing allocates nothing, and the frame-loop test (`engine_view.frame_loop`, [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#the-frame-loop-allocates-nothing)) holds it with a live scene among its flights. Each registered step's zone is the GPU timer's, summed into `Stats::gpu_frame_steps`, into the benchmark record's `PassMs` as **`frame_steps`** (`PassMs` version 13) and into the summary's `PassPercentiles` as **`frame_steps`** (`PassPercentiles` version 10, one entry per `PassMs` field), counted in `total` and named, so it never falls into `unbucketed`. The step's line in the frame budgets is W195's ("the state's lines in the frame budgets"), set on W191's live scene at native resolution with no upscaler once that scene exists.

12. **Absent, refused, switched off or lost, the scene draws its baked answer and says so once.** A scene that declares no live domain, or whose tier is baked — the default for every scene that says nothing (ADR-0080 decision 6) — makes no capability object, registers no step, installs no system and makes no scheduler in engine-view: the renderer's cost is a loop over no registered step. A domain the device cannot step (`DeviceStep::create` refused it) or whose worst-case tiles, printed at load, pass the memory the capability may hold, runs baked: one line in the log at load naming the domain and the reason, and the summary's block saying `baked` and why. A tile whose pages cannot be grown when it wakes stays asleep, counted, said once per domain: `prepare` tries the growth once, before the waking tick is staged, and on a refusal **the tick is staged without that wake**, its other wakes and events intact, rather than held, because holding it would wait on memory that may never come and turn one tile's refusal into a freeze of the whole domain; the refusal leaves decision 8's bytes, and says so. A flight's restart (decision 7) clears the host's list, the card's queue, arena, cursor and ledger with the state. A lost device takes the card's state with it: the host reloads it as it reloads the scene, and nothing hashed is touched. Nothing is retried a frame, and nothing is said a frame.

13. **The capability graph stays honest.** The renderer declares the registration point and names no capability. `water_live` depends on `gfx`, `sim` and `water_state` and **not on the renderer**: its step is `LiveWater::record(gfx::RenderGraph&, gfx::GpuTimer*, slot, ...)`, and the host's few lines adapt a `FrameStepContext` to it, so a host with a device and no renderer — a device case, a future headless host stepping a state with no picture — records the same step into a graph of its own, submits it and never waits. A host links the capability and registers it; nothing below a host names it. W191 makes engine-view link `sim` unconditionally (today it does only where `scene_collision` or `world` is configured), and keeps its scheduler, its ticks and the adapter in files of the app's own, so `main.cpp` and its two loops grow by a call each and `#if ENGINE_VIEW_WATER_LIVE` appears once, in the file that starts the capability.

14. **A capability whose state is its own data ticks through the scheduler's table, not through `ecs::register_system`.** `water_live` has no entity and no component, and installs its systems with `sim::SimScheduler::add_system`, as `systems/npc` already installs its own. ADR-0038 (proposed) forbids, once accepted, "a capability that ticks outside `ecs::register_system`", and its own consequences call the table "a live executor beside flecs'"; this record reads that rule as binding a system with entity state, and plan 02 §2.8's "anything that ticks links `domain/ecs`" as true of what ticked when it was written. To be written into ADR-0038 when it is accepted.

15. **Two counters in engine-view, kept in step by construction.** ADR-0038 rejected its option 3 because a host holding two clocks would have to keep them in step by hand, and engine-view will hold an executor-less `SimScheduler` beside the animated world's `SimWorld::step` until ADR-0038 moves it. They are kept in step by construction, not by hand: both are 60 Hz, both are stepped from the same tick observer chain by the same integer rule (step k at session tick k·tick_hz/60) or by the same frame index, and they share no state, so a count is all they must agree on; a case holds the scheduler's tick to the animated world's step count after every frame where both step. In a timed flight the animated world does not step at all (its characters are drawn at rest) and the scheduler steps one tick a path frame; nothing reads one from the other.

16. **The registration point is built by a row of its own, before W191 attaches through it** (ADR-0027 decision 3: a missing registration point "lands as its own change before the capability does", and plan 02 §2.8's table gains it in the same change). Its cases are the renderer's: a registered step that adds nothing leaves every capture's bytes unchanged under every rasterizer, the ray path, `--raster direct` and every debug view; a step armed once is recorded once and committed once under a measured-runs redraw, `render_offscreen`'s growth and a capture with the accumulation's cycle and normals; its first pass is the graph's first (`RenderGraph::pass_name(0)`); registering one makes no linear image; the frame after the last removal is the bytes of the frame before the first registration; the registration is refused past `k_max_frame_steps` and without a function or a zone; its zone reaches `Stats`, `PassMs` and `PassPercentiles`; and its record and commit allocate nothing in a counted flight. W191 builds the rest.

## Decisions that are the owner's, each with a recommendation

**A. When the card falls behind its ticks.** With decision 6 a slow frame only lags; the bytes stay those of a run that kept up. But a lag can grow without end on a card or a domain too slow for the frame, so something must happen past a bound. Each choice, with what it does to the bytes and to a pond that filled:
- *(a)* **Past a quarter second of lag, freeze the domain as it stands, say so once, drop and count the ticks it does not step (their events still reach the inputs, one events-only record a frame), and resume when the queue has drained.** The lag that decides is the one a read-back reports for its own frame, but the freeze acts when that read-back is folded, `frames_in_flight` frames later (or when the host's list fills, if sooner), so which tick is dropped first depends on read-back latency, and the lag itself on pacing. The bytes leave those of an unlagged run at the first dropped tick either way, and every later comparison says so; the pond keeps the water it had, and falls behind the simulation's time by what was dropped.
- *(b)* Retire the domain to the baked answer, as ADR-0080's seventh decision does past its rate, and wake it again from there. The bytes start again from the start state; the pond loses the water it filled.
- *(c)* Never drop: let the lag grow, held on the host's list. The bytes stay exactly an unlagged run's and the pond is intact, but its picture falls further behind with every second, without bound; and the list is made at attach (decision 11), so growing it allocates in steady state, while bounding it brings back (a) at the list's bound.
- *(d)* Stall the frame until the card has caught up. The bytes stay exact and the pond intact, but the host waits, which is the spiral a fixed-step simulation exists to avoid.

*Recommended*: (a). The card's state is `derived`, so a frozen or lagging state changes no gameplay and no hash, and a run that has fallen this far behind has already left the unlagged bytes whichever tick is dropped first; a picture minutes behind the world is no picture of it. An offscreen run and a replay keep up by construction (one tick a frame against up to sixteen slots, for a domain whose ticks take up to sixteen sub-steps), so (a) changes no test's bytes, and every case asserts that nothing was dropped.

## Where this strains ADR-0080

- **Decision 10's "a frame submits the ticks it owes (none, one or two)".** None, one or two is the steady case at 30 to 120 frames a second. After a hitch a frame stages up to fifteen ticks (the clock's quarter-second cap), and a frame that lags carries ticks into the next; what bounds a frame's work is its slots (`max_slots`, sixteen: four ticks of four sub-steps, enough for a frame at 15 frames a second whose ticks each take what a surf zone or a fall's foot asks), not a count of ticks.
- **Decision 10's "anything read back from the card arrives a fixed number of ticks later".** A read-back arrives `frames_in_flight` *frames* later; each is labelled with the tick it describes, so the rule holds as "no earlier than", and exactly only while frames keep pace with ticks. Nothing W191 hands on is `hashed`.
- **Decision 10's "the state advances by the simulation's ticks".** It does, tick for tick, while the card keeps up; a lagging frame draws the state behind its ticks, possibly inside one, and under the owner's decision A(a) a domain past a quarter second of lag drops ticks, counted and reported (decision 8).
- **Decision 10's "a tile's pools are made when it first wakes".** Kept, in `prepare` between frames; the tick that wakes a tile waits on the host's list for its pages, a lag that changes no byte. The frame-loop rule then holds from the frame after the woken set stops changing.
- **Decision 4's "live runs the state in every domain the scene declares, within range of an observer".** engine-view has no simulation observer set but the walker's body in a walking session with its physics; a flying session's only observers are the views' eyes, which are the camera and may not wake a tile, and a flight and an offscreen run have none. There, only events wake tiles (a source, an edit, the scene's authored wakes at tick 0), and water flowing towards an asleep tile meets its closed edge until the rows that build the simulation's observers in engine-view and the wake by need. A pond meant to fill beyond the tiles its scene wakes stops at their edge; W191's live scene wakes its whole footprint by authored wakes, and says so.
- **The owner's seventh decision, "a domain follows the world clock up to a stated rate (four times, to start)".** It follows only as far as `max_slots` lets a frame keep up. A domain that takes two sub-steps a 1/60 s tick takes eight at four times; a frame at 15 frames a second owes four such ticks, 32 sub-steps against 16 slots, lags and, past a quarter second, freezes (decision A). So "up to four times" holds for such a domain only above about 30 frames a second (two ticks of eight), and for a domain of one sub-step a tick above about 15; `max_slots` is the tunable that moves it.
- **Decision 4's "a tile starts from the baked answer".** Until W176 builds the baked start, a waking tile, a domain back from past four times the world rate, and a flight's repeat start from the domain's load state (dry, or its authored depths).
- **Decision 10's "reported by tag".** No device-memory tags exist (`domain/gfx`'s `resources.h`); this record keeps W191's form, the module's own byte counters by kind.

## Consequences

- The renderer gains a second registration point beside the frame pass: one header, two registration calls, a loop of calls at the top of `record_frame` and a loop of commits in `submit_recorded`. `record_frame`'s count of inline `add_pass` calls does not grow (the step's passes are the capability's). Raises, in files of the rows' own under `tools/structure-raises/`: `renderer.record_frame.lines` by that loop (the registration point's row); `engine_view.main_cpp.lines`, `engine_view.run_view.lines`, `engine_view.run_offscreen.lines`, `engine_view.main_cpp.if_engine_view` and `engine_view.if_engine_view` by W191's calls and its one switch.
- Every frame mode steps a live state, so a debug view, the direct path and a capture show the water where its ticks put it. A picture that stops changing while its simulation keeps time — what a frame pass would have given — cannot happen.
- **A frame's recording count stops mattering to anything that steps.** The commit is the one place a capability learns that a recording reached the queue; the renderer's own texture feedback marks its read-back pending at record and could move to the same rule (not done here).
- The state's work on the card is a queue the card walks: the host stages, the card steps, a read-back reclaims. Its cost is two one-workgroup passes and three or four empty indirect dispatches for each idle slot, and its gain is that no choice the host makes for a frame — slots, timing, a redraw — reaches the state's bytes.
- engine-view owns a scheduler whenever a capability installs a system (decision 15). The world rate that a tick's length follows is published by the host, so it follows the world clock exactly as the world clock does: engine-view acts on a rate key after the frame's ticks have run, so a rate change takes effect at the next frame's ticks, today for the water as for the waves; its tick therefore depends on where the frames fell, and a recording must log the tick at which a rate change took effect, or its replay is a different input (W191 checks what the recording header's `clock` block holds today).
- A source on the coarser clock (`set_source(..., every > 1)`) cannot follow sub-steps the card chooses, as `advance` already refuses it; the frame step takes the compensated sum only.
- `water_live`'s determinism is `derived` (ADR-0080 decision 11; W175 made it so on 2026-11-26, after two cards' states were found to part); the same bytes twice on one device stays a case, to which W191 adds two runs at different frame rates and slot counts and an offscreen run of the same ticks, each asserting that no tick was dropped and no wake refused.
- New mirrored structs — the queue's record, an event's header in the arena, the tile table's entry, the cursor and the read-back's record — take `ENGINE_EXPECT_SIZE` entries in `water_live`'s size table and are mirrored once in its shader. The renderer gains no hot type (`FrameStepContext` is one per recording).
- Forbidden from here on: a simulation's step in a frame pass; a step that reads or writes a frame's image or depends on a view, a camera or a frame's clock; a step that counts its own time from `dt_s` or a frame's clocks; anything stepped per recording rather than per handed-over frame; a tick shortened, or its events moved off its boundary, to fit a frame; a read-back waited for in a frame; a read-back deciding which tiles are awake; anything `hashed` reading the host's list, the queue, a read-back or the card.

## What it costs

- **Absent**: nothing measurable. The renderer loops over zero registered steps and zero commits; no linear image, no buffer, no pass, no pipeline. engine-view makes no scheduler. `domain/sim` is already in every build.
- **Present, nothing awake and nothing queued**: one indirect call a recording and one a commit; the tick system's per-tick work (wakes, events, a list append); the scheduler's eight phases with one system in one of them, per 60 Hz tick (to be measured by W191 against an empty scheduler).
- **Present and stepping**: W175's measured step — about 0.1 ms a sub-step at 2,048² cells on the RTX 5090, an upper bound beside other work — plus, per slot, the begin pass and the length and ledger pass (one workgroup each), per idle slot three empty indirect dispatches (four where faces are stored); the copy of newly staged records and events, an apply pass and a prime over what it changed at each tick with events or wakes, and the read-back's copy, all inside the `frame_steps` zone. Memory: the card's queue (1 KiB), the event arena (a tile's patch and a quarter second of small events), the slots' blocks and read-back blocks (a few kilobytes each), beside the state's own (ADR-0080's tables).

## Revisit when

- A second simulation registers a frame step (a cloth, a smoke): the context's contents, the cap of four, and whether two steps' order should be declared rather than taken from registration order.
- A domain would be cheaper on a cadence of its own, a whole fraction of the world's step (a slow pond on a coarser tick): a shortcut of ADR-0080 decision 3's kind, with its switch, judged by the owner's eye.
- A step needs something only the frame knows — a reader on the frame's visible list, a simulation level of detail by the cut (which ADR-0080 decision 4 forbids for the water) — or a second queue makes it worth overlapping the step with the frame's rasterization: an async-compute form of the frame step is then a new decision.
- The card can loop on its own commands (device-generated commands): decision 6's recorded slots and their idle cost go.
- ADR-0038 is accepted and engine-view moves to the one scheduler: decision 15's second counter goes, and decision 14's reading is written into ADR-0038.
- W191's live scene measures the idle slots, the scheduler's empty phases or the commit at more than noise in a frame budget.
