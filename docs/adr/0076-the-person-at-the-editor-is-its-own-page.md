# ADR-0076: The person at the editor is the editor's own page, and a close is tied to what that page showed

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Plan references:** docs/plan/06-agent-tooling.md §6.8, §6.10; ADR-0033; ADR-0072; roadmap A24 (on A4 and A23), A50 to A56
- **Docs touched:** `docs/subsystems/engine_shell.md` ("The review queue (designed, not built)"); later, as the rows land, `docs/subsystems/apps.md` (engine-editor's flags), `docs/subsystems/protocol.md` (the seat, the digest) and `AGENTS.md` (the agents' rule, A52)

## Context

A review item raised for a person is closed only under a role the roles file marks `human` (A23, [ADR-0072](0072-the-task-ledger-beside-the-document.md)). engine-editor loads no roles file, its page is the operator's (A4: unbound, by a cookie a printed ticket bought), and the printed token and `shell` address are on stdout, which whoever started the editor reads — sometimes an agent, and an agent may be attached to the same editor through engine-mcp with the operator's announcement or with a credential. So "the operator" is not "a person", and nothing said which connection is the person.

The first pass of this design let a host's own start line confer the human role on stdio. A review of it found that this is the cheapest route an agent has, not a corner: `engine-cli` passes `--role` on to the host it starts, so `engine-cli --roles … --role reviewer review.close …` closes a person's item. The same review found that a close was bound to nothing the person saw (a length of the item's history, which stops moving at its cap, and journal numbers stamped by two processes' histories); that on an editor with a wide view policy the printed token would read classified bytes with no look; that the seat's cookie could be replayed by any local program through routes the stated limit left out; that the served shell files are code that runs as the person; that the first seat kept the seat; that a name in the queue is the caller's claim; that a classified item could be forged and its look rested on a decision per placement the content classes' design does not have; and that the test hook is a scripted seat. This record is the second pass.

The person also has to keep editing as the page does today (as the editor's `director`), while the shipped human role, `reviewer`, may change no document.

## Decision

1. **No start line confers a human role.** A person is known by the editor's seat or by a credential the operator issued for a role marked human, and by nothing else. `engine-host` refuses `--role <a human role>` in every mode, `engine-cli` refuses it before any host starts, and `engine-mcp` refuses to start in one or to forward one. How a caller is known to be a person is one field (`Caller::human_via`: none, the seat, or a credential's name), set only where the caller is resolved and read there alone; every ledger write records how its caller was known (`via`); ids are random; an item carries a revision. Those are built beside this design as the protocol's follow-up to A4 and A23, and the seat stands on them.
2. **The person is the editor's own page, a seat.** `engine-editor --roles <file> --person <name> [--person-role <role>]`: the editor makes a seat ticket in process and gives it only to its own web view's navigation, never printed or written; spending it buys a seat cookie; the socket carrying it is the seat. **The newest seat wins**: every navigation of the view, a reload included, goes through a fresh ticket, and spending one closes every socket that presented an older seat cookie; a page refused the seat has one button, which asks the editor to navigate it again. Where the platform says which process owns a socket's peer, a seat socket is accepted only from the web view's browser process or a child of it. The seat's web view keeps its profile in memory, outside the document, has no developer tools, and does not exist while a variable that changes the web view's runtime or its arguments is set. With `--person`, `--shell` is refused and the shell's files are hashed at start; a file whose bytes changed is not served to the seat and ends it until a restart. engine-host has no seat.
3. **The seat holds two roles.** Its working role is the editor's (`director`), for everything a call runs as; its conferred human role (the roles file's one role marked human, or `--person-role`) is what `human_via` names, and only the checks that ask for a person read it.
4. **With a seat, every other connection is an agent's as far as classified content goes.** On an editor started with `--person`, every connection but the seat — the printed token, the printed shell address, any credential naming no policy — runs under `agent-default` narrowed by the editor's policy; the editor's own policy is reached only by the person's look, in the native window. **The look is a scene of its own**, holding the subject alone, instantiated under the editor's policy and drawn in place of the main viewport while it lasts; the main scene is never widened, so a scene stays instantiated under one policy. The look reads the classes from the scene's own decisions, never from the item, pins the subject's record, mesh and class, and ends when any changes. Only the host raises a classified item.
5. **A close is tied to what the person saw.** The host computes a digest of an item's subject — the composed properties of its records and their ancestors, the layer stack's order, and the content hash of every asset and blob they name — stamps it at the raise, and computes it again when the item's detail is opened and at the close, never for a list's rows. The close carries the item's revision and the digest the page showed, and is refused on any difference; the decision records the digest it was made at. Promotion carries its dry run's digest. **The check hashes content and never trusts a file's time.**
6. **A name is a claim unless the connection is bound.** `--person <name>` reserves that actor name against every other connection; an unbound connection's actor is drawn as what it says it is, marked as the operator's connection.
7. **The test hook is a scripted seat**, so `--person` with `--test-page` or `--capture` is accepted only on a document under the tests' scratch root, its closes are recorded as the test page's, and it acts only on the ids its command line names.
8. **What this is not**, in the content classes' words: against a protocol client a wall, against a process of the same user a rule and an audit trail. Whatever can drive the desktop can click the seat; that an agent never starts an editor with `--person` outside a test's scratch document and never operates its queue is the repository's rule, with a lint (A52).

## Consequences

- A person's item can be closed at an editor and nowhere a browser reaches; a browser against engine-host reads the queue only for those items. Until the seat is built (A53, A54), a person's item is closed only over a listener with a credential the operator issued for a human role.
- With `--roles`, A4's credentials presented to the editor bind to the roles the file defines, so an agent with a writing credential may write there (today every credential there only reads). The editor refuses a roles file without its own working role.
- `review.close` and `doc.promote` gain a `seen` that a client must fill from what it drew; a client that sends none closes an agent's item as today and is refused a person's. A subject naming large assets costs their reading at a detail's opening and at the close (estimated about a second for 200 MB; A56 measures it).
- `editor.layout` becomes the seat's on an editor with a seat. The test page cannot run against a real document with a seat.
- The seat certifies a click in the editor's window, not a person. The audit (`closed_via`, with how the editor was started) says what it can; it cannot tell a synthetic click from a person's.
- Until the rest under a seat is built (row 12 of the design), an editor started with `--person` refuses a view policy wider than `agent-default`.

## Alternatives

- **A start line that confers a person only on a real console** (`engine-host --console` with stdin a console, never a pipe), with `engine-cli` still refusing. Kept as the answer if the owner wants a person at a terminal; not taken, because an agent can open a console as readily as a pipe, and the editor is where the picture is.
- **One role per connection**, a role marked human that may also edit (an `owner` role in the operator's roles file), the seat bound to it. Simpler, but the person's edits then depend on what the operator's human role allows, and the shipped `reviewer` would no longer be the person's role at the editor.
- **The shell's bytes embedded in the executable at build time** instead of hashed at start. Not taken: a process that can write the shell directory can write the executable beside it, so embedding buys nothing against it, and it would give the shell a second serving path for the seat alone.
- **Widening the subject's placements inside the main scene** for a look. Not taken: it needs a decision per instance, which costs the content classes' design its one-policy-per-scene invariant and opens the route by which a widened placement, its mesh changed, draws what nobody chose.
- **Journal numbers as the drift that decides.** Not taken: two processes' journals are two histories, an asset rewritten makes no journal entry, and an undo-redo around a close leaves the numbers equal.

## Open questions

None of the four questions for the owner is open. Decided on 2026-11-18: (1) **the viewport at rest is an option of the editor** (the owner): an agent that may have trouble with classified content runs the editor held, and an agent or a person that has none runs it not held. In the coordinator's reading: held is the default (the coordinator's choice) and is decision 4 above; not held has no look, draws at the editor's policy from the start, and runs the editor's connections under the policy the content classes give any host, every output naming its policy; the option is not a view policy and widens none, and an editor at `agent-default` draws the same either way; how it is given is the building row's to decide, and only the held state needs the rest and the look built. (2) **no sign-in prompt on closing** (the owner: "No prompt"): the question was whether closing a person's item should also ask for a system sign-in (Windows Hello) on each close, which a synthetic click cannot pass; with no prompt, the protection against a program that drives the desktop stays the agents' rule (A52) and the record of how each item was closed (`closed_via`). (3) **The seat holds two roles**, and (4) **a browser against engine-host never closes a person's item**: both decided by the coordinator as this record recommended, and reversible. Of the design: (5) whether a person can judge a subject without its surroundings, and if not, whether the look's scene carries the main scene's sky and lights or the content classes take a decision per instance; (6) "the shell never names a path" is recommended, not decided.

## When to revisit

When a listener is reachable from another machine; when a browser can present a credential without a printed address; when more than one person shares an editor; when the platform names a socket's peer reliably (or does not, which leaves the seat its cookie alone); and when the owner answers the questions above.
