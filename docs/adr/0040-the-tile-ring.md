# ADR-0040: The world's tile ring is tier assignment over tiles, with consumers in a declared order

## Context

Phase 3 had every piece a streamed world needs and nothing that tied them together. The simulation's tier assignment scores entities against a set of observers with hysteresis and rate limits (`sim::TierAssignment`, 05 §5.4); the materialization driver takes a document a tile at a time (`sim::Materializer`, `MaterializeScope::of_tile`); reconciliation reads a tile back from a `sim::TileStore` (05 §5.5), for which only a test's fake existed; the ruins capability assembles a tile's building from its seed in sections or block by block (E34); and the renderer drew a scene that was fixed at load. Nothing decided which tiles are live, when, in what representation, or in which order the pieces act on a tile.

The questions a world streamer has to answer, and the alternatives that were on the table:

1. **What decides a tile's ring.** A radius round a camera in square tiles is the usual answer and a second LOD policy beside the simulation's, which would disagree with it at every ring boundary and knows one observer. Tier assignment already is "the minimum over observers of distance divided by weight, banded, with hysteresis and a budget".
2. **Who acts on a tile, and in which order.** A virtual interface per subsystem, or a table like `sim::MaterializationHooks`. The order matters: reconciliation resolves the entities the document's materialization made, and a tile's projections must be written while the entities are still held.
3. **When the ring runs.** As a system in the scheduler's table, or between ticks. The entity store's materialization hook refuses a call while its world is deferred, which is to say inside a tick.
4. **How the renderer takes a tile's instances.** A scene reloaded per change, a per-tile instance block with its own pair range, or a tail replaced between frames behind a fixed prefix.
5. **The defaults**: tile size, ring radii, what each ring draws, the hysteresis, the budget.

## Decision

1. **A tile's ring is `sim::TierAssignment` over tiles.** One tier per ring and one more for "inactive"; boundaries are the radii times the tile size; the score is the ground-plane distance from each observer to the tile's centre, divided by its weight, minimum over observers. The ring inherits the tier assignment's hysteresis (multiplicative), its rate limits (nearest promoted first, farthest demoted first, the rest deferred) and its determinism. Observers are projected to the ground: a tile is a column (03 §3.7). An update scores only the tiles within the outermost radius plus one tile of some observer, merged with the tiles still active, **sorted by tile key (x, then z, signed)**, so the changes it reports are in tile order and the same observer sequence gives the same events byte for byte.
2. **Consumers are a table, called in a declared order.** A row is a name, a context, the rings it acts in and four function pointers — `activate`, `change_ring`, `deactivate`, `commit`. Activation and a move between rings call consumers in registration order; deactivation calls them in **reverse**; within one event the consumers it deactivates run before those it activates or moves. A tile leaving a consumer's rings is a deactivation to that consumer while it stays active for others. `commit` runs once per update per consumer whose tiles changed. The three consumers this change registers, in order: the ruins (`RuinsTiles`), the document (`DocumentTiles`), the store (`StoreTiles`).
3. **The ring runs between ticks, driven by the host.** It is not a scheduler system: engine-view updates it once a frame before the frame is begun, engine-host's headless run once between every two ticks. A first fill (a new camera, a flythrough's repeat, a headless call's start) lifts the budget; a flythrough's repeat clears the ring first, because with hysteresis the state depends on the path to it.
4. **The renderer takes a tail behind a fixed prefix.** A dynamic scene is its load's instances followed by a tail the caller replaces whole between frames, after the device has finished the frames in flight. The prefix's instances and pairs keep their numbers. The ruins consumer hands the tail over in tile order, so a set of tiles has one numbering of its pairs whatever order they arrived in. Every per-pair buffer is laid out by a stride that grows (×1.5 in pages of 4,096 pairs) rather than by the pair count; only the cull's dispatch takes the count. The 2^24-pair limit stays a refusal, checked before anything is allocated; the ruins consumer keeps its tail under it by deciding, at its commit and over every tile it holds, which tiles are drawn — by ring, innermost first, then nearest first — so a world withholds its farthest buildings and never a frame, and never a building beside the camera for one at the horizon (deciding per event, in tile order, did exactly that; E35). A dynamic scene is rigid and rasterized: no ray tracing chain and no deformed-vertex pool.
5. **Defaults**: 32 m tiles; rings of 1.5 tiles drawn in blocks, 8 tiles in sections with their debris and 24 tiles in sections without (`Detail::walls`); hysteresis 0.15; 8 activations and 16 deactivations an update (tunables `world.ring.max_activations`, `world.ring.max_deactivations`); the simulation — materialization and reconciliation — in the inner two rings. A scene file's `world` block and a headless run's `stream` override every one of them; the ruins entries' own tile size must be the world's.
6. **The store's tile id** is x in the high 32 bits and z in the low, each its two's complement — the packing engine-host's write-back events already used — so a tile's events and its snapshot share one id.

## Consequences

- Two observers, a camera and a quest marker are observers alike; the ring and the simulation's tiers can never disagree about distance, because they are one function.
- A new consumer is a row, and a world without ruins, a document or a store has no row for it and pays nothing (the world capability requires the store capability; the ruins consumer is compiled only where the ruins capability is).
- The order is a rule a consumer can rely on: the store's reconciliation sees the document's entities on activation and writes a tile's projections while they are still held on deactivation.
- The ring cannot run inside a tick; a system that wants a tile's state reads what the consumers left.
- The renderer's tail is rewritten whole on every change after the prefix. E35 measured what that costs with the kits this project has (3.2 ms a change for a thousand buildings of one-cluster boxes, 15–17 ms for E33's ashlar sections, whose tail is eight million pairs, and 34 ms at the 2^24 budget); per-tile blocks whose pair entries are expanded on the GPU, and double-buffered tables, are the next step, and they are a change inside `GpuScene` that keeps this decision.
- A ring's representation must cut **pairs**, not only triangles: the cull pays per (instance, cluster) of the whole DAG, so a walls-only ring of the ashlar kit costs nearly what its sections do. The default outer ring holds about 970 buildings along the desert flythrough, which the ashlar kit would take past 2^24 (33 thousand pairs a building); E35 ran that kit with rings of 1.5, 6 and 10 tiles. The far tier (E33's slab) is what lets the outer ring grow.
- A streamed world has no traced shadows or rays and no deformation until the tail learns them.
- Nothing cross-fades a tile's two instance sets; E35 measured the pop.

## Revisit when

- A second observer kind needs a ring shape tier assignment cannot express (a cone, a portal, a vertical extent).
- A consumer needs to act inside a tick, or two consumers need an order the declared one cannot give.
- The tail's rewrite cost, measured at a game's own movement speed rather than a flythrough's 30–50 m/s, is a frame budget line; or the tail needs ray tracing or deformation.
- The far tier exists and the outer ring's radius or representation should change with it, or a game's tile size (the plan's 64–128 m) replaces 32 m.
