# ADR-0046: Scene generators register themselves; the scene reader and the world ring look them up by name

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-26; built 2026-09-27; accepted by the owner on 2026-09-29, after he had flown and walked the scenes it reads (["As built"](#as-built-2026-09-27) says where the building departed from the decision as written)
- **Plan references:** docs/plan/02-architecture.md §2.8 (the registration points a capability attaches through), docs/plan/07-content-pipeline.md §7.6 (procedural generation: ruins, terrain, buildings as generators), docs/plan/03-data-model.md §3.7 (one tile grid, many consumers). Builds on [ADR-0027](0027-additive-capabilities.md) (decisions 1 and 3) and [ADR-0040](0040-the-tile-ring.md) (the ring's consumers in a declared order); **supersedes [ADR-0037](0037-scene-reader-links-ruins-where-configured.md) when accepted and built**, and closes the revisit clause of [ADR-0043](0043-dunes-as-a-function-of-time.md) and [ADR-0044](0044-island-city-plan-and-building-grammar.md) that named this design as the owner's.
- **Docs touched:** [02 §2.8](../plan/02-architecture.md#28-adding-a-capability) (a row in the registration table), [07 §7.6](../plan/07-content-pipeline.md#76-procedural-generation-volume-content) (status note); when built: [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md), [world](../subsystems/world.md), [ruins](../subsystems/ruins.md), [terrain](../subsystems/terrain.md), [city](../subsystems/city.md), and a new subsystem page for the registry.

## Context

Three generators now expand content the renderer draws and the world ring streams, and each attaches by a different route:

- **The ruins** ([ruins](../subsystems/ruins.md)): the scene reader (`renderer::read_scene_file`, `systems/renderer/src/scene.cpp`) links the capability when the configuration has it and expands a scene's `ruins` entry under `ENGINE_RENDERER_RUINS`; the world ring's `RuinsTiles` consumer is compiled into `systems/world` under the same condition. That was accepted as an interim by [ADR-0037](0037-scene-reader-links-ruins-where-configured.md), with "the moment a second generator exists" as its revisit trigger.
- **The dune generator** ([terrain](../subsystems/terrain.md), [ADR-0043](0043-dunes-as-a-function-of-time.md)): the same conditional link, a second time, under `ENGINE_RENDERER_TERRAIN` in `terrain.cpp` (the sampler asks `desc.generator == dunes`) and `ENGINE_WORLD_TERRAIN` for `TerrainTiles`. ADR-0043 said so and named this record as the owner's to write.
- **Island City** ([city](../subsystems/city.md), [ADR-0044](0044-island-city-plan-and-building-grammar.md)): deliberately linked by nothing; a city reaches the renderer only as a scene fragment `engine-content city fragment` writes, and the per-tile query that a world consumer would call has no consumer.

Two of the three are the pattern ADR-0037 allowed once and forbade extending; the third has no way in. The renderer, which is not a capability, names two capabilities in its `CMakeLists.txt`, and `systems/world` names the same two; `modules.json` shows edges the capability graph does not describe as requirements. Each new generator would add another `#if`, another conditional dependency, another consumer file, in two modules — exactly the surface ADR-0027 decision 1 forbids ("a capability never becomes a dependency of a module that is not part of it") and decision 3 says to design as a registration point rather than grow by exception.

What the three have in common, seen from the reader and the ring: a scene names a generator (a field, `ruins`; an enum, `terrain.generator`; a fragment on disk, the city), the generator is a **pure function of the scene's parameters, the world seed and a tile**, and its output is one of two things — **placements** (instances of a few meshes, with tags: ruins, city buildings, tomorrow scatter and roads) or **ground** (a height, normal and material function of position and time, and a mesh of it per tile or per grid: the dunes, the waves the renderer has always drawn). Placement generators need to ask the ground where it is (the ruins' `Ground`, the city's lots). The ring's consumers are the same functions applied per tile as it activates and deactivates ([world](../subsystems/world.md), "The consumers").

## Decision

1. **A registry of scene generators, below every module that reads a scene or streams a tile.** A new plain module (working name `domain/scene_gen`; not a capability, no dependency above `core` and `schemas`) declares two descriptor kinds and one registry:
   - `GroundProviderDesc { name; make(const TerrainEntry&, Context&) -> GroundProvider }` where a `GroundProvider` answers `height(x, z, t)`, `normal`, `material`, builds a tile or a grid of itself at a spacing, and re-evaluates a grid at a new `t` (what the renderer's `TerrainSampler` and `TerrainTimeLapse` need, and what the ruins' `Ground` and the city's lots query).
   - `PlacementGeneratorDesc { name; expand(const GeneratorEntry&, const Ground&, Context&) -> Placements; tile(const GeneratorEntry&, TileCoord, const Ground&, Context&) -> Placements }` where `Placements` is instances of named meshes (a path the reader resolves through the derived-data cache as it does today) with a world transform, a material base and a tag; `expand` is the whole scene, `tile` one tile of it, and the two must agree (a tile's placements are the whole expansion restricted to the tile, which the ruins and the city already test).
   - `GeneratorRegistry::global()`: constant-initialized descriptors added by a static `Registrar` in the capability's own source, the way `schema::Registry` and `MaterializeRegistry` are filled today; whole-archive linking (`engine_module(... WHOLE_ARCHIVE)`) keeps the registrar alive. **A host links the capabilities its configuration has, as it already does; nothing below the host names one.** Lookup is by name; a scene naming a generator the executable does not carry is refused with a sentence naming it, as the `#else` branches do now.
2. **The scene names its generators as data.** `engine.scene.Terrain` gains `provider: string` (`@since`; the `generator` enum stays readable and maps to the names `waves` and `dunes`), and a scene's placement entries become a list of `{generator: string, params: json}` beside the existing `ruins` field, which stays readable as the entry named `ruins`. Names are the capability's (`ruins`, `dunes`, `city`); `waves` is the renderer's own provider, registered by the renderer itself, so the one path serves the default too.
3. **The reader and the ring consume the registry and nothing else.** `read_scene_file` resolves the ground provider first, then walks the placement entries in file order and hands each the ground; the ring's document, ruins and terrain consumers become one `GeneratorTiles` consumer per registered generator the scene names, in the declared order ADR-0040 keeps, so a tile's ground is built before the placements that stand on it. `ENGINE_RENDERER_RUINS`, `ENGINE_RENDERER_TERRAIN`, `ENGINE_WORLD_RUINS` and `ENGINE_WORLD_TERRAIN` disappear, with the conditional dependencies that carried them; `renderer -> ruins` and `world -> terrain` leave `modules.json`.
4. **The registry carries no state and adds no seam for one capability to reach another.** A generator sees the scene entry, the world seed, the tile, the job system and the ground it was handed — the `Context` — and nothing of any other generator. A generator that wants another's output (scatter that avoids ruins) asks the ground or the placements it is handed in the declared order, never the registry; that keeps the per-tile determinism ADR-0043 and ADR-0044 rest on, since every generator stays a function of (entry, seed, tile, ground).
5. **The proof stays the same.** A test-only generator registered from the renderer's tests proves the reader expands through the registry with no capability linked; the minimal build proves nothing depends on any of them; the ruins', the dunes' and the city's golden hashes do not move when they move onto the registry, because the functions do not change, only who calls them.

## Consequences

- A fourth generator (scatter, roads, a rock field) is one module with a registrar; nothing in `renderer`, `world` or the hosts changes to draw or stream it.
- Island City gets its way in: the city's per-tile query becomes its `tile`, and the proxy fragment stops being the only route to a picture.
- The renderer's `TerrainSampler` and the waves heightfield are split: the sampler becomes the reader's view of a `GroundProvider`, the waves a provider like any other, and the time-lapse's re-evaluation and the rings ([terrain](../subsystems/terrain.md), Not-yet) attach to the provider interface rather than to `terrain.cpp`'s `#if`.
- Cost: one indirection per generator per scene read or tile activation — a lookup by name and a call through a function pointer — which is nothing beside what a generator does. No per-frame cost.
- A schema change to `engine.scene.Terrain` and the scene's placement list, both `@since`-versioned; every committed scene keeps reading.
- ADR-0037's exception ends; its record stays as the history of why the interim was taken.
- **Which generators are the engine's and which a game's** (owner, 2026-09-27). A mechanism with parameters is the engine's; a choice of parameters, seeds and rules is a game's. So the dune generator stays in the engine as the terrain capability's generator — bands and primitives on a lattice, the wind record, Bagnold's celerity, the repose bound, tiles and sampler, the rings, the time-lapse, and every stylization knob such as a per-band celerity scale, because a knob is a mechanism even when one game is the only one turning it — with the erg and the overlook as its neutral test scenes under Apache-2.0, since it is also the capability's acceptance case and its benchmark in the engine's own CI, and it would rot outside them. Desert Survival's repository ([ADR-0047](0047-reference-games-are-licensed-apart-from-the-engine.md)) holds the desert itself: its band table and wind climate, its storm schedule, the seeded rules that place oases and ruins, its stylization choices, its gameplay, and its assets; and it brings generators of its own — an oasis solver among them — through this registry, never by editing the engine's scene reader. The same split holds for a sandstorm: the volumetric is a capability, the storm's schedule and its effect on the player are the game's. An Apache generator hands nobody the game: a clone needs the tuned world and the assets, which is where ADR-0047 puts the protection.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keep linking where configured, three times.** Each generator adds a conditional dependency to two modules that are not capabilities and an `#if` to each; ADR-0037 forbade the second case for that reason, and the third (the city) could not even take the route without a third `#if`.
- **A caller-supplied expander in `SceneFileOptions`** (ADR-0037's option 2). Two hosts, the content tool and every scene-reading test would wire the same callbacks, and the ring would need its own set; the reader would still have to know each field to hand over.
- **Make the generators foundations.** A studio without a desert or a city would build and ship a dune field and a building grammar; the brief every capability was built to asks for it to be removable, and the minimal preset is the proof.
- **One `Generator` kind for both ground and placements.** Ground is a function the placements consume; making it a placement with a special tag would put the ordering rule inside the data instead of the registry. Two kinds, one order.

## As built (2026-09-27)

Built in six steps, each a commit that built and passed: the registry ([scene_gen](../subsystems/scene_gen.md)); the waves as the renderer's own provider and the dunes as the terrain capability's; the ruins as a placement generator; the dunes' world tiles and Island City; the consumer order; the proofs. The decision stands. Where the code departs from the text above, it is here, with why:

- **`scene_gen` depends on `geometry`**, not only on `core` and `schemas`: a ground's rings round a camera are chunks of cluster LOD DAGs, so the rings' table names `geometry::ClusterLodMesh`. Both are domain modules; nothing above the domain layer and no capability is a dependency.
- **A ground provider answers `height`, `floor`, `grid`, `evaluate`, `travel_m`, `make_rings` and `open_tiles`**, a table of plain functions over the state `make` allocated, not `height(x, z, t)`, `normal` and `material`. The surface is at the ground's own time and another time is `evaluate` over a window in 64 × 64 blocks, because that is how the time-lapse re-evaluates and a call a point would cost it its budget. `floor` is what a building stands on (the dunes' interdune floor), which the ADR's `Ground` query meant and did not name. Normals and materials stay the renderer's, computed from the heights and the scene's ridges and basins as before: moving them would have changed arithmetic the goldens pin, and neither ground has a classification of its own yet. The scene's ridges and basins, which every ground adds the same way, moved into `scene_gen` (`terrain_features.h`) in one copy.
- **A placement generator has state**: `open` reads what the entry names once (a kit, a plan) and `close` frees it; `expand` is the whole entry, `meshes` what a streamed scene holds resident, `tile` one tile in its ring's representation, and `occupies` and `representation` let the ring skip a tile with nothing on it or a ring change that draws the same. A stateless `expand(entry, ground)` would have read a kit or a plan per tile, thousands of times a flight. `Placements` carries no material base: a placement names a mesh, and the mesh's materials come through the scene's mesh table as every mesh's do.
- **The `Context` grew** the scene's directory and where the entry is (for paths and for sentences), a streamed world's block and the tile's ring (for the representation), and a job pool made on first ask.
- **The ring has one ground consumer and one placements consumer**, not one `GeneratorTiles` consumer per generator: the renderer takes one tail, and the pair budget is decided at the commit over every tile of every entry, which a consumer per generator could not do without a second arbiter. Inside it the entries are asked in the scene's order. The declared order is **document, store, ground, placements** (`world::add_scene_consumers` states the last two): ADR-0040 as written registered the ruins first, and nothing rested on that; the ground goes after the store so its tile record is written before the store snapshots a tile, and before the placements so it is built before anything stands on it.
- **The dunes' world tiles split** along the registry's line: the ground's half (the tile's field, its overlay, its lag) is the terrain capability's, handed out by `open_tiles`, and the store's half is the world's generic ground consumer, which files each tile's record under the provider's record type through callbacks (`scene_gen::TileRecords`). The record's key did not change, so a save's overlays still read.
- **The legacy spellings read in a fixed place**: a `ruins` entry is the generator `ruins` with the entry as its parameters, and the `ruins` entries come before the `placements` entries; a terrain naming both a `provider` and a `generator` that disagree is refused, naming both.
- **The check behind decision 3 is mechanical now**: `engine_module()` refuses, in every configuration, a module that is not a capability listing one among its dependencies, and a capability listing another it has not declared with `engine_capability_requires`. `modules.json` shows no `renderer -> ruins`, `renderer -> terrain`, `world -> ruins` or `world -> terrain`, and a configure that tried one would stop.
- **The `TerrainTimeLapse` of decision 1 is `TerrainMotion`** (`terrain_time.h`), and the rings the consequences name as Not-yet had been built by the time this was: both attach to the provider (`evaluate`, `travel_m`, `make_rings`), and neither's arithmetic moved.

## Revisit when

- A renderer draws the ground's world tiles: the ground consumer's sink is nobody's today (engine-view's world draws the scene's one terrain mesh), and the sink's type is the tile coordinates alone; a renderer that takes tiles wants the provider to say how to mesh one, which is `GroundTilesOps` growing a mesh entry, not a second path.
- A ground wants its own normals or materials (a rock field with strata, a city's paving): `GroundOps` grows `normal` and `material`, null meaning the renderer's classification from heights as today.
- A generator needs to read another generator's placements rather than the ground (scatter round ruins): the `Context` grows a read-only view of the placements produced before it in the declared order, still never the registry.
- The content build wants the same descriptors for its `derived` steps (`engine-content ruins`, `city fragment`, `terrain`): the descriptor gains a `report`/`write` entry point and `engine-content` drops its per-capability command tables.
- A generator wants GPU work at scene read (a pass that bakes something): it registers a render-graph pass through `RenderGraph::add_pass` from its `make`, the registration point that already exists; the registry does not learn about the GPU.
