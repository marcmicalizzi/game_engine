# ADR-0026: Deformable volumes as a first-class physics primitive: cage separate from the render mesh, solver-agnostic, simulation-LOD'd

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-17
- **Plan references:** docs/plan/05-simulation.md §5.14, docs/plan/04-renderer.md §4.3 and §4.5, docs/plan/07-content-pipeline.md §7.10, docs/plan/09-testing-profiling.md §9.6, docs/plan/10-roadmap-risks.md §10.2 (Phases 1 and 6), §10.4, §10.5 (E19–E25). Extends ADR-0020; does not supersede it.

## Context

ADR-0020 made destruction and surface deformation defaults of the world and put gameplay soft bodies on Jolt's XPBD at LOD0. It left volumetric deformation as a narrow case ("jelly-like objects") and finite-element deformation out of scope. The owner wants volumetric soft-body and soft-tissue deformation as a general engine primitive instead: an asset describes deformable volumes and their physical structure, and the engine computes deformation from animation, collision, contact, pressure, acceleration, and external forces. The consumers are soft tissue under locomotion, flesh pressed against objects, grabbing and impact, cushions and foam and fruit, layered anatomy with cavities, wounds in games that model them, and creatures whose shape answers to forces rather than only to bones.

Engines usually get this wrong in one of three ways: the deformer is a character-specific effect that no other asset can use; the simulated representation is the render mesh, so cost follows source-asset complexity; or the feature has no LOD, so it works on one hero character and is switched off for everything else. The decisions below exist to close those three doors before anything is implemented. Which solver wins is deliberately not one of them — the experiments (E19–E25) come before solver details.

## Decision

1. **Animation and deformation are separate systems with one direction of flow.** Skeletal animation sets the intended pose and motion; the deformable solver adds secondary motion and contact response on top, under attachment and material constraints. Within a tick, animation writes the skeleton and the solver reads it as a kinematic constraint. A deformable volume never moves a bone.
2. **The cage is separate from the render mesh, always.** A volume is simulated as a coarse representation — the v1 default is an adaptive lattice generated from the mesh's signed distance field, with authored overrides; a particle-and-shell cage with pressure covers closed volumes; tetrahedral cages are a later cage kind behind the same abstraction. The high-resolution surface is bound to the cage at build time and deformed from it. Hand tetrahedralization does not scale and the automatic tetrahedralizers that work are either AGPL (TetGen, excluded by ADR-0014) or heavy; fTetWild (MPL-2.0) is the candidate if the tetrahedral kind is built.
3. **The abstraction is solver-agnostic; XPBD through Jolt is the first backend.** The engine's `DeformableVolume` owns cage topology, materials, layers, attachments, constraint edits, and a per-tick solve; Jolt's soft bodies (skinned soft-body constraints, pressure, deterministic mode) implement the CPU path. Replacing the backend must not change the asset format, the binding data, the LOD policy, or the renderer path. FEM stays out of v1 (ADR-0020) as a backend plus cage kind that the abstraction already allows.
4. **Simulation LOD is mandatory, not optional.** The tiers, near to far, are full cage, reduced cage, authored secondary motion, skinning only, none. Tier assignment uses the observer function of plan 05 §5.4 and is a property of the deformation system (ADR-0010). Every boundary carries hysteresis, and every transition blends state over several ticks, because the tiers do not produce the same shape.
5. **Gameplay-relevant cage state is deterministic CPU state.** It lives in the fixed-step sim, enters the sim hash, and replays exactly. GPU deformation — the per-frame transfer to render vertices, the cluster acceleration-structure work — is derived output and is never read back into gameplay. `gameplay_relevant` is a per-asset property, so a cosmetic volume costs nothing in the sim.
6. **Damage is constraint edits on the cage.** Weakening or removing elements, changing or breaking attachments, retagging a region's material, marking a surface region. Nothing else. This composes with the destruction system of ADR-0020 and gives games that model wounds a mechanism without the engine shipping an injury model.
7. **Deformation costs the LOD cut, not the source asset.** Cage-to-surface transfer runs in the renderer's per-frame deformed-vertex pool, shared with skinning, over the clusters of the current cut only; the per-vertex binding is built with the clusters and streams in their pages.

## Consequences

The content build gains a `derived` cage-generation step and a per-vertex binding slot in the cluster page format; the slot has to exist before assets are built, so a spike of the transfer path runs in Phase 1 alongside the deformed-vertex pool (E25) even though the primitive itself lands in Phase 6. The renderer's skinning path and its deformation path become one path, and deformed instances instantiate cluster acceleration-structure templates on NVIDIA (ADR-0025) or refit under the BLAS budget manager elsewhere. Simulation carries a deformable budget of 1.5 ms per 60 Hz tick shared with the soft bodies of plan 05 §5.13, and the renderer 0.5 ms per frame at 3840×2160; both are merge gates (ADR-0018) and both are provisional until E19–E25 measure them.

What is now forbidden: simulating a render mesh directly; a deformable path that writes bones; a volume with no LOD policy; reading GPU-deformed positions back into gameplay; a damage system with its own deformation solver; and an asset format or renderer path that assumes XPBD. Soft-against-soft contact is out of v1 — pair cost is quadratic in the interacting set — so v1 covers soft against rigid and soft against its own skeleton, which is enough for grabbing, pressing, impact, and contact with the world.

## Revisit when

E19–E25 return numbers that contradict the budgets or the tier design; a use case needs soft-against-soft contact; a layered asset needs a stiffness ratio that XPBD cannot condition at the iteration budget (E23), which is the trigger for an FEM backend; a permissively licensed tetrahedralizer makes tetrahedral cages cheap enough to be the default; or GPU soft-body solving becomes accurate and deterministic enough that the CPU tier stops being the gameplay path.
