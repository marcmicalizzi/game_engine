# ADR-0043: The desert's dunes are a function of time; the player's changes are a bounded overlay with one saturating feedback

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Review, 2026-09-25 (coordinator):** the two departures from the brief are accepted — the per-tile lag in place of a per-primitive phase offset (an offset kept with a migrating primitive would live in whatever upwind tile nudged it, possibly unloaded; a tile's lag holding a band back as it passes is what an obstacle does to sand), and the lossless bounded overlay in place of 05 §5.13's low-resolution copy (a copy would change the world when a tile unloads, a determinism hazard). Ruins stand on the fixed floor and the dunes bury them: the only placement that keeps a ruin a function of seed and tile alone, where riding the surface at time t would make its position depend on when it was first seen. The scene-generator registration point ADR-0037 asks for (a placement-generator table for things like the ruins beside a ground provider for the dunes, superseding 0037) is the coordinator's to design after the merge; until then both links follow the one pattern. The ADR as a whole stays Proposed until the owner's fly-through.

## Context

Desert Survival's world is an endless desert that a player may survive in for weeks ([13 §13.1](../plan/13-reference-consumer-games.md)). Its dunes should behave like dunes: fields migrating downwind at a rate that falls with height (Bagnold's relation, celerity = sand flux / height), crests advancing, slip faces steepening to the angle of repose, a seasonal wind moving them. The player deforms the sand — footprints, digging — and walls gather drifts ([05 §5.13](../plan/05-simulation.md#513-deformable-surfaces-and-soft-bodies)). The owner would love footprints to change the dunes' future.

The constraints: the persistent record of a tile must not grow with play time ([03 §3.5](../plan/03-data-model.md#35-persistent-world-state): state is event-sourced and snapshotted, and a desert of footprints must not be a log of footprints); the world is deterministic and replayed from its inputs ([05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay)); a seeded result must be the same bits on MSVC, GCC and Clang at either CPU baseline, as the content build and the ruin assembler already are ([ADR-0035](0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)); and a tile the player has not seen for a year must cost the same to show as one seen yesterday.

The alternatives on the table:

1. **A stepwise simulation with snapshots.** Integrate sand transport per tile per step (a cellular or height-field erosion model), and snapshot each tile's heights. Physically the richest. But every tile anyone ever saw keeps a height map forever, a tile unseen for a year has to be stepped a year to be shown (or shown stale), neighbouring tiles must be stepped together for their borders to agree, and the result depends on the step and on the order tiles were loaded in — which is history, and history is what a replay would have to reproduce exactly.
2. **Unbounded event replay.** Keep the base as a function but record every deformation as a `WorldEvent` and fold them on load, letting footprints perturb the base's parameters. Faithful to [03 §3.5](../plan/03-data-model.md#35-persistent-world-state) in form, but a footprint every step is thousands of events an hour, the fold grows with play time, and a footprint that perturbs the base makes every later evaluation of the field depend on the whole history: the butterfly effect, open.
3. **The dunes as a closed-form function of time, the player's changes as a bounded overlay beside it, and a bounded feedback.** Chosen.

## Decision

1. **The base terrain is a pure function of (world seed, position, game time)**, integer throughout (millimetres, micrometres, Q16, binary angles, a sine table built at compile time by integer arithmetic), and nothing about its evolution is stored. It is three bands of parametric dune primitives — long transverse draa, transverse crest segments, barchans — placed by the seed one per lattice cell, and **each band's lattice is displaced by the wind's flux integral divided by the band's height**, so the height at p is the primitives at p − I(t)/H_band. A primitive belongs to a cell of its band's moving lattice, not to a world tile: a tile-owned primitive migrates out of its tile and the neighbours a tile would have to consult grow with t, where the moving lattice keeps them a fixed number of cells. A slip face's side and sharpness come from the last 120 and 30 days of wind across it, blended continuously; a barchan points down the last month's wind.
2. **The wind is a seeded record of days over an eight-year period, with prefix sums**, so its integral to any t is three lookups. The period is what makes an arbitrary daily sequence — storms, calms, seasonal swings — have a closed-form integral; eight years is long enough not to notice the repeat.
3. **Evaluating any tile at any t costs what t = 0 costs, and t₂ directly equals t₁ then t₂**, because an evaluation reads the description and t and nothing else. A test holds the closed form to the day-by-day sum a simulation would compute, exactly, and golden hashes of a reference tile at three times hold on every toolchain.
4. **Fixed features are part of the function.** A scene's ridges and basins do not move; the sand thins over rock and flattens in a basin, and each band's lattice is held back upwind near a ridge, so crests bow round the rock at every t.
5. **What the player changed is a per-tile overlay of fixed resolution and fixed size**: 128 × 128 millimetre deviations from the steady state (the base plus every declared drift) at 25 cm, which is also 05 §5.13's CPU gameplay grid. It **decays toward the steady state** by an amount proportional to the flux that has blown since (closed form), snapping to zero within a threshold, with each vertex's amount a difference of floors of one absolute function of time — so the overlay is the same bytes whatever cadence it is advanced at, and a tile caught up after a month is the tile advanced every tick. A tile's persistent record is its header, its non-zero 16 × 16 blocks and a queue of at most 32 stamps not yet baked in: **at most 33,848 bytes**, and nothing once everything is buried. Stamps are not logged as events: they follow from the characters' movement, which follows from the inputs, so the input log replays them, and the overlay is their projection.
6. **The only feedback on the base is a lag**: per tile and per band, a quantized distance (8 cm units, at most 96) by which the band's lattice is held back while it passes the tile, interpolated between tile centres. It is nudged by large obstacles only — a pit of a cubic metre or more nudges the stored lag a unit at most once a game day; a declared drift of a cubic metre or more holds its tile back a unit a day from the day it was declared, a closed form that needs no storage — and it saturates. **The base field never reads the overlay.**
7. **The generator is a capability in `domain`** (`domain/terrain`, [ADR-0027](0027-additive-capabilities.md)) with determinism stance `derived`; the renderer serves its `TerrainSampler` from it when a scene's terrain names the generator, the world ring's consumer builds a tile's field on activation and keeps its overlay as a projection in the store, and `engine-content terrain` reports a tile.

**The renderer links the capability where it is configured, as it links the ruins** ([ADR-0037](0037-scene-reader-links-ruins-where-configured.md)): the scene reader is where a terrain is expanded, and a build without the capability builds the renderer and refuses a terrain that names the generator with a sentence. That makes the dune generator the second capability expanded inside the scene reader, which is the trigger ADR-0037 names for designing a scene-generator registration point from two examples and superseding it. It is not done in this change, deliberately: the two examples are different kinds — the ruins expand into *instances*, the dunes are the *ground's height function* the instances and the camera stand on — and a registration point that serves both (a placement generator table beside a ground provider) is a design for the owner to choose, not one to fall out of a desert generator's integration. Until then the renderer's links to `ruins` and `terrain` follow the one pattern and are removed together.

The per-tile lag departs from the owner's note, which asked for "a small, quantized, per-primitive phase offset stored in the tile's fixed-size state". A primitive migrates through tiles, so an offset kept with the primitive would have to be found wherever the primitive had got to — a lookup into the state of the tile that nudged it, arbitrarily far upwind after years — and written into tiles that are not loaded. Kept with the tile, the same bytes hold back every primitive of a band while it passes, which is what an obstacle does to sand.

## Consequences

- A tile of any age is shown at the cost of any other; nothing about the dunes is saved, and a save or a replay carries only overlays and lags.
- Storage is bounded per tile by construction (33,848 bytes), and in time by burial: every deviation fills within the flux its depth needs (`max_depth_mm`, 2 m: about 33 days of the mean wind). A player who survives for weeks stores what the last weeks left unburied.
- The butterfly effect is bounded: at most 96 units of history per band per tile, and a field that reads (seed, lags, t).
- Dunes do not interact the way a simulation's would. Bands pass through one another rather than merging; where a crest's slip face coincides with a draa's the two slopes add, and 0.5% of sand vertices stand steeper than the angle of repose (the worst 53°; [terrain](../subsystems/terrain.md#not-yet)). A repose limiter in closed form is the fix.
- Footprints never move a dune. A pit or a wall can, by at most 7.68 m.
- The daily weather repeats every eight years.
- A change to the overlay's rules is a change to the world: a record carries the rules' hash and is refused under others. A change to the field bumps `k_generator_version` and re-pins the golden hashes.
- Low-resolution copies of unloaded tiles (05 §5.13's suggestion) are not used: the lossless sparse blocks of a trampled tile are a few kilobytes, a buried tile's nothing, and a low-resolution copy would change the world when a tile unloads.

## Revisit when

- The owner's fly-through finds the migrating lattices read as sliding rather than as dunes advancing, or bands passing through one another is visible — the next step is per-band interaction terms that stay closed-form (a band's height modulating the next band's celerity locally), not a simulation.
- A game needs footprints to matter beyond a day, or tracking over weeks: the fill rate is a tunable, and the bound moves with `max_depth_mm`, not with play time.
- Snow or mud want the overlay: the overlay and the lag are field-agnostic, and the base function is the part to replace.
- Multiplayer: the overlay's stamps are derived from inputs; a server that does not run every client's movement needs them as events.
- The scene-generator registration point ADR-0037 asks for is designed: the renderer's link to this capability moves onto it with the ruins'.
