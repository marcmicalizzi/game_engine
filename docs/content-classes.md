# Content classes and view policies: the design (C3, A26)

**Designed 2026-11-18, and revised the same day after a review of the design and of its first module** (each finding, and the rule and test that close it, are listed at the end: "What a review found, and where each is closed"). **Built so far: the vocabulary and the decision function** (C61, [content_class](subsystems/content_class.md)), with its second leg, which mends three faults a review found in its readers and adds what this page's second pass needs; the rest is not built. [ADR-0033](adr/0033-content-classes-and-view-policies.md) decided what an output of the engine may contain: a **content class** on the content, a (category, level) pair per category; a **view policy** on the output, a cap and a substitution per category; and substitution at the earliest point where the two meet, so that classified data never reaches a restricted view's render. It left open where a class is written, how it survives derived data, where a policy lives and how a host picks one, what stands in for an instance above a cap, and how a refusal reaches a person. [ADR-0073](adr/0073-classes-travel-with-derived-data-and-policies-are-the-operators.md) (proposed) decides those. This page is the design the build rows read: the ways content leaves the engine today, roadmap C3 (classes on assets), roadmap A26 (policies on captures, reads and reviews), what it costs, and the rows that build it.

Three rules hold throughout, and every row below is checked against them.

- **Nothing classified is needed to build or test it.** Every example and every test uses neutral fixtures: a cube that carries a made-up class, under a made-up game category (`fixture_glow`, levels `none`, `dim`, `bright`) or under an engine category it does not depict. No row opens a classified asset, an authoring folder or an image of a body.
- **Against a protocol client a policy is a wall; for whoever starts a program it is the starter's choice, on the record.** No protocol method, root, role or MCP tool reaches a policy, so a client cannot widen its own ("Where a policy lives, and what keeps it from a client", below). Which of the operator's policies a program runs under is chosen by whoever starts it, and **an agent may choose one wider than `agent-default`** (the owner, 2026-11-18: "Agents too"; ADR-0073's context has their words): the default protects an agent that would rather not meet classified content, and is not a wall against one that chooses otherwise. What the engine does is put the choice on the record: every result and every picture names its policy, where the policy came from, and the policies file's hash; and a host started wider than `agent-default` says so on stderr and in its summary ("Every output says what it was made under"). An agent never writes or edits a policies file outside a test's scratch, and never changes a policy's caps ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).
- **The engine stays general purpose.** The engine knows three categories and their ladders and nothing about what a game depicts. A game adds categories as data, and their names and ladders travel with the data that uses them. Nothing keys on a generator's output, a file name or a mesh's shape: a class is what an asset declares, and an asset that declares nothing is `none` in every category.

## C3: classes on assets

### Where a class is declared, and by whom

**On the asset, in its sidecar.** A source asset's class is written in the provenance sidecar beside it, `<stem>.provenance.json`, the `engine.generation.provenance/1` file `tools/generate.ps1` already writes, or a minimal one a person writes by hand (`schema`, `name`, `content_classes`). Its `content_classes` field, already present, becomes a schema type, `engine.content.ContentClasses`: `levels`, a map from category name to level name, plus an optional `materials` map from a material's name to its own levels, for an asset whose classified part is one material. The author declares it: a person, or a game's tooling under that person's review. It is not the document's, because the document is what agents edit all day.

- **Absent is `none`** in every category, as ADR-0033 decision 1 says. A Khronos sample, a generated prop and a procedural mesh declare nothing and are `none`. The engine cannot detect classified content, and does not pretend to: a game that ships it declares it, the way it declares a licence.
- **One reader, two forms, every caller.** The generation tools write `content_classes` today as a flat map of category to level (`{"nudity": "none", "sexual": "none", "violence": "none"}`). The reader takes that form as the asset's `levels`, and the schema's form (an object holding `levels`, `materials` or both) as itself. An object that mixes the two is refused, and `levels` and `materials` are reserved: neither may be a category's name. So every sidecar written so far reads as what it says and the tools need no migration. The reader reads only a sidecar's `content_classes`; `content_class: general` is the tools' own claim about what they make and is not read. It is one function of the module (`content_class::read_sidecar_classes`), called by the content build, the load and the store alike, so a sidecar cannot mean one thing to the build and another at load.
- **A class declared per material is the asset's too.** v0 decides per mesh, so the reader joins every material's levels into the asset's, and validates them alike: an unknown category under `materials` is refused as under `levels`. The per-material values are kept beside the joined class for the part-wise substitution of C11 and C12.
- **A sidecar that cannot be read is never `none`.** A sidecar that is not JSON, holds the same key twice in one object, has a field of the wrong type or shape, or names a category nobody defines: **the content build refuses the source**, naming the sidecar and why. At load (a sidecar beside a glTF or an image, a container's section, a pack set's entry), **a class that cannot be read is the highest level of every category the reader knows**, so only a policy that caps every one of them at its top admits it, and the load report counts it as unreadable. The reader writes that class into its output whenever it refuses, so a caller that ignores the answer still fails closed. A duplicate key is refused rather than resolved, because the JSON parser's ordinary rule (the last one wins) would let a text merge or a tool's append declare a class and silently lose it.
- **A category is the engine's or the game's, and its name travels with the data.** The engine's are `nudity` (`none`, `partial`, `full`), `sexual` (`none`, `suggestive`, `explicit`) and `violence` (`none`, `mild`, `graphic`), compiled in. A game adds its own in a categories file of its content (`engine.content.ContentCategoriesFile`: each category's levels, lowest first), which **the content build** is given; a category neither knows is refused there, because a level that cannot be ordered cannot be capped. **Every built thing that records a class records, beside each category it names, that category's ladder by name, lowest first.** So a host is given no categories file and has no flag for one: it learns a game's category, and its order, from the data that uses it.
  - A category's name is lower-case letters, digits and `_`, so `Nudity` and `nudity ` are refused rather than read as a second category beside `nudity`.
  - At a host, the first ladder met for a name is that name's. Data naming the same category with another ladder (a game that reordered or renamed a level between two builds) is unreadable, which fails closed, and the load report names the category. An engine category's ladder is the compiled-in one, and data recording another for it is unreadable.
  - More than sixteen categories in all is refused by the build and unreadable at load.
- **A level a ladder does not have reads as the ladder's highest**: an unknown level fails closed.
- **One sidecar for every source format.** A glTF could carry the class in `extras` and a PNG cannot, so the sidecar is the one place for meshes, images, audio and material sets alike. A material pack's set declares it in its `pack.json` entry, read by the same reader (none of the shipped sets declare one).

**On the record, raising only.** A `MeshPlacement` record (`MeshPlacement` version 2, `content_classes: ContentClasses?`) may **raise** the class of the asset it places: a neutral asset placed in a way that makes it classified, such as a material override or a pose. Its effective class is the per-category maximum of the asset's and the record's, so a record can never lower an asset's class. `doc.place_asset` and `editor.import_mesh` copy the asset's declared class onto the record they make, so the record says what it places without opening the sidecar. A raise is open to every role that may write the record, because a raise only narrows what is shown. **Decided by the owner, 2026-11-18** ("Yes, as proposed", question 1 of ADR-0073); the alternative was classes on the sidecar alone, which is simpler and cannot classify a neutral asset by context.

**Only a person's role lowers a class, and the rule is on the composed document after every transaction.** For every placement a transaction touches, the session compares the placement's **composed** `content_classes` (its value through the whole layer stack, not the value in the layer written) before and after, and refuses the whole transaction (1008, `data.reason` `"content class"`) when any category falls, unless the caller's role is marked `human`. Under A4's credentials a connection has such a role only from a credential the operator issued for a person.

- **Every transaction**, whatever produced it: a `doc.apply`; an undo; a redo; the application of a merge's result; a rollback to a checkpoint; a proposal's promotion, checked against the promoter's role and the document's composition (a write into a proposal layer is checked against the proposal's own composition, so a lowering is caught where it is written and again where it lands); and a change to the layer stack itself (a layer added over the placement's, removed, reordered or muted).
- **A deleted placement is all `none` after the transaction**, so deleting a placement that carries a class is a lowering. Deleting it and placing the asset again therefore cannot shed a raise, and nor can a higher layer's override or the removal of the layer that held the raise, because the composed value is what is compared.
- **What follows for an agent**: it cannot undo its own raise, delete a classified placement or roll back past a raise. Each is a person's edit.
- **What it does not cover**: an edit to a document's files outside the protocol is not a transaction. It is the file-system access ADR-0033 decision 4 concedes, and its trail is the document's history.

### How it travels through derived data

A built thing records the class of exactly what its cache key covers, and a cached build cannot shed that class. **What its key does not cover is joined at load, every load.**

- **A container** (`.clusters`) gets section **54, `ContentClasses`** (53 is the door leaves'): for each category it names, the name and its ladder as strings, and, per scope (the whole asset, or a material index), the level by name. It records **the source's own class**, its sidecar's with the `materials` map, which covers the images embedded in the source: a `.glb`'s buffer or a data URI is inside the source's bytes, so inside the key. **It is written only when some level is above `none`.** So every container built today, and every unclassified one built later, is byte-identical to what it is now. No golden hash, no determinism pin and no `k_cluster_cache_version` moves.
- **The cache key holds the class, by name**, the way `source_mesh_hash` already mixes a tag into the key of a file that states `KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` ([assets](subsystems/assets.md)). `cluster_cache_key` mixes in a hash of the source's canonical class **only when it is not all `none`**: its (category name, level name) pairs sorted by category name, each game category's ladder, and the same per material. Names and ladders, never indexes, so a game that reorders its categories or renames a level gets a new key, never a stale section. An unclassified source keeps its key. A source whose sidecar gains a class gets a new key, so the cached build made before the class existed is never the answer to it. **The key must not hold** an external image's class, a pack set's or a record's: an image named by path is outside the container's key on purpose, so that a repainted texture shows without a rebuild ([geometry](subsystems/geometry.md#built-textures-where-each-images-texture-is)), and its class is outside the key with it.
- **A texture** (`.tex`) gets section **6, `ContentClasses`**, the same form with no material scope, holding **its image's own class** (the image's sidecar). It is mixed into `texture_cache_key` only when not `none`. A texture is not built from the meshes that sample it, so it inherits nothing from them, and its key holds nothing about who samples it: one image is one entry, whoever uses it. The instance's decision joins the two (below).
- **A node built from several inputs it covers carries their join**, and its key covers each input's class through that input's own key: a tile set over its forms, a far-forest impostor over the meshes it was baked from (its pixels are theirs), a merged scene mesh, a generator's output over the material sets it baked in. An engine generator declares nothing of its own, so its output is the join of its inputs.
- **The identity** an incremental build trusts (`read_cluster_file_identity`) holds the key, so `engine-content build-all` rebuilds an entry whose sidecar changed. A copy of a document (`session.save_game`, exit 24) copies the sidecars that sit in it, and the records carry their own raise.
- `engine-content info` prints the classes with their ladders, and `content.build`'s result reports them, as data. Numbers about classified content are allowed (exit 20).

**A texture that gains a class after its mesh was built is caught at load, without rebuilding the mesh.** The load already reads every image named by path to find its `.tex` by the file's bytes as they are now. It reads the image's sidecar beside it through the same reader, mixes the class into the texture key it computes (so it finds the classified `.tex`, or none, and decodes), and joins the class into the decision of every instance that samples the image. A pack set resolved by role at load brings its `pack.json` entry's class the same way. A container's section 54 is never the whole answer for an instance, so nothing found by a container's key alone can admit an image that has since become classified.

**Two limits, stated.**
- **A container named directly** (`--mesh x.clusters`, a scene naming a `.clusters`) is what its section says, joined with a sidecar beside it if there is one, and with its images' classes at load. A container built before C3 from a source that was classified even then has no section. That is one way to hold classified content with no class, and it is a guard rail's limit: such a file was built by a person's tool before the rule existed.
- **An engine binary built before C64** (an exported SDK, an older packaged game) skips section 54 as it skips every kind it does not know, and enforces nothing. It is the same limit from the other side.

### What "decided at instantiation" means

**In the renderer, per instance, when it is admitted, before its mesh's geometry is read.** `SceneDesc` gains `view_policy` (`content_class::ViewPolicy`, a few bytes), and so does `PlacementMeshOptions`, so a mesh built after the load, such as a world tile's, is decided the same way. **An instance is decided on the join of everything it draws with:**

- **its mesh's own class**: from the container's section table, which the load reads anyway (one more kind looked up, nothing read when it is absent), or, for a glTF source, from the sidecar the content build reads to compute the key;
- **the class of every texture its materials sample**: each image's sidecar, read at load beside the image (above), and its `.tex`'s section 6; each pack set's entry;
- **its own raise**: `SceneInstance::content_classes`, filled by `doc_scene` from the placement record's composed `content_classes`, and by the scene-file reader from an instance's optional `content_classes`, read by the same reader.

**The ground is decided as a mesh is** (C64): its class is the join of what the sidecars beside its heightfield source and beside each of its material layers' images declare, read before any of them is, and its cache key holds that class as a mesh's does. A ground the policy refuses is left out whole (never a box) and counted on its own in the load report ([renderer](subsystems/renderer.md#content-classes-at-load)). A neutral stand-in for one refused layer, so that the rest of the ground still draws, is not built.

Then it decides, through one function (`content_class::decide(classes, policy)`):

- **within the cap**: loaded and drawn as today;
- **`omit`**: the instance is not made. Its mesh's geometry and textures are never read into `SceneData` for it, never uploaded for it, and never in the cull pass, the acceleration structures, the shadow maps or any channel;
- **`proxy`**: the instance is made from the engine's own unit box, scaled to the mesh's bounds (from the container's header and LOD section, numbers only) and placed by the instance's transform. It gets one neutral material, so silhouettes, framing and the place of the thing stay meaningful. Collision is not changed: the world is not an output (below).

Two placements of one mesh with different raises are two decisions, and a mesh shared by an admitted instance and an omitted one is read once, for the admitted one.

**One door into a scene.** Every instance that reaches `SceneData`, at the load or after it (a world tile, a placement's form, a mesh swapped by any path, whatever a game module asks the host to place), comes through one admission function that makes this decision. There is no other way to add one, so a path that forgets the policy cannot be written without going around the module's own type. **A live edit re-decides.** `doc_scene` and the editor's viewport follow the document's change feed. An entry that touches a placement's `content_classes` or `mesh`, or a layer that composes them, re-decides that instance, and one that rises above the cap is removed, or replaced by its proxy, before the next frame is recorded. A reload of an image re-reads its sidecar and re-decides the instances that sample it.

`cover` (a mandatory garment or decal slot) and `obscure at capture` are **not built**. Both need the character work (roadmap C11, C12). A policies file naming either is refused when the host starts, rather than silently treated as `omit`. A substitution happens per mesh in v0, because a whole-asset class is what C3 records first. Per-material and per-section substitution, which drops a tagged layer or hides a section with the mask garments use, comes with C11 and C12. The per-material records of section 54 are kept for them.

**The scene remembers the policy it was made under and every decision it made.** `SceneData` gains:

- `instantiated_under`, the policy it was loaded with;
- `decisions`, each distinct class it decided with that decision's outcome. Under one policy a class always decides the same way, so the table is a function of the classes the scene has met: one entry (`none`, admit) for a scene with nothing classified. It is **append-only**: an entry stays while the scene is loaded even when no instance has that class any longer, so it bounds every frame the scene draws, and its length is its generation;
- `held`, the join of the classes it admitted, which results report.

`SceneLoadReport` gains `substituted`, a count per category and outcome, unreadable classes counted apart. The capture check (refusals, below) reads the table.

**What a frame that holds nothing classified costs: nothing measurable.** No GPU struct changes (`gfx::InstanceDesc`, `ClusterDesc` and `MeshDesc` and their size tables stay), no shader changes, and no per-frame or per-instance work. The decision is per instance at admission, and is one branch for an all-`none` class. Its I/O is one section-table lookup per container; for a glTF source, the one sidecar open the key needs; and one sidecar probe per image named by path, beside the read the load already makes of that image. The probes are the only new I/O, and the build row measures them on the load of a many-mesh, many-image scene with and without sidecars, on a quiet machine, against the load's own wall time. A capture adds one decision per entry of the scene's table, twice.

**In the world: nothing is decided, and that is the decision.** The runtime world materializes every record whatever its class. A policy affects outputs, never the source of truth (ADR-0033 decision 6), and a game's state must not depend on who is watching. A streamer-mode player and anyone else run the same simulation, and `session.state_hash` is the same under every policy (a test). What the world gives out is ids and component values (exit 21), never an asset's bytes.

## A26: the policy on captures, reads and reviews

### Where a policy lives, and what keeps it from a client

**One file, the operator's: `engine.content.ViewPoliciesFile`**, a list of named policies, each a cap and a substitution per category. The engine compiles in one more, **`agent-default`**: ADR-0033 decision 7's `nudity: none`, `sexual: none`, `violence: mild`, a game's categories `none`. A host with no file has only that one, so **the default is what every host runs unless its operator says otherwise**. Its substitutions were **decided by the owner, 2026-11-18** ("Box, as proposed", question 2 of ADR-0073): `proxy` for `nudity` and `sexual`, so that an agent's picture keeps a box at the bounds and the framing stays meaningful, and `omit` for `violence`. A game's category is capped at `none` and substituted by `omit`: the owner's answer named the engine's three, and `omit` is the stricter reading for a category nobody has judged.

**A policy caps categories by name.** A category it does not name is capped at `none` with `omit`. An engine category's cap must be a level of its ladder, or the file is refused at start. A game's category is a name the host may not have met yet, so its cap is kept by name and resolved when data brings the category's ladder; a cap naming a level that ladder does not have is `none` with `omit`, the stricter reading of a cap nobody meant ([content_class](subsystems/content_class.md)). A misspelt category or level therefore narrows, never widens.

How the operator names it, and why each way a client works is kept away from it:

- **The command line of the hosts the operator starts**: `engine-host`, `engine-editor` and `engine-view` take `--view-policies <file>`, at most once, and `--view-policy <name>` (the host's own policy, `agent-default` when absent). A name the set does not hold refuses the start rather than falling back. The file is read once, at start, and never again: no hot reload, so a change takes effect when the operator restarts the host.
- **Never under a root, never in a document.** The start compares **canonical absolute paths**, links resolved, and refuses a policies file that is reached through a symbolic link or junction, has more than one hard link, or lies inside the open document's directory or inside any root a client may name (roadmap T174, readable or writable), the way it refuses a writable root that holds the shell's served directory. It checks again whenever the host opens a document after the start. So no client-named path can read or write it.
- **No client-named write lands on a sidecar.** A capture, a container or any other file a method writes where a client names is refused onto a name ending `.provenance.json`, so a protocol write can neither make a sidecar unreadable nor replace one.
- **No protocol method writes a policy, names its file or returns its path.** `engine.view_policy` (read-only) answers the caller's effective policy by name, caps, substitutions, source and the file's hash, never the file's path. A per-call `view_policy` parameter on `render.load`, `render.capture`, `render.evaluate`, `render.compare` and `doc.blob` may **narrow** for that call (a per-category minimum with the effective one), never widen.
- **Not in the roles file.** `content/roles/roles.json` is in the repository and edited in ordinary work, so a policy is never a field of a role. A role says what a caller may change; a policy says what a caller may see; and the two are named separately.
- **A credential names its policy** (on top of A4's credentials): `--issue-credential … --view-policy <name>` records the name in the credentials file. A connection that presented a credential runs under that policy or `agent-default`, **never the host's own**, which fails closed exactly as A4's decision does for a role a host cannot enforce. The operator's own token and the shell's cookie run under the host's policy, with one exception: on an editor started for a person at its seat, every connection but the seat runs under `agent-default` narrowed by the editor's policy, the operator's printed token and the printed shell address included, and the editor's own policy is reached only by the person's look, in the native window (built by the hosts' row, C65). (The placement rule for the policies file above is proposed for A4's credentials file too, since it names each credential's policy.)
- **engine-cli and engine-mcp forward no policy.** Agents run both routinely, and engine-mcp's command line is written into an agent's client configuration. The host either one starts therefore always runs `agent-default`. An agent that is to see more is attached by the operator to a host the operator started, with a credential the operator issued.

**What this is, and what it is not.** Against a protocol client it is a wall: nothing a client sends reaches a policy. For whoever starts `engine-view`, `engine-host` or `engine-editor` it is **the starter's choice, on the record**: a person or an agent names a policy of the operator's file with `--view-policy`, a wider one included (the owner, 2026-11-18, "Agents too"), or names none and runs `agent-default`. The engine cannot tell an agent's command line from a person's, and since the owner's decision it need not.

- **The rule is the repository's** ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)): an agent never writes or edits a policies file outside a test's own scratch directory and never changes a policy's caps; a check that needs a policy the operator's file does not define becomes a person's review item.
- **No lint against the flags.** The `view-policy-flag` lint the first passes proposed for C68 is dropped: it enforced a rule the owner removed (that an agent never passes a view-policy flag).
- **The record is the next section**: every output names its policy, where it came from and the file's hash, and a start wider than `agent-default` says so.
- **Open, a design question of this page's, still to be settled:** whether an agent running under a policy that covers a classified item may close the review item the host raised for it, or whether that item stays a person's (a role marked `human`, as every person's item is today). Until it is decided it stays a person's; the 1011 path, who raises the item, and the human-only rule for lowering a record's class (the owner's, question 1) are unchanged.

**Who may look at full policy** is the operator's statement, made twice: a policy wide enough, in the operator's file, and a credential or host that names it. Closing a person's review item takes a role marked `human` (A23). Seeing the content takes a policy that covers it. The two are separate on purpose, and a reviewer's credential carries both. Under ADR-0033 decision 7 an operator may also raise an agent's caps: the engine encodes no provider's terms, and does not forbid it.

### Every output says what it was made under

`RenderCaptureResult`, `RenderEvaluateResult` and `RenderBenchmarkResult` gain `view_policy` (`RenderViewPolicy`), `held` and `substituted`. **`RenderViewPolicy` names the policy, where it came from, and the file**:

- the name, the caps and the substitutions;
- the **source**: `compiled-in` (`agent-default` with no file), `start line` (the host's `--view-policy`), or `credential` (a credential the operator issued);
- the **policies file's content hash**, absent for `compiled-in`. The file's path is not recorded: a picture travels, and a path names a machine. The hash is enough for an operator to tell their file from any other.

So a benchmark of a substituted scene says it measured a different scene (exit 8), and a picture made under a file the operator did not write says so by a hash the operator does not know.

- **Every PNG the capture path writes**, the host's and `engine-view --capture`'s, carries the same as one `tEXt` chunk (`engine-view-policy`, compact JSON), because a picture separated from its result must still say it was restricted. The chunk changes a capture's bytes and not its pixels; the build row checks that every golden comparison in the tree decodes pixels and does not hash files.
- **`engine-view`'s JSON summary and a packaged game's `report.json`** carry it too.
- **A host started under a policy wider than `agent-default`** says so. Wider means that narrowing it by `agent-default` changes it: some category capped higher, or `proxy` where `agent-default` omits. Such a host prints one line on stderr at start, naming the policy, its source and the file's hash, and carries `wider_than_agent_default: true` in its start line or summary (engine-host's and engine-editor's first stdout line, engine-view's JSON summary) and in every result and chunk it makes. A connection that authenticates under a credential naming a wider policy is logged the same way when it connects.

### Refusals, and the review item for a person

**What is refused**, with **1011 `ContentAboveViewPolicy`**: `data` names the policy, the category, the level found, the cap, and the review item raised (below), and never the content.

- **`render.load` of a single mesh** (`mesh`) above the cap: a look at one asset, where a proxy of the whole subject would answer nothing. A scene is substituted, not refused.
- **`render.capture` and `render.evaluate` of a loaded scene, unless the caller's policy makes the scene's decisions.** A scene is drawn for a caller only when, for every entry of the scene's decision table, `decide(class, caller's effective policy)` has the entry's outcome: the caller's policy admits what was admitted, proxies what was proxied, and omits what was omitted. Otherwise the answer is 1011, and the remedy is a scene loaded under the caller's own policy. **The same outcome, not merely "within"**:
  - a caller whose policy omits where the scene put a proxy would otherwise receive the box: the bounds and the place that `omit` exists to withhold;
  - a caller whose policy is wider would receive a picture missing what its own policy shows, under a result that names that policy: the restricted capture that does not say it is restricted (ADR-0033 decision 4).
- **The capture check is made twice.** Once before the first frame, to refuse cheaply. Then again after the last frame is recorded and before the readback is returned, over every entry the table gained since the first check (the table only grows, so its length is its generation). Content admitted while a capture ran is in that capture's frame only if it was admitted before the frame was recorded, and then the second check reads it.
- **`GET /capture/<id>`**: a kept capture keeps its scene's table as of its second check. A connection whose policy would not make the same decisions gets **404 and no body, exactly the answer to an id that does not exist**, so the endpoint tells nobody which kept captures exist.
- **`render.compare`** of an image whose class (its sidecar, or a capture's own chunk) is above the cap. **A limit, stated**: an image re-encoded or copied without its chunk, and with no sidecar, is `none`. The heat map is of pixels the caller already holds and sent, so it reveals nothing the caller did not have.
- **`doc.blob` with `data`** of a blob above the cap. `doc.put_blob` takes an optional `content_classes`, and the store keeps it beside the blob, joined on every later put of the same bytes and never lowered.

**What it raises.** The host raises a ledger item itself, through the ledger's own write (A23, `review.raise`'s path):

- **Kind and audience**: kind `classified content review`, audience `Human`.
- **Raiser**: attributed to the caller's actor, marked as raised by the host. The caller's role need not name `review.raise`, because the refusal is the host's own record and must not be lost because the caller may only read.
- **Subject**: the records, the blobs, and, in `ReviewSubject` version 2, `assets` as portable paths (a root's name and a path under it, never an absolute path), with the document's version the host stamps.
- **Report**: numbers only. The asset's identity (source hash, build key, the container's content hash), the class found against the cap, and the validator numbers the build has: triangles, clusters, bounds, texel density, the `content.*` checks.
- **Deduplication**: by (the subject's identity and the build key of what was refused, category). An open item for the same thing is named again, with the latest document version it was met at, and not raised again. Every edit stamps a new version, so an agent at work refusing the same thing after each edit fills one item, not one an edit.

**What an agent's session sees instead**: in a picture, the proxies or nothing; in the result, `held`, `substituted` and the policy with its source; for a direct ask, the 1011 with the item's id; in `engine.view_policy`, what it may see; and in reads of the document, each record's class. MCP tools say in their descriptions that a 1011 is a handled outcome: the check has become a person's item and the work continues. The bridge never retries one or rephrases around it (ADR-0033 decision 7).

**How the item is closed.** A person opens it at full policy, through a host whose operator gave that person's credential a policy that covers it: `review.list`, then the subject captured or read there. The person closes it Accepted or Rejected under a role marked `human`. The raiser cannot withdraw it (A23 already holds both). Closing changes no class and no policy. A Rejected item says what must change, and the work is an asset's, a task's or a record's.

### Reads of the document

`doc.get`, `doc.describe` and `doc.query` return a record's effective class (the asset's joined with the record's raise) and `above_policy` for the caller. Record properties are returned as they are: the document is authored text and numbers, and a review needs the subject's identity. The authoring rule that goes with it is ADR-0033's own: a classified asset is named neutrally, as its base texture is authored neutral. **Decided by the owner, 2026-11-18** ("Text, as proposed", question 3 of ADR-0073); the alternative withheld a record's string properties above the caller's cap, which is safer but breaks diffs and merges for the caller, and the identity a review needs.

### Logs and reports

A log field, a profile zone name and a ledger report hold ids, paths and numbers, never an asset's bytes or a decoded image. That is already so, and the rule is written down so that it stays so. No mechanism beyond review enforces it.

**Two stay open, as a decision**, because a mend of either would change what ADR-0073 decision 1 states. A sidecar's level that the build's ladder lacks reads as that ladder's top, as the ADR says, so a container built against a shorter ladder records the shorter ladder's top by name, and a reader that learns that ladder from it takes it for a level a policy written for the longer ladder admits. And the class of what cannot be read is the top of each ladder the reader knows, as the ADR says, so a policy that caps a category at that reader's top admits that category of it; a reader holds the first ladder it met for a name (`Categories::learn`), and refuses as unreadable data with another.

**A third, also for the decision record's owner** (found in C64): ladders are learned from data in the order it is met, and a sidecar or a raise names levels with no ladder of its own, so a stale container listed first that records a reordered ladder (say `fixture_glow: none, bright, dim`) decides for the whole load how every image sidecar and raise naming that category is ordered, and an image declaring `bright` (meant as the top) is then admitted under a cap of `dim`. A record that is refused teaches nothing (C64 mended that half: learning is all or nothing for a record); which ladder a load should trust when two disagree is left open here.
