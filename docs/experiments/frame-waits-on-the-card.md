# Where the frame waits on the card (R219, 2026-11-24)

Roadmap R219, from the owner's walk of the forest's v59 on batch 74's binaries (the proof of concept's engine needs, item 179). The owner reads about 400 W in the forest where a full load of the card draws 600 to 610 W; at 3840 × 2160 the walk's frame is 20.9 ms with the GPU's timeline full, and Nsight's GPU metrics over the walk's poses (one run the owner made that afternoon) found the SMs active all the time, warps in flight at 30 % of the maximum and an instruction issued on 17 % of cycles, with DRAM at 2 to 5 % of its peak. So the card is not starved, not transferring and not spilling: its warps wait on latency inside the passes. This page asks which passes hold that wait, what in them waits, and what returns it, with what an agent may measure — the frame records' pass timers and `nvidia-smi dmon`, which needs no rights — and prepares the one capture that can split the counters by pass, which only the owner may run.

**In four lines.** On the forest staging at the owner's settings the card draws 380 to 410 W with the SMs busy every second and the memory controller at a tenth, as on his walk. **Three consumers hold most of the frame's time, and each is a ray or a lookup that waits**: the water's reflection ray (4.0 ms of a 17.6 ms frame where the lake is in view), the bounce (the cache's read in the resolve and its sample passes, 2.9 ms standing and 5.0 walking), and the primary rays with the cluster structures they need (4.8 ms of the walk's 20.9, which the hardware rasterizer draws for 1.1 with the same visibility buffer; standing over the lake it is 0.7 ms dearer instead). The micromaps are what keeps the sun's masks from being a fourth: without them the standing frame is 10.2 ms longer and the card draws 38 to 56 W *less*, the clearest wait the night found. At 11520 × 2160 the resolve follows the pixels (2.97 times for three times the pixels with the view held, 3.3 with the surround's view) and the primary rays do not: they cost what the pixels cost up to 7680 wide and 5.4 times the 4K figure at 11520, so the surround's outermost angles, not its pixel count, are the extra.

## How it was measured

- **The scene and the settings: the owner's.** The forest staging (`<local>\staging\v41\scene-staging.json`, its own derived-data root and `materials-b69`), offscreen, 3840 × 2160 one view, `--raster rt --shadows rt`, micromaps on, `renderer.gi.mode=bounce` with `renderer.gi.guide=true` and `renderer.gi.memory=true`, `renderer.reconstruction.accumulation=on`, flora motion at wind 0.7 with `renderer.wind.speed_m_s=3`, `--no-lights --time-of-day 15 --world-rate 1 --lod 1 --deform-pool-mib 64 --rt-budget-mib 0 --time-rate 0 --terrain-tiles --terrain-far 6 --terrain-async`, `--memory-report`. With accumulation on a record carries its times under `reconstructed.gpu_ms` (ADR-0064: never a cost on its own); every figure below is read from there and set beside a flight of the same settings, never against a native figure. **The GPU frame** is each record's `gpu_frame.span_ms`, the GPU's own first to last mark; the passes are its `gpu_ms` buckets.
- **One path, two parts.** `stand-walk.json` (in the row's scratch, built from the staging's two paths): the lake-range standing pose (`standing-path.json`'s, at 80, 14, 130 looking across the lake) held 20 s, frames 0 to 600 at 30 fps; a cut to the grounded walk's start held 3 s (left out); then the grounded walk (`walk-grounded.json`, x 180 to 540 at z 1509, 4.5 m/s, the eye 1.7 m over the ground) for 80 s, frames 691 to 3090. Read: the standing frames 30 to 600 and the walk's 721 to 3090, each part's median (the tables) and 99th percentile (the row's scratch). The width flights fly the standing pose alone, 960 frames over its 30 s, read from frame 60.
- **One switch a flight**, by its own tunable, everything else the base's: `renderer.shadow.micromaps=false`; `renderer.gi.mode=off`; `renderer.water.reflect=0` with and without `renderer.water.refract=0`; `renderer.reconstruction.accumulation=off`; `--raster hw` (the primary rays replaced by the hardware rasterizer, which writes the same visibility buffer, shadows still traced); `renderer.shadow.opaque_rays=true` (the sun's and every shadow ray take every caster as opaque: the masks' cost, a measurement switch); `renderer.shadow.light_tolerance=1` (every point light takes the sun's answer instead of a ray); `renderer.foliage.backlit_tier=0` (a leaf lit from behind asks no ray); and `renderer.resolve.stop_after=cycle` for the resolve's stage curve ([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#where-the-resolves-time-goes)).
- **The card's own figures beside every flight**: `nvidia-smi dmon -s pucvmt -o DT -d 1` from before the load to after the flight, read over each part's wall-clock window (the records' `frame_ms` summed back from the flight's exit line) and over the card's busy seconds only (`sm` at least 50, as the forest session read the owner's): power (W), the SM-busy figure (`sm`: the share of the second some kernel ran, not how full the SMs were), the memory controller (`mem`), the clock, PCIe. A standing part is 11 s of wall, so its power is a median of 5 to 13 samples and good to about 10 W; a walk's is 32 to 48.
- **The binary**: main's release build at `0ac9fbaf` (main `962a9bd6d`'s code; nothing after it touches the engine), copied out of the tree. The labels' pair (below) flies this row's own release build.
- **The machine**: RTX 5090 (32 GB, driver 621.24, the clock at 2,872 to 2,880 MHz in every busy second and no power or thermal limit hit in any flight), i9-10980XE, 64 GB, Windows 26300; shared with seven other rows' builds, tests and flights, the GPU lock held for each flight and nothing but the desktop's processes on the card (`nvidia-smi` at each start and end). Other processes' CPU at the flights' starts ranged from 1 to 100 %; that moves the load's length and the CPU's figures, not the GPU's. **The machine was hard-reset at 20:44** (cause unknown) and every flight after it started only with 26 GB of physical memory free (a flight peaks at 18 to 21.5 GiB private, at 4K and at 11520 alike) and the device at least 22 GB free for a wide one, with two minutes between flights for the card's shorter users; four flights were skipped on that rule and flown later.
- **The reference is the base flown in the same hour** (22:47); the base flown at 20:29 is beside it: 0.57 ms (3 %) apart standing and 0.42 walking, every pass within 0.15 ms, so a frame difference under about 0.6 ms is not read. Two flights of the standing pose alone 2 minutes apart (23:22, 23:27) are 0.05 ms apart.

**The resolve by stage, standing** (`stop_after=cycle`, 112 frames a stage): `decode` 0.12 ms, `reconstruct` 0.53, `material` 1.69, `lighting` 3.35 (the bounce's read is in it), `sun_shadow` 4.07, `light_shadows` 4.06, `sky` 4.29, and the whole picture 9.78 — **the water stage is 5.49 ms of the 9.78**, a quarter of the pixels being the lake and the open sea beyond it, and 4.0 of those 5.5 are its reflection ray (the flight above).

**What it says about the wait.**

- **The micromaps are the night's clearest wait.** Without them the standing frame grows by 10.2 ms (the resolve by 7.7: the sun's ray through the crowns runs the masks' any-hit work on every candidate; the bounce's rays by 1.8), the card draws 38 to 56 W less than the two bases, the SMs stay busy 100 % of every second and the memory controller falls from 10 % to 8 %: more time, less work per second. That is a pass waiting on its own traversal, and it is the shape the owner's 400 W walk has everywhere, at a smaller scale.
- **The water's reflection ray is 4.0 ms of the standing frame** (and 0.9 of the walk's, where a stream or the lake is at the edge of the view), with the frame's power up 2 to 10 W without it. The resolve traces it per water pixel at full rate; the half-rate checkerboard the water already has is for the reflection's *shadow* ray only (`renderer.water.reflect_shadow`, [water](../subsystems/water.md)). The refraction ray adds nothing measurable here.
- **The bounce costs 2.9 ms standing and 5.0 walking**, two thirds of it in the resolve (2.2 and 3.1 ms: the read of the world-space cache at every pixel, which R148 found the largest term of the resolve on every covered pixel) and the rest its sample and fold passes. Its removal leaves the power where it was: the read is dependent lookups (hash, cell, its neighbours), the work that issues little and waits.
- **On the walk the primary rays and the structures they need are 4.8 ms of 20.9**: `trace` 3.1 and, inside the chain, the per-frame cluster structures of every drawn cluster (92,000 a frame; under `--raster hw` the chain builds the casters' 13,000 and `rt` falls from 3.5 to 1.5 ms). The hardware rasterizer draws the same 417,000 pairs in 1.1 ms. **Standing over the lake it is the other way**: 1.1 million pairs at a fine cut, the rasterizer 1.95 ms against the rays' 1.53, and the chain built only 6,200 structures there anyway (the static ones carry the rest). Which is cheaper follows the frame's pairs and how many of them the chain must build each frame, not the scene.
- **Accumulation's own passes are 1.4 ms standing and 1.9 walking** (the motion pass and the second write of every deformed vertex, and the history's encode), with 0.2 ms of the bounce's samples that only the accumulation asks for; nothing in the frame's other passes moves.
- **What returns nothing here**: the lights' rays (the staging's fires and lamps are out of reach of both poses at 15:00), two-sided foliage's ray (−0.11 ms in the resolve, under the noise), the masks with the micromaps on (−0.8 ms standing, −0.7 walking: the micromaps have already taken most of it), and the half-rate sun at 11520 (below).
- **Power is a coarse instrument at this grain**: between the two bases of one setting the standing power moved 18 W, and every switch but the micromaps moved it by less. It does say two things clearly: the micromaps' removal (time up, power down) and the width flights (below), where the frame that adds coherent pixels draws the most power of the night.

**Pass by pass.**

- **The resolve follows the pixels.** With the view held it is 2.97 times for 3.00 times the pixels; widened to 7680 it is 1.91 for 2.00. At 11520 it is 3.32: the surround's side thirds hold more of the lake's water than the 4K view's sides, and the water stage is the resolve's dearest per pixel (above); the 0.3 ms over the pixels is what those pixels are, not how many. At this pose the half-rate sun (on by default wider than 2.5:1) saves nothing measurable — its pass costs 0.29 ms and saves the resolve 0.13, so the frame is 0.16 ms better without it — because the sun's stage is 0.7 of 9.8 ms here; on the v25 walk's replay it returned 3 ms ([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#a-shading-rate-by-region)).
- **The primary rays follow the pixels up to 7680 and not at 11520.** 1.99 times at 7680 (whose outer quarters already look 43° to 62° off the axis, the path's 55° vertical field) and 3.06 with the view held; at 11520 5.37 times, so the two side thirds (62° to 70° off the axis) cost 3.4 ms each against the centre's 1.5, **2.2 times the centre's cost a ray**. Neither the pixels nor the geometry in view explain it: the visible pairs grow 1.96 times, and the 7680 frame's 1.69 times as many pairs cost exactly its pixels. A grazing ray along the forest floor walks more boxes before it commits (R195 and R135 found the same on the walk and on v34); the surround's outermost columns are the most grazing rays the frame has.
- **The shadow casters jump 3.9 times at 11520** (769,275 against 199,418, where 7680 adds 5 %): clusters the cone test keeps out of the side views' picture because they face away from the eye, kept as casters because they may face the sun. The chain builds 17,000 structures a frame against 6,200, and its records and emit passes grow 2.9 times. This is the one count that follows neither the pixels nor the drawn pairs, and it is new: R195 counted 1.27 times on the walk.
- **The chain follows the union of the cuts** (1.19 at 7680, 1.89 at 11520, 1.09 with the view held), **the bounce its cells and its samples** (1.04 with the view held: it samples a fixed count of rays a frame; 2.2 at 11520 where more of the world is in view), **the accumulation its pixels** (motion and encode: 2.2 to 2.5).
- **Power says the same in watts**: the supersampled 4K, three times the pixels of the same coherent view, draws the most power of the night (453 W, 11 % memory controller) and its GPU frame is only 2.44 times the 4K one; the surround, the same pixel count with its grazing side rays, draws 393 W and costs 3.11 times. The width the owner plays at adds waiting work, not work.

## The base: two things no frame record said

- **The walk moves 1.1 to 1.5 GB/s over PCIe each way** (receive 1,180 MB/s and transmit 971 at the median on the base of 22:47; the earlier base 1,384 and 1,196) **with `upload_bytes` 0 in every frame**, against 0.1 to 0.2 GB/s standing. The streamed world's tile, texture and mesh-room traffic goes through its own copies, which the frame record does not count; the forest session saw 0.5 GB/s on the owner's replay and could not place it either. Not a cost at 1 % of the link, but a number the records should say.
- **Standing, the frame's wall is 3.2 ms longer than the GPU's own span** (20.25 ms against 17.04 on the 20:29 base, `elsewhere_ms` 0.01, the frame's thread waking 3.46 ms after the GPU finished the frame it waited for, `wait_late_ms`); walking, the two are equal (20.45 against 20.47). So about a sixth of the standing frame is inside no frame's timestamps. The record names the wait and not its cause.

## 4. The owner's one run, prepared and not run

**What an agent cannot see.** `dmon`'s `sm` is the share of a second in which some kernel ran; it cannot tell a pass whose warps issue every cycle from one whose warps wait. The forest session's Nsight metrics say the second (17 % issue with the SMs 100 % active) for the frame as a whole, and the device's counters are the only source of the same figures per pass. On Windows they are read by an elevated process; **opening them to all users is never the way** (the control panel's switch reset the owner's driver about fifty times on 2026-11-24 and did not stay set), and no agent asks for them. So this row prepares the capture and its reader and leaves the one run to the owner: `tools/gpu-pass-metrics.ps1` ([bench](../subsystems/bench.md#the-frames-gpu-metrics-by-pass) has its flags, what it records, and how the join is checked).

**What Nsight Systems' own documentation says, and what it does not** (read 2026-11-24 through the web; the installed 2026.6.1's `--help` was not run, since no agent runs Nsight):

- The Vulkan trace (`--trace=vulkan`, `--vulkan-gpu-workload=individual`, the default) records each workload's GPU activity, and the user guide says "you can see Vulkan debug util labels on both the CPU and the GPU". `nsys stats` has a `vulkan_gpu_marker_sum` report among its defaults: the labels' GPU time by name, which is the per-pass share of time without any join ([Nsight Systems user guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/UserGuide/index.html)).
- The GPU metrics are **device-wide samples** ("it does not know which process or context is involved"): GR active, SMs active ("cycles SMs had at least 1 warp in flight"), SM issue ("each SM can issue 4 instructions/cycle", so the forest's 17 % is about 0.7 instructions a cycle an SM), compute warps in flight, unallocated warps in active SMs, DRAM read and write bandwidth, PCIe. On Windows they need an administrator prompt or the UAC dialog. So a per-pass figure is a join by time: the samples that fall inside a pass's GPU label range.
- The SQLite export's schema as an older version documents it ([the export reference, 2022.1](https://archive.docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/2022.1/nsys-exporter/exported_data.html)): `VULKAN_DEBUG_API` (start, end, textId, ...), `VULKAN_WORKLOAD` (start, end, gpu, correlation ids), `StringIds`; the current GPU metric tables are `GPU_METRICS` (timestamp, metricId, value) and `TARGET_INFO_GPU_METRICS` (metricId, metricName), which the forest session's own reader used on the owner's export. Since 2026.1 the JSON export is replaced by `jsonlines` ([release notes, 2026.1](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-systems/2026.1/ReleaseNotes/index.html): known from a search result's summary of the page; the page itself was not read); its row shape is **not documented on any page read**, so the reader finds the four tables by their names and columns and, where one is missing, lists what the export holds and stops.
- **The labels' GPU ranges are not to be trusted blindly**: NVIDIA's forum has reports (2025, and with 2026.3.1 in June 2026) of GPU label ranges that overlap, come out of order or end early around ray-tracing work, which NVIDIA's reply put down to ray-tracing running on async compute queues or missing barriers ([the thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/vulkan-gpu-marker-seem-off/325909)). Hence the reader prints the engine's own `gpu_ms` from the same flight beside the labels' times, and `-Serialize` records the serialized frame so no two passes overlap.

**What is missing for the join, as far as can be known without a capture**: only the jsonlines export's row shape (which key names a row's table, and whether the label rows carry a GPU flag or come from a separate GPU table). If the reader stops on it, the fix is the four table patterns at its top, nothing else.

## 5. The ranked list: what would return the most on the forest

Proposals only; nothing here is built. Ranked by the milliseconds the tables above put on the forest at the owner's 4K, the walk and the stand weighed alike.

1. **The bounce's read, once per 2×2 block where the block is one surface** (up to 2.2 ms standing and 3.1 walking of the resolve; the bounce's passes are another 1.3 to 1.9). *Evidence*: the bounce off returns 2.9 and 5.0 ms, two thirds of it in the resolve, at unchanged power — dependent lookups, the waiting kind; R148 measured the read at 0.15 to 0.32 µs a covered pixel, the largest term of the resolve on every class. The indirect light is low-frequency by construction (a world-space cache of cells about ten pixels wide), so the half-rate sun's block rule — one read where the block's four words name one pair, per pixel elsewhere — would take up to three quarters of the reads where whole blocks are one surface (ground, trunks, a crown's interior; not the crowns' edges, where the sun's rule found its blocks mixed). *Picture*: the indirect term interpolated over a block inside one surface; edges, normals and the direct light stay per pixel; the accumulation hides what is left. A pass's worth of risk: the read's own filtering already spans cells, so the error is the block's quarter-pixel shift in the cell lookup.
2. **The water's reflection ray at half rate, the accumulation filling the rest** (up to 2.0 of the 4.0 ms standing at the lake, 0.4 walking). *Evidence*: reflection off returns 4.0 ms of 9.4 in the resolve with the lake a quarter of the frame, the water stage 5.5 of the resolve's 9.8; its shadow ray is already at half rate by a checkerboard (`reflect_shadow` 1), so the same checkerboard for the reflection itself, reprojected by the history the accumulation keeps, is the known shape. *Picture*: a reflection's detail at half the rate on a moving surface, which the ripples' own normal already blurs; with accumulation off it would show as a checkerboard, so the switch follows the accumulation.
3. **Primary visibility by the hardware rasterizer where the frame's cut is coarse and its structures are rebuilt** (4.75 ms of the walk's 20.9; a loss of 0.67 ms standing over the lake). *Evidence*: `--raster hw` writes the same visibility buffer ([gfx](../subsystems/gfx.md), the rasterizers and the ray path are pixel-compatible by their tests) and removes `trace` (3.1) and the chain's per-frame structures for the drawn clusters (`rt` 3.5 → 1.5: 92,000 structures built → 13,000); standing, 1.1 million pairs at a fine cut make the rasterizer the dearer by 0.4 ms and the chain had little to build. A per-frame choice by the cut's pairs and its built structures, or the owner's choice of `hw` for the forest, is the lever. *Picture*: none (the same visibility buffer, word for word); the owner judges performance under traced primaries, so this is his call, and it is the one lever here that changes no pixel.

**Below the three**: accumulation's own passes (1.9 to 2.4 ms; the owner's quality setting, the motion pass and the deformed vertices' second write being most of it); the surround's grazing side rays (6.7 ms at 11520 beyond the pixels: a coarser LOD is not taken for the side views under traced shadows, [one cut for every view](../subsystems/renderer.md#one-cut-for-every-view), so the lever would be the side views' primary rays by the rasterizer, lever 3 again, or a shorter ray against a far proxy); the 3.9-fold casters of the surround (a chain 2.9 times dearer in its records for clusters that only cast); the masks with the micromaps on (0.7 to 0.8 ms).

## What this does not cover, and stage 2

- **Why a pass waits is inferred, not measured.** Power and `sm` say that time grew without work; only the device's counters say on what (issue stalls, warps in flight, L1 and L2 hit rates) and only per pass with the labels. That is the owner's one run (above), and stage 2 reads it: the worst pass's stalls, then whether the bounce's read and the water's ray wait on memory latency or on the RT cores.
- **Two poses, one path, one scene.** The stand is a quarter water; the walk none. The tarn's streams (11.5 ms of resolve on the owner's walk with only 6,000 pairs drawn) are not on the staging's paths, and the water stage there is the first thing to read.
- **The CPU's side is not read**: every flight shared the machine, and the frame's wall equals the GPU's span on the walk.
- **Not flown**: the bounce at half its rays (`renderer.gi.rays_per_frame`), the refraction alone, the walk at 11520 (the R195 page has it at main's code).
