# The first owner session on the desert overlook: what it found

- **Question ([09 §9.4](../plan/09-testing-profiling.md#94-benchmark-scene-corpus)):** the owner flew the desert overlook by hand and reported five things — (1) near an asset, turning with the mouse while moving with the keyboard, the asset stutters; (2) at different distances different parts of the textures appear and disappear on assets, marked in places; (3) patches on the terrain appear and disappear continuously while moving; (4) none of the generated landmarks were in the scene; (5) the session started very far from the scene. Which of them are the engine's, what causes each, and what fixes them.
- **Date:** 2026-09-24. **Machine:** Intel i9-10980XE (18 cores / 36 threads), 64 GB, Windows 11 Pro; **GPU:** RTX 5090 (32 GB), driver 610.88. **The session:** a `msvc-release` build of the interactive branch made at 16:48 UTC on `385c41e` (so before the texture pipeline of `1f76005` and before the interactive camera landed as `7af15f4`), 11520×2160 `--views surround3`, vsync, `--shadows rt`, `--tunable view.fly.speed=30`, the committed Khronos stand-ins. **The diagnosis:** `msvc-release` builds of this branch's parent `a43b7e5` and of each fix on top of it, replaying the recording offscreen.
- **Machine state:** the session's own summary: other processes at 26.6% of the CPU at the start and 22.2% at the end, the GPU 3% and 21% busy with 8.7 and 8.1 GB of its 32.6 GB held by other tenants, the GPU lock the owner's, and the WARNING raised — its frame times are upper bounds, and the owner reported that performance felt good. The replays ran under the GPU lock (`agent-session-diag`) with other processes at 25% of the CPU and the GPU 99% busy with another tenant at the start of the first one; every result below is a picture, an id buffer or a count, none of which a busy machine changes, and no timing from a replay is used.
- **Decision:** two engine fixes and two conveniences, each with its tests and its page — the material map follows the scene's merge ([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#invariants), [geometry](../subsystems/geometry.md)), a live frame draws the camera between the last two ticks, a scene names its camera path and a session starts there or at `--start`, and `--overlay` is documented with `--interactive` ([apps](../subsystems/apps.md#--interactive-a-camera-somebody-flies)) — and two findings handed on: present pacing, and a derived-data root nothing has built textures into.

## Method

1. **Replay with more markers.** The recording's M presses were replaced with presses at the owner's five ticks and at −240, −4, −2, +2, +4 and +240 ticks around each (`make-marker-log.ps1`), and at ticks inside the moves between them. M moves nothing, so the copy flies the same trajectory; `--marker-captures` then draws every one of those ticks from its own camera after eight frames there.
2. **Capture what a picture cannot say.** `engine-view --capture-channels ids,normals` (added for this; [apps](../subsystems/apps.md#flythroughs)) writes the id buffer — (instance, cluster, triangle) per pixel — beside each marker capture, and a second run with `--view albedo` writes the textured base colour, unlit. The id buffer is what tells a change of **surface** (another cluster, another instance under the pixel: a LOD swap, or one of two coincident layers winning) from a change of **colour on the same surface** (a material, a texture).
3. **Before and after.** The same replay through the build at `a43b7e5` (the owner's code for everything that draws: over a derived-data root with no built textures it takes the decoded path the owner's build had) and through the build with the material fix; ids compared word for word, albedo compared pixel by pixel and attributed to instances through the ids.
4. **The texture pipeline.** The same ticks over a derived-data root where `engine-content build --cache` had built the seven samples' BC7/BC5 mip chains, against the root without them (decoded, one level, level-0 sampling — what the owner's build did).

Everything is under `D:\workspace\game_engine_local\flythrough\diagnosis-2026-09-24\`: `sheets\index.html` is the contact sheet per marker (the second before, the marker, the second after; colour, albedo and the id buffer before the fix, colour and albedo after, and where the albedo moved), `analysis\report.json` the numbers below, and the scripts beside them.

## (2) and (3): one bug — clusters drawn with other clusters' materials

**Cause.** `geometry::merge_cluster_meshes` joins a scene's meshes and, by the scene convention, puts each mesh's level-0 clusters first. Every mesh that comes through the derived-data cache — each glTF, and the terrain — arrives in page order, coarsest first and leaves last, so the merge moves nearly all of its clusters. `SourceMesh::part_of_cluster`, the primitive (and so the material) of each cluster, stayed in the mesh's own order, and `GpuScene::upload_materials` read it by the merged position: every cluster was drawn with the material of whichever cluster had sat at its index. It has been so since the renderer became a library on 2026-09-17, in every non-streamed scene of more than one mesh — the streamed path merges with `ClusterOrder::keep` and a single mesh is not merged, which is where every test and every single-asset capture looked. A mesh with one material is immune, and in this scene only two have more: **the terrain** (sand, ridge rock, basin sand and the basin's floor, by a vote of each cluster's vertices) and **the FlightHelmet** (six). The owner's six landmarks have one material each, so the overlay set was never affected.

**Why it reads as appearing and disappearing.** A frame draws a cut of the LOD DAG, and the cut moves with the camera: every cluster that enters it brings a material that is some other cluster's. So at 100 m the helmet's leather cap was patched with the metal-parts and glass-plastic textures, at 160 m different patches were wrong, and at 270 m the cap happened to come out right while the wooden base was drawn in metal; the ridges were sand with rock patches on the plain dunes, and a patch of another material crossed the dunes in front of the Lantern — exactly "different parts of the textures at different distances" and "patches that come and go while moving". (2) has a second, smaller part the fix does not touch: the owner's build sampled every texture at level 0, so far single-material assets shimmered as the camera moved; the built mip chains of `1f76005` remove about two thirds of it where they have been built ([Moving](#moving)).

**Evidence.** At each marker, the albedo that moved when only the fix changed, per instance (moved by more than 8 of 255 in some channel, of the instance's pixels):

**The ids are the same words before and after at all five markers** — the fix moves no geometry, no cut and no visibility — and nothing but the two multi-material meshes changed colour. The renderer's new test fails on the old code the same way: 18 of the fixture terrain's 182 clusters drew with the wrong material, on a cache miss and on a hit ([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#testing)).

**Fix.** `merge_cluster_meshes` reports where every merged cluster came from (`source_of_cluster`, mesh-local indices), and `load_scene` permutes each mesh's material map with its run of it (`permute_cluster_array`, the function the page layout already used for the same reason). `flythrough_tests.cpp` checks every cluster of a terrain against the vote of its own vertices after the merge; `cluster_lod_tests.cpp` checks the permutation. Engine-host's `render.*` goes through the same `load_scene` and is fixed with it.

### The layered-surface hypothesis

The owner asked whether what appears and disappears could be **layers** — coincident or nearly coincident surfaces resolved by the visibility buffer's tie rule on the 16-bit grid, so that which one wins changes with distance and cut. The two are told apart by the id channel, and it says no, for these reports:

- the patches are in the same (instance, cluster) before and after the fix, so the pixel's **surface** never changed — only its material;
- with the camera at rest (every marker), the ids at −4, −2, +2 and +4 ticks are identical to the marker's at every one of its 5.3–21.5 million covered pixels (the far markers are mostly sky): nothing alternates;
- **while moving** (ticks inside the owner's moves, two apart): see [Moving](#moving) below.

**The FlightHelmet does have layers**, measured on its source: of 94,722 triangles, 79 have a parallel triangle lying on them within one grid step (11 µm on the helmet's own 16-bit grid), 847 within ten and 7,312 within a hundred (1.1 mm: straps, buckles and rivets laid on the leather and the rubber — `MetalParts` over `RubberWood` and `LeatherParts` above all; `analysis\flighthelmet-coincident.json`). Fitted to 95 m, a millimetre of separation is 13 cm, and the coarser levels' simplification error is larger than that, so a strap can sink into the leather it lies on at a distance. That is a real property of layered content and will show on the owner's generated kits too, but it is not what these markers show. Two things follow for later, neither done here: a content-build validator row for **coincident surfaces within the grid step** (the E10 validators are the place), and the simplifier's treatment of thin layered parts; a **depth bias between layers is not a fix** — the tie rule is deliberate ([gfx](../subsystems/gfx.md), "The tie rule").

**The terrain does not overlap itself.** A cut of the DAG is a partition of the surface by construction (`validate_cluster_lod`: level 0 covers every source triangle once; a cluster draws exactly when its own error is under the threshold and its parent's over it), so two terrain clusters of one cut never cover one point; the terrain's patches were entirely the material map.

## (1): the stutter near an asset

**Measured from the frames JSONL** (17,717 records): the ticks per frame were

| ticks | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| frames | 5,674 | 3,055 | 31 | 224 | 29 | 4,249 | 4,441 | 14 |

in two phases. **The first 2.98 s** (frames 12–241) ran at the display's 12.21 ms (82 Hz, p99 12.64 ms) with 211 frames of three ticks and 17 of two, one every 14 frames (170 ms): 240 Hz is 2.93 ticks a refresh. A frame drew the state at the last tick boundary, so on a two-tick frame the camera moved a third less than on its neighbours, six times a second. A far scene barely moves on the screen between ticks and hides it; a near object under a camera turning and strafing at once — the case where the two motions nearly cancel on the object and the object's own screen motion is large — jumps. That is the report.

**From frame 242** (one 31.4 ms frame at 2.99 s, with no input event near it: the first Escape came 70 ms later) to the end, **98.6% of the session**, frames came in **pairs**: one 1.2 ms after the previous (median; 0.3–0.6 ms at the start) and one 23.0 ms after (median), a pair every 24.4 ms, two refreshes. The frame that waited sampled the clock after 23 ms and ran five or six ticks; the other sampled 1 ms later and ran none or one (pairs 0+6: 3,007; 1+5: 2,060; 6+0: 1,406; 5+1: 944; 0+5: 826; 5+0: 407). With FIFO presentation both images are shown, a refresh apart, so the camera the owner saw moved at 41 Hz in steps of five or six ticks and a duplicate — and because the pointer motion of the whole 23 ms arrives at one poll and is fed on one tick, a 1+5 pair shows the turn in one frame and the move in the next. **The owner reported that performance felt good**, and the engine's side of the frame says so: GPU 1.24 ms median, 2.24 p95, 2.38 p99, 3.20 worst; CPU 0.35 ms median, 1.06 p95, 2.75 p99. This is queue pacing, not a stall: when a frame samples the clock against when it is shown.

**Fix (the first phase).** A live frame now draws `FlySession::camera_at(alpha)`: the camera between the tick before the last and the last, at the fraction of a tick the clock holds past it — the drawn camera moves by the frame's own time, one tick (4.2 ms) behind. The ticks, the trajectory hash, the recording, the replay's frames and every marker capture are untouched (they read the state at tick boundaries, as before). `fly_tests.cpp` checks that each of the synthetic session's 2,400 drawn cameras (480 ticks at five fractions) lies on the segment between its two ticks — within 1 µm of the line and 2 × 10⁻⁴ of its fraction along it — with the yaw taking the short way across ±π, and that asking for them leaves the trajectory's hash alone.

**Not fixed here (the second phase)**: a frame sampled 1 ms after the previous one and shown 12 ms after it is drawn at the wrong time however it is drawn. That is present pacing — sampling the clock at a steady cadence, or predicting the moment a frame will be shown — and is the task that owns it; the interpolation makes its steps even (no more 5+1 against 0+6) but cannot make a 41 Hz cadence 82. What the numbers say for it: the pattern set in 3 s after the session started and held for the remaining 3.5 minutes, and it is visible in any `--benchmark` of a live session as `frame_ms` alternating around 1 and 23 ms with `ticks` alternating around 0 and 6.

The session started at the orbit camera, which frames the whole scene's bounds — (8,486, 4,067, 117), 9.4 km from the oasis, looking west and 24° down — because the command line gave no `--camera-path`. The first owner input came at 1.3 s and the first marker at 138 s; at the session's 30 m/s, 9.4 km is over five minutes of flying, and over a minute with Shift held. **Fix:** a scene file may name its camera path (`Scene.camera_path`, schema version 2); the desert overlook names `camera-path.json`, and a live session with no `--camera-path` starts at its first frame, 900 m south of the oasis looking north at the saddle. `--start <x,y,z> <yaw,pitch>` (metres, degrees) starts anywhere. `interactive_tests.cpp` checks both from a recording's header.

## Moving

At rest nothing alternates, so the same questions were asked of ticks inside the owner's own moves (`sheets\moving.html`): backing away from the helmet with S held (ticks 33,700–34,300), and flying at the Lantern with S and then W and Shift (46,600–46,750 and 46,950–47,070).

**The cut moves continuously.** Between frames half a second apart, 58–66% of the covered pixels change their (instance, cluster) while backing from the helmet — terrain 11.8–12.9 million of about 21 million, the helmet 0.49–1.32 million — and 43–53% between frames an eighth of a second apart flying at the Lantern. Before the fix every one of those changes could change a pixel's material; the strips show the helmet's patches and the ridge's rock rearranging frame to frame, and after the fix, the same cuts, a leather cap, a wooden base and rock ridges throughout.

**Layers, while moving.** Across ticks two apart (8 ms), 3,582 of 21.4 million covered pixels at tick 34,000 and 10,244–10,451 of 5.5 million at 46,670 and 47,018 show an A-B-A flip — 0.02% and 0.2%. They are 2–6-pixel dashes scattered over the far terrain at grazing angles (`analysis\flips-46670-crop.png`), nearly all within one instance (3,566 of 3,582; 10,442 of 10,451): sub-pixel triangles point-sampled a little differently as the camera moves, which is geometric aliasing on a picture with no antialiasing yet, not two layers taking turns. After the fix they are invisible in colour, because the dunes there are one material: the terrain's albedo changes between those two ticks on 6,368 pixels before the fix and 275 after at tick 46,670. One short line of them sits on the Lantern's base, where its top face meets the post, and is the only candidate for a coincident pair in these frames; it is 198 pixels.

**Textures.** The owner's build decoded every image and sampled level 0, so a far textured asset aliased; `1f76005` builds BC7/BC5 mip chains and samples them with the triangle's derivatives. Over a derived-data root where `engine-content build --cache` had built the seven samples' textures, the albedo that changes between ticks two apart falls on the Lantern from 48,112 to 16,524 of 185,041 pixels (−66%) at tick 46,670 and from 47,219 to 15,606 at 47,018, and on the FlightHelmet from 103,193 to 74,436 of 939,524 (−28%) at tick 34,000 — the shimmer that reads as "parts of the textures appearing and disappearing" on a single-material asset, and the part of (2) the material fix does not touch. At the markers the built and decoded pictures differ by more than 8 of 255 on 1.4%, 3.2% and 6.2% of the helmet's pixels at 100, 160 and 270 m, and on 13% and 10% of the Lantern's at markers 4 and 5. **The texture pipeline already removes most of it — but only where the textures have been built.** engine-view's own cache fill writes a mesh's container and the records of where its textures would be, and builds no `.tex`, so a scene flown on a derived-data root that only engine-view has filled draws the decoded path, as this diagnosis's own first root did; the built path needs `engine-content build --cache` (or `build-all` over the scene's meshes) first. That is for the texture follow-up to settle (build on a miss, say so in the summary, or both); it is noted here because the owner's next session would otherwise still alias.

## What remains, and whose it is

- **Present pacing** (the second phase of (1)): the pacing task.
- **Coincident layers in content**: a validator row for surfaces within the grid step, and the simplifier on thin layered parts — the content pipeline's (E10's validators), not this change.
- **Textures that were never built**: a scene whose derived-data root only engine-view has filled draws decoded textures and aliases on far assets ([Moving](#moving)); building on a miss, or saying so, is the texture follow-up's. The built path itself showed nothing here that needs a change to the resolve's sampling.
- **Antialiasing**: the 2–6-pixel id dashes on the far terrain at grazing angles are point sampling, and uniform colour hides them; a textured or multi-coloured ground at that distance will not, and that is the renderer's open antialiasing row, not a defect of the cut.
- **The session corpus**: this recording is the first real session; it names the scene's old hash and a build before the fixes, so it is kept locally rather than committed, and the next session flown on a fixed build is the one to keep.
