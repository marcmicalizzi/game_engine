# Animation graphs as data: the grounding and the design (roadmap S35, 2026-11-18)

**Status: designed; stage 1 built (2026-11-18, [animation](../subsystems/animation.md#graphs-s35-stage-1)).** Where stage 1 differs from the text below: the compiled condition and transition records are named `ControlCondition` and `ControlTransition` (the records keep `GraphCondition` and `GraphTransition`, and one namespace cannot hold both); a parameter's range is `range_min`/`range_max` (`min` and `max` are macros under `<windows.h>`); the masks (`GraphMask`) are left to stage 3, which reads them; and the control pass is a function the walker calls from its tick observer, not yet a scheduled system. This page is the row's whole result: what the engine plays today and where its blending is written by hand, what the published systems do and what each would cost an instance here, and the design that follows — the graph as schema data in a document, compiled at load into a flat program evaluated per graph over its instances, its state in bytes, its determinism and its LOD — with the stages that build it, smallest first. Nothing was built, run or measured for it: the batch's gate held the machine (a `read-only` row), so every number below is either read from a page of this repository or from a source cited beside it, and every number the design needs and does not have is written as **to measure** with the command that takes it. Plan: [05 §5.11](../plan/05-simulation.md#511-integration-notes) ("blend trees and state machines as data"); the capability it extends: [animation](../subsystems/animation.md); the data half under it: [anim](../subsystems/anim.md).

## What exists, and where the blending is written by hand

**The data half** ([anim](../subsystems/anim.md)): SoA poses (`Pose`, `PoseView`), clips sampled by key search (a binary search past eight keys since A48), `blend` (one channel at a time; the rotation by `core/math`'s `slerp`), `make_additive` and `blend_additive` (built and tested, **called by nothing**), `local_to_model`, 3×4 skinning matrices, and the standard skeleton's 23 roles with `map_joints` and the bind-difference retarget.

**The tick** ([animation](../subsystems/animation.md)): one `AnimationPlayer` per entity (64 bytes, persisted: a clip, a time, a speed, a weight toward the bind pose, and **one** cross-fade in flight), a pose pool, three systems (`advance_players`, `sample_poses`, `build_skinning_matrices`), four tiers chosen by pixels of limb travel (A44) under two ceilings (`animation.lod.full_max` 256, `skinned_max` 1,024), and the renderer contract: one span of joint matrices and a `{first, count}` run per instance. `play(entity, clip, seconds)` is the whole control surface: a fade started while one is in flight collapses the one in flight first.

**The walker's body is a state machine written in C++** (`apps/engine_view/player.cpp`, `PlayerBody::tick` and `tick_gait`; [apps](../subsystems/apps.md#the-players-body)). What it does each tick, after the walker's step and before a game module's tick:

| What | How it is written today | What a graph calls it |
|---|---|---|
| stand / walk / run | a `u8 gait` of 1, 2 or 3 from `moving` and the `fast` action; on a change, `play(clip, 0.2 s)`, the first tick a cut (`k_gait_fade_s`) | a state machine of three states, transitions on two parameters, a cross-fade of 0.2 s, an entry that cuts |
| the cadence | the phase advanced by the distance walked over the stride (`gait_stride_m`), **both** clips of a fade in flight set to that phase at rate 0 (`set_playhead(entity, time, fade_time, 0)`) | a sync group whose phase is a parameter the host drives: phase-locked states |
| the idle | the idle clip at its own rate 1 while the gait's clips are held at rate 0 | a state outside the sync group, with its own clock |
| the crouch | `crouch_pose`: a trunk lean, a two-bone law-of-cosines leg solve, a shift that plants the feet — applied in **model space to a copy of the skinning matrices** after the step (`CrouchTurn`, `set_joint_crouch_pose`) | an additive layer and a two-bone IK node, in joint space, before the matrices |
| the head look | turns of the torso, neck and head on the same copy (`LookJoint`) | an additive layer driven by two parameters |
| the first-person head | the head's subtree collapsed on the copy (`JointCollapse`) | not animation: a draw-side mask, and it stays one |

The three model-space layers are outside the pose on purpose: the pose, the pool and every hash a summary takes stay the animation's, and the copy is what the frame draws ([apps](../subsystems/apps.md#the-head-look-layer)). Their own page names the joint-space form as the follow-up "if the blend at the neck looks wrong on a body that moves its head". The crouch's arithmetic is `+ - * / sqrt` with `fly_sin_cos` and `fly_atan2` (engine-view's `fly_camera.h`), so it is the same bits on every compiler ([crouch grounding](crouch-grounding-2026-11-17.md)).

**The village's residents have no animation at all** ([npc](../subsystems/npc.md#the-capability-at-each-tier-the-lod-policy)): its LOD0 row says "animation + physics + perception + detailed nav — none of which exists yet, so LOD0 is LOD1". A resident is a routine with a closed form; what it would play is the second consumer this design is for.

**What an instance costs today**, read from the pages (none re-measured here):

|---|---|---|---|
| one LOD0 instance, 23 joints, sampled and skinned (10⁴ instances, one worker, x86-64-v2) | 2.53 µs a tick | animation.md, "Performance notes" | quiet (8.3 / 8.7 % others) |

So a sample of one clip is the unit of cost — about 1.6 µs for a 23-joint rig — and every design choice below is counted in samples, blends and bytes.

## What the published systems do, and what each buys this engine

Each source below was read on this run (2026-11-18); a part that could not be read is marked so. A web page is data: nothing here was taken from one as an instruction.

**State machines over blend trees** — Unity's Animator and Unreal's Animation Blueprint, the two engines' own documentation.
- Unity, *Animation transitions* (Unity 6.6 User Manual): a transition carries a **duration** (seconds with *Fixed Duration*, else a fraction of the source state's normalized time), an **offset** (where the destination starts, as a fraction of its timeline), an optional **exit time** (a normalized time of the source after which the transition may fire, checked once per loop below 1), **conditions** on parameters of which all must hold, and an **interruption source** with an ordered queue; one transition is active at a time and transitions from *Any State* queue first.
- Unity, *Animation Blend Trees*: a blend tree is a *state* that mixes similar motions by one or two parameters (1D, 2D, and *Direct*, a weight per clip), and blends "in normalized time", so a walk and a run of different durations blend once their foot contacts sit at the same normalized times (the page's example: left foot at 0.0, right at 0.5). A transition moves between unlike states; a blend tree mixes like ones.
- Unreal, *State Machines* and *Transition Rules* (UE 5.9 documentation): a state holds a graph that yields a pose; a transition's rule is a boolean; **when several rules are true, the smallest priority value wins**; a transition's *Blend Logic* is standard (a duration and a curve), **inertialization** (which needs an Inertialization node after the state machine; the page advises durations under 0.4 s and not between very different poses), or custom; an *automatic rule* fires on the remaining time of the state's most relevant player.
- **What it buys here**: the walker's three-state machine above, written once as data and read by every host; a resident's routine states (stand, walk, sit, work) as a graph a content author edits without a build. **What it costs**: the machine itself is a few compares a tick; the cost is the poses its states evaluate — one sample a state, two during a cross-fade.

**Layered and additive blending with masks.**
- Unity, *Animation Layers*: a layer **overrides** the layers below it or **adds** to them, limited to a body part by an **avatar mask**; a *synced* layer reuses another layer's state machine with other clips (a wounded walk over the base locomotion).
- ozz-animation's runtime (Guillaume Blanc, MIT; its *Animation runtime* documentation and `blending_job.cc`, read): poses are SoA; the blending job is **a weighted sum of the layers' quaternions, each negated when its dot with the sum is negative, normalized once at the end** — not slerp; per-joint weights are a mask (a joint left unweighted counts as 1); the rest pose fills whatever weight a joint is short of a threshold; additive layers are applied after, as a rotation interpolated from identity, with a negative weight subtracting. Jobs are stateless: each reads its inputs and writes its outputs and touches nothing else, which is what makes them safe on any thread.
- **What it buys here**: the crouch and the look as joint-space layers on the pose rather than model-space edits on a copy; an upper-body layer for a resident carrying something while its legs walk. **What it costs**: an override layer is one more sample and one blend over the masked joints; an additive layer is one more sample (or none, for a pose built at load) and one composition.

**Inertialization** — Bollo, *High Performance Animation in Gears of War 4*, SIGGRAPH 2017 Talks (doi 10.1145/3084363.3085069; read in full, two pages). A blended transition evaluates both the source and the target state for as long as it lasts, which the talk calls effectively doubling the cost. Gears of War 4 cuts to the target at once and adds a post-process: at the transition it records the source's velocity and the difference between the source and target poses, builds **a quintic that matches that difference and velocity at the start and reaches zero by the end**, and adds it to the target; the initial acceleration is chosen so the curve does not overshoot. Measured on Xbox One: one animation sequence 20 µs, a blend 10 µs; the curve 12 µs against the cross-fade's 30 µs, **60 % less**. The same talk prices blend spaces: a 1D one is two sequences and a blend, 150 % over one sequence; a 2D one three sequences and two blends, 300 %. *Not read*: Bollo's GDC 2018 talk "Inertialization: High-Performance Animation Transitions in Gears of War" (the GDC Vault video is members-only); its coefficient rule and the clamp of the transition time are therefore not pinned here (stage 4 reads them before it builds). Holden's *Inertialization Transition Cost* (2022-06-02) keeps per transition an initial offset, its velocity and the time since; his *Dead Blending* (2023-02-25) extrapolates the source pose with a decaying velocity and cross-fades from it, needing "only ... the current pose" and its velocity.
- **What it buys here**: the walker's walk–stand–run changes and a resident's state changes at the cost of one evaluated state rather than two for 0.2 s, and a velocity-continuous change where today's linear fade is not. **What it costs**: per joint an offset and its velocity while a transition is in flight (48 bytes a joint, below), and one extra evaluation of the outgoing pose at the transition's tick for its velocity.

**Motion matching** — Clavet, *Motion Matching and The Road to Next-Gen Animation*, GDC 2016 (Ubisoft Montreal): the session's abstract on the GDC Vault (read; the video is members-only and *not read*) describes long mocap takes imported with markup, and a runtime that "continuously" finds the database frame matching both the current pose and the desired future motion and blends briefly into it. Holden, Kanoun, Perepichka and Popa, *Learned Motion Matching*, ACM TOG 39(4), 53:1–53:12 (SIGGRAPH 2020): the abstract and Ubisoft La Forge's announcement (2020-07-08) say motion matching's memory "generally scales linearly with the amount of data used", which the learned form replaces with network weights; the paper's own memory and per-character figures were *not read* (its PDF is past what this run could fetch). Holden's *Code vs Data Driven Displacement* (2021-09-23): a character moved by its data and a simulation object moved by code drift apart, and each way of syncing them trades responsiveness against foot sliding.
- **What it buys here, today: nothing it can be given.** It needs minutes of captured locomotion with markup; the owner's authored bodies have no walk clip (C57 is a content job), the walker's gait is generated from the literature, and residents have no clips at all. Roadmap S37 stays deferred ([10 §10.6](../plan/10-roadmap-risks.md#106-decisions-deliberately-deferred)); the graph below is what it would plug into (a state whose pose is a search), and nothing here forecloses it.

**Root motion** — Unity, *How Root Motion Works*: the *body transform* (the mass centre) is the clip's one world-space datum and the *root transform* its projection on the ground plane, computed at run time; each frame the root's change moves the object, and *bake into pose* keeps rotation, height or ground travel in the pose instead. **Here**: the walker is code-driven — a Jolt capsule ([physics](../subsystems/physics.md#the-character)) — and the gait is in place with its phase driven by distance, which is Holden's "sync the animation to the simulation" in its simplest form. So the design keeps clips in place and reads root motion as data (a clip's root track, extracted at load, offered as an output a host *may* apply), never as the thing that moves an entity on its own.

**Sync markers and phase** — Unreal, *Sync Groups* (UE 5.9): the animation with the highest weight leads, sets the cycle's length, and the followers' rates are scaled to it; **sync markers** align clips by their position between named markers, and a marker missing from any clip of the group is ignored for the whole group. Unity's blend trees sync by normalized time. Bollo 2017's third section computes a scalar **phase** from the left foot's position relative to the right projected on the velocity, tabulates it per frame at start-up, and starts a locomotion state at the frame whose phase matches the current pose. **Here**: the gait already is one phase in [0, 1) driven by distance, and its walk and run share it; a sync group whose phase is either the leader's or a host-driven parameter is that rule written down. Markers wait for clips that have them (an authored walk with foot contacts); phase by normalized time is enough for the gait's generated cycles, whose heel strike is at phase 0 by construction ([gait grounding](gait-grounding-2026-11-04.md)).

**Crowds at a distance** — Unreal's *Animation Sharing* plugin (UE 5.9): a few animation instances per state "bucket", each with randomized start offsets, whose poses drive many characters through the leader-pose mechanism; going from 100 to 1,000 characters is a "minimum increase in performance cost" (no figure given). Unreal's *Animation Budget Allocator*: a game-thread budget in milliseconds (`a.Budget.BudgetMs`, default 1.0), met by lowering the less significant meshes' update rates (to every *n*th frame, `MaxTickRate` default 10) and interpolating between updates. **Here**: the budget already exists (`cap_tiers`, A44), and so does the update divisor; what sharing adds is the rule that a far resident playing a looping state **reads a pose computed once for every resident at that state and phase**, so a thousand residents at LOD1 cost a few dozen samples rather than five hundred a tick.

## The design

### 1. The graph is schema data in a document

A graph is a **document record** and its parts are its **child records**, declared in `systems/animation/schemas/animation_graph.schema` (the capability's own file, ADR-0027's first registration point; no shared schema file moves):

| Record (`@kind(record)`) | Parent | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `engine.animation.Graph` | — | `name`, `skeleton_roles` (whether clips are named for the standard skeleton or a library's skeleton), `outputs` (`pose`, optionally `root_motion`) |
| `GraphParam` | a `Graph` | `name`, `kind` (`Float`, `Bool`, `Trigger`), `default`, `min`, `max` |
| `GraphNode` | a `Graph`, or a `GraphState` (the state's pose) | `kind` (closed enum below), `inputs: id128[]` (child nodes), `param` (a `GraphParam` by name), `clip` (a library clip by name), `rate`, `looping`, `sync_group`, `mask`, `thresholds: f32[]`, `weight` |
| `GraphMachine` | a `Graph` | `entry` (a state's id), `parameter_phase` (a sync group's phase taken from a parameter, the gait's distance drive) |
| `GraphState` | a `GraphMachine` | `name`; its pose is its one child `GraphNode` |
| `GraphTransition` | a `GraphMachine` | `from` (a state, or `any`), `to`, `conditions: GraphCondition[]` (a parameter, an operator, a value; all must hold), `exit_phase: f32?`, `duration_s`, `blend` (`Cut`, `CrossFade`, `Inertialize`), `offset` (`Zero`, `KeepPhase`, `MatchPhase`), `priority` |
| `GraphMask` | a `Graph` | joints by standard-skeleton role, each with its subtree or alone, and a weight |

**Node kinds** (an enum, append-only by the schema's evolution rules): `Clip`, `Blend1D`, `Override` (a layer over a base, through a mask), `Additive` (a difference built at load from a clip and a reference pose, applied with a weight), `Machine` (a `GraphMachine`'s output as a node, so machines nest), and later `TwoBoneIK` and `Aim` (S34's), `RootMotion`. **Why a closed enum and not a node interface**: plan 11 §11.4 forbids a virtual call a node; a closed set compiles to a kernel table, and a studio that needs another kind adds an enumerator and a kernel in this capability — the same rule the schema's enums already follow.

**Why child records and not one record with arrays.** Authored layers merge structurally, per object and per property ([doc](../subsystems/doc.md)): with a state per record, two people retuning two transitions of one graph both keep their edits, and the change feed names the state that moved rather than the whole graph. A graph of the walker's size is about a dozen records; a resident's a few dozen. **A graph has no `materialize` declaration**: it is an asset entities refer to, not an entity — like a clip, it stays a record.

**Clips are named, not keyed**: a `Clip` node names a clip as the library names it (`<prefix>/<name>`, or a standard-skeleton clip set once one exists), and a name the library lacks is refused at compile with the graph's and the node's names — never a silent rest pose, which is the rule `--player` already holds ([apps](../subsystems/apps.md#the-players-body)).

**Parameters are components.** Two components, in the same schema file:

| Component | Persisted | Bytes | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `AnimationGraphInput` | yes | 56 (align 8) | `graph: id128`, `values: f32[8]` (the graph's parameters in declaration order; a bool is 0 or 1), `triggers: u32` (one bit a trigger, cleared when a transition takes it) |
| `AnimationGraphState` | yes | 64 (align 8) | `graph: id128`; two machines, each `state: u16`, `next: u16`, `elapsed: f32`, `duration: f32`, `time: f32`, `next_time: f32` (20 bytes); `phase: f32[2]` (two sync groups) |

**Eight parameters, two machines and two sync groups are stated limits** ([ADR-0017](../adr/0017-no-hidden-limits.md)): a graph that declares more is refused at compile with the count, and the limit is raised by a new component version, not worked around. They are fixed-width because they are small and persisted: unlike a pose (variable length, private, recomputable: animation.md's argument), the inputs are written by gameplay and read by a save, and the state is exactly what a save needs to put the same pose back — the generalization of `AnimationPlayer`'s "a pose is a pure function of (skeleton, clip, time)". Both are `AnimationPlayer`'s size class; a resident's whole persisted animation is 120 bytes. `AnimationPlayer` stays for an entity that plays clips directly; an entity is graph-driven or player-driven, never both (the archetype says which, ADR-0028's "discriminator in the archetype"), so the two samplers' queries are disjoint.

**Size-table entries** (`systems/animation/tests/size_table.cpp`): `AnimationGraphInput` 56/8, `AnimationGraphState` 64/8, and the compiled program's records below: `GraphOp` 16/4, `GraphTransition` 16/4, `GraphCondition` 8/4, `InertialJoint` 48/4.

### 2. Compiled at load into a flat program

`GraphLibrary::compile(graph records, skeleton)` runs once per **(graph, skeleton)** pair — the masks resolve to per-joint weights and the clips to library indices for that skeleton — outside the tick, and produces:

- **A control table per machine**: the states, each with a contiguous run of its outgoing transitions sorted by `(priority, declaration order, Id128)`, `any` transitions first (Unity's queue order; Unreal's smallest-priority rule); each transition's conditions a run of `GraphCondition {u16 param; u8 op; u8 _; f32 value}`. A state index is its position in the sorted list of the machine's states by `Id128`, so the same graph gives the same indices on every machine.
- **A pose program**: the nodes in post-order as `GraphOp {u8 kind; u8 dst; u8 a; u8 b; u16 node; u16 param; u32 data; f32 k}` — `dst`, `a`, `b` are **pose registers**, allocated by a linear scan over the post-order so a node's register is reused once its parent has read it. The walker's graph needs two registers; a blend tree under a layer three or four. The compile records the count `R`.
- **The padding** the cull needs (`bounds_padding`, [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#bounds-the-cluster-spheres-are-the-rest-poses-and-a-skinned-vertex-is-not-in-them)): the largest `clip_displacement_bound` over every clip the graph can play, a blend node's bound taken over its parameter's grid (a rotation blended on the short arc can swing a vertex past both ends by a little), and every additive layer's bound added (the triangle inequality: conservative). This is the gait's rule — pad for the run, play the walk inside it ([apps](../subsystems/apps.md#the-procedural-gait)) — made general, because a clip played outside the padding the cull was given would move vertices outside their spheres.

**Evaluation: per graph, over all its instances, a batch at a time, op by op.** Each graph keeps a dense list of its instances (their pool slots and state, in slot order, changed only by attach, detach and tier changes, which happen outside the parallel phase). Two passes a tick:

1. **Control** (`animation.graph_control`): per instance, read its `AnimationGraphInput`, evaluate the current state's transition run, start or advance a transition, advance the clocks and the sync groups' phases, and write a weight per op of the pose program (zero for an op in a state that contributes nothing). This pass branches on data — which transition fires is the data — but it is a few compares over a few bytes an instance and touches no pose.
2. **Pose** (`animation.graph_sample`): for a batch of `B` instances (a run-time parameter, `animation.graph.batch`, sized so `B × R × joints × 40` bytes stays in L2: 64 instances of a 23-joint rig at three registers is 177 KB), op by op: **compact** the batch's instances whose op weight is non-zero into a list, then run the op's kernel over that list. The kernel is selected once per op per batch from a table indexed by `GraphOp::kind` (plan 11 §11.4's idiom), and its inner loop is over instances and joints with no branch on the data: a state that is off for an instance is absent from the list, not skipped inside the loop ("rare-case handling moves to a separate pass over a compacted list").

**Why op-major in batches and not instance-major.** Instance-major (each instance runs the whole program) keeps an instance's registers in L1, but dispatches a kernel per node per instance — the indirect call a node plan 11 forbids, a few thousand a tick for a village. Op-major over the whole population dispatches once per op but streams every instance's registers through memory once per op: a thousand residents at three 23-joint registers is 2.8 MB a pass. The batch is the compromise, and its size is a tunable because the right size depends on the rig and the cache, not on the data.

**The kernels are `anim`'s, not new arithmetic**: `Clip` is `Clip::sample` into a register; a two-input cross-fade is `anim::blend`; an n-input blend (`Blend1D`, and the cross-fade from stage 2 on) is ozz's normalized weighted sum (below, "Determinism"); `Additive` is `blend_additive`; `Override` is a masked blend. The registers live in a scratch arena the capability owns, sized at attach for the largest `R × joints` of the graphs in use and grown geometrically, so the steady-state tick allocates nothing (AGENTS.md's frame-loop rule). The pose program's last register is copied into the instance's pool slot, and `build_skinning_matrices` is unchanged.

### 3. Determinism

**`hashed`, as the capability is.** The control pass and the pose pass are per-instance work over disjoint writes, in an order fixed by the dense list, with no shared accumulator, so one worker and eight give the same bytes and so do two runs — the bit-identical worker case extends to graph-driven instances.

**Across compilers the graph adds no arithmetic that differs.** Floating-point contraction is off tree-wide ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)), so every `+ - * / sqrt` is the same float on MSVC, GCC and Clang. What does differ is a C library call: `core/math`'s `slerp` takes `std::acos` and `std::sin`, which are not correctly rounded, so a clip's rotation sampled inside a key interval, and `anim::blend`, may differ by an ulp between C libraries — already true today, and why the ancestor tests pin hashes at times that keep out of a slerp interval ([animation](../subsystems/animation.md#non-joint-ancestors)). The graph's own additions keep to the portable set:
- **The n-input blend is a normalized weighted sum**, as ozz's blending job is: each quaternion negated when its dot with the running sum is negative (written as a multiply by a sign, not a branch), added with its weight, the sum normalized once. No transcendental function, the same bits everywhere, and cheaper than a chain of slerps.
- **The inertialization curve is a polynomial** in `+ - * /`. Its rotation offset needs an angle; that angle is taken once per joint per transition through the portable `atan2` the crouch already uses (`fly_atan2`), not `std::acos`.
- **The phase** is a fraction advanced by a multiply and wrapped by a subtraction of its floor, as the gait's is.

So a graph-driven pose is portable to the bit wherever its clips are sampled at their keys and its two-input cross-fades are not used — which stage 2 makes the norm by moving the cross-fade to the weighted sum. The remaining C-library dependency is `slerp` in `Clip::sample`; it is `anim`'s and is named here, not changed. **One prerequisite is a lower-layer change**: `fly_sin_cos` and `fly_atan2` live in `apps/engine_view/fly_camera.h`, and a capability cannot include an app; they move to `core/math` (as `portable_sin_cos`, `portable_atan2`) in a commit of their own, argued as anim's pose views were ([02 §2.8.1](../plan/02-architecture.md#281-the-first-capability-built-through-the-contract)), with engine-view's call sites unchanged in bits (owner question 4).

### 4. Inertialization, and its state

A transition whose `blend` is `Inertialize` cuts the machine to the target state and opens an **inertial slot**: per joint an `InertialJoint {Vec3 dx; Vec3 dv; Vec3 dr; Vec3 dw}` — the translation offset and its velocity, the rotation offset as an axis times an angle and its angular velocity, 48 bytes — plus the transition's duration and elapsed time. Slots come from a per-skeleton LIFO arena as pose slots do, held only while a transition is in flight: a 23-joint walker in a 0.2 s change holds 1,104 bytes for 12 ticks, and a crowd holds a slot per resident *changing state*, not per resident. At the transition's tick the outgoing state is evaluated twice, at this tick and one tick back, for `dx` and `dv` (Bollo's "the source animation's velocity and the difference between the source and target poses"); after that the outgoing state is never evaluated again, which is the whole saving. The curve decays each channel to zero at the duration with zero velocity and acceleration (a quintic); its initial acceleration and the clamp that keeps it from overshooting are Bollo's GDC 2018 rule, **to be read before stage 4 builds it**.

Expected cost per change, to measure: one sample of the target state for the duration plus the curve per joint, against today's two samples and a blend — by Bollo's Xbox One figures 12 µs against 30 µs, here in the order of 1.6 µs against 3.2 µs plus a blend per 23-joint instance, **to measure** with `animation.graph.transition/<mode>` (below).

### 5. The LOD policy

The tiers stay A44's (pixels of limb travel), the bands and the budget unchanged; what a tier does to a graph:

| Tier | Control | Pose program | Transitions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LOD0 | every tick | the full program | as authored | |
| LOD1 | every 2nd tick | the program with every node marked `detail` removed (an additive lean, a breathing layer: a node may say it is not worth a pixel at 1–4 px of travel) | `Inertialize` and `CrossFade` become **cuts at the transition's midpoint**, as today's LOD1 cuts a fade | **a looping state with no transition in flight reads the shared pose** (below) |
| LOD2 | every 4th tick | the dominant state's dominant clip, sampled for gameplay, not skinned | cuts | draws the rest pose, as today |
| LOD3 | never | — | — | the machine's state advanced in closed form at the promotion: a looping state's time is affine in elapsed time, as a player's is; a transition that would have finished in the gap is taken as finished; a condition that would have fired in the gap is evaluated at the promotion on the inputs then |

The simplified programs are compiled with the full one (three programs a graph a skeleton), so a tier change switches a pointer and never compiles.

**A far resident's pose is shared.** At LOD1, an instance whose machine sits in a looping state with no transition in flight is assigned to a **shared slot** keyed by (graph, skeleton, state, phase bucket); its own pool slot is released (as LOD3 releases one) and its renderer run points at the shared slot's matrices — the contract already allows two instances one run. The shared slots are evaluated once a tick each, at their bucket's centre phase, through the same pose program. **The bucket count follows from the band**: a limb travelling `b` pixels through a cycle moves at most about π·b pixels per cycle (a sinusoidal swing of amplitude b/2), so a phase rounded to the nearest of `n` buckets is off by at most 1/(2n) of a cycle and the limb by π·b/(2n) pixels; LOD1's ceiling is 4 pixels of travel, so **16 buckets keep the error under 0.4 px** and 8 under 0.8. A thousand residents at LOD1 walking one graph's walk then cost 16 samples a tick and a closed-form phase each, where today's LOD1 would sample 500 a tick (every second tick). What a shared pose draws is a function of the state and the phase, never of when the instance was demoted, so two runs and two cameras agree — the argument that chose the rest pose at LOD2 ([animation](../subsystems/animation.md#the-lod-policy)). It changes a picture at LOD1 by under half a pixel by construction; whether that is taken is the owner's (question 1), and it is measured as the tier boundaries were, by FLIP on both sides of the switch.

Today's ceilings (`full_max`, `skinned_max`) cap graph-driven instances exactly as they cap players; a shared instance counts as skinned for the picture and costs nearly nothing on the CPU, so the GPU's deformation (0.06 µs an instance, read above) is what a shared crowd still pays.

### 6. Hot reload through the document's change feed

The host that holds the document (engine-host's runtime world; engine-view when it reads graphs from a document directory) calls `GraphLibrary::refresh(document)` between ticks: `Document::changed_since(revision)` ([doc](../subsystems/doc.md)) names the changed ids; a changed `Graph` or child record recompiles that graph for each skeleton using it; the new program replaces the old outside the parallel phase. Instances keep their state **by `Id128`**: a state that still exists keeps its index's meaning through the new sort; a removed state sends its instances to the machine's entry with a cut; parameter values are carried by name and a removed parameter is dropped. A reload never re-reads the document wholesale and never touches an instance whose graph did not change. A reload that changes the padding (a new clip) re-pads the instances on the next frame, through the path a tier promotion takes.

### 7. What the deformed-vertex pool and the skin read

Nothing new. The pose program ends in the instance's pool slot (or a shared slot), `build_skinning_matrices` writes the 3×4 matrices, and the renderer reads one span and a run per instance; `deform.slang` skins into the per-frame pool for the visible entries ([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#skinned-instances)). The graph is invisible past the span, which is why it needs no renderer change and why the renderer must never learn of it.

## Stages, in build order

Each stage is one branch, with what it builds, its size, what must stay byte-identical, the case that pins it and the bench it adds. Proposed rows, unnumbered.

**Stage 1 (S): a state machine that drives the player it already has.** The schema file (the records and the two components), the compiler for machines whose states are single `Clip` nodes, and `animation.graph_control`, which for this stage **writes the entity's `AnimationPlayer`** through the same collapse-and-fade code `play()` uses, so `sample_poses` runs unchanged. engine-view's `PlayerBody::tick` and `tick_gait` are replaced by a graph built at load from the tunables it reads today (`walk_clip`, `run_clip`, `idle_clip`, the 0.2 s fade, the cut on the first tick) and three inputs a tick (`moving`, `fast`, and the distance phase as a sync group's parameter); the control pass for the player runs where `tick_gait` ran, in the tick observer before a game module's tick, so a module's `actor_play` on `player` still overrides it as the hotload ABI promises today. **Byte-identical**: every summary hash of the walker's existing cases — `player_gait_tests.cpp`, `player_view_tests.cpp`, `player_rest_tests.cpp`, `walk_tests.cpp` (the crouch's planted feet) — unchanged, because the player receives the same calls in the same ticks. **New cases**: the compile's refusals (an unknown clip, a ninth parameter, a third machine, a transition to a state of another machine), transitions taken by priority then declaration order, the first tick a cut, one worker against eight. **Bench**: `animation.graph.control/<n>` at 10⁴ instances; expected well under the tick's 2.5 µs an instance — to measure with `tools/dev.ps1 bench -Preset msvc-release -Filter 'animation.*'` (`--wait-quiet=1800`, the machine state quoted).

**Stage 2 (M): the pose program.** `Blend1D` with sync groups (the leader the highest weight, Unreal's rule; or the machine's parameter phase), the batched op-major evaluator with compacted lists and registers, `animation.graph_sample` for graph-driven entities (which then carry no `AnimationPlayer`), and the n-input normalized blend. The walker's walk-to-run becomes a `Blend1D` on speed over one phase instead of a fade between two states. **Changes** the walker's pose hashes where the walk and the run meet (the owner sees it), so the existing cases are re-pinned with one sentence of attribution each; **byte-identical**: at a blend weight of exactly 0 or 1 the pose equals the one clip's to the bit (a case), and a stage-1 graph run through the pose program gives stage 1's pose (a case). **Bench**: `animation.graph.blend1d/<joints>`, `animation.graph.batch/<B>` across 16 to 256 to choose the default.

**Stage 3 (S): layers and masks.** `GraphMask` by role and subtree, compiled per skeleton; `Override` and `Additive` (with `make_additive` at load). A resident's upper-body layer over its walk is the case. Nothing of the walker moves yet. **Case**: a masked override at weight 1 equals the overlay on the masked joints and the base elsewhere, to the bit.

**Stage 4 (M): inertialization.** The slot arena, the curve after reading Bollo 2018, and `Inertialize` as a transition's blend; LOD1's cut. The walker's 0.2 s changes become inertialized (the owner judges the walk–stand change in third person). **Cases**: the offset is zero at the duration with zero velocity, matches the source's velocity at the start (finite difference), never overshoots on a property sweep, and a slot is held only while a transition is in flight. **Bench**: `animation.graph.transition/{crossfade,inertialize}` per 23-joint instance.

**Stage 5 (M): the LOD programs and the shared pose.** The three programs per graph, the LOD3 closed form for a machine, and the shared slots at LOD1 behind `animation.lod.share` (off until the owner's answer). **Measured as the tiers were**: FLIP on both sides of the LOD0/LOD1 boundary with sharing on and off, and the tick of 1,000 and 10,000 residents walking a village graph (`animation.tick.graph.village/<n>`, and an engine-view pair on the village scene at 3840 × 2160, `--raster rt --shadows rt`, the bounce on, main's copied-out build against the branch's, back to back in one lease).

**Stage 6 (S): hot reload.** `refresh` through `changed_since`; engine-host's runtime world first. **Case**: a transition's duration changed by `doc.apply` is the one the next tick uses, the instances keep their states by id, and a removed state sends its instances to the entry.

**Then**: the crouch and the look move into the pose as `TwoBoneIK` and `Aim` nodes in joint space (with S34's IK), which **changes** the pose and its hashes and puts the crouch inside the gameplay pose a foot-plant query reads (owner question 2); a `RootMotion` output; the protocol's `animation.*` methods (S36), which then set a graph's parameters by name.

## What it declines, and why

- **Motion matching now** (S37 stays deferred): no captured locomotion exists to match, memory grows with the data, and the walker's gait is generated. The graph leaves room for it as a state kind.
- **2D blend spaces** until a consumer needs strafing: Bollo prices a 2D blend space at three sequences and two blends, 300 % over one clip, and neither first consumer strafes.
- **A node interface, a graph interpreter per instance, or a script in the tick**: plan 11 §11.4, and ADR-0027's ban on an extension mechanism that costs an indirect call per entity per tick.
- **Root motion moving an entity on its own**: the walker is code-driven and its gait is synced to the simulation by distance; a resident's root motion is offered as an output its capability may apply.
- **Sync markers** until a clip carries foot contacts; normalized phase serves the generated gait.
- **Moving the first-person head collapse into the graph**: it is a draw-side mask, not a pose.
