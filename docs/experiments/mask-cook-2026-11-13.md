# The mask level's cook, and the tile set's point read without its lock (T128, 2026-11-13)

What the change bought, as pairs of binaries built from one tree (`msvc-release`: the base 61ccce67 and the branch), on the machine as it was. The design is on [tileset](../subsystems/tileset.md#the-mask-levels-cook) ("The mask level's cook", and "The reader's lock" above it); the finding this starts from is T125's: the forest's 763 s of cold "placement litter" is this cook, on one thread over every mask tile of the heightfield's frame.

**Machine state, for every number here**: shared. Other agents' builds, tests and a GPU-locked walk ran beside every run; the CPU was 22 % to 84 % busy with others' work (each table says what it saw), and the bench harness raised its WARNING on each bench run. Every figure is an upper bound; the ratios within one table, taken minutes apart, are what the decision rests on.

## The scene

T125's 3 km by 3 km engine scene, copied with its paths moved: a 3,073-point square heightfield at 1 m (`engine-content terrain-synth --kind mountain`, 37.8 MB of float32) as a `tiles` ground in 64-cell tiles; masks by priors (soil, scree, bedrock, snow) at 1 m in 32-cell tiles, **9,409 mask tiles, 70.8 MB of records**; one litter entry over 16 by 16 tiles of 32 m in 4 m cells. A second copy adds a scatter entry of stumps over the same 16 by 16 tiles (0.05 a square metre, a budget of 64 a tile), for the scatter's whole read on a tile set.

## The load's cold `mask level`

`engine-view --scene <scene-scatter.json> --offscreen --width 640 --height 360 --frames 1 --ddc <root> --startup-trace <trace.json>`, under `tools/gpu-lock.ps1 run`, each binary given its own root holding the scene's tile set and no mask level (a copy of a root `engine-content terrain-masks` had built the set into, with an output directory of its own, so the root never held a level). The stages from the startup trace:

| Stage | base | T128 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mask level` (the cook, before any placement) | 26.07 s | **0.66 s** | 39x |
| `placement scatter` (its whole read, on the pool) | 1.90 s | 1.67 s | the reader is not its cost |
| `placement litter` (its whole read, now on the pool) | 0.037 s | 0.024 s | |
| `scene file` (all of the read) | 28.14 s | 2.50 s | |
| `terrain masks` (the upload's second ask, a hit) | 0.068 s | 0.087 s | |

CPU busy with others' work when each run started: 22 % (base), 34 % (T128). The level each wrote is the same bytes (below).

## The cook alone: `engine-content terrain-masks`

`engine-content terrain-masks <scene.json> <out dir> --ddc <root> [--jobs <n>]`, the set already in the root, the level cooked into a fresh directory each run; the figure is the command's own `ms` for `ensure_mask_level`. **Every run wrote the same archive** (SHA-256 `32EDABC86FC409E9…`) and the same level hash (`910dfad20f1be587`).

| Binary | Threads | Cook | CPU busy with others |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | 1 | 29.7 s | 74 % to 83 % |
| T128 | 1 (`--jobs 1`) | 4.07 s | the same |
| T128 | the pool (`--jobs 0`, 35 workers and the caller) | 0.59 s | the same |

So of the base's 29.7 s, the window of heights a tile and the regions a tile take it to 4.1 s on one thread (7.3x; the lock's two takings a height, the five `point_mm`s a point and a pass over every region a point were the cost), and the pool to 0.59 s (50x in all, on a machine whose other work held most of its threads).

**Taken again at about 23:35 on a quieter machine** (others' work 5 % to 9 % of the CPU before each run), with the branch's last binary (batches sized by points, and a window's tiles found with no list), and a second copy of the scene whose masks are in **8-cell tiles, the forest's size** (148,225 tiles):

| Scene | base, 1 thread | T128, 1 thread | T128, the pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32-cell tiles (9,409) | 22.28 s | 3.06 s | 0.69 s |
| 8-cell tiles (148,225) | 26.28 s | 4.47 s | 0.77 s |

The same archive from every binary and thread count (SHA-256 `32EDABC86FC409E9…`, level `910dfad20f1be587`; 8-cell `07979DF5F35C20A5…`, level `6f33454c3979ff21`). Before the batches were sized by points, the 8-cell level took 1.70 s on the pool: a batch of 16 small tiles a lane was a pause every few hundred microseconds.

## The cook's bench: `engine_litter_bench`

`litter.mask_cook` (T125's): the cook over the bench's rolling ground at 1 m, priors only, 32-cell tiles, a 1 km by 1 km frame (argument 1) and a 3 km by 1 km one (argument 3), on one thread; `litter.mask_cook.workers` (new): the 3 km frame on a pool of so many performance workers and the calling thread. Medians of three.

| Case | base | T128 |
|---|---|---|
| `mask_cook/1` (one thread) | 2.723 s | 0.433 s |
| `mask_cook/3` (one thread) | 7.520 s | 1.217 s |
| `mask_cook.workers/1` (2 threads) | — | 0.622 s |
| `mask_cook.workers/3` (4) | — | 0.500 s |
| `mask_cook.workers/7` (8) | — | 0.308 s |
| `mask_cook.workers/15` (16) | — | 0.191 s |
| `mask_cook.workers/0` (the whole pool) | — | 0.216 s |

Others' work: 37 % to 44 % of the CPU (base), 77 % (T128's pool rows, after the batches were pipelined), so the pool's rows say more about the machine than the cook: with three quarters of the threads taken, sixteen lanes bought 6.4x over one.

## The litter's whole read on a tile set

`litter.expand.*`, the 1 km frame's 16,384 cells (T125's cases), the same pair; others at 38 % (base) and 47 % (T128). The table is on [litter](../subsystems/litter.md) ("Since T128 the pool is the default"): one thread 37.5 to 18.3 ms; the whole pool 199.9 to 6.6 ms, so the pool, slower than one thread at every size before, is now the faster at every size, and `litter.whole_read_jobs` defaults to the pool.

## What it leaves

- One thread of the litter's read still costs 3.6 times a ground with no reader (18.3 against 5.1 ms): each height is its own request (a hint, a pin raised and let go, a hit counted). A caller that asks many heights of one patch can ask `TileSet::surface_mm` once, as the cook does.
- The scatter's whole read on a tile set (1.90 to 1.67 s for 256 tiles) is its own work, not the reader's.
