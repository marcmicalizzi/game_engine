# Indirect light: the world-space cache, judged (2026-11-07)

Roadmap R69, stage 1. The resolve's whole indirect term was the sky's nine harmonics at the normal
(the stand-in's two halves without a sky) times the material's occlusion, with nothing from the
scene. Stage 1a puts a world-space hash-grid cache in front of it, fed by one ray per sampled pixel
against the frame's acceleration structures and read per pixel in its place
([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#indirect-light-the-world-space-cache) has the design). This
page is the judging: against the reference path tracer on two fixtures, and the cost and the look
on the owner's v25 walk.

**Sources.** The cache's shape is NVIDIA's SHaRC (the spatially hashed radiance cache of the RTXGI
SDK) and the hashed path-space filtering it rests on — N. Binder, S. Fricke, A. Keller, "Massively
Parallel Path Space Filtering", 2019 (arXiv:1902.05942): a cell found from a point's quantized
position and normal alone, its level by the distance from the eye, accumulated over frames. What is
estimated is the cosine-weighted integral of the environment's radiance over the hemisphere through
the scene's visibility, the term the reference tracer's single bounce computes (Kajiya's rendering
equation truncated after one scattering event). The ray budget comes from R127's measurement of the
primary trace on the forest (15.4 ms for 24.9 million rays at full width).

## The fixtures, against the reference

**Method.** `systems/renderer/tests/gi_cache_tests.cpp`, `gi cache: the cache moves the picture
towards the reference path tracer`: each fixture at 128×128 with the shadows traced and the lights
off, the stand-in sky (no sky model, so the environment is the resolve's two halves), the resolve's
picture with `renderer.gi.mode=off` after one frame and with `sky` after 64 frames at the same pose,
against the reference tracer at one bounce, 512 samples a pixel, the jitter off — the resolve
agreement case's settings (`reference_tests.cpp`). FLIP mean (lower is nearer), its 95th
percentile and PSNR over the 8-bit pictures. RTX 5090, `msvc-debug` at the stage's commit.

| fixture | mode | FLIP mean | FLIP p95 | PSNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a closed 4 m box with a 1.6 m opening and a unit sphere inside | `off` | 0.626 | 0.839 | 10.6 |
| | `sky` | **0.089** | 0.278 | 26.8 |
| a field of 28 cubes on a 24 m slab (the world translation test's) | `off` | 0.093 | 0.268 | 30.0 |
| | `sky` | **0.056** | 0.203 | 33.6 |

**The box** is the case the harmonics cannot draw at all: inside, every surface took the full sky
as if the walls were not there, and the reference sees the opening's solid angle; the cache brings
FLIP from 0.63 to 0.09 and PSNR up 16 dB. **The field** is mostly open: the harmonics are right on
the slab away from the cubes and on the cubes' tops, and wrong in the contact shadows and on the
faces turned to each other; the cache takes 40% off FLIP and 3.6 dB onto PSNR. What is left in both
is the cache's resolution (a cell is about ten pixels wide at this distance, so a contact shadow is
softened over it), its noise, and the specular lobe, which reads the same diffuse ambient as before.

**Convergence** (`... at one pose the change from frame to frame falls and does not grow back`, the
box): the colour steps between consecutive frames, summed over the picture, are 71,000 over frames
3–6, 21,700 over 18–21 and 11,600 over the last four of forty. The floor is the running mean's
history of 256 samples, a sixteenth of one sample's spread.

**The bytes it keeps.** The cache's first frame clears it and reads nothing, so every pixel takes
the harmonics: that frame is byte-identical to `off` (`gi cache: its first frame reads nothing and
draws the bytes the mode off draws`). The field and its camera moved by 6,548 and 156,250 world
cells draw the same bytes after twelve frames (`... a scene moved by whole cells draws the same
bytes with the cache on`): the keys are the eye's integer cell and the point's millimetres from it,
and every cell size divides the world cell. Sixteen frames make no device buffer and no engine
allocation, as with the cache off.

## Stage 1b: the bounce, against the reference at three bounces

**Method.** `gi cache: the bounce moves the picture towards the reference at three bounces`: the two
fixtures as above, the reference at `max_bounces = 3` (the light as it is, near enough: a fourth
bounce off 0.7 albedo grey walls adds under a tenth of the third's light), `off` after one frame,
`sky` and `bounce` after 64.

| fixture | mode | FLIP mean | FLIP p95 | PSNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the box with one opening | `off` | 0.573 | 0.777 | 11.9 |
| | `sky` | 0.200 | 0.379 | 22.1 |
| | `bounce` | **0.125** | 0.212 | 27.1 |
| the field of cubes | `off` | 0.065 | 0.230 | 33.0 |
| | `sky` | 0.109 | 0.331 | 26.9 |
| | `bounce` | **0.048** | 0.174 | 35.5 |

**`sky` alone is worse than the harmonics on open ground.** In the field the harmonics' lower half
is the sunlit ground the sky's model assumes, which is roughly right on a slab in the sun; `sky`
replaces it with what the rays meet — the slab and the cubes — and brings nothing back from them,
so every face turned sideways or down loses the ground's bounce. The box shows the opposite case,
where the harmonics' sky is the error. `bounce` beats both in both.

**The first bounce cut, and why it was wrong.** The first version of the bounce took the harmonics
where the hit's cells were empty. Only the picture's pixels sample, so the walls behind the eye had
no cells, and their hits read the open sky: the box came out at FLIP 0.443 against `sky`'s 0.200.
Reading nothing there (the hit's own light, the reference's answer at two bounces for that hit)
brought it to 0.125.

## The v25 walk at 3840×2160: what it costs, and what it looks like

**Method.** `msvc-release` at c6301ed8, the walk between markers 1 and 2 (the run's records
6693–6993, one key a record, 301 path frames, fov 55°, W82's `make-path.ps1`), the owner's flags
without the game module (`--terrain-tiles --terrain-far 6 --time-rate 0 --world-rate 1 --time-of-day
15 --no-lights --lod 1 --deform-pool-mib 64 --rt-budget-mib 0 --raster rt --shadows rt`, flora motion
at wind 0.7), offscreen, 120 warm-up frames, one repeat, `--wait-quiet 120`, one process a mode under
the GPU lock, the coordinator's copy of the v25 derived-data cache (no miss, nothing cooked).
**Machine state** (the harness's): the GPU idle at each start with 22.6 of its 32.6 GB held by
other processes, other processes using 22–49% of the CPU; the numbers are upper bounds. Medians of
the 301 frames' pass timers (p95 in brackets):

| ms | `off` | `sky` | `bounce` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gi_trace` (the sample pass, 1.66 M rays) | 0 | 1.62 (1.91) | 3.88 (4.39) |
| `gi_fold` (a million cells) | 0 | 0.014 | 0.009 |
| `resolve` | 5.05 (7.13) | 7.95 (9.62) | 7.80 (9.62) |
| the frame's GPU total | 17.8 (25.0) | 22.4 (29.2) | 24.7 (31.4) |

**The cost is over the brief's 1.5 ms, three times over for `bounce`.** The sample pass's ray is
about a millisecond a million (`sky`, first-hit) and 2.3 for `bounce` (nearest hit, a material fetch
and a shadow ray). The larger surprise is **the read: 2.8–2.9 ms of the resolve** — sixteen bucket
scans a pixel (eight cells at two levels where the first is empty, eight slots each), every one a
dependent scattered load. The levers, in the order they would pay: read one level only and keep the
coarser one for an empty neighbourhood; read the eight cells' keys as one 32-byte block a bucket
(they are strided by 32 now); sample at half the rate (the stride is a tunable); and the read at
half rate in the half-rate columns R126 already defines.

**What it looks like** (marker 2, `cap-off/00300-marker-2.png` against `cap-bounce/...`, the agent's
scratch): under `bounce` the crowns' undersides and the understory leaves go a deep shaded green
where `off` lit them as under open sky, the trunks are darker on the side away from the gaps, the
ferns sit in the shade instead of glowing, and the sun-shadowed floor near the sunlit patches is
lifted a little by their bounce while the floor deep under the canopy stays dark. The forest gains
the depth the owner asked for. Two faults: the rocks' faces show **cell-sized blocks** (a few
centimetres to a decimetre) where the cache's value steps between cells the trilinear weights do not
smooth — the face key splits a rounded rock's normals among its faces, and the read does not blend
across faces — and the distant crowns, whose cells are coarse, read uniformly dark. No swimming was
looked for frame to frame: the cells are world-space by construction and the translation test holds
the bytes, but a flight's frame-to-frame noise at the walk's speed was not measured.

**The default stays `off`.** `bounce` is the mode the judging favours (it alone beats the
harmonics on open ground at three bounces), and it costs 6.9 ms of the 3840×2160 frame on the walk.
`sky` alone is cheaper but further from the real light than the harmonics outside. The owner turns
it on with `--tunable renderer.gi.mode=bounce`; the read's cost and the rocks' blocks are what stand
between it and the default.

## Stage 2: the fill, the blocks, the brightness, the read

Roadmap R69 stage 2 (2026-11-08), after the owner's v35 walk under `bounce`: the indirect light
"loads in like a checkerboard as we move", takes five to ten seconds to fill the screen, and seems
a little darker than it should. [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#indirect-light-the-world-space-cache)
has the design; every lever is a knob whose stage-1 value gives the stage-1 read.

**Sources.** The young cell's blend with its parent is the multi-level fallback of SHaRC's
hash-grid lookups and of Binder et al.'s path-space filtering (the coarser level is the same
estimator over a larger footprint, so blending by the finer cell's sample count trades bias for
variance exactly where the finer one has too few samples). The openness fallback is the classic
ambient-occlusion falloff by hit distance (Zhukov, Iones and Kronin, "An ambient light
illumination model", 1998: obscurance as a smooth function of the distance to the first hit), here
bounded at one and ten metres.

**What the checkerboard is.** A camera path of the owner's v35 records 300–1,200 (one key every
ten records at their `pose_position` and `pose_yaw`, looking 5° down, 55°, 1,400 frames at 60:
`walk2-path.json` in the agent's scratch), `--raster hw --shadows rt`, 3840×2160, `bounce`, the
owner's other flags, offscreen; the same `msvc-release` build twice, once with every stage-2 knob
at its stage-1 value and once at the defaults, marker captures at path frames 1, 10, 30, 60, 120,
300 and through the turn at 900–1,200 (`e1-walk-stage1/`, `e2-walk-stage2/`). At frame 120 the
birch trunk on the left is **cell-sized rectangles of light and dark** under stage 1 and smooth
under stage 2; the floor and the oak in front differ much less. So the checkerboard is the
**young cells read whole**: a cell's first estimate is the mean of the few rays its face of its
cell got in its first frames — on a trunk, where the face key splits the round normals into four
cells per height and each cell sees a handful of stride pixels, that is one to four rays, and
neighbouring cells start bright or dark at random and converge only over the history. Blending a
young cell with its parent (four to eight times the samples) by `samples / 32`, and the faces
near their edges, is what removed it; the saturation of the hash (a full bucket keeping new keys
out for 240 frames) was the second candidate, and is now freed at 16 frames whether or not it
was the cause — the walk's counts (`renderer.gi.log`) did not reach the log at `--log
warn,renderer=info` in this build, so the share of young pixels on the walk is **not measured**
here; the held-pose fixture's is (below).

**Not measured this stage**, for lack of the shared GPU's time (two hours of the night went to
waiting for the lock): the walk's timings quiet against `off` (the runs in the agent's
`timing-todo.ps1`), the forest's luminance against the reference at 128×128 (`lum.ps1`, which was
running at hand-off), and the frame budgets with `bounce` on. The two unquiet walks above, with
the GPU 100% busy with other work: the sample pass 2.21 ms (stage-1 knobs) and 2.08 (stage 2, a
build before the wave-aggregated counts), the resolve 14.7 and 15.5 — upper bounds, and the
resolve's extra stage-2 millisecond is the faces' second lookups and the young blend's parent
level, which the timing runs were to split.
**The fixtures against the reference at three bounces** (`gi cache: stage 2's levers one at a
time, ...`, `msvc-debug`, RTX 5090; FLIP mean, PSNR, mean 8-bit luma against the reference's):

| fixture | mode | FLIP mean | FLIP p95 | PSNR | mean luma (reference) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the box with one opening | `off` | 0.573 | 0.777 | 11.9 | 111.8 (54.4) |
| | `sky`, stage 2 | 0.198 | 0.378 | 22.5 | 38.8 (54.4) |
| | `bounce`, stage 1 (the table above) | 0.125 | 0.212 | 27.1 | — |
| | `bounce`, stage 2 | **0.105** | 0.212 | **30.8** | 58.8 (54.4) |
| the field of cubes | `off` | 0.065 | 0.230 | 33.0 | 149.1 (149.6) |
| | `sky`, stage 2 | 0.109 | 0.331 | 26.9 | 143.2 (149.6) |
| | `bounce`, stage 1 | 0.048 | 0.174 | 35.5 | — |
| | `bounce`, stage 2 | **0.042** | 0.155 | **37.0** | 148.5 (149.6) |

**Where the energy goes.** On the field, `sky` is 6.3 steps of luma darker than the reference and
`bounce` 1.1: the hit's sunlit albedo restores the ground's bounce the `sky` ray brings nothing
back from, as the stage-1 judging said, and what is left is the bounces past the third and the
material's specular lobe reading the diffuse ambient. In the box, stage 2's `bounce` is 4.4 steps
*brighter* than the reference where stage 1 erred dark: the hit's coarser levels and the openness
fallback put light back on the walls behind the eye. Neither fixture shows the "too dark" the owner
saw as a whole-picture bias; the forest's own numbers are below.

**The rock** (`gi cache: a rounded rock draws no cell-sized blocks where its faces meet`, a sphere
1.5 m across on a slab, cells three to four pixels wide): the cache's own term — the picture with
it less the picture without — steps by more than 2 in 255 between 143 neighbouring pixel pairs
without the faces' blend and 138 with it. On this fixture the face split is a small part of the
term's steps; the rest is the cells' own trilinear structure at three pixels a cell.

**The fill at a held pose** (`gi cache: a held pose fills in within a second's frames`, the box
under `bounce`): no stride pixel's own cell is young from frame 9, and the change between frames
is under a twentieth of the first estimate's from frame 3.

**The ray budget** (`gi cache: the young cells' rays never pass the frame's budget`, 3,000 rays
over the box's 16,384 pixels): 2,497 rays a frame at most while the box fills and 1,242 once it
has, against 1,490 trickle windows.

## Stage 3: the fill on a turn, and the read's cost

**A negative result, carried from its branch.** Stage 3 lives on `b57-R69-3` (5decb5ab, four
commits on eeb8077c) and did not merge: none of its levers met the turn's bound, and the merge gate's
first GPU run of its tests (the agent's own had queued on the GPU lock) found its **defaults** 8
colour steps apart under a move by whole cells where eeb8077c and the cache off draw 0 — the
cascade 57 apart and the probes 8 — and its turn test asserting a fill the cascade does not reach
within 64 frames (stage 2's read reaches it from frame 55). The read's integers without a 64-bit
division and the shared corner hashes were the suspects for the drift; stage 4 did not take them
(below). The knobs this section names (`renderer.gi.cascade`, `probes`, `wave_read`, `hit_coarse`,
`history`, `count_waves`, `sample_stop`) exist on that branch only. What it taught stage 4: the
turn's fill is a slow tail, and its probes round the eye did nothing on an open hillside because the
frame's structures held nothing round the eye for them to find (stage 4, below, counts it). The
section as the branch wrote it:

Roadmap R69 stage 3 (2026-11-09, batch 57), after the owner's v36 walk under `bounce` at
3840×2160 (`--raster rt --shadows rt`): after a rapid turn, and when flying, the indirect light
fills in over a second or two as **cell-sized patchwork** — blocks of light and dark on trunks and
ground that converge frame by frame (three contact sheets at 8 frames a second). "It fills a lot
faster than before, but it's still incorrect looking." [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#indirect-light-the-world-space-cache)
has the design; every lever is a knob whose off value is stage 2's read.

**Sources.** The cascade is the hierarchical estimate of Binder et al.'s path-space filtering read
as a shrinkage estimator: a finer cell's mean is trusted in proportion to its own sample count and
the rest is taken from the coarser cell, which estimates the same integral over a larger footprint
with more samples (lower variance, more bias), down to the sky's own ambient where nothing is
known — so a young region shows the low-variance, biased estimate rather than the high-variance
one, which is what reads as light rather than as noise. The probes round the eye are a
camera-centred version of the irradiance probes of Majercik, Guertin, Nowrouzezahrai and McGuire,
"Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields" (JCGT 8(2), 2019), whose
probes are traced every frame in every direction whether or not the view sees them; here they feed
the world-space cells a turn reads rather than a probe grid of their own.

**Method.** One camera path from the owner's v36 start (the `cave-mouth` view: `-18.513, 152.037,
1469.316`, yaw 112.64°, pitch −1.15°, fov 70): frames 0–119 held, a **180° yaw over frames 120–150**,
held to 330, then **flying 20 m/s for 3 s** in the turned direction (climbing 6 m), held 120 frames
at the flight's end (631 frames at 60; `turn-fly-path.json` in the agent's scratch). `msvc-release`,
1920×1080, `--offscreen`, the common v36 flags with `--raster hw --shadows rt`, the v36 scene with
the shared cut tile set (no tile cook, no mesh cook), **flora motion off** (`renderer.flora.motion=false`:
with the wind on, the swaying crowns move every pixel of every held frame by more than the cache
does — the first runs' fill against the held frame never fell under the wind's own 1 to 2 steps of
luma), `--capture <dir>/shot.png --capture-every 8 --capture-channels ids`: a capture every eighth
frame of one continuous flight (not `--marker-captures`, which jumps to each marker and draws eight
frames there, and so never shows a turn's fill). The same path under `renderer.gi.mode=off` gives the
picture without the cache, frame for frame. Per capture, scripted (`metrics.ps1`):
- **fill** — the mean |luma − luma of the reference frame| (8-bit), the reference the last held
  frame of the segment (327 for the turn, 623 for the flight); **fill (term)** the same over the
  cache's own term, the picture less the `off` picture at the same frame, which cancels what moves
  in both;

  by more than 2 of 255;
- **error blocks** — the same count over the term's *error*, the term less the reference frame's
  term: at 1080p the forest's albedo textures step the term itself at most pixels, so the brief's
  count is the texture's (it is 584,000 at the converged turn and only 1.2 times that just after
  the turn), while the error's is the patchwork the owner saw.

**What it says.** The fill is a **slow tail**, not a few frames of young noise: thirty frames after
the turn the term is still 35% of its first distance from the converged frame under stage 2, and
the curve falls nearly linearly for 150 frames more (a wind-free hold; the `off` picture moves by
0.18 steps over the same frames). A cell's first samples on a turn are taken while the cells its
rays' hits read are themselves empty, so they carry the hits' bounded fallback (the harmonics times
the openness: bright), and a mean of up to 256 samples forgets them at a cell's samples a frame over
256 — the whole multi-bounce answer then iterates at that pace. Twice the rays (c) shortens the
tail by a tenth; (a) takes a seventh off the first frame's distance and lengthens the tail (its
coarse levels are biased, and the finest cells reach full confidence no sooner); (b) changes
nothing measurable on this view, an open hillside the probes' coarse cells already saw. **No lever
reached the brief's bound** (under a twentieth of the first post-turn distance within 30 frames;
stage 2 reaches it only at the reference frame itself, 176 frames on). The flight is the same story at 511–575. The two knobs
aimed at the tail — the bounce's read from coarser levels (`renderer.gi.hit_coarse`) and a shorter
history (`renderer.gi.history`) — were built and queued behind the GPU lock; their runs are in the
agent's scratch (`driver5.ps1`, `driver6.ps1`) and their rows are not in this table.

## Stage 4: the light round the eye

Roadmap R69 stage 4 (2026-11-09, batch 58), after the owner's v37 walk under `bounce` at
3840×2160 (`--raster rt --shadows rt`): "it's down to about 1 second to fill, but the problem is the
filling being visible at all instead of having the indirect lighting already applied when turning
... maybe it needs to be baked into the scene somehow?" The target changed from stage 3's: not a
faster fill but none on a turn. [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#stage-4-the-light-round-the-eye)
has the design: a caster volume round the eye (`renderer.gi.shell_m`) and samples over the sphere
from the eye (`renderer.gi.shell_rays`), each a knob whose 0 is the frame as it was.

**What other engines keep warm outside the view.** Every one keeps its bounced light in a store
that covers every direction round the player, traced against geometry that does not depend on the
frustum:

- **DDGI** — Majercik, Guertin, Nowrouzezahrai and McGuire, "Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination
  with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields", JCGT 8(2), 2019: a grid of probes in a volume that moves with
  the camera, **every probe traced every frame** in a randomly rotated set of directions whatever
  the camera looks at, against the scene's ray tracing structures, its irradiance and the distances
  it saw blended into octahedral maps with a hysteresis. What it keeps warm: the whole volume.
- **Lumen** — Wright, Narkowicz and Kelly, "Lumen: Real-time Global Illumination in Unreal Engine 5",
  SIGGRAPH 2022 Advances in Real-Time Rendering: a **surface cache** of cards over the meshes round
  the camera (their material and their lighting, updated a budgeted few a frame, nearest first) and
  a **world-space radiance cache** of probes in clipmaps round the camera, traced against a scene
  representation (distance fields, or the hardware structures with their own far field) that holds
  the region round the camera whatever the frustum; the screen's probes are the final gather and
  fall back to the world-space cache. What it keeps warm: every surface of the scene round the
  camera, and the probes round it.
- **SHaRC** — NVIDIA's spatially hashed radiance cache (the RTXGI SDK's integration guide), which
  this cache is modelled on, and Binder, Fricke and Keller, "Massively Parallel Path Space
  Filtering" (2019): the update pass traces paths from a sparse subset of the screen's pixels and
  **inserts at every vertex of the path**, so a path's second and third vertices fill cells off the
  screen and behind the eye; the hash key is the vertex's position and normal alone. What it keeps
  warm: whatever the update paths' later vertices reach — which this cache's bounce, reading the
  cell at its hit but inserting only at the pixel's, did not do.
- **The baked family**: lightmaps and probes precomputed offline, relit at run time — Gilabert and
  Stefanov, "Deferred Radiance Transfer Volumes: Global Illumination in Far Cry 3" (GDC 2012), and
  Stefanov, "Global Illumination in Tom Clancy's The Division" (GDC 2016): radiance transfer probes
  baked from the static scene and relit every frame by the current sun and sky, so a dynamic time of
  day costs a relight, not a bake. (The brief named "Global Illumination in Far Cry 4", SIGGRAPH
  2016 Advances; no such talk could be found, and these two are the Far Cry lineage's published
  ones.) What a bake keeps warm: everything, from the first frame — at the price of a precompute per
  static scene, and of only the transfer (not the light) being static.

**What a bake would add.** The forest is static per tile, so the cache's converged cells could be
kept per tile as derived data. What is relightable by the hour is the part that does not depend on
the sun: `sky` mode's ratio (the sky's visibility through the scene, coloured by what the gaps see)
is a ratio to the harmonics already, so a baked `sky` cell is right at every hour; `bounce`'s ratio
carries the sun's light off the ground and the trunks and is right for one sun only, so it would need
a few sun positions baked and blended, or the sky part baked and the bounce warmed at run time.
What it would cost: the cache's levels follow the distance from the eye, so a bake must hold every
level a tile can be seen at, or only the coarse ones; at 40 cm (level 4) a 64 m tile's ground is
about 25,600 cells a face, 36 bytes each with its key — about a megabyte a tile, 4.7 GB over v37's
4,753 far tiles, and 25 times that at 10 cm; at 256 samples a cell, about 6.5 million rays a tile, a
few milliseconds of the GPU, so a minute or two of build per sun position for the whole world
(estimates from the cache's own sizes, not measured). What it would buy beyond tonight's change is
**the start and fast travel**: light on the first frame after a load or a jump, where the cache
starts empty. It would not buy the turn, which is a matter of what the cache holds round a still
eye, and tonight's change is aimed there. Not built.

## Stage 5: the light is already there

Roadmap R69 stage 5 (2026-11-10, batch 59), after the owner's v40 walk under `bounce` at
3840×2160 (`--raster rt --shadows rt`), the third update running with the same sentence: "it's
down to about 1 second to fill, but **the problem is the filling being visible at all** instead of
having the indirect lighting already applied when turning ... maybe it needs to be baked into the
scene somehow?" [renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#stage-5-the-light-is-already-there) has the
design: the ladder and its walk, the samples round the eye feeding the levels above a surface's
own, a history in frames for the coarse cells, and the pre-roll at a load and a cut.

**What a bake stores, and what the run-time caches do instead** (the sources of
[stage 4](#stage-4-the-light-round-the-eye), read again for what each keeps and what it leaves to
the frame; where a source does not say, this says so):

- **Lightmaps and baked probes** store irradiance itself, per texel or per probe, for one
  lighting. Nothing fills because nothing is computed at run time; nothing can move either, and a
  time of day is a set of bakes to blend.
- **Precomputed radiance transfer** — the Far Cry lineage's radiance transfer volumes (Gilabert
  and Stefanov, GDC 2012; Stefanov, GDC 2016, as stage 4 cites them) — stores per probe the
  *transfer*: what the static scene does to light arriving from the sky and the sun, relit every
  frame by the hour's sun and sky. The bake needs the static scene only; the price is a build per
  scene and a store per probe.
- **DDGI** (Majercik et al., JCGT 2019) stores nothing offline: every probe of a volume round the
  camera is traced every frame whatever the view, and blended into its history with a hysteresis.
- **Lumen** (Wright, Narkowicz and Kelly, SIGGRAPH 2022) stores a surface cache of cards whose
  lighting is updated a budgeted few a frame and a world-space radiance cache of probes in
  clipmaps round the camera, both independent of the frustum.
- **SHaRC**, the hash cache this one is modelled on (NVIDIA's integration guide, read 2026-11-10):
  the update pass traces from "one random pixel per 5×5 block, ~4% of paths" and inserts at each
  hit of a path, the resolve keeps up to a maximum number of accumulated frames ("larger values
  can increase quality but also increase response times"), and the guide says nothing about what
  a query returns for a voxel with few samples, and describes no warm-up at a start or a camera
  cut. So the two things stage 5 adds are not SHaRC's published answers; they are this page's.

What stage 5's read is, statistically, is a shrinkage estimate with the levels' weights set by
their samples (stage 3's reading of Binder et al.'s path-space filtering), where a level's samples
are counted as a weighted mean's effective sample size — Kish's form, (Σw)² / Σw² for equal
variances (L. Kish, *Survey Sampling*, 1965), here with each cell's mean carrying a sample's
variance over its own count. Its numbers (255, 16 frames, an eighth, a sixteenth, four levels) are
derived on the renderer page from the cache's own arithmetic, not taken from a source.
**Method.** `msvc-release`, 1920×1080, `--offscreen`, the v40 subset of the owner's flags
(`--raster hw --shadows rt --rt-budget-mib 1024 --world-rate 1`, the bounce on), flora motion off
as in stages 3 and 4, the v40 scene with the shared tile set and mesh cache (no tile cook, no mesh
cook), a capture every eighth frame of one continuous flight (frames 8k+7; without `--benchmark`,
which flies a path three times and keeps one capture). **The metric is temporal and against a
long-held reference**: after each event the pose is held; the *reference* is the mean picture of
ten captures 144 to 224 frames after the event, the *floor* the mean distance from it of the ten
captures before those (two estimates of the same held view), and each capture from the event on
stands *apart* from the reference by the mean |luma − reference| over the picture, in 8-bit
steps, with *bias* its signed mean; the *change* is the distance between captures 8 frames apart,
against the floor's. Stage 4 compared against one late frame, which carried the cache's own noise
and, on main, was still converging. `76d96ced` is that commit's binary flying one path of 3,848

Read with its three qualifications:

- **The measure is contaminated, equally for both.** The error maps

- **What changed is what the maps show, and it is two things.** 76d96ced's error is speckle over
  every crown and mottling on the ground, red and blue mixed, zero in the mean: the variance of
  cells with few samples, as stage 4 found. Stage 5's ground and near trees are nearly clean —

  coarser, not noisier" met a forest: a crown's coarse cells, 3 to 6 m wide at that distance,
  average the canopy's lit outside with its inside, and the inside is darker — the light's detail
  in a forest is mostly occlusion, so sharpening is a darkening, the same sign everywhere, and a
  band that settles together is a thing an eye can catch where speckle of the same mean size
  was. Whether it reads better or worse than the speckle it replaced was not judged by a person.
- **The count the level is read whole at is the lever, and it was set on a box.** At 255 a cell
  ten pixels wide takes twenty-three frames to be read whole, which is the band's twenty frames;
  the box's white walls have little detail between a level and the next, so the box's turn
  stands inside twice its floor and said nothing about this. `renderer.gi.confident` is a
  tunable; the same flight at three counts, with 76d96ced beside them (the turn's bias at the first

**Why the crowns wait so long.** A cell is keyed by where it is and by which of six ways its
surface faces. On ground one face holds every sample of a cell; in a crown the leaves face every
way and stand at every depth, so a cell fifteen pixels wide on the screen is a dozen cells in the
cache, each with its share of the samples: the count that reads a level whole (255) is reached
several times later than on ground, and for the own level never. The levels above fill
proportionally later too, which is why the band on these trees is still +0.10 two seconds on
where the forest's stage 5 measured twenty frames.

**What stage 6 builds: the reveal.** The count's rule (a level is taken by its samples over 255)
is the right rule where the level above holds the same light — there the coarser mean is as good
and quieter — and the wrong one where the levels differ, which is exactly where the band is. So
a level its count does not read whole is compared with the next one up, and takes what their
difference shows beyond the two means' noise: with means m and m′ over n and n′ samples and a
sample's variance s² = 2 · max(m, m′)², the detail is d² = max((m − m′)² − s² (1/n + 1/n′), 0)
and the share d² / (d² + s²/n), the shrinkage weight of a noisy mean towards a prior of known
spread with the spread estimated by its positive part (W. James and C. Stein, "Estimation with
quadratic loss", Proc. Fourth Berkeley Symposium, 1961; B. Efron and C. Morris, "Stein's
estimation rule and its competitors", JASA 68, 1973, for the positive part and the
empirical-Bayes reading). The 2 is a model's figure, not a fit: a cosine ray from a half-hidden
leaf escapes to the sky at about 1.2 of the cell's scale or meets a leaf in the shade at 0.2
(three in ten escaping: a variance of 0.84 of the mean's square) or one in the sun at 1.7 (a
fifth of the hits: 1.6). Where the levels agree within that noise the share is zero and the
count's rule stands to the bit; where it is not zero, the noise the level adds is less than the
difference it shows; and it is continuous in both means and both counts, so a cell's first
sample is still no step. It reads no level the count's walk did not read.

levels of a crown is small (each octave flattens a little: the table above), and at the counts a
crown's cells hold in their first frames a small difference is inside the noise. Main still
stands nearer on these crowns.
