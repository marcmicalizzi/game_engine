# Indirect light: the world-space cache, judged (2026-11-07)

Roadmap R69, stage 1. The resolve's whole indirect term was the sky's nine harmonics at the normal
(the stand-in's two halves without a sky) times the material's occlusion, with nothing from the
scene. Stage 1a puts a world-space hash-grid cache in front of it, fed by one ray per sampled pixel
against the frame's acceleration structures and read per pixel in its place
([renderer](../subsystems/renderer.md#indirect-light-the-world-space-cache) has the design). This
page is the judging: against the reference path tracer on two fixtures, and the cost and the look
on the owner's v25 walk.

**Sources.** The cache's shape is NVIDIA's SHaRC (the spatially hashed radiance cache of the RTXGI
SDK) and the hashed path-space filtering it rests on — N. Binder, S. Fricke, A. Keller, "Massively
Parallel Path Space Filtering", 2019 (arXiv:1902.05942): a cell found from a point's quantized
position and normal alone, its level by the distance from the eye, accumulated over frames. What is
estimated is the cosine-weighted integral of the environment's radiance over the hemisphere through
the scene's visibility, the term the reference tracer's single bounce computes (Kajiya's rendering
equation truncated after one scattering event). The ray budget comes from R127's measurement of the
primary trace on the forest (15.4 ms for 24.9 million rays at full width).

## The fixtures, against the reference

**Method.** `systems/renderer/tests/gi_cache_tests.cpp`, `gi cache: the cache moves the picture
towards the reference path tracer`: each fixture at 128×128 with the shadows traced and the lights
off, the stand-in sky (no sky model, so the environment is the resolve's two halves), the resolve's
picture with `renderer.gi.mode=off` after one frame and with `sky` after 64 frames at the same pose,
against the reference tracer at one bounce, 512 samples a pixel, the jitter off — the resolve
agreement case's settings (`reference_tests.cpp`). FLIP mean (lower is nearer), its 95th
percentile and PSNR over the 8-bit pictures. RTX 5090, `msvc-debug` at the stage's commit.

| fixture | mode | FLIP mean | FLIP p95 | PSNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a closed 4 m box with a 1.6 m opening and a unit sphere inside | `off` | 0.626 | 0.839 | 10.6 |
| | `sky` | **0.089** | 0.278 | 26.8 |
| a field of 28 cubes on a 24 m slab (the world translation test's) | `off` | 0.093 | 0.268 | 30.0 |
| | `sky` | **0.056** | 0.203 | 33.6 |

**The box** is the case the harmonics cannot draw at all: inside, every surface took the full sky
as if the walls were not there, and the reference sees the opening's solid angle; the cache brings
FLIP from 0.63 to 0.09 and PSNR up 16 dB. **The field** is mostly open: the harmonics are right on
the slab away from the cubes and on the cubes' tops, and wrong in the contact shadows and on the
faces turned to each other; the cache takes 40% off FLIP and 3.6 dB onto PSNR. What is left in both
is the cache's resolution (a cell is about ten pixels wide at this distance, so a contact shadow is
softened over it), its noise, and the specular lobe, which reads the same diffuse ambient as before.

**Convergence** (`... at one pose the change from frame to frame falls and does not grow back`, the
box): the colour steps between consecutive frames, summed over the picture, are 71,000 over frames
3–6, 21,700 over 18–21 and 11,600 over the last four of forty. The floor is the running mean's
history of 256 samples, a sixteenth of one sample's spread.

**The bytes it keeps.** The cache's first frame clears it and reads nothing, so every pixel takes
the harmonics: that frame is byte-identical to `off` (`gi cache: its first frame reads nothing and
draws the bytes the mode off draws`). The field and its camera moved by 6,548 and 156,250 world
cells draw the same bytes after twelve frames (`... a scene moved by whole cells draws the same
bytes with the cache on`): the keys are the eye's integer cell and the point's millimetres from it,
and every cell size divides the world cell. Sixteen frames make no device buffer and no engine
allocation, as with the cache off.

## Stage 1b: the bounce, against the reference at three bounces

**Method.** `gi cache: the bounce moves the picture towards the reference at three bounces`: the two
fixtures as above, the reference at `max_bounces = 3` (the light as it is, near enough: a fourth
bounce off 0.7 albedo grey walls adds under a tenth of the third's light), `off` after one frame,
`sky` and `bounce` after 64.

| fixture | mode | FLIP mean | FLIP p95 | PSNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the box with one opening | `off` | 0.573 | 0.777 | 11.9 |
| | `sky` | 0.200 | 0.379 | 22.1 |
| | `bounce` | **0.125** | 0.212 | 27.1 |
| the field of cubes | `off` | 0.065 | 0.230 | 33.0 |
| | `sky` | 0.109 | 0.331 | 26.9 |
| | `bounce` | **0.048** | 0.174 | 35.5 |

**`sky` alone is worse than the harmonics on open ground.** In the field the harmonics' lower half
is the sunlit ground the sky's model assumes, which is roughly right on a slab in the sun; `sky`
replaces it with what the rays meet — the slab and the cubes — and brings nothing back from them,
so every face turned sideways or down loses the ground's bounce. The box shows the opposite case,
where the harmonics' sky is the error. `bounce` beats both in both.

**The first bounce cut, and why it was wrong.** The first version of the bounce took the harmonics
where the hit's cells were empty. Only the picture's pixels sample, so the walls behind the eye had
no cells, and their hits read the open sky: the box came out at FLIP 0.443 against `sky`'s 0.200.
Reading nothing there (the hit's own light, the reference's answer at two bounces for that hit)
brought it to 0.125.
