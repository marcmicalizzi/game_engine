# Grass swards and their shadows: what other pipelines do, and what ours costs (2026-10-31, G119)

**Question.** The owner, on the forest proof of concept's v23 clearing: without shadows the grass swards look weird, and a lot of AAA games do grass well. What do they do about a sward's shadow and its self-occlusion, what does ours cost today under each shadow mode, and what is the cheapest route to grass that reads right under traced shadows? Research and measurement only; no engine code changed.

**Answer, in short.**

1. **Under `--shadows rt` the sward is lit and shadowless by design** (`traced = false`, R110): no ray meets it, so no blade shadows a blade, and nothing darkens the sward's own base. That is the "weird" look: a uniformly lit mat. Under `--shadows csm` the same blades darken each other through the nearest cascade, and the clearing reads right, at the cost of the whole cascade pass (9.1 ms of a 11.7 ms frame at 1920×1080, almost all of it trees: the sward's own share is estimated, not measured, at under half a millisecond, see [what the sward costs](#what-the-sward-costs)).
2. **Every pipeline whose grass reads well that I could find evidence for does not cast per-blade shadows from a shadow map at distance.** They darken the blade's root (a height-in-blade gradient, our section 41 `ao` byte's job), keep the receiving terrain's shadow, and add a short screen-space or analytic contact term. The evidence is thinner than the brief assumed ([sources](#sources), which says what could not be read).
3. **Recommended first step:** make the resolve read the stored `ao` byte and use it on the sky's ambient and on the sun term of an untraced field pixel (no new pass, no new cost beyond one byte read per sward pixel, measured in the table below as the resolve's 0.5 to 2.1 ms that already runs). It gives the root darkening every source starts from. If the owner still finds the sward flat, a **screen-space contact shadow over the sward's pixels** is the second step; the rest-pose cascade blended into the ray path (R102's band) is the third and the most expensive. See [the proposal](#proposal).

### What the sward costs

- **In the cascades, with the policy (a):** the policy keeps the sward to one cascade at rest. Comparing (a) with (d), the same frame with the sward cast like a tree (every cascade, posed), the policy removes **311,464 shadow pairs** (3,510,311 to 3,198,847), **1.07 ms of the cascade pass** (10.17 to 9.11) and **200 MB of pool** (378 to 177). The pairs it removed are the sward's in cascades 1 to 3 (the cascade summary above: 156 k, 76 k and 80 k more entries without the policy). Cascade 0's 117,969 entries are identical either way, so they include whatever the sward draws there. At the cascade pass's measured rate for the sward's removed pairs (1.07 ms over 311 k pairs, 3.4 µs per thousand) an upper bound for the sward's own slice, **if every one of cascade 0's entries were a sward pair, is 0.4 ms**; the real share is smaller, since cascade 0 holds the trees near the camera as well. **This is an estimate from rates, not a measurement:** an isolated measurement needs a scene without the sward, which no flag produces (next paragraph).
- **A switch to take the sward out of the cascades:** none exists. `renderer.flora.caster_policy` (default true) is all or nothing across species, and its off position casts the sward *more*, as a tree (row d). `--no-shadow-casters` did not take the casters out under `--shadows csm`: row (e) is row (a) within noise (9.28 against 9.11 ms, the same 3,198,847 pairs), so it applies to the ray chain's caster entries only. `--shadow-distance` is the brief's named stand-in and moves every species' cascade at once. A per-species cascade count is the policy's `cascades` field in the flora catalogue, an engine change; **not made, as the brief forbids it**. Request: a `renderer.flora.sward_cascades` tunable (0 to 6) or a per-species override of the catalogue's policy, so the sward's cascade cost is measurable alone.
- **Under the rays (b, c):** the sward costs the chain nothing as a caster (no records, no structure; the 337,012 casters are the other species) and is drawn lit. Row (c)'s whole traced frame is 14.7 ms (the chain's blas and clas 4.8 ms, the trace passes 5.2 ms), 3 ms dearer than (a); row (b), a raster picture with traced shadows, is 10.4 ms against (a)'s 11.7, **1.3 ms cheaper than the cascades on this view**. The cascades cost 9.1 ms here because the clearing is ringed by forest with 2.8 million cascade pairs in the two far slices, not because of the grass.
- **The measurement the brief asked for, and what it shows:** the owner's complaint is about the look, and the look is the shadow-less sward of (b) and (c). Moving the sward back into the cascades (what (a) does) is **not** the expensive part; the maps cost 9 ms for the trees. A sward-only shadow under traced shadows would therefore cost the sward's slice (an estimated 0.4 ms at most) plus whatever it costs to put the rays' near shadows and the sward's cascade answer in one resolve, which is [the hybrid's](#the-hybrid-measured-g), measured next.

## Proposal

All three routes put the information the maps gave the sward into the resolve's shading of an untraced field pixel. None changes the ray path's structures or the caster policy.

1. **Read the sward's `ao` byte in the resolve (recommended first).** Section 41 holds `ao`, `thickness`, `hue` and `age` per vertex (`geometry::ClusterFileSwardVertex`, 4 bytes, parallel to the vertices); [grass-sward.md](grass-sward.md) records that "main's resolve does not yet read" it (R95 did not reach it). The resolve would read `ao` at the pixel's three vertices, interpolate it, and multiply the sky's ambient and the sun term of an **untraced field** pixel by it (the Tsushima reimplementation's and AMD's trick: root darkening by height in the blade), fading toward 1 with distance past a few tens of metres so a far sward does not read as dark spots. **Cost:** one dependent byte read and a multiply per sward pixel inside a resolve that costs 0.5 ms (csm) to 2.1 ms (traced) here; the stream is already uploaded for the section, nothing else is added to the frame. **Needs:** the resolve shader and its CPU reference (`visibility_resolve.slang` and `domain/gfx/tests/brdf_reference.h`, changed together per AGENTS.md) and the renderer's per-mesh binding of the section-41 stream: the `gfx`/`renderer` owners. **What it does not do:** the byte is a root-to-tip gradient, "not measured patch sky occlusion" ([grass-sward.md](grass-sward.md)); it darkens every blade's base equally and cannot darken the shadowed side of a clump or a hollow, so the mottle of (a) comes back only as a uniform darker base. Expect it to remove the flat look, not to reproduce (a).
2. **A screen-space contact shadow over the sward's pixels (second).** March from the pixel toward the sun through the frame's depth for a short distance (tens of centimetres to a few metres), multiply the visibility of an untraced field pixel by the result. Bend's sample is the published form; Unreal and Unity ship it. **Cost:** a march of N samples per sward pixel, which on the clearing is about half the frame's 2 million pixels; N is the budget, and **I have no measurement of ours**, only that the sources give no number (Bend's blog gives none, Epic's does not for this). The first prototype should be timed on this same path. **Needs:** a branch in `shadow_visibility` or a compute pass before the resolve, reading the depth the visibility buffer already gives; the grass is the sources' known weak case (a thickness assumption, noise), so the march's thickness is the thing to tune. This gives the clump-scale mottle route 1 cannot. It is also what the Ghost of Tsushima PC build is reported to offer for foliage.
3. **The sward's rest-pose cascade blended into the ray path's near shadows (third, the most expensive).** This is R102's band applied to one species: the cascades draw the sward (and only the sward) into one near slice, the resolve reads the map for sward pixels and the rays for the rest. [The hybrid, measured](#the-hybrid-measured-g) above is the cost of the maps beside the chain today, and a sward-only cascade would draw 0.1 to 0.4 ms of pairs (estimated above) plus a cull and a pool allocation of its own; what it needs is a per-caster cascade selection the policy would extend (the `cascades` field is already per species) and the renderer's cascade fit running with a near slice only. It is the route that reproduces (a)'s look in the traced picture exactly, and it needs the engine to run two shadow systems at once, which R102 found loses on forests.

**Recommendation:** build 1 first, because it adds no pass and a flat look is its target; look at it against (a) and (b) on this path; build 2 only if the clump-scale shadow is still missed, and time it before deciding. Do not build 3 until a per-species cascade switch exists (the request above) and the sward's slice has been measured alone.

## Not measured, and why

- **The sward's cascade cost in isolation.** No flag removes the sward from the cascades and a scene without it needs a 69 MB scene edit the brief did not ask for (only the cascade-0 upper bound above is an estimate).
- **A quiet run.** None of the rows was quiet ([machine state](#setup)).
- **The sources' primary texts.** The Ghost of Tsushima, Horizon, Jahrmann and Bend primaries were not readable here; their entries state what a secondary source reports.
- **Captures at 11520×2160** and under the owner's `--game` module (never passed, a POC binary).
