# The camp fire's flame, its smoke and the far clouds' static (R112, 2026-10-31)

The owner, moving round the forest proof of concept's camp fire and the engine's own [campfire](../../content/test-scenes/campfire/README.md), saw **the smoke and the flame turn blocky and weird**, and on the erg and the sky scenes **the far clouds turn static-y**. This page names the causes with captures and numbers, says what was changed, and grounds the flame's cadence in the combustion literature (the owner's standing rule, AGENTS.md "Rules that are reviewed"). The rules that follow from it are on [volumetrics](../subsystems/volumetrics.md#fire); roadmap R112, continuing R85 (the flame) and R83 (the clouds).

**How it was measured.** `msvc-debug`, the RTX 5090, offscreen at 1920 × 1080. The fire: the campfire's new [`orbit-path.json`](../../content/test-scenes/campfire/orbit-path.json) — a turn in place at 12 m, in to 5 m across R85's switch, a turn at 5 m, round to 2 m, a strafe out to 20 m — flown with `--world-rate 0`, so the flame and the smoke stand still and whatever changes between frames is the camera's, `--shadows rt`, a capture every 30 frames; and the same on two scratch copies, the dust off (the proof of concept's block: `{"dust": {"scale": 0}}`) and **a calm hour** (the terrain's `time` 72 hours earlier, 94,428,000 s, where the record's wind is 0 and the smoke stands straight up, the worst case for a column). The clouds: a scratch copy of the erg at a 1,025-sample grid with `{"clouds": {"coverage": 0.3, "type": 0.5, "seed": 7}}` (the block [What it costs](../subsystems/volumetrics.md#what-it-costs) uses), a walker's eye on the western floor turning slowly across the sky, `--shadows csm`, at 07:00 and at noon. Captures and crops in the agent's scratch, not committed.

## The fire: the causes

**1. The froxels sampled the flame once a cell, in slices 13% of the distance deep (the dominant cause).** Past R85's 8 m the flame was the froxels': one sample of `flame_density` at each cell's centre. A slice ends 1.133 times as far as it begins (64 slices from 0.5 m to 1.5 km), so at 12 m it is 1.6 m deep against a flame whose bounds are 1.0 m across (2 × 1.6 r, r = 0.32 m); the centre of the slice that holds the flame falls inside it or outside it depending on the distance, and a cell that catches it spreads its light over the whole slice. Walking away from the fire, **the flame vanished and came back whole** (the orbit's frame 209 at 9–10 m: no flame at all; frames 989 and 1049 at 12–15 m: none; frames 89 and 1019: a lit rectangle, 3× too bright for its size), and across a column the noise's 0.144 m cells against 8.6 cm columns at 12 m drew **the flame as a block**. Confirmed by changing the grid alone, the store unchanged: with 256 slices (the tunable's ceiling) the flame was present in every frame but still a rectangle; with 512 × 288 × 256 cells it was the flame's shape at every distance. Against that finest grid the default grid's frames differed by up to **145 codes** (252–255 on the calm copy), where frames with the flame marched differed by at most 14.

**2. The smoke over a calm fire stacked into blocks** for the same reason: a column 0.38 m wide (r0 = 0.6 r) in slices 0.65 m (5 m) to 1.6 m (12 m) deep, sampled once a cell — on the calm copy at 11 m, the column over the flame as three stacked blocks. In the campfire's own wind (11 m/s, half at the fire) the column lies over within a metre of rise and widens fast (0.22 m a metre of a path 4.7 times the rise), so it was a broad faint sheet there and the grid's error in it was at most 14 codes.

**3. R85's 8 m switch** — the flame changed from its marched form to the froxels' in a frame — was a step of the size of cause 1's error.

**Not the cause: the half store.** The grid alone, with the same four halves a cell, took both defects away. A half's 11 bits are 1/2048 of a value; the error was a slice's.

## The fire: what changed

- **The flame is marched per pixel while it is wider than about two froxel columns**: to `k_fire_march_widths` (60) of its bounds' width, 61 m for the campfire, where it is 1° across, two columns of the default grid at a 60° view. The per-pixel march costs the flame's pixels, which fall as the square of the distance, so a fire at 40 m costs a sixteenth of one at 10. Over the last quarter of that distance the march hands its share to the froxels smoothly (`fire_march_weight`, a smoothstep), the march drawing `share` of the density and the froxels the rest.
- **Past it the flame is a Gaussian blob** of the flame's light in the froxels, **integrated over the cell's depth in closed form** (two erfs: a Gaussian along a ray is a Gaussian in the distance) and widened across by half a cell, so it never vanishes into a gap between slice centres or swims between columns. Its shape is the marched flame's own moments with the noise left out: its emission (density squared, the base 1.3 times the tips) integrates to 0.129 of the cylinder π r² h's, its centroid is 0.26 h up, its spread 0.17 h up and about 0.35 r across. Its fill was then measured against the marched flame itself, on the calm copy with the smoke off, 30 m out and 8 m up, at a fixed EV100 of 9 so nothing clipped (the codes the flame added over `--no-medium` in a 200 × 250 window): the moments' 0.129 raised 15% for the noise's variance, 0.15, drew 58,660 codes against the marched flame's 32,036, 1.8 times — the noise carves more of the envelope than it adds — so `k_flame_blob_fill` is 0.08. (Codes are not linear in light; the two shapes are about the same size on the screen, so the ratio is close to the light's.)
- **The smoke a froxel holds is its mean over the cell's depth**, in closed form: the column's offset from its centre line is linear along a ray (its centre leans downwind linearly with height), so its Gaussian integrates to two erfs; the radius and the fade are the column's at the cell's centre, and the radius is widened by half the cell's width (the cell's share of a column narrower than it, its optical depth conserved). Tiled by 1.6 m slices at eight offsets, a ray's optical depth through the column now varies 0.5–12% with where the boundaries fall (the cell freezes the radius a climbing ray sees change) where the point sample varied from all of it to none (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`).
- **The march's offset is gradient noise**, not a white hash (the clouds' fix below; Jimenez 2014).

After the change, the orbit's frames show the flame's shape at every distance (frames 89, 209, 959, 1109 against the finest grid: the same shape, the flame's tongues where the grid's are), and the flame alone's light times the square of its distance moves by **0.8% at most between eye positions 0.25 m apart from 9 to 13 m** (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`, "a flame does not vanish or jump").

## The flame's top (R113, 2026-10-31)

The owner, walking the v24 forest after R112's fire landed: "the fire volumetric looks a lot better, but at the top the flame clips, so we get a horizontal line as the flame flares up a bit and it doesn't continue beyond it."

**What it looked like.** The campfire scene from a fixed pose 2.5 m east of the fire and 1.4 m up, looking at the height of R112's top (a scratch camera path; 1280 × 720, `--offscreen`, `msvc-debug`, the RTX 5090 under the GPU lock), at world time 5 s (`--world-rate 60`, frame 5), the time of the 18 s lap at which the scratch port below found a tongue most strongly across that height. Before: a tongue above the flame's body, narrowing and then **ending in a horizontal edge at about row 365 of 720, which is the picture's middle and the height of the old bounds' top** (1.3 h = 1.1 m over the base). After: the same tongue, a little thinner, rising about twenty rows further to a soft point with no edge. (Scratch PNGs, not in the tree: `before-t5.png` and `after-00005.png` under the agent's scratch `r113/cap/`.)

**The cause is not the rise.** R112's per-pixel march runs inside a cylinder to `k_flame_top` (1.3) of the flame's height, and the brief for this row supposed the noise's rise lifts the field past it. It does not: the rise translates the noise's lattice through the field, and the envelope stays where it is. What happens above the envelope is that the field has **no top of its own**. The envelope, `(0.75 + 0.25 smoothstep(0, 0.2, h')) (1.12 − h')^0.8`, is zero from h' = 1.12; the density is `saturate(1.6 (envelope − r + (n − 0.5)(0.35 + 0.9 saturate(h'))))`, and the noise's term has the amplitude 1.25 at every height from h' = 1 up, so wherever the noise is high and the radius to the swaying axis small (`(n − 0.5) 1.25 > r`) there is flame, at any height. A scratch CPU port of the shader's field (not in the tree; the tree's `flame_density` below is the same arithmetic) over 64 phases of the noise's lattice, three seeds, a grid of 2 cm in the bounds and slabs of 0.02 h:

| Height (of h) | 0.97 | 1.03 | 1.09 | 1.15 | 1.33 | 1.99 | 2.35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phases in which the slab holds any density over 0.02 (seeds 7, 12345, 99991) | 1.00, 0.92, 1.00 | 0.89, 0.80, 0.94 | 0.84, 0.58, 0.69 | 0.61, 0.47, 0.41 | 0.58, 0.50, 0.48 | 0.61, 0.45, 0.39 | 0.59, 0.42, 0.44 |

The highest point with any density was the scan's own limit (2.4 h) in all three. **So a taller cylinder moves the line and never removes it**: the reach of the field the brief asked the bounds to hold is unbounded. What the flare carries is little light and a lot of look: the emission above the envelope is 0.3% of the flame's (the field uncapped against capped at 1.3 h: 0.1390 against 0.1386 of the cylinder π r² h for seed 7), but it stands as isolated tongues on a dark sky, which is where an edge shows.

**The rule: the field is windowed to nothing at the end of the intermittent region, and the bounds end there.** The density is multiplied by `1 − smoothstep(1.12, top, h')`: exactly 1 up to the envelope's end, so **everything R112 grounded and tuned below 1.12 h is unchanged**, and exactly 0 at `top`, where the chord ends. A window rather than a taller cylinder, for the reason above; the cylinder's top *is* the window's end, so the march does not pay for height the field does not use. Where `top` is comes from the literature:

(The brief's "about twice": 0.2 / 0.08 is 2.5 as a ratio of the region's two ends, and against the mean height the end is 1.5 L for the campfire. The report's own tables are scanned images that this change could not read, so the limits are SP 1169's, which tabulates them from it (its extracted text reads "Continuous <0.08, Intermittent <0.08–0.2, Plume >0.2"). A search result also said the visible flame height corresponds to the top of the intermittent region; that was not found in a source this change could read, and Heskestad's height is taken as the mean, as R112 did.)

The window's shape between the two ends, a smoothstep over 0.385 h (0.33 m for the campfire), is a choice: McCaffrey gives where the regions end, not an intermittency profile inside them, and the field below 1.12 h already carries the intermittent flame's flicker as the noise's tongues. What the window stands for is intermittency falling to nothing over the top of the region: a tongue past the mean height is there for part of the time and then not, and where it is still there at the old top (1.3 h) the window leaves it 0.55 of its density, which falls to 0.1 by 1.43 h and 0.012 by 1.48 h.

**The far blob is unchanged, and why.** The blob (`flame_blob_cell`) is a Gaussian 0.26 h up with 0.17 h of spread, the marched flame's own moments with the noise left out, and the flare is 0.3% of the marched flame's light, so windowing it moves the flame's moments by nothing a picture shows. The scratch port, the field capped at 1.3 h (R112), windowed 1.12 to 1.5, and uncapped:

The blob's fill was fitted against pictures (R112's 0.08), not to these, and stays. **Nor is the blob cut at the top**, because a hard cut would be a line of its own: a froxel's Gaussian is widened by half the cell's width (about 0.37 m at the hand-over's 58 m), which puts light above the top by design, and a cell whose depth straddles the top would step. What it means for the blob to have the near flame's extent is that it has nothing the window removed: the top is 7.3 of its spreads from its centre (`(1.505 − 0.26) / 0.17`), a factor of 2e-12, and the test pins it. The hand-over itself is unchanged: the flame's light times d² from 9 m to 13 m holds within 5.4% and steps at most 1.5% between eyes 0.25 m apart, and across the march's end from 46 m to 62 m rises 1.98-fold with at most a 10% step, the same figures R112 measured (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`, "a flame does not vanish or jump").

**What it costs.** The march's 40 steps are unchanged, spread over the chord, so a ray's cost is the same; the bounds are 15% taller (1.555 against 1.35 h with the base's margin), which puts the flame's few more rows in the march. The campfire from the pose above at 1920 × 1080 (`--benchmark`, 300 frames × 3 repeats after 30 of warm-up, `msvc-debug`, the RTX 5090 under the GPU lock), the summary's `medium.gpu_ms`: **0.3939 → 0.3966 ms** (+0.7%, inside what two runs of the same binary differ by here), the frame's median 2.496 → 2.460 ms, the resolve's 0.1995 both. `machine_state`: before, the CPU 9–11% in other processes and the GPU 0% (`--wait-quiet 120` called it quiet); after, 21% and 0 → 30% at its end: the numbers are upper bounds.

**What the tests hold** (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`): `flame_top` is 0.2 Q̇^(2/5) / L for the campfire (1.505 h, the continuous flame ends at 0.51 m and the mean flame stands inside the intermittent region), is clamped, and is in the fire's record; the CPU port of the field, over a lap of the noise, finds density above R112's top (0.27), **none at or above the new one, nowhere more than the window lets through, and 0.009 in the last 0.03 h under it**; and a flame alone, drawn from the side with the eye at the old top's height so a row is a height, adds light in the rows between the old top and the new in 52 of 64 phases of a lap and none beyond the new top — **against none at all, in every phase, with R112's shader** (run against it for this page: the four checks on the band failed, the flame's own light below the old top passing). How fast the flare falls is the CPU port's test and not the picture's: at the test's exposure a row's light did not scale with the fire's intensity (0.4 against 0.12 gave the same rows to within 10%), most likely because a tongue's pixels clip.

**What was not done.** The flame's brightness and the envelope's exponents are still R85's; the window's profile is a smoothstep; Heskestad's L is taken as the scene's `height_m` for every fire, so a scene that sets a height by eye gets the top that height implies (the clamp bounds the cost); a fire seen at 60 m or more is the blob, which has no flare.

## The clouds: the cause and the fix

**The static is the march's per-texel sampling error, drawn as salt by a white hash and a bilinear upsample.** Along a grazing ray the slab's chord is tens of kilometres and the 48 samples are a kilometre apart against features of 512 m (the detail's Worley cells), so each texel's estimate depends strongly on its offset; a white hash of the texel gives neighbouring texels unrelated offsets, and the bilinear upsample at a quarter of the view's pixels draws each as a 4 × 4-pixel speck — a fixed screen pattern the clouds then drift through. At 07:00 on the erg, the low clouds near the horizon were speckled; **384 samples** took the speckle away (the reference), and **48 samples with no offset** traded it for smooth slices. Not the store: the reference uses the same halves.

**The fix**: the offset is **interleaved gradient noise** (Jimenez 2014, the neighbours of any 3 × 3 block covering [0, 1) nearly evenly), and the upsample is **a cubic B-spline over the 4 × 4 texels round the pixel**, which filters and upsamples in one; still a function of the frame alone. Against the 384-sample reference, the high-passed difference's RMS in the horizon's crop fell from 0.437 to 0.345 codes and the whole difference's from 1.24 to 0.83; in the high clouds from 0.212 to 0.145 and 0.456 to 0.196. The march's 48 samples and its quarter resolution are unchanged; adaptive steps were not needed for what the pictures showed.

**The forest proof of concept** names no clouds (its block is `{"dust": {"scale": 0}}`); to see the layer there its scene would add `"clouds": {"coverage": 0.3, "type": 0.5, "seed": 7}` beside the dust in its `medium` block (the erg's scratch block above). Without a dune field the layer drifts on the stand-in's steady 16 m/s aloft towards +x, and its fire's smoke leans on the stand-in's 8 m/s, half of it at the fire.
