# water (systems)

**Purpose.** Water surfaces in a scene — the oases of Desert Survival first ([plan 13 §13.1](../plan/13-reference-consumer-games.md#oases), "water with reflection and refraction"; roadmap S63, P8) and the "Fluids and water surfaces" row of the capability inventory ([plan 05 §5.15](../plan/05-simulation.md#515-capability-inventory)). A scene's `water` block (`engine.scene.WaterBody`) names bodies — a circle or a star-shaped polygon at a level — and each becomes **an opaque mesh with a water material**, drawn by the rasterizers like any instance and shaded by the material resolve's water branch; a walker wades in it and stops at the chest. At the renderer's `water_quality` high (the default since 2026-10-18) the water is **translucent by volume** — the ground under it seen through the surface, more and more obscured as it deepens ([below](#translucent-by-volume)); low is the opaque surface it was. It deliberately does not yet show anything under the surface but the ground, let anyone swim, float anything, or run a ripple simulation: the rest of stage 2, roadmap S86.

**Why this shape: opaque with a branch, not a forward pass.** The renderer is a visibility buffer: every surface is a triangle id in one 64-bit word per pixel, shaded once by the resolve, and the plan's forward and transparent pass (plan 04 §4.3 step 7) is not built. A water surface that is transparent needs that pass, a second depth, and an order — weeks of renderer for one material. But what the eye sees of a shallow pool is three things the resolve can evaluate at the pixel with what it already has: the **sky** reflected along the view (the sky's model is a function of a direction, `sky.slang`), the **sun's glitter** (the GGX lobe the resolve already evaluates, at a low roughness on a wave normal), and the **bottom's colour fading with depth** (which a vertex attribute can carry: the depth under every vertex is known when the mesh is built). So a body is a flat mesh in the visibility buffer, and a material class tells the resolve to shade it as water. Everything downstream is unchanged: the cull, the four rasterizers, the occlusion, the shadows (the surface receives the sun's shadow as any surface does; it also casts one, onto the ground under itself, which nobody sees), the LOD DAG and the ray path. What it costs is what opaque water cannot show — the rocks and the bottom's own texture under the surface, seen bent through it — which is refraction and needs the forward pass; S86 rows it.

**Why a capability, and how it reaches the renderer.** The renderer is not a capability and the water is (ADR-0027), so the renderer may not call it. The registration point is the scene-generator registry (ADR-0046): the capability registers the **placement generator `"water"`**, and the renderer's scene reader (`systems/renderer/src/scene_water.cpp`, one call in `read_scene_file`, after the placement entries and before the terrain, which stays the last mesh) hands it the block as `{"bodies": [...]}`. Its `expand` returns one mesh and one placement per body, in the block's order — mesh *k* and instance *k* are body *k*, the one contract the reader relies on. A build without the capability (`ENGINE_WITH_WATER=OFF`, `msvc-minimal`) refuses a scene with a `water` block with a sentence naming the switch; a scene without the block is read exactly as before, byte for byte. The **material** is the renderer's and gfx's, not the capability's: `gfx::k_material_water` is a class of the material table, `gfx::set_water_material` packs a body's numbers into it, and the resolve's branch is `domain/gfx/shaders/water.slang`.

**Owned data.** A body as read and checked (`water::Body`, `read_body`); its mesh (`build_mesh`) and the GLB file it is written to; its depth map (`build_depth_map`) and the `.depth` file beside the GLB ([below](#the-depth-map)); the depth at a point (`depth_at`). Nothing at run time: no state per tick, no GPU resource of its own (the renderer uploads the map).

## The block

`engine.scene.WaterBody` (schemas/scene.schema, `Scene.water`, scene version 8):

| Field | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | | what the mesh is called (`water/<name>`) |
| `level_m` | 0 | the surface's height, a world y (f64) |
| `shape` | | exactly one of `circle: {center, radius_m}` and `polygon: [worldpos...]` (x and z read); a polygon must be star-shaped from its centroid, in either winding |
| `shore_m` | 2 | how far in from the edge the surface fades from the bottom alone to reflecting |
| `shallow`, `deep` | pale green-blue, dark blue-green | linear RGB over a shallow bottom and where the bottom is gone |
| `depth_fade_m` | 3 | the depth at which the bottom has faded to `deep` |
| `wave_amplitude_m`, `wave_length_m` | 0.02, 2.5 | the largest wave; 0 amplitude is a mirror |
| `chop` | 0.5 | how far the shorter waves turn from the main direction, [0, 1] |
| `wind_follow` | true | the waves travel along the ground's wind (`GroundProvider::wind` at the scene's time) when it has one |
| `direction_deg` | 0 | otherwise, about +y from +x towards +z |
| `roughness` | 0.08 | the surface's GGX roughness, [0.02, 1] |
| `seed` | 1 | the waves' phases |
| `absorption` | 0.45, 0.065, 0.03 | the volume tier's: what the water absorbs per metre, linear RGB (clear fresh water: the red gone in two metres, the blue kept for thirty) |
| `scattering` | 0.03, 0.035, 0.04 | what it scatters per metre: the colour deep water tends to |
| `transmission` | 1 | the share of the bottom's light that reaches the surface beyond those, [0, 1] |
| `turbidity` | 0 | suspended sediment, ≥ 0: each unit adds scattering (0.9, 0.82, 0.68) and absorption (0.03, 0.06, 0.14) per metre |

The last four are `WaterBody` version 2 (2026-10-18) and only the high tier reads them; `shallow`, `deep` and `depth_fade_m` are the low tier's alone.

Checked when the scene is read; a body that cannot be drawn (no shape or both, a radius of 0, a polygon of two points or not star-shaped, a roughness or chop out of range, a negative absorption, scattering or turbidity, a transmission outside [0, 1]) refuses the file with a sentence naming the body.

## The mesh

**Rings to a fan.** The outline is sampled every `spacing` metres along its edge — a circle at angles from a polynomial sine of the module's own (`water::sin_cos`, + − × ÷ only, so the points are the same bits on every toolchain), a polygon along each edge — and copies of that edge scaled towards the centre stand `spacing` apart along the ray through each sample, closed by a fan at the centre. Every ring has the edge's vertex count, so the strips between rings are quads with no stitching, and a vertex's distance from the edge is `(1 - t) |b|`: exact for a circle, and for a star-shaped polygon the distance along the ray from the centre, which is what the shore fade is drawn by. A grid clipped to the outline was the alternative; it puts slivers or snapped vertices along the shore, which is exactly where the fade needs clean triangles. The rings' cost is density near the centre (every ring has the edge's count), which the LOD DAG simplifies away.

**Spacing and budget.** `spacing = clamp(shore_m / 3, 0.25, 1)` m: three vertices across the shore fade. **A 20 m pool** with the default shore: 0.667 m, 95 samples a ring, 15 rings, **1,426 vertices and 2,755 triangles**; the oasis pool (shore 1.5 m, 0.5 m): 126 a ring, 20 rings, 2,521 vertices, 4,914 triangles, 111 clusters. A body whose mesh would pass `k_max_vertices` (65,536) has its spacing grown by a quarter until it fits — a 4 km lake is 45,136 vertices at 23.7 m — which the fade then draws coarser; no limit is hidden.

**What a vertex carries.** Positions are metres from the body's centre at height 0; the instance stands at the centre and the level (`SceneInstance::origin`, ADR-0053), unrotated. The normal is +y and every triangle is wound to face it (the mesh is built with normal cones on: a pool faces up). **The UV is (the distance from the edge, the depth under the vertex)**, metres: the depth is the level less the ground's surface under the vertex (`scene_gen::Ground::surface`, the provider's heights at the whole millimetre), clamped at zero where the ground stands above the water — the shore. The UV, not a vertex colour, because the glTF import carries `TEXCOORD_0` and no colours (AGENTS.md, glTF import), and the half-float UV holds a depth of a few metres to a millimetre.

**Stored through the derived-data cache.** The mesh is written as a GLB (positions, normals, `TEXCOORD_0`, one material named `water` whose base colour is `shallow`) under `water-<hash>.glb`, and its [depth map](#the-depth-map) beside it as `water-<hash>.depth`, where the hash is over the GLB's bytes and then the map's (`body_file_stem`; before 2026-10-19 the GLB's alone), in `<ddc>/water`, where `<ddc>` is **the host's derived-data root** (`--ddc`, `render.load`'s `ddc`: `scene_gen::Context::ddc`, [scene_gen](scene_gen.md#the-derived-data-root), 2026-10-18) — so a game repository's scenes write where its host keeps derived data, not into a `ddc/` of their own — and with no root named, `<repo>/ddc/water` found from the scene's directory (as the scatter capability's rocks still are), or `water/` beside a scene outside any checkout (`water_scene_tests.cpp`, "the meshes go under the host's derived-data root"); a file already there under its name is the right one (the map is written first and the mesh last, so a mesh under its name means both are there), and the renderer's cluster cache builds and keys the `.clusters` from it like any mesh — by the GLB's bytes, so the rename cost no cluster build. The name is the bytes' hash, so it covers the body's parameters, the ground under it, `k_mesh_version` and `k_depth_map_version` alike. **The depth is the load's**: a body over migrating dunes keeps the depths of the moment the scene was read (the moving ground's `stand`, when a host has one) until it is read again.

**LOD.** The mesh is cluster geometry, so the DAG applies: far away a pool is a few clusters of large flat triangles. Their interpolated vertex depth is coarser, which is what a body with no depth map is shaded by; a body with one is shaded by the map at every distance, so the LOD cut changes the surface's triangles and not the bottom seen through them.

### The depth map

**Why.** Shading the bottom from the vertex depths shows the mesh. The depth interpolated across a triangle is the ground drawn as a piecewise-linear surface over the 1,426 samples of a 20 m pool's rings and fan, so the low tier's fade is Gouraud-shaded per triangle and the high tier's bottom (before 2026-10-19) was a plane per triangle, its slope lighting the sand and its run deciding where the refracted view landed. Looking down on the oasis at high, the owner saw concentric arcs across the pool and lighter triangles near the rim: the ring/fan topology in the picture. Dithering would not have helped: the output encode already dithers, and the facets were geometric. The surface is flat and its normal analytic, so nothing else of the water is faceted.

**What.** `build_depth_map` samples the ground at scene load, from the same `Ground::surface` the vertices sample, on a square grid over the body's bounding square (the edge ring's bounds, the square centred on them). Its spacing is **a quarter of the mesh's**: `size = clamp(ceil(side / (spacing / 4)) + 1, 64, 1024)` texels a side, then `texel_m = side / (size − 1)`, so the grid spans the square exactly. Each texel is the depth under its centre, level less ground, clamped at zero. Texels outside the outline are the bank there, so a refracted view that runs under the shore still finds ground. **The oasis pool**: spacing 0.5 m, so 161 texels a side at 0.125 m over its 20 m square; 51,874 bytes on disk and a 103,684-byte image (the device's allocation of it: 180,224). A 20 m pool at the default shore is 121 texels at 0.167 m. A puddle keeps the floor of 64; a 4 km lake at 23.7 m about 677 at 5.9 m. A body that would pass 1,024 is sampled coarser rather than refused.

**Format.** The file holds a 32-byte header — `EWDM`, the version (`k_depth_map_version`, 1), the size, 0, the body-local (x, z) of texel (0, 0)'s centre, `texel_m` and `depth_max`, little-endian — then the texels as **16-bit unsigned, `depth / depth_max · 65535` rounded**, rows along +z. `depth_max` is the body's own deepest texel, so the oasis's 2.27 m keeps 0.035 mm a step. The renderer's scene reader (`scene_water.cpp`) reads it from beside the GLB into `SceneWater::depth`, mirroring the layout because the renderer may not call the capability (`water_scene_tests.cpp` holds the two readers to each other). A missing file, or one it cannot read, is no map, with a warning.

**On the GPU.** The GPU scene uploads the map as an **R8G8B8A8 UNORM** image: red is the 16-bit depth's high byte and green its low byte, decoded as `(r · 65280 + g · 255) / 65535 · depth_max`. Why not R16 UNORM: the RHI's format list has no 16-bit single channel. Why not R32 float: linear filtering of it is optional in Vulkan. RGBA8 filters bilinearly on every device, and the decode is linear in red and green, so filtering the two bytes and recombining them is filtering the depth (to the filter's sub-texel weight precision, a step's fraction of the neighbours' difference). It is sampled through the scene's linear sampler at level 0, with the UV clamped half a texel inside the map, which is clamp-to-edge whatever the sampler's address mode. The material entry after the water's carries it (`gfx::set_water_depth_map`, `k_material_water_depth_map` on the water material): `albedo_texture` the image, `samplers_mr_normal` its sampler, `uv_offset` the map's UV at the body's centre, the UV a metre and `depth_max`, and `uv_transform.w` the half texel. That is the optics' entry at high and an entry of its own at low. The UV frame is body-local metres, computed in double on the CPU: no world position reaches the GPU. **A body with no map** packs nothing, and both tiers read the vertex depths and the triangle's plane as before.

**What stays.** The vertex depth stays in the mesh's UV.y and the GLB is the bytes it was. `depth_at`, which the walker wades by, is untouched, so every walk recording replays bit for bit. The map is the load's, like the mesh: a body over migrating dunes keeps the depths of the read.

## Shading

**The branch** (`domain/gfx/shaders/water.slang`, called as `water_pixel` from `visibility_resolve.slang` where the material carries `gfx::k_material_water`, in place of the BSDF; every other material takes the opaque path exactly as before, which the shading and attributes tests hold at 0 of 255). Per pixel:

- **The normal**: four directional sines over the **body-local point** — the reconstructed surface point less `instance_from_eye`, the instance's own place in the frame, so the waves belong to the body and never to the eye or a world coordinate (ADR-0053), and do not swim as the eye crosses cells. Wave *i* has the body's wavelength and amplitude times {1, 0.62, 0.38, 0.24} and {1, 0.55, 0.32, 0.18}, is turned from the main direction by `chop` × {0, 0.55, −0.8, 0.35} × 90°, starts at a phase hashed from the seed, and travels at the deep-water speed √(g k) **rounded to a whole number of cycles per 1024 s** (`k_water_period_s`), so the frame's clock may be reduced modulo the period in double (`gfx::water_clock`) with no jump at the wrap, and each term of the phase is reduced to a fraction of a cycle before it is added, so `cos` is asked a small angle. Only the slope is evaluated — the mesh stays flat. A wave shorter than twice the pixel's footprint (the larger of the reconstruction's position derivatives, as the ground's detail takes them) is faded out rather than aliased into sparkle, whole at four footprints; every wave dies over the last 0.25 m of depth, so the shore is calm.
- **Fresnel**: Schlick at water's F0 = ((1.33 − 1)/(1.33 + 1))² = 0.0201 on the wave normal and the view.
- **The reflection**: the sky's radiance along the view reflected off the wave normal, folded above the horizon (`sky_environment`: the scattered sky and the night's glow; never the sun's disc, which the glitter is), or the stand-in's clear colour (`ResolveParams::sky`) in a scene with no sky.
- **The glitter**: the BSDF's own GGX lobe (`brdf_d_ggx`, `brdf_v_smith`, Schlick at F0) on the wave normal at the body's roughness, times the sun's irradiance as the opaque path takes it — through the air with a sky, and through the same shadow query on the flat surface, so a rock's shadow lies on the pool.
- **The bottom**: `shallow` fading to `deep` by the depth over `depth_fade_m` — the depth under the pixel sampled from the body's [depth map](#the-depth-map) since 2026-10-19 (the mesh's interpolated UV.y for a body without one, and before), which the waves' calm at the shore reads too — lit as a level surface by the sun (E/π) and the ambient of a level surface (the sky's harmonics, or the stand-in's hemisphere), darkened by `exp(−3 · slant / depth_fade_m)` where `slant = depth · (1/cos θt − 1)` is the extra path of the refracted view (θt by Snell at 1.33) — so looking straight down shows the authored colours and a grazing view sinks into the deep.
- **The shore**: `k = clamp(edge distance / shore_m, 0, 1)`; colour = bottom · (1 − F k) + (sky · F + glitter) · k, so the very edge is the bottom alone — wet sand — and the reflection comes in over the shore band. Then the resolve's aerial perspective and exposure, as for every surface.

**The clock.** `ResolveParams::water_time_s` (the block's last pad word) is the frame's world time — `FrameDesc::world_time_s`, real time scaled by the world rate, since 2026-10-20 (roadmap R88; [renderer](renderer.md#one-world-clock-and-the-days-length)): the waves run at real speed whatever the day's length, pause at a world rate of 0, and no longer freeze under a held sun; until then it was the ground's surface time plus `FrameDesc::sun_time_s`, the one clock the sky read, so a 60× day ran them at 60× — reduced modulo the period in double. The sun's glitter on them is the sun's place at the sky's hour and the waves' at the world's: a sixty-minute day draws the same waves a real day does at the same world time, and only the sun moves faster (`water_scene_tests.cpp`: with the sky held and the world 7.5 s on, every pixel that changes is the pool's). It is filled where the resolve's block is written, `scene_renderer.cpp` (since 3b92cd06, 2026-10-18; before it the waves stood still at their phase at time zero); a replay draws the same water either way.

**The reference** (`domain/gfx/tests/water_reference.h`, the branch again in double; `water_shading_tests.cpp`): a level quad with the water mesh's UVs under a known sun and the stand-in sky, five blocks — waves at two clocks and two roughnesses, a mirror, the shore — every fourth pixel of the middle half against the reference: **worst 1 of 255** on the RTX 5090's mesh path (2026-10-17), within the BSDF's 2. The clock moves the picture. The phase hash and the cycle rounding are integers and are mirrored exactly.

**What it costs** (`engine-view --benchmark` over a five-second path looking down on the pool, the water about 70% of a 1920 × 1080 frame, `msvc-debug`, RTX 5090, 2026-10-17, `machine_state` start: GPU 0%, others' CPU 6–10%; end: GPU 90–94% — this run's own): the resolve's median **0.178 ms with the water drawn as a plain dielectric (stage 1) and 0.189 ms with the branch**, +0.010 ms, about 7 ps a water pixel and 6% of the resolve. The footprint's derivatives and the four waves are most of it.

**The branch is in the water modules only.** `visibility_resolve.slang` compiles it under `RESOLVE_WATER`, which `visibility_resolve_water.slang` and `visibility_resolve_rt_water.slang` define before they include it; the plain and ray-traced modules every other scene draws with have no branch at all, and are the SPIR-V they were before the water. The renderer draws with a water module only when its scene has a water material (`GpuScene::has_water`; [renderer](renderer.md#materials)). Why: the frame budgets (ADR-0018). A fragment shader's registers are allocated for its largest path whether a pixel takes it or not, so the branch in the one resolve cost every pixel of every frame — `gpu_ms.resolve` on `erg-walk-surround3-rt`, a ray-traced surround frame with no water in it, went from 2.316 to 2.793 ms median and failed its budget (2026-10-18, on a quiet machine), while a 1080p frame *with* water had measured +0.010 ms. A change to the branch is measured on a frame without water as well as on one with it.

The material entry (`gfx::set_water_material`): `albedo` = shallow, roughness; `emissive` = 0; `uv_transform` = deep, `depth_fade_m`; `uv_offset` = amplitude, wavelength, chop, `shore_m`; `normal_scale`, `occlusion_strength` = the waves' direction (x, z); `sampler` = the seed; `flags` = `k_material_water`. Those are the slots an untextured material with no UV transform never reads, so every reader that is not the branch — the reference path tracer, the albedo view — sees an untextured dielectric of the shallow colour, the UVs passing through unscaled.

**What the low tier looks like** (the oasis pool's markers, `engine-view --marker-captures`, 2026-10-17). From the east shore with the sun behind (`east`), the pool reads as water: blue-green and dark over its 2.3 m middle, pale where the sand comes up through the shallows, a thin bright rim of wet sand at the edge, the waves a fine regular chop that catches the sky's paler blue at grazing; the near rocks stand at the shore. Looking down from nine metres (the cost path below) the sun's glitter is a scatter of bright crescents on the wave crests where the reflected view meets the sun, the bottom darkening from the shore to the middle. Low across the middle (`across`) the waves fill the foreground, lighter where they face the sky, under the far shore's rocks. What does not look right yet: four sines make a visibly regular pattern overhead, the waves stood still until the clock was wired (above; they move since), the rocks do not reflect (the sky's model has no geometry in it; a reflected rock needs the forward pass or a ray, S86), and nothing is seen through the surface — which the high tier, below, is.

## Translucent by volume

**The tier** (roadmap S86, its first part; 2026-10-18). `RenderSettings::water_quality` — `--water-quality low|high` in engine-view, `settings.water_quality` over the protocol (`RenderSettings` version 13) — is **high by default on every device**. Low is the surface above: when the tier was added its capture of the oasis pool was byte-identical to the low tier before it at all three markers and the cost path (RTX 5090, the mesh path, traced shadows; checked by drawing the low tier once with that build's water module and once with `main`'s, loaded through the shader manifest). **Re-pinned 2026-10-19**: the low tier's fade now reads the [depth map](#the-depth-map), so its pictures are no longer those bytes; the low picture of record is the one the depth map draws. Why high everywhere rather than only where the device traces: it costs only a scene with water — the branch is in the resolve's water modules, which only such a scene draws with — and there 0.06 ms at 1080p with the pool filling the frame (below); the second shadow query it asks is the frame's own query, a ray where the device has them and the cascaded maps where it has not, so nothing in it needs ray tracing. A device for which even that is too much is the reason `low` exists.

**How it reaches the resolve: the material table, not a `ResolveParams` word.** The brief for this work asked for a field of the resolve's block beside `water_time_s`. The block has no pad word left (416 bytes since the HDR row), and the tier is a property of what the table holds anyway: at high, `GpuScene::upload_materials` writes each body's **optics** into the table entry right after its water material and sets `gfx::k_material_water_volume` on the material (`gfx::set_water_optics`); at low that entry holds only the body's [depth map](#the-depth-map) (since 2026-10-19; before it the low table had no such entry and was the table before the tier, entry for entry), and a body with no map has no entry at low at all. The setting lives in `RenderSettings`, where a change rebuilds the GPU scene, which is when the table is written. The optics entry: `albedo` the extinction (absorption + scattering, the turbidity's included) and the transmission, `emissive` the scattering, `uv_transform` the ground's UV at the body's centre and the UV a metre, `sampler` the ground's material index (`k_water_no_ground` without a terrain). No cluster names it (the water mesh has the one material), so no reader takes it for a surface.

**Which way the bottom is seen: analytically** — option (a) of the two the work weighed. The visibility buffer holds the water's triangle and nothing beneath it. The pixel's view is refracted at the wave normal by Snell at 1.33 and **marched down to the ground as a height field** (`water_path_map`, since 2026-10-19). It takes six fixed steps along the ray, out to three times the flat answer (the depth here over the ray's descent), against the bilinear [depth map](#the-depth-map). The first step that passes under the sampled ground brackets the hit, and one secant between that step's ends places it, so a level bottom lands exactly, a bank rising under the ray is met sooner and a bottom falling away later. A ray still above the ground at the end of its reach stops there, which is the cap the plane had. The bottom's normal is the map's slope at the hit (one texel's forward differences). That costs at most nine texture reads: the depth here, six steps and the two differences. **Before it, and still for a body with no map**, the view ran to the **triangle's plane**: the depth attribute at its three corners, a plane over body-local (x, z), with the run capped at four times the flat answer. That made the bottom a plane per triangle, its landing and its sunlit slope stepping at every edge of the mesh — the rings and trapezoids [the depth map](#the-depth-map) was made to remove. The ground's colour there is **the terrain's own material**: its baked base-colour map, read at the hit point's place in the map's UV frame — the body's centre's UV, which the GPU scene computes in f64 from the water instance's cell and local and the grid's millimetre frame (`terrain_uv_frame`), plus body-local metres times the UV a metre — so the sand under the water is the sand on the shore, and no position the GPU sees is a world coordinate. The bottom is lit as a Lambertian surface by the sun refracted at the level surface and by the sky's light, both dimmed on the way down; **its shadow is the sun's shadow query asked where the sun's refracted ray to the bottom leaves the surface** — at the bottom itself the query would find the water's own surface over it, which casts as every surface does — and in the shallows (under `k_water_calm_depth`, 25 cm) it blends to the surface's own, because there that exit point lies over the bank, inside the sand, and drew a dashed dark line along the mesh's edge until it did. **Option (b)**, a second visibility pass of the scene without the water over the pool's tiles, resolved and composited through the surface, was not built: (a) gives the owner's picture on the oasis, whose floor is the terrain and under whose water nothing else stands (the rocks keep 10.6 m from a 10 m pool). Its cost was not measured; its floor is this frame's own cull, raster and resolve over the pool's pixels — 0.014 + 0.027 + 0.19 ms on the cost path below, before a second Hi-Z and the composite — so it would cost several times what (a) does for what (a) cannot show, which today is nothing.

**The optical model** (`water.slang`, "the volume tier"; `water_reference.h` mirrors it in double). With σₜ the extinction and σₛ the scattering per metre (RGB), τ the transmission, the refracted view's run `s` to the bottom and the bottom's depth `d` there, the sun's angle under the water θₗ (cos θₗ by Snell from the sun's elevation), `μ = d / s`, `E` the sun's irradiance at the surface (Schlick's share of it refracted in: `E (1 − F(sun))`) and `A` the sky's light a level surface takes (the opaque path's ambient, `(1 − F₀)` of it entering):

- **the bottom**: albedo × (E · vis_bottom · max(n_b · l, 0) / π · e^(−σₜ d / cos θₗ) + A · e^(−1.25 σₜ d)) × e^(−σₜ s) × τ — Beer–Lambert down both ways the light comes and up the view; 1.25 is the sky light's mean path for a mean cosine of 0.8;
- **the water's own light**: isotropic single scattering of both, integrated along the view: σₛ × (E · vis_surface / 4π · g(σₜ(1 + μ / cos θₗ), s) + A / 4 · g(σₜ(1 + 1.25 μ), s)), with g(a, s) = (1 − e^(−a s)) / a. As `s` grows the bottom dies away and this tends to a colour the light makes — the deep is no longer painted;
- **the surface**: Fresnel (Schlick at 0.0201) between that and the sky along the reflected view, the sun's glitter and the shore fade exactly as in the low tier.

Turbidity adds `(0.9, 0.82, 0.68)` of scattering and `(0.03, 0.06, 0.14)` of absorption a metre per unit (`gfx::k_water_silt_*`): sediment that scatters the sand's light and takes the blue first. Not modelled, said so: the n² law (light leaving the water loses 1/1.77 of its radiance) and the total internal reflection of upwelling light that gives part of it back pull opposite ways and are both left out, so the bottom at the waterline is as bright as the dry sand beside it; caustics; the waves' effect on the light coming down (the sun is refracted at the level surface); the bottom seen bent by the waves (the bottom is smooth, so the refraction moves it but shows no ripple of its own); anything under the water that is not the ground (that is (b)).

**The reference holds it**: `water_shading_tests.cpp` draws three volume blocks beside the five low ones — the sharp waves over a ground of sand at 500 s, the same over a bottom that slopes from 0.5 to 1.5 m (the plane through the triangle's depths), and broad waves at the shore over no ground, silty and passing 60% — **worst 1 of 255** against the double-precision mirror on every block, low and high (RTX 5090, mesh path, 2026-10-18), and the volume block differs from its low twin in more than 100 of the compared pixels. **With the depth map** (2026-10-19) three more blocks carry a synthetic map, 64 texels a side over the quad's 10 m (`water_reference.h` mirrors the bilinear sample in double from the same 16-bit grid, and the march step for step): the sharp waves over the sand through a map of the same slope, 1 + 0.1 x; the same through a bowl, 2.3 m deep in the middle and 0.3 m at the corners; and the broad waves of the low tier fading by the bowl. All three are worst 1 of 255 against the reference (RTX 5090, mesh path), and the sloped map's march draws within 1 of 255 of the plane through the same slope, since a straight bottom is what the secant lands on exactly.

**What it costs** (`engine-view --benchmark`, `msvc-release`, 1920 × 1080, five seconds looking down on the pool from nine metres with the water about nine tenths of the frame, RTX 5090, traced shadows, two runs of each tier interleaved under the GPU lock, 2026-10-18; `machine_state`: others' CPU 6–14%, not quiet by the harness's rule, so upper bounds): `gpu_ms.resolve` median **0.189 ms low and 0.249 ms high**, both runs within 0.0001 ms of each other — **+0.060 ms**, about 32 ps a water pixel; cull and raster unchanged (0.014 and 0.027 ms). The two surround budget scenes draw with the plain modules, which are the bytes they were (`AD0A0386…` plain, `5C77B086…` rays, the same as `main`'s), and `tools/frame-budget.ps1 -WaitQuiet 120` on them (2026-10-18, a busy machine: others' CPU 23–32% and then 61–100%, GPU 4–21%) measured `erg-walk-surround3-rt` **pass**, GPU total 3.399 / 4.891 ms median / p99 against its 3.373 / 4.127 (resolve 2.327 ms median), and `endless-surround3-rt` GPU total 4.431 / 5.805 ms against 4.436 / 5.418 (resolve 1.966 ms), over only in `cpu_ms` with every core of the machine busy beside it — the inconclusive verdict the tool gives a busy run. **With the depth map** (2026-10-19): the same setup (`msvc-release`, 1920 × 1080, five seconds looking down on the pool from 8.55 m with the water nearly the whole frame, RTX 5090, traced shadows). Each run was set against `main`'s water modules, loaded through a shader manifest (`--shaders`), the two interleaved over two rounds under the GPU lock. The `machine_state` was not quiet: others' CPU was 30–44% at the start of six runs and 100% at two, with a Linux container building beside them, and the GPU at 98% at the end (the run's own). `gpu_ms.resolve` median: **high 0.244 / 0.245 ms before and 0.263 / 0.263 after, +0.018 ms**, the march's nine reads at most, about 9 ps a water pixel; **low 0.185 / 0.184 before and 0.194 / 0.193 after, +0.009 ms**, one read. Cull 0.014 ms either way. The oasis's map is 51,874 bytes on disk and a 103,684-byte image (180,224 as the device allocates it). The plain modules are still `AD0A0386…` and `5C77B086…`, so a frame with no water, and the two surround budget scenes, draw with the bytes they did.

**What it looks like** (the oasis pool, `engine-view --marker-captures` at 1920 × 1080, high, 2026-10-18). From the south-west towards the morning sun (`sun`) the pool is a lens of colour in the hollow: the sand runs on under the water at the shore, pale and only a little cooler, turns sea-green over the first metre of depth, and the 2.3 m middle is a deep blue-green that is the water's own light; the sky's paler blue and the waves' fine chop lie over all of it at grazing, brighter towards the far side. From the east shore (`east`) the same at a lower angle: a darker teal in the middle where the low tier paints a paler blue, and the near shallows sand-coloured where the low tier draws a pale turquoise rim. Low across the middle (`across`) the water in the foreground is a deep green-teal with the chop's highlights, the far shallows a band of sand seen through the water under the far shore's rocks. Looking straight down from nine metres the sand shows through a fringe a couple of metres wide and the middle is a flat deep teal with the sun's glitter on the crests. What does not look right yet: the bottom is the terrain's smooth map, so it has no texture to bend — the waves show only in the reflection and the glitter; the rocks still do not reflect; and with no caustics a sunlit shallow bottom is flatter than real water makes it.

## Wading

**The depth at the feet.** `water::depth_at(bodies, ground, at)`: the deepest body whose outline holds the point's x and z, of its level less the ground's surface there, never below zero; 0 outside every body. engine-view's walker asks it at its feet before every step (`view::Walker::set_water`, attached in `main.cpp` from the bodies on the scene's mesh infos and the scene's ground at its own time, `ENGINE_VIEW_WATER`), and scales the step by **the declared curve** (`view::wade_scale`, `walk.h`): full speed to the ankle (0.3 m), falling linearly to half at the waist (1 m) and to a quarter at the chest (1.4 m). **At the chest the water is a wall**: a step whose heading leads deeper — the depth a quarter metre ahead along it greater than at the feet — is not taken (a scale of 0, counted as a stopped tick), so the walker stops chest deep and wades back out the way it came. No swimming: S86.

**The water's mesh is not a collision surface**: `scene_collision` gives no body to a mesh whose `renderer::SceneMeshInfo::water` is set (it keys meshes by their index in the scene, and this is the mark it reads beside `terrain_mesh`), so the physical walker stands on the ground under the surface, and the ground-follower on the provider's height as always.

**Determinism.** The depth is a function of the position and the scene, so a walk through water replays bit for bit (the end-to-end case replays one to the same trajectory, walker hash and wading block). A walker with no water, or at a depth of 0, scales by exactly 1 — `x * 1.0f` is `x`, contraction is off (ADR-0035) — so every walk recorded before water existed replays as it did, and a scene with no water attaches nothing. The summary's `walk` block gains `wading` — `max_depth_m`, `ticks` in water, `stopped_ticks` — only where there is water.

**What the tests hold.** `walk_tests.cpp` (no device): the curve's points; a walker over a shore that deepens half a metre a metre walks east for twenty seconds and stops at x = 4.80 (the chest's 1.4 m is at 4.8), 1.400 m deep, stopped 3,717 ticks of 4,800, and wades back west; the same walk twice is the same; no depth function and one that answers 0 walk the same floats and hash. `walk_water_view_tests.cpp` (a window on the tests' private desktop and a device): on the committed oasis pool, a walker started on the east shore facing the middle holds W for twelve seconds and stops chest deep — 1.417 m at the most, 1,184 ticks stopped, the eye at x = 5.26, short of the middle — with physics on (2026-10-17, RTX 5090); its recording replays offscreen to the same trajectory, walker hash and wading block.

## Determinism

The mesh is a function of the block and the ground in f32/f64 + − × ÷ and sqrt (the GLB bytes are pinned by `water_tests.cpp`'s hash, taken on MSVC). The waves are a function of the body-local point and the frame's clock reduced in double: a replay draws the same water, and a picture is the same bytes twice (the GPU case). `depth_at` is a function of the position and the scene.

**Public API.** `include/systems/water/water.h`.

**Depends on.** `base`, `containers`, `math`, `hash`, `json`, `schema`, `schemas`, `io`, `log`, `geometry`, `scene_gen`. No renderer.

**Testing.** `tools/dev.ps1 test -Filter water`. `tests/water_tests.cpp`, no device: the sine against the C library's to 1e-14; the block read and every refusal; inside, edge and outside for a circle and a polygon; `depth_at` inside, outside, at the shore and over two bodies; the mesh over a synthetic bowl — vertex and triangle counts, every normal up and every triangle facing +y, every vertex's depth the level less the ground to the bit, its edge distance, the edge ring dry; a 20 m pool's budget and a lake's grown spacing; a polygon's edge on its outline; the GLB as the importer reads it, the same bytes twice and a pinned hash; the generator through the registry; the material's packing and the volume tier's optics (`set_water_optics`: the flag, the entry after the material, the turbidity in both rates) and their refusals; **the depth map** over the bowl (145 texels a side at a sixth of a metre for a 24 m pool, every texel the level less the ground to half a quantization step, every vertex under water within half a millimetre of the map's bilinear depth there, the bytes read back as written, the same twice and a pinned hash, the puddle's floor of 64, the generator's `.depth` beside every `.glb`). `tests/water_scene_tests.cpp`: a scene's block read by the renderer (one mesh and one instance a body before the terrain, the body and its shading on the mesh's info, a fixed direction, the load carrying it), a scene without the block and with an empty one the scene it was, a polygon body, a refusal's sentence; the oasis pool's ground (the level under every crest 25 m out and under the whole edge, two metres deep at the centre); and on a GPU the oasis pool captured with and without the block — **every differing pixel is a pixel of the pool or within three pixels of one** (its instance in the ids channel; the band is for the cascaded maps' filter, which on a device without ray queries lets the pool's own shadow touch the shore's last texels past the edge — gate b23 saw 33 such pixels of 153,600 on the Titan Xp — where traced shadows stop at the edge itself), the same frame twice the same bytes, two hours of sun on changes the pool's pixels, and the GPU scene with the block has a water material (`GpuScene::has_water`, so the renderer draws with the resolve's water module) and the one without it none. On a GPU too, **the two tiers** of the oasis pool: the two tables the same length (the entry after the water's material carries the depth map at both tiers, the optics only at high), the high frame the same bytes twice, and every pixel the tiers differ in a pixel of the pool or within the same three-pixel band (2026-10-18, RTX 5090: 47,618 of the pool's 47,628 pixels differ, none outside it). **No ring and no fan**: the oasis pool at high with its waves calmed, from nine metres over the east shore and from 20 m straight over the middle, drawn with its map and again with the map made unreadable (the fallback to the triangle's plane, the picture before the map). Every two neighbouring pool pixels away from the shore are sorted by the ids channel into pairs inside one triangle and pairs across an edge (a ring, a spoke of the fan or a quad's diagonal, as the LOD cut drew them). An edge's **excess** is its pairs' mean colour step less its two triangles' own mean steps, and it is set against what the output's dither alone would give it (sqrt(2/π) times its standard error, from the scatter inside the triangles). The edges' mean excess over that noise must be under 1.15 with the map and over 1.4 without it. Measured 2026-10-19 (RTX 5090, 480 × 320): **with the map 0.96 and 0.81, with the planes 2.47 and 1.64**, over 3,571 and 2,058 edges. The water shading test (`domain/gfx/tests/water_shading_tests.cpp`) draws with `visibility_resolve_water`, the low blocks and the volume tier's ([above](#translucent-by-volume)); the shading, attributes and visibility tests draw with the plain module, which has no branch.

**Performance notes.** Nothing per frame on the CPU. The mesh and the depth map are built once per scene read: a 20 m pool is 1,426 ground heights for the mesh and 14,641 for the map, the oasis 25,921 for its map, and a 1,024-texel map at most a million.

## Capability contract (ADR-0027)

This is a capability: it was added without editing `core/`, `foundation/`, the render graph, the scheduler, or another capability, and it can be removed from the build the same way.

| Registration point | This capability | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Capabilities it requires | none | stands alone; its tests link the terrain, sky and scatter capabilities for the oasis scene |
| Component and event types | none | a body is scene data (`engine.scene.WaterBody`, the scene format's), not entity state |
| Tick scheduler entry | none | nothing ticks: the waves are a function of the frame's clock, drawn by the resolve |
| Render-graph passes | none | the surface is an instance; its shading is a material class of the resolve |
| Content-build derived step | the GLB per body, clustered by the renderer's cache | done |
| Scene generator | placement generator `"water"` | done |
| Protocol methods | none | not needed |
| Tunables | none | every number is the body's |
| LOD policy | the cluster DAG of its mesh | done |
| Determinism | derived: drawn, and `depth_at` a function of position | done |
| Zero cost when unused | a scene with no block calls nothing; no material carries the flag | done |
| Tests and size table | `tests/water_tests.cpp`, `tests/water_scene_tests.cpp`, `tests/size_table.cpp` | done |
| Bench | none: no hot path on the CPU | |
| Removal proof | `ENGINE_WITH_WATER`, off in the minimal build | works: a block is refused with a sentence |

**Removing it.** `cmake --preset msvc-minimal` (or `-DENGINE_WITH_WATER=OFF`) drops the module and its tests; the module disappears from `build/<preset>/modules.json` and is listed there under `disabled_capabilities`. Everything else builds and passes, and a scene with a `water` block is refused.

## Not yet

**What is under the water that is not the ground**: the volume tier sees the terrain through the surface analytically and nothing else — a rock, a prop or a wading walker below the surface is hidden by it. Option (b) of [Translucent by volume](#translucent-by-volume) is the answer: a second visibility pass of the scene without the water instances over the pool's screen tiles, resolved for its colour and depth and composited through the surface by the same optics, with the refracted offset read from its depth; it costs a second cull, raster and resolve over the pool's pixels, and it is also what would let the bottom's own detail bend under the waves. Then: caustics on a sunlit bottom; reflections of what stands round the water (the forward pass, plan 04 §4.3 step 7, or a ray); swimming; buoyancy (a fluid volume and a submerged-volume query); a ripple map stepped by a wave equation; the moon's and the point lights' glitter; depths that follow a moving ground; polygons that are not star-shaped: roadmap S86.
