# atlas (domain)

**Purpose.** The content build's **atlas step**: re-chart an imported mesh, pack one square atlas per textured material, give every vertex a UV in it, and rebake every texture the material samples from the old UV space into the new one — so that a generator's UV layout stops deciding how far the cluster LOD can collapse. `engine-content build --atlas repack` runs it ([apps](apps.md)); `keep`, the default, is the source's atlas as it is. A content-build step only: nothing the renderer links depends on it, and a container it wrote is an ordinary `.clusters` file whose images happen to be new.

**Why it exists.** The LOD builder may slide a collapse along a UV island edge but never across one ([geometry](geometry.md#what-the-simplifier-is-given-and-why), `uv_seams = protect`), because a collapse across an island edge makes the coarse triangle interpolate its texture between two unrelated parts of the atlas. That rule is right, and it makes the atlas a **cost**: an atlas cut into thousands of small islands leaves the simplifier nothing it may collapse. E10 measured it on sixty generated props ([experiment](../experiments/e10-generated-props.md)): Meshy's triangle remesh and TRELLIS.2 put 34–98% of their vertices on a seam, and their coarse cut at 1 px still drew 70–100% of the finest cut's clusters, the share following the seam fraction at r = 0.94; Tripo's atlases were sound, but a sound atlas at 50,000–250,000 triangles lost relief the finest cut showed. **No generator gave both a collapsing LOD and a clean coarse picture**, because the generator's atlas decided the first. The UV repair ([geometry](geometry.md#uv-degenerate-triangles-the-repair)) folds islands under a texel and is deliberately not a fragmentation repair; the builder cannot do better with the atlas it is given. So the pipeline makes its own.

## The rules

1. **Which materials.** A material is repacked when some primitive uses it and it names at least one image, and every image it names can be read and decoded (`image::decode_image`, embedded bytes or a file beside the glTF). A material that names none keeps its UVs (nothing samples them); one with an image that cannot be read keeps its UVs *and* its images, and the build's JSON says why in `atlas.notes` — a texture the rebake could not carry across would be sampled through the wrong UVs, which is worse than a fragmented atlas. A material used by no primitive is left alone.
2. **One atlas per material.** Every primitive of the material is charted together (their triangles in primitive order), because they sample the same textures. Two materials are two atlases even when they share an image: each gets its own rebake.
3. **Charted on one vertex per position, never on the generator's seams.** Before anything is cut the material's vertices are welded by position (bit-equal, the weld's rule), and nothing but positions is used: not the normals, not the old UVs. A generator splits its vertices, and its normals, along its own chart borders (E10's second pass: every one of the tin cup's 22,862 seam pairs differed in normal), and xatlas reads an edge whose two sides are different vertices at one position as a seam it charges a chart for crossing — given the split mesh it redraws the atlas this step exists to replace. The corners are mapped back to their own vertices afterwards, so a vertex's normal, skin binding and morph deltas are untouched. The positions are also moved into a box whose largest side is 256 units, because xatlas cuts with absolute epsilons (a face under `FLT_EPSILON` of area is refused, two positions within 1e-4 are one in places): the charts are a function of the mesh's shape and not of the units it was authored in — the same reasoning as the LOD builder's normalized attribute weights.
4. **The charts are cut on a proxy** ("Why a proxy", below). A material of more than twice `--atlas-proxy` triangles (`RepackOptions::proxy_triangles`, default 2,000) is simplified by meshoptimizer to about that many, positions only, and **xatlas charts the proxy** (its `ChartOptions::maxCost` is `--atlas-chart-cost` in thousandths, 2000 by default, which is xatlas's own; everything else at its defaults). Every full-resolution triangle then takes the chart of the proxy triangle nearest its centroid among those facing its way (a proxy triangle turned away counts four mean proxy edges further off, which keeps the two sides of a thin wall apart); the proxy's charts of fewer than four faces — its own folds and spikes — are not candidates. The borders are cleaned: up to three majority passes move a triangle whose other two neighbours share another chart into it, and a connected piece of a chart under 32 triangles beside another chart joins the one it shares most edges with. Each (point, chart) then gets a UV by projecting the point onto the chart's proxy triangle nearest it among those its triangles there were given and carrying that triangle's proxy UVs across by barycentrics — extrapolated, not clamped, past its edge. Finally **every connected piece of every chart is parameterized again on its own triangles by least-squares conformal maps** (LSCM: Lévy, Petitjean, Ray and Maillot 2002), two points pinned, solved by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients started from the projection; the conformal map is kept when it is finite, has area, is no wider than a hundred times the side of a square of its area, and folds no more triangles than the projection did, and the projection stays otherwise. Each piece is scaled so its UV area is its surface area, since the packer gives every chart one texel density. A smaller material is charted **directly** by xatlas, with the same `maxCost`; `--atlas-proxy 0` asks for that on any mesh.
5. **The gate.** A material whose new charts leave more than 5% of its triangles folded against their chart or with no UV area (`k_max_fold_share`) is **declined**: it keeps its own UVs and images, `atlas.materials_declined` counts it, and `atlas.notes` says why. A folded triangle shares its texels with another and the rebake cannot give both their own; that is what a barrel cactus's spines and a cattle skull's teeth do to a proxy of 2,000 triangles ("The gate", below).
6. **One square atlas per material, as large as its largest texture.** The atlas side is the largest side of the material's images — times `--atlas-supersample` (1, 2 or 4; "What it costs the picture", below) — clamped to 256..8192. xatlas packs the charts — a proxy's as a UV mesh, one material id per chart so two charts whose parameter spaces overlap are never merged — with 4 texels of padding beyond the ring bilinear filtering reads (`PackOptions::padding`, `bilinear`), rotations allowed, random placement (its `KISSRng`, seeded per packing, so deterministic). It is asked first for its own estimate of texels per unit, which grows one atlas to fit; that estimate is scaled so the grown atlas would fill the square, and then shrunk 4% a packing until every chart fits one atlas of exactly that size (at most 24 packings; the JSON reports how many). The UVs are the chart positions over the side.
7. **A vertex per (vertex, chart).** Every triangle keeps its place in `indices` and its corners' positions, normals, skin bindings, morph deltas and canonical vertex ids; each corner gets a vertex whose UV is the new one, so a source vertex a new chart boundary runs through becomes one vertex per chart. The new vertices are **appended**, a morph channel's deltas follow a vertex into every copy of it, and every copy keeps the vertex's canonical id — it is the same point of the base ([geometry](geometry.md#canonical-vertex-identity)), so a file written beside the mesh still names it and the weld, which keys on the id, merges copies as before. The old vertices a repacked triangle no longer references are left for the weld that follows, which drops them — and which also merges two old vertices the generator had split for its own seam, once they land in one chart with one UV and one normal. A face is **refused** when xatlas left a corner of it out of every chart (a zero-length edge, or no area on the surface or in UV) *or put its corners in two charts* — which xatlas does: it builds a UV mesh's charts by flood fill over *equal UV values*, a sliver with no UV area is left out of the fill, and where a sliver is what joins two parts of a piece, the parts are packed as two charts and the sliver's corners go with them. A refused face is not baked, and its corners take UVs in **one** chart. Each point of a refused face gets a *source*: a charted copy of itself if a chart holds it, else the source of the nearest such point through the refused faces (breadth first, in face order); the face goes to the chart of its first corner's source, and each corner takes the copy of its own point in that chart if there is one, else its point's source if that is in the chart, else the first corner's. So it is at most a sliver inside one chart, beside the charted points it touches, and a run of slivers stays joined to the chart through the points they share. `unatlased_triangles` counts them. This was learned the hard way: the first version gave each refused corner the UV of whichever chart held its point first, or (0, 0), and such faces were stretched across the atlas between charts — on seven of the twenty Meshy props their summed UV area came to 1.7–3.7 times the whole atlas (31 faces carried 3.0 of the ribcage's 3.5), each drawing a smear of whatever lay between. After the rule the ribcage's summed UV area is 0.47, where its own atlas's is 0.55.
8. **No crumbs.** A *piece* of a chart — faces joined through the UV vertices they share — whose triangles cover less than one texel of a 4,096 atlas as stored is a **crumb**: a triangle or two the transfer left on its own across a non-manifold edge, a sliver. Pieces are sized by surface area, so nothing stops one coming out that small, and an island under a texel is exactly what the source's UV repair exists to fold away ([geometry](geometry.md#uv-degenerate-triangles-the-repair)) and what E10's island check fails. A crumb's faces are refused like the others and go to a chart beside them by the same rule; `crumb_charts` and `crumb_triangles` count them. Only a crumb with nothing charted beside it — a floating speck of geometry — keeps its own UVs, and fails the island check as it would with the source's atlas (the Meshy tin cup's one such triangle does). The UV repair itself was tried here first, run again over the new atlas: it folded crumbs, but on a mesh with thousands of refused slivers it also stretched 21 triangles across 0.64 of the TRELLIS.2 limestone's atlas, because it chooses a neighbour for each island by position, and a refused sliver's corners can sit on two charts. The rule above keeps the choice inside the step, where the chart structure is known.
9. **UVs as the container stores them.** The new UVs are rounded to half floats before anything is baked against them, and the old UVs are read that way too, because `geometry::VertexAttributes` stores half floats: between 0.5 and 1 a half float's step is two texels of a 4,096 atlas, and a texture baked for UVs the renderer does not interpolate is off by that much.
10. **Every image the material samples is rebaked, in its own space, at its own size.** One new image per distinct (source image, space) of the material: base colour and emissive are **sRGB** and are filtered in linear light, metallic-roughness and occlusion are data and are filtered as stored (an ORM texture named from both slots is rebaked once), and a tangent-space normal map is **converted** (below). Each is square at its source's largest side times the supersampling (clamped the same way), so a material whose normal map is half the size of its base colour keeps it half the size. The images are PNGs (`image::encode_png`), embedded in the container with no path; the source images no material still names are dropped from the mesh, and the material slots renumbered — **nothing of the source's UV layout is kept**.
11. **The build key moves with it.** `atlas::repack_options_key` hashes every option that changes the bytes (padding, the resolution range, the supersampling, the proxy size, the chart cost, the normal-map rule, the dilation) with a module version (`k_repack_version` in `repack.cpp`) and a word for the xatlas pin; `engine-content` mixes it into the recorded build key (`atlas_key` in `main.cpp`) only when the step runs, so a repacked container is a different derived-data entry and `keep` leaves every existing key — and every existing container — exactly as it was. That is why `k_cluster_cache_version` did not move.

## Why a proxy

The first version handed xatlas the material's triangles and took its charts. On a hand-made mesh that is what xatlas is for; on E10's generated meshes it was not good enough, for three reasons found in turn:

- **It reads a split vertex as a seam.** xatlas joins two faces into one chart only across an edge they share by index; an edge whose sides are different vertices at one position is a boundary it pays to keep. A generator's mesh is split along its own islands and often along every normal crease, so xatlas handed back the generator's islands. The position weld of rule 3 fixed that — and exposed the next two.
- **It cuts at every fold.** Its charts grow by planarity and normal deviation, and refuse a face that turns more than about 73° from the chart's normal (`computeCost`'s 0.707, "~75 degrees" in its comment). A generated surface at 50,000–250,000 triangles is full of small folds, spikes and one-triangle ridges the generator's remesh left, and every one ends a chart; the smallest charts are single faces. Charted directly, the Meshy and TRELLIS.2 props still came back with 30–60% of their vertices on a seam — less than their own atlases, but not the step's point.
- **It is slow at that size.** Growing and scoring charts over a quarter of a million faces took minutes per mesh, and the cattle skull did not finish in ten.

A proxy of 2,000 triangles has the object's shape without its folds. xatlas charts it into 22–185 charts on E10's props, and each chart of the proxy is a region of the object's surface, not of its triangulation. The full-resolution triangles are then sorted into those regions by nearness and facing (rule 4), and each region is parameterized on its own triangles — which is why the projection is only a start: a proxy triangle's plane is not the surface under it, and LSCM on the real triangles removes the shear the projection leaves while staying one piece. `--atlas-proxy 0` keeps the direct path for a mesh small enough not to need any of this, and the tests run both.

**What the proxy costs.** The proxy is positions only, so a chart may cross a crease the generator had split for its normals; that is harmless, because a normal crease is not a texture seam and the vertex keeps its own normal. A chart border is where the proxy's charts meet, a few proxy edges from the fine mesh's features, so a border can run through a flat region rather than along a crease; the rebake does not care where a border runs, and the simplifier cares only how long the borders are. And a fine feature much smaller than a proxy triangle — a spine, a tooth, a finger — is charted as part of whatever the proxy put there: LSCM on its triangles may fold it. That is what the gate is for.

## The gate

A folded triangle — one whose UV winding is the opposite of its chart's, or which has no UV area — shares texels with another triangle of its chart, and the rebake gives each texel to one owner, so the other samples the wrong part of the source. A few such triangles in a crevice are invisible; a few thousand on a cactus's spines are not. The step counts them per material after charting (`RepackedMaterial::folded_triangles`) and **declines a material over 5%**: it is left exactly as the source had it, UVs, images and all, and the build carries on with the others. 5% sits in the gap E10's sixty props left ([measured](#what-it-measured)): the 49 it repacks leave at most 2.7% of their triangles folded or flat (TRELLIS.2's cart wheel; the median Meshy prop 0.22%), and the eleven it declines 5.4% (TRELLIS.2's thin-walled broken pot) to 50% (TRELLIS.2's rope, strand by strand). Declining is the conservative answer because the source's own atlas is at worst fragmented; a folded atlas is wrong.

## The rebake

Three passes over each new image (`rebake.cpp`), with the triangles given corner by corner — old UV, new UV, and for a normal map the position and normal:

1. **Coverage** (`build_coverage`). Every texel whose centre lies inside a triangle's new UVs belongs to the first such triangle, edges inclusive and ties to the lower index. Then the **ring**: a texel outside every triangle but within 1.5 texels of one belongs to the nearest (a strictly smaller distance takes it over, so ties keep the earlier triangle). Bilinear filtering at a point inside a chart reads texels whose centres are within one texel of it on each axis, so every texel the renderer can read from inside a chart has an owner; the packer left `padding` texels beyond that ring, so two charts' rings never meet.
2. **Shading** (`rebake_image`). An interior texel samples the source at the old UV of its own centre, through the barycentrics of the owning triangle; a ring texel at the old UV of the **closest point** on the triangle, which is constant extrapolation outward across the chart border. Sampling is bilinear with repeat wrap — glTF's default sampler and the one the renderer binds — so a source that tiled is baked out into one unique atlas. One row per job.
3. **Dilation.** Up to 16 passes: an empty texel with a filled 8-neighbour takes the mean of its filled neighbours as they were before the pass. What no pass reaches — the unused part of the atlas — takes the mean of the covered texels, which is what a mip chain built from the image later will want there.

**Why texel coverage and not a GPU bake.** The rebake is a pure function of an image and some triangles, it runs on a build machine with no GPU (the content build has none — [apps](apps.md)), and its output has to be the same bytes at any thread count. Rasterizing on the CPU with an owner per texel, in index order, gives all three, for about a second to rebake a generated prop's three 2048² images and half a second to encode them (E10's medians; the charting is the other nine seconds).

## Normal maps are converted, not resampled

**A tangent-space normal map is not a colour.** Its three channels are coordinates in a frame — tangent, bitangent, normal — and the renderer builds that frame per triangle from the triangle's positions and **UVs** (`tangent_frame` in `domain/gfx/shaders/material.slang`: the tangent and bitangent from the position edges and UV deltas, Gram-Schmidt against the interpolated normal). A new UV layout rotates, mirrors and shears every chart against the old one, so the same stored vector, read through the new frame, points somewhere else. A quarter-turned chart swings a normal tilted 30° towards +u into one tilted towards another axis — 41° of error — and a mirrored chart flips it, 60°; those are the unit test's numbers.

**So each texel is decoded and re-encoded.** For a normal-map texel the rebake samples the old map at the old UV, decodes it through the **old** triangle's frame (built exactly as the shader builds it, with its thresholds, from the old UVs, and with the material's `normal_scale` applied to x and y first), which gives the object-space normal the renderer shaded with; then expresses that normal in the **new** triangle's frame and divides x and y by the scale again, so that the renderer, reading the new map through the new frame, reconstructs the same vector. Where the renderer builds no frame (UVs of no area) it perturbs nothing, so a texel whose old frame is degenerate encodes the interpolated normal itself and a texel whose new frame is degenerate stores the flat (0.5, 0.5, 1). On the test's rotated and mirrored quads the conversion reproduces the object-space normal to 1e-5° (the 8-bit storage rounds that up to a fraction of a degree on real maps), where resampling is 41° and 60° out.

**What it does not cover.** The frame is per triangle in both the rebake and the renderer, and both use it the same way, so the conversion is exact for the renderer as it is. A future renderer that took MikkTSpace tangents from the file would need the rebake to take them too. `--atlas-normal-maps resample` skips the conversion — it exists so the error can be measured, not to be used — and the build JSON's `rebake_error` for a normal map is always measured through each side's own frame, in degrees, which is what makes the two modes comparable.

## What it costs the picture

**Measured as the renderer draws it.** The build's `rebake_error` is the texture on the surface; the picture is judged by drawing the kept and the repacked container's finest cut from **one camera** through `engine-host` and comparing them (`tools/e10-harness.ps1 -CompareKept`, [content generation](../content-generation.md#the-e10-harness-toolse10-harnessps1)), **with the renderer's two orbiting point lights off**. Both the default camera and those lights are placed from the scene's bounds, which are the union of the leaf clusters' spheres (`systems/renderer` `view_set.cpp`, `lighting.cpp`), and a different atlas makes different clusters: with the lights on, a repacked clay jar whose three textures matched its source to a tenth of a level at every sample drew 4 levels redder overall, and a limestone 11 levels brighter — the lights had moved, not the textures. With them off (the sun is a direction, the same for both), the jar's two pictures differ by 0.09 of a level on average.

**What is left, and why.** On E10's Meshy limestone (259,168 triangles, strong relief in its normal map), from one camera with the lights off:

| rebake | PSNR against the kept build | FLIP |
|---|---|---|
| normal map converted (the default) | 40.8 dB | 0.0111 |
| normal map resampled (`--atlas-normal-maps resample`) | 38.6 dB | 0.0179 |
| converted, `--atlas-supersample 2` | 44.5 dB | 0.0076 |

Three things make up the rest. **Resampling**: every texel is filtered once more than the source's was, and the renderer samples textures at level 0 with no mips, so fine detail loses contrast; baking at twice the side halves that extra filter's width, at four times the texels (the PNGs grow about fourfold), which is why `--atlas-supersample` exists and why it is not the default. **The normal map across triangles**: a texel is encoded in the frame of the triangle that owns it, and bilinear filtering near an edge reads it through a neighbour's frame; the source's map was authored in a smooth frame and has the same mismatch against this renderer's per-triangle one, differently placed. **Folds**: the 0.02–2% of triangles whose new UVs wind against their chart share texels with another. The medians over E10's sets are in [the experiment](../experiments/e10-generated-props.md#repack-the-pipelines-own-atlas).

## Determinism

The output is a function of the mesh, the images and the options. **xatlas is built single-threaded** (`XA_MULTITHREADED=0`, `cmake/EngineAtlas.cmake`): its own scheduler starts a thread per logical CPU for every atlas whatever the caller's budget — under `build-all` that would be one scheduler per mesh in flight — and although it is designed so that every task writes a slot of its own, nothing here could vary its thread count to prove it. The parallelism the step has is its own: the source images decode one job each, the materials chart and pack one job each and are merged in material order, the rebake's rows are independent, and the PNGs encode one job each. `repack_tests.cpp` builds the same mesh with no pool, one worker and six and compares every byte, and `engine-content`'s test does the same through `--jobs 1` and `--jobs 8` and through `build-all`. It is **not** promised across compilers: xatlas's floating point is its own, and a GCC or Clang build at x86-64-v3 contracts to FMA where MSVC does not, which moves a chart boundary. Nothing depends on that — a container records the key it was built with, not the compiler.

## xatlas, and why

**xatlas** (github.com/jpcy/xatlas, MIT; the thekla_atlas and Fast-BVH code inside it MIT, the OpenNL solver BSD-3) segments a mesh into charts by planarity, normal deviation, roundness and straightness, parameterizes each by LSCM with a piecewise fallback for a chart that would overlap itself, and packs them into an atlas of a requested size with padding and rotations. It is two files and plain scalar C++ with no dependencies and no intrinsics, which is what a build-machine step wants. On a generated mesh it does two of those three jobs — it charts the proxy and it packs — and all three only on a mesh small enough to chart directly; the LSCM that parameterizes the full-resolution charts is this module's own (`proxy_charts.cpp`, about a thousand lines with the chart transfer), because xatlas parameterizes charts it segmented itself and cannot be handed a segmentation. The alternatives, and why not:

- **Microsoft UVAtlas** (MIT) — the other permissive, production-grade charter (isochart segmentation and packing); it builds on DirectXMath and is the larger dependency for the same job, and xatlas is the one the game-tools and lightmapping world has used most, which is where its failure modes are known.
- **Blender's unwrapper** or **UVPackmaster** — GPL and commercial respectively; not usable (ADR-0014).
- **Boundary First Flattening** and **libigl** — parameterization only (no segmentation, no packing), and libigl is MPL-2.0, which ADR-0014's list does not include.
- **Our own** — segmentation plus LSCM plus a packer is several weeks of work that xatlas has already had years of; the step's own value is in the rebake, which no library does for this renderer's frame anyway.

The pin is a commit (the repository cuts no releases; the last change is from 2022), recorded in [third_party/LICENSES.md](../../third_party/LICENSES.md). Its print hook is silenced (`xatlas::SetPrint(nullptr, …)`), because its default is `printf` to stdout, which is where `engine-content` writes its JSON.

## What it measured

On E10's three generated sets of twenty (`msvc-release`, orbit 16; [the experiment's "Repack" section](../experiments/e10-generated-props.md#repack-the-pipelines-own-atlas) has the tables, the per-prop results and what they decide), against each prop's own atlas and against the **ceiling** — the same mesh built with `--uv-seams none --uv-weight 0`, which is how far any atlas could let the LOD go:

| set | median coarse / finest pairs: own atlas → repack (ceiling) | median seam vertices | props that do not coarsen (of 20) | declined by the gate | kept vs repacked, one camera: median FLIP (worst) | repack, median ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meshy, triangle remesh | 0.69 → **0.46** (0.39) | 70% → 24% | 6 → 3 | 1 (barrel cactus) | 0.0069 (0.0206) | 10,948 |
| Tripo | 0.52 → **0.45** (0.38) | 21% → 21% | 0 → 0 | 1 (barrel cactus) | 0.0037 (0.0116) | 9,952 |
| TRELLIS.2 | 0.88 → **0.81** (0.72) | 66% → 40% | 13 → 10 | 9 | 0.0062 (0.0206) | 11,338 |

The step takes Meshy's remeshed props most of the way to the ceiling, does little for Tripo's (its atlases were sound, and the proxy's charts leave about as many seams as Tripo's did), and on TRELLIS.2 is stopped by the gate: nine of its twenty — the rope, the cactus spines, the canvas folds, the skull, the thin-walled pot and skin, the bucket, the driftwood, the lamp — would fold or flatten 5–50% of their triangles on a 2,000-triangle proxy's charts and keep their own atlases, and the ceiling shows seven of those nine do not coarsen even with no atlas at all. The repack costs 10–11 s a prop on the build machine (median; charting and packing about 9 of it), where the rest of the build takes under one. The fixture numbers are `repack_tests.cpp`'s `MESSAGE` output: the shredded torus's 128 islands become 13 charts on a proxy (15 charted directly), its 2,048 seam vertices 790 after the weld, and the probe texture comes back at a mean of 0.26 of 255 on the surface (p99 1, largest 57: the probe jumps from one saturated hue to another at every slot edge of the old atlas, and within a texel of one the source filtered once and the rebake filtered twice mix the two hues differently).

## Public API

`domain/atlas/repack.h`: `NormalMapMode` (`convert`, `resample`), `TextureSpace` (`srgb`, `linear`, `tangent_normal`), `RepackOptions` (`padding`, `min_resolution`, `max_resolution`, `supersample`, `proxy_triangles`, `max_chart_cost_milli`, `normal_maps`, `dilation`, `error_samples`), `repack_options_key`, `RebakeError`, `RepackedMaterial` (per material: charts, atlas side, utilization, packings, the proxy's triangles and charts, folded, refused and crumb triangles, times), `RebakedImage`, `RepackReport` (with `materials_declined` and `notes`), `repack_atlas`; the rebake on its own for tests and tools: `RebakeTriangles`, `TexelCoverage` (`k_ring`, `k_uncovered`), `build_coverage`, `rebake_image`, `measure_rebake`.

**Depends on.** `base`, `containers`, `math`, `hash` (the options key), `time`, `jobs`, `io` (an image beside the glTF), `image` (decode and PNG encode), `geometry` (the half-float helpers, and the UV repair's texel measure and threshold), `assets` (`MeshData`, which it rewrites in place); meshoptimizer (the proxy's simplification); xatlas (MIT with BSD-3 OpenNL inside; [third_party/LICENSES.md](../../third_party/LICENSES.md)). Layer: domain, after `assets`.

## Testing

`tools/dev.ps1 test -Filter atlas`. **The rebake** (`rebake_tests.cpp`), against answers known without it: coverage of one triangle and its duplicate over an 8×8 image — 36 interior texels (the diagonal's centres count as inside), 13 in the ring at 0.71 and 1.41 texels out, 15 uncovered, and the duplicate never winning a tie; an identity layout reproducing a gradient source **byte for byte**, as data and as sRGB through linear light and back; a quarter-turned and a mirrored layout at twice the density reading the same texture at the same surface point to within 0.17 of 255; ## Not yet

- **Fewer pieces.** The seams the step leaves (24–40% of vertices on the fragmented sets) are mostly the borders between the *pieces* a chart's triangles fall into, not between charts: on E10's ribcage 21 proxy charts at `--atlas-proxy 500` still made 346 islands. A transfer that grows a chart across non-manifold edges and loose parts, instead of cleaning up specks under 32 triangles, is where the next seams come from; the proxy size and xatlas's chart cost move them by a few points either way.
- **Area distortion.** Each piece is scaled to its surface area as a whole, and LSCM on a piece that is not a disk (a tube round a thin part) stretches its texel density across it: on E10's Meshy crate some triangles span 200 texels of a 350-texel chart. Cutting such pieces, or an area-preserving parameterization in place of LSCM, would even it out.
- **The renderer's lights follow the leaf clusters.** Two builds of one mesh are framed and lit differently by `engine-view` and `render.*`, because the default camera and the orbiting point lights come from the union of the leaf clusters' spheres rather than the mesh's positions ("What it costs the picture"). The harness works round it; the renderer could take its bounds from the positions.
- **Time.** About ten seconds a prop, nine of it charting and packing on one thread per material: xatlas is single-threaded here, and the per-piece LSCM solves are independent and could run as jobs.
- **A seam-aware gutter.** A ring texel is extrapolated from its own triangle; the ideal is the surface on the other side of the seam, which in the new atlas lives in another chart. Dilation hides it at the resolutions measured; a mip chain built later from these images will want it.
- **Mips and block compression.** The rebaked images are 8-bit PNGs; the texture pipeline that turns them into BC7/BC5 with mips ([07 §7.3](../plan/07-content-pipeline.md#73-content-build-the-derived-data-graph)) is where they go next.
- **Texel density as an input.** The atlas is as large as the largest source texture, so the step keeps the source's texel budget rather than choosing one; a per-category density ([07 §7.4](../plan/07-content-pipeline.md#74-validation-rules-automatic), "texel density") is the rule that would.
- **Decimate and bake.** Re-charting fixes whether the LOD *may* collapse; it does not put detail the geometry carries into a normal map, which is what a sound atlas at a triangle budget still loses at a coarse cut (Tripo in E10). That is the next repair in 07 §7.7's chain, and it needs this step's rebake machinery with a high-poly source instead of the same mesh.
- **Untextured materials** keep their UVs; if a generator ever emits a fragmented atlas on a material with no image, the seams still block the LOD for nothing. Zeroing such UVs is a one-line rule nobody has needed yet.
