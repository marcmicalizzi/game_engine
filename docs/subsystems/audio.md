# audio (domain)

**Purpose.** The device and mixing half of the plan's audio design ([05 §5.11](../plan/05-simulation.md#511-integration-notes): "miniaudio for devices and mixing; Steam Audio for spatialization and occlusion"), shaped by the direction note beside it — **audio is an object-based sound stage, decoded last**. The module owns output devices and a null backend that is the same mix with no device behind it, decoded clips, the streams that play the clips too long to hold decoded, a fixed pool of voices that are *sources* (objects with a spatial block, never pre-panned channel pairs), a bus tree whose master declares its speaker layout, one isolated decode stage from sources to that layout, and the master's hard clip with an optional look-ahead limiter in front of it; it is driven through two lock-free queues so the audio thread never waits for the simulation. It knows nothing about entities: the `AudioEmitter` and `AudioListener` components and the system that turns their changes into commands are [audio_system](audio_system.md), because `<flecs.h>` belongs to `systems/` and not to a domain module ([ADR-0028](../adr/0028-ecs-and-persistent-store.md) seam 5). An optional capability ([ADR-0027](../adr/0027-additive-capabilities.md)): `ENGINE_WITH_AUDIO=OFF`, or a `*-minimal` preset, takes the module, its schema library, its tests, its bench and the miniaudio download out of the configuration, and `audio_system` with it.

## The shape v0 takes, and what it fixes now

The direction note in [05 §5.11](../plan/05-simulation.md#511-integration-notes) ("Direction note, 2026-09-24: audio is an object-based sound stage, decoded last") says what the engine's audio is to become and names five things a first version must not get wrong, because they cannot be retrofitted. Its second sentence puts the decode at the furthest calibrated stage — ideally the sound card or the receiver, which already knows the room — and that is why the note's "fourth output", an object stream to an external renderer, is a decoder like the others rather than a special case. v0 builds none of the later decoders; it builds the shape they plug into. Item by item:

1. **The mixer's unit is a source with its spatial block.** A voice is a clip plus a `SourceSpatial` — position, orientation (a quaternion), distance model and its two distances, a directivity pattern (omni or a cone, stored as cosines), spread, a 2D flag and a channel mapping — 56 bytes, carried whole by every `Play` and `SetSource` command (which is why a command is 128 bytes; [commands.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/commands.h) has the arithmetic). Nothing upstream of the decode stage produces a channel gain. The stereo panner ignores the parts of the block it cannot use — the direction's vertical and front/back components, orientation beyond what the cone does with it — and they are there for the decoders that can.
2. **The master carries a declared layout, and the decode is one stage behind an interface.** The layout is a **profile from a shipped table** ([format.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/format.h)): mono, stereo, headphones, quad, 5.1, 7.1 and 7.1.4, each row plain data — the enumeration, the channel count, the channel order as speaker roles (which is the mapping onto the device's channel map) and each channel's azimuth and elevation at the canonical ITU-R angles (BS.775 for 5.1; BS.2051 Systems I and J for 7.1 and 7.1.4, whose height ring is at 45 degrees up). The decode stage is a `Decoder` — a constant table of two function pointers and the size of the per-source state it wants ([decoder.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/decoder.h)) — with the **stereo panner** as its first implementation and a **null decoder** for tests. A later VBAP or ambisonic decoder reads every row's positions; a calibration file overrides the same rows; neither touches the mixer.
3. **Non-diegetic sources are 2D sources, flagged on the source and on the bus.** A source with `k_source_2d`, or any source on a bus declared 2D (the default tree's music bus), bypasses the source model — no distance, no direction — and carries a `ChannelMapping` from its own channels to the layout: `FrontPair` (the default: stereo left and right to the front pair at unity, mono panned between them at constant power) or `Direct` (source channel *k* to layout channel *k*, for a stem authored for the layout). The panner reads the mapping; it does not know what music is.
4. **The device reports the endpoint's real layout and rate, and the mixer's layout is chosen from it.** Enumeration opens each playback endpoint in its native format without starting it and reads the channel map the platform reports (WASAPI's speaker mask, PulseAudio's channel map), classifying it against the table; the owner's default device, a Sound Blaster X3, comes back as 7.1 at 48 kHz. `resolve_layout()` returns the `audio.layout` setting when it names a profile; when the setting is `auto` (the default), headphones for a worn two-channel endpoint (its form factor, below) and otherwise the device's own layout; and stereo only when nothing says — the consumer path, with no configuration. The device is then opened with the mixer's layout and its channel map, so a stereo setting on a 7.1 endpoint is the platform's conversion and not an assumption inside the mix.
5. **The determinism claim includes the layout.** Same commands, same clips, same layout, same bytes: `hash_mix()` covers the layout as well as the samples, and the determinism test pins one hash for stereo and one for 5.1.

Headphones in v0 is the stereo profile — the panner feeds the same two channels the same way — and becomes binaural when Steam Audio arrives. An endpoint cannot say it is a pair of headphones through its channel map, so the headphones profile is chosen from the endpoint's **form factor** where the platform reports one (Windows; see "Headphones" below), and by the setting elsewhere.

## Owned data

The `Mixer` owns the voice pool on both sides (the controlling thread's `VoiceSlot`s and the audio thread's `VoiceState`s and `SourceSpatial`s), the decode stage's per-source state, the bus tree, the listener basis, the master's limiter (its delay line and windows, audio-thread state like the voices), both rings and the stats seqlock, and the **streams** — a fixed set of records, each a ring of decoded frames, the source and decoder that fill it, and the counters the fill and the voice share (see "Streaming"). The `ClipStore` owns every decoded clip's samples, and frees one only by evicting it, which it does only to a clip no voice holds; the voices hold raw pointers into it. It also owns what a streamed clip is read from — the bytes it was loaded from, or the path of its file — and a count per clip of the voices that hold it, which the mixer keeps up to date from the controlling thread. An `Output` owns the device, the miniaudio context it came from, and (on Windows) the endpoint watcher that reports a new default device. Nothing else may hold or mutate any of it.

## The pipeline, per block

```
render(out, frames):  apply every queued command (at most a ring's worth)
                      compose the bus gains, root first
                      for each 1024-frame block of the call:
                        for each voice, in slot order:
                          the voice loop:  clip (or its stream's ring) at its pitch -> scratch signal
                          the level:       gain x bus path x source model's attenuation
                          the decode:      signal + SpatialParams -> layout channels  (decoder.h)
                          ended, or its stop faded out? -> free the slot, post a VoiceEvent
                        master: peak, [look-ahead limiter, off by default], hard clip, count
                      publish the stats
```

**The voice loop** reads the clip through a **32.32 fixed-point playhead** with linear interpolation between neighbouring frames. It runs in segments that keep both interpolation taps inside the clip, so the inner loop has no bounds test, no end test and no branch on the data; the one frame whose right tap would be past the end is handled outside it (the loop's first frame for a loop, silence for a one-shot). The channel count is a template parameter chosen once per voice per block, because it changes the loop's shape ([11 §11.4](../plan/11-performance-principles.md#114-branch-free-hot-paths-and-constexpr-dispatch)). At pitch 1 the fraction is zero and the interpolation returns the sample unchanged, bit for bit.

**Why pitch is the engine's own interpolation and not miniaudio's resampler per voice.** miniaudio's linear resampler is used where quality matters more than per-sample cost — once per clip, at decode time, with its low-pass filter (below). Per voice it would cost a per-frame loop with branches and a heap block of state per voice, a loop across a clip's end would take two calls, and its rate change carries a defect this module would inherit: `ma_linear_resampler_adjust_timer_for_new_rate` multiplies two `u32`s that each go up to the reduced output rate, and `set_rate_ratio` reduces over 10^6, so a pitch whose ratio does not reduce (a semitone, 1.059463) overflows and jumps the playhead by a fraction of a sample at every pitch change. The engine's loop is ten lines, has none of these, and is the thing the bench prices.

**Parameter changes ramp in fixed time.** Every gain the decode stage derives — the voice's gain times its bus path times the source model's attenuation, split by the pan law — moves to a new value along a linear ramp of `audio.ramp_ms` (10 ms, 480 frames), starting from wherever the gain is when the change arrives, so a gain, pan, bus, position or listener change is never a click. The ramp's progress is per-source decoder state, so a ramp longer than a block carries on into the next one and one shorter than a block ends inside it: the gain on any frame is a function of how many frames ago the change arrived, and the block size — which is the device's period — does not appear. A change that arrives mid-ramp starts a new ramp from where the old one had got to. A **stop** ramps to zero over the same time and then frees the voice, at the end of the block in which the fade finished. A **new voice starts at its full level**: ramping it up would smear the attack of every percussive sound across the ramp, and a clip's first samples are its attack as it was authored. A source with no change in flight costs the plain multiply-add, and one at rest at zero (past its `max_distance`, on a muted bus) costs nothing, which is bit-identical to adding its zeros.

**Why a time and not the block, and why 10 ms.** v0 ramped each change across the block it landed in, so its length was the device's period: 10 ms at the default 480 frames, 21 ms at 1024, 5 ms on a device that opened with 256 — and a stop took one block, however long that was. Two devices given the same commands mixed different bytes, and a period short enough to lower latency made every change clickier. The length is now declared. A linear ramp of length T filters the step it replaces by sinc(fT), whose envelope is −20·log10(πfT): at 10 ms that is −30 dB at 1 kHz and −40 dB at 3 kHz, where the ear is most sensitive, against −24 and −33 dB at 5 ms and −10 dB at 1 ms, and psychoacoustic practice gates its test tones with 5 to 10 ms ramps for exactly that reason. 10 ms is the top of that range: it also finishes inside one 60 Hz tick (16.7 ms), so a source the tick moves every tick follows it rather than lagging a ramp behind, and it is what v0 did at the default period, so the default configuration sounds as it did. The tunable's range is 1 to 100 ms: below a millisecond a ramp is a step, and above a tenth of a second a stop is heard as a fade.

## The thread model

Five lines:

1. **One controlling thread** — the tick (through [audio_system](audio_system.md)), or whatever single thread drives audio — owns the top half of `Mixer`: it allocates voices, writes fixed-size commands into a single-producer single-consumer ring, and drains ended-voice events from a second one.
2. **One audio thread** — the device's callback, or the caller's own thread under the null backend — owns `render()`: it applies every queued command at the top of each call, mixes, decodes, clips, posts ended-voice events, and publishes a stats snapshot through a seqlock.
3. **The audio thread allocates nothing, takes no lock and logs nothing**: every buffer it touches was sized in the constructor, both rings are wait-free, and the seqlock's writer never waits.
4. **Clips are decoded on the job system's Efficiency pool** into engine-owned buffers under a byte budget, and a voice gets a clip's pointer only once the controlling thread has taken the decode and made the clip Ready. **Streams are filled there too**: the controlling thread's `Mixer::update()` schedules a fill for a stream running low, and the fill and the voice meet only in three counters (stream.h).
5. **Nothing crosses from the audio thread into simulation state**: an ended-voice event only frees a slot on the controlling side.

**Why two queues and not a mutex.** The audio thread runs at real-time priority (the output registers miniaudio's device thread with MMCSS as "Pro Audio" on Windows; elsewhere it runs at the highest priority the platform grants) and has a deadline every period; a mutex around the voices is a way for a lower-priority thread — the tick, preempted while holding it — to make the audio thread wait past that deadline, which is priority inversion, heard as a dropout. With a ring each way, both sides are wait-free: a push is one store into a slot and a release store of an index, a pop the mirror image, and each side re-reads the other's index only when its cached copy says the ring is full or empty. The worst case of the audio thread's call is then bounded by the ring's capacity and the voice count, not by anything the tick does. And the commands are an ordered log: the mixer's state is a function of the commands it has applied, which is what makes the mix reproducible from a command sequence at all.

**Why the rings cannot overflow in a way that matters.** The command ring (`audio.command_queue`, 1024) refuses a push when full and `ControlStats::queue_full` counts it; a refused **stop** is kept and re-sent by `update()`, because a lost stop is a loop that plays for ever. The event ring holds twice the pool, and `play()` drains it before it starts a voice: between two drains at most one voice starts and each voice ends at most once, so it can never hold more than the pool plus one. `MixerStats::events_dropped` exists to say so, and the tests assert it stays zero.

**Why the mix runs on the device's thread, not on the Efficiency pool** that plan 11 §11.5 names for audio mixing: on a machine without efficiency cores that pool runs at below-normal priority, and the one property an audio callback must have is that nothing else on the machine can make it late. Decoding, which can be late, is the Efficiency pool's.

## The voice policy

A play takes a free voice if there is one — the lowest free slot, so allocation is a function of the command sequence. With none free it **steals** the voice that matters least among those it may take: a voice already stopping first (it is fading out anyway), otherwise the lowest priority, the oldest among equals. A **one-shot may take an equal priority**, so the newest of a burst of equal-priority sounds is heard — the usual answer for footsteps and gunfire; a **loop may take only a lower one**. That asymmetry was found, not designed: with "equal steals" for everything, three looping emitters of one priority over a two-voice pool took the pool from each other on every tick — each stolen loop asked again, stole the oldest, and was stolen in turn — and the emitter system's test caught it as three plays a tick where it expected none. If nothing is takeable the play is **refused**, `play()` returns a null handle and `ControlStats::refused_pool` counts it. A stolen voice is replaced at once, without a fade: the steal is a `Play` into a live slot, and its old generation's commands are refused on both sides from then on. The pool is `audio.voices` (64): the number of sources a busy scene has audible at once is in the tens, and past that the quietest are masked by the loudest, which is exactly the set the policy takes first.

## The mix format

**48 kHz, 32-bit float, interleaved, one rate for everything.** Every clip is converted to it when it is decoded, off the audio thread, so the voice loop never converts a format and a voice at pitch 1 reads its samples unchanged. 48 kHz rather than 44.1 because it is what current output devices run at natively, so the device path usually resamples nothing. The rate is a constant rather than a tunable: every clip and every voice step is expressed against it, which makes it a data-selected dimension, and [ADR-0011](../adr/0011-tunables-before-calibration.md) keeps those out of the tunables. The channel count is the declared layout's (above).

## Sources are objects

The **source model** — `spatialize()` in [spatial.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/spatial.h) — turns a 3D source's block and the listener's basis into a `SpatialParams`: the unit direction toward the source in listener space (+x right, +y up, -z ahead), an attenuation, and the spread. That struct is the seam Steam Audio fills later from its own simulation, adding occlusion, transmission and air absorption to the attenuation; the mixer multiplies the voice's level by the attenuation and hands the rest to the decode stage.

**The distance laws**, between `min_distance` (full level at and inside it) and `max_distance`:

| Model | Law | At `max_distance` |
|---|---|---|
| `InverseTapered` (default) | `(min / d) · (max − d) / (max − min)` | silent |
| `Linear` | `(max − d) / (max − min)` | silent |
| `None` | 1 | full |

Inverse distance is the free-field law (−6 dB per doubling). The taper is there so the level **reaches zero** at `max_distance` instead of stopping at min/max and staying audible for ever, which is what lets [audio_system](audio_system.md)'s LOD policy take a voice away past `max_distance` without anyone hearing it go. Both are continuous and never rise with distance, and the tests walk them to check.

**The cone** is OpenAL's model — full level inside the inner cone around the source's facing (its −z axis, rotated by its orientation), `cone_outer_gain` outside the outer one — blended linearly in the *cosine* between them, so the audio thread never takes an inverse cosine; the widths arrive in degrees and are turned into cosines once, on the controlling thread, by `make_cone()`. **Spread** narrows the stereo panner's image toward the centre (a fully spread source is centred); a VBAP decoder will spread it over speakers instead.

**The listener** is reduced to an orthonormal basis once, when it is set, and the audio thread reads the basis: right = forward × up (core/math's right-handed, y-up, −z-ahead convention), with a degenerate forward or up repaired rather than propagated.

## The decode stage

`Decoder` is `{name, state_floats, supports(layout), decode(input, layout, state, out)}`. The mixer calls `decode` once per source per block with a `DecodeInput` — the source's signal after pitch and before any gain, its level, the ramp time, whether it is fresh, whether it is 2D and its resolved mapping and pan, the `SpatialParams`, and pointers to the whole `SourceSpatial` and the listener basis for a decoder that emits objects rather than channels — and the decoder adds the source into the layout's interleaved channels, keeping whatever per-source state it declared (the stereo panner keeps nine floats: four gains where the current ramp started, the four it is heading for, and how many frames of the ramp have run). **Every decoder ramps in fixed time**: a gain it derives that differs from the one it was heading for starts a ramp of `DecodeInput::ramp_frames` from where the gain is, spanning blocks; one that ramped across the block instead would make the mix depend on the device's period again, and the determinism test's split renders would catch it.

**Why a table of function pointers and not a virtual interface**: registration points in this engine are constant-initialized tables ([ADR-0027](../adr/0027-additive-capabilities.md), [02 §2.8](../plan/02-architecture.md#28-adding-a-capability)), and the call is once per source per block with the loop over frames inside it, so there is one indirect call per source per block and none in the loop. A decoder is chosen when the mixer is built and never changes under it; one that does not support the declared layout is replaced by the stereo panner with a log line.

**The stereo panner** pans a mono 3D source between the layout's front pair at constant power (left = cos θ, right = sin θ, θ = (pan + 1)·π/4, so the power is 1 anywhere and the centre is −3 dB per channel) by the lateral component of its direction, narrowed by its spread; a stereo source by balance (unity at the centre — a constant-power law would take 3 dB off a centred music bed for nothing); a 2D source by its mapping and pan. On a profile wider than stereo it feeds the front pair and leaves every other speaker silent, which is honest about being a stereo panner; the tests assert that 5.1 and 7.1.4 carry exactly the stereo mix on channels 0 and 1 and zeros elsewhere. A mono profile gets the fold: a mono source at its level, a stereo one at half of each channel. **The null decoder** writes nothing — every voice still plays, ends and reports, which is what makes it the proof that the stage is one.

## Buses

A tree of `BusDesc {name, parent, gain, two_d, mapping}` in which every bus's parent is an earlier bus, so one forward pass per block composes each bus's effective gain as the product of the gains on its path to the master, and a voice's level is its own gain times its bus's effective gain. The default tree is master ← {music (2D), sfx, voice, ambient}; a configured tree may add any depth (the test hangs footsteps under sfx). A malformed tree — a parent that is not an earlier bus, a gain that is not finite — is replaced by the default one with a log line rather than half-honoured. Buses are gains and flags in v0, not submixes: there is no per-bus signal to run an effect on yet (see "Not yet").

## The master

**A hard clip at full scale, counted.** After every voice is decoded, each output sample is clamped to [−1, 1], the largest magnitude before anything touches it is published as `MixerStats::peak`, and every sample the clamp changed is counted in `MixerStats::clipped_samples`. The clamp is branch-free (`maxss`/`minss`) and exact, so it costs nothing the determinism claim has to account for.

**A look-ahead limiter in front of it, off by default** ([limiter.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/limiter.h)), for content that cannot leave headroom. It is a brickwall: no sample leaves above its ceiling (`audio.limiter.threshold_db`, −1 dBFS), and material that never reaches the ceiling leaves bit for bit, only later. Per frame it takes the gain the frame needs (ceiling over the frame's peak across channels, 1 when under — the channels are linked, so the image does not move), holds the smallest such gain over a 241-frame window with a monotonic queue, averages the held gains over the same window with a box filter, takes a deeper reduction at once and lets a shallower one go by a one-pole of `audio.limiter.release_ms` (100 ms), and applies the gain to the input from 240 frames — **5 ms** — ago. A peak entering at frame p makes every held gain in the next 241 frames no larger than it needs, so their mean, the gain on the frame where that peak comes out of the delay line, is no larger either: the gain is fully down when the peak arrives. The attack is instantaneous in the sense a brickwall's is — there is no attack time to set and nothing overshoots — and the box filter shapes it as a linear ramp across the look-ahead rather than a step at its start, because a step in gain is the click the fixed-time ramps exist to avoid. A final clamp to the ceiling only ever moves a sample by the rounding of the gain's arithmetic, and the counted hard clip behind it then never fires. `MixerStats::limited_frames` counts the frames it turned down and `limiter_gain` is its lowest gain in the last block, so the counter still says when a mix is too hot.

The numbers. **5 ms of look-ahead** because the attack is a linear ramp across it, and 5 ms is the shortest ramp that is not a click (the same threshold `audio.ramp_ms` sits at the top of); every frame of look-ahead is also a frame of latency on every sound while the limiter is on, so it is no longer than that (`Mixer::latency_frames()` says 240). **−1 dBFS** leaves room for what happens after the mix — the platform's conversion when the endpoint is not in the mix's format, and the inter-sample peaks a DAC's reconstruction makes of samples near full scale, which is the margin EBU R128 and the streaming services ask of a master for the same reason. **100 ms of release** is twice the period of 20 Hz, so the gain does not ride the individual cycles of the lowest content (heard as distortion), and short enough that the mix is back at full level a few ticks after a burst rather than audibly ducked. A reduction under 2⁻¹⁶ (0.00013 dB) is let go entirely: without that the one-pole creeps towards zero for ever, stalling a few thousand ulps under 1, and the limiter would never again be at rest or bit-exact.

**Why it is off by default.** This page's policy for the master was, and is, that a mix too hot for its headroom is *said so* — by `clipped_samples` — rather than hidden, and the engine's default gains are unity with headroom left to the content; a limiter on by default would turn every over into a quiet, uncounted gain change, and it would delay every sound in every game by 5 ms whether or not anything was ever loud. So the limiter is a choice a game makes (`audio.limiter`, or `MixerConfig::limiter`) when its content cannot leave headroom — a loud shooter, a mix of many uncorrelated sources — and `limited_frames` is how it keeps the counter's honesty. The determinism pins of the default configuration do not move because of it.

**What it costs, and why it is deterministic.** At rest — no reduction in flight, nothing in its windows — a block under the ceiling is only swapped through the delay line, a contiguous run at a time; otherwise every frame takes the per-frame path. It allocates nothing after construction (the delay line of 240 frames of the layout, the queue's two columns and the box filter's deficits of 241 entries each, all sized by `configure()`), and `Limiter` is pinned in the size table at 136 bytes. The box filter sums its deficits (1 − held gain, in units of 2⁻²⁴, rounded up so the gain errs low) as integers, so the running sum cannot drift however long it runs; the release coefficient is 1/release_frames rather than an exponential; the rest is IEEE-754 arithmetic with contraction off and `ceil`, which is exact everywhere. Everything is per frame and carried across calls, so the limited mix does not depend on how frames are divided into blocks either. The one input that goes through the C library is the dB tunable's conversion to a linear ceiling (`pow`, once, at construction): the determinism test hands the limiter its ceiling linearly, and a dB setting is the toolchain's `pow` in its last bit.

## Clips

`ClipStore::load(key, bytes)` copies the bytes and decodes them — inline with no job system, on the **Efficiency pool** with one — into f32 at 48 kHz, mono or stereo: WAV, FLAC and MP3 through miniaudio's built-in decoders (dr_wav, dr_flac, dr_mp3), a rate other than 48 kHz through miniaudio's linear resampler with its low-pass filter at the highest order it offers (`MA_MAX_FILTER_ORDER`, since this runs once per clip and never per block), and more than two channels folded to stereo by miniaudio's channel converter. `load_file(key, path)` does the same from a file, which the decode job opens. `add_pcm` takes samples already in the mix format. A key the store already holds returns the existing handle; `clip_key(bytes)` is two independent 64-bit hashes of the bytes, and an asset id works as a key just as well.

**Whether a clip needs a decoder at all** comes first: a `.clip` the content build wrote, named directly or found in the derived-data cache, is copied or streamed as it is ("Built clips"). **Whether a clip is decoded whole** is decided next, by the decode job, from the decoder's own answer to "how long, how many channels" — the decoder is opened and asked, which reads the file's header and nothing more — against `audio.stream_threshold_kb` (below). A clip whose decoded size would be over it is **not decoded**: it is Ready at once with no samples, `ClipView::stream` set, and a voice that plays it streams it. One loaded from bytes keeps them, and they are what the store holds for it and what the budget counts; one loaded from a file keeps its path, and the store holds nothing. A decoder that cannot say how long a clip is without decoding it (dr_mp3 on a file with no Xing header answers by walking every frame, which it does; a decoder that answers 0 is decoded whole) is decoded whole.

**The budget.** `audio.clip_budget_mb` (256 MB, about eleven minutes of stereo) caps the decoded bytes the store holds — the samples of resident clips and the bytes a streamed clip loaded from memory keeps. A decode that would exceed it **evicts** clips nothing plays first ("Eviction" below) and is refused only when it still cannot fit. **Lifetime**: a resident clip's samples do not move or disappear while a voice holds the clip, which is the guarantee a voice's raw pointer rests on; the store must outlive every mixer that plays from it, and its destructor waits for decodes in flight.

**A decode lands at the tick.** The decode job decodes into a staging area of the clip's own and says it is done; the **controlling thread** takes it — makes it Ready, evicting what it must, or refuses it — in `ClipStore::update()`, which `Mixer::update()` calls every tick and `wait()` calls after waiting (with no job system, in the call that started the decode). So a clip is Pending until the tick after its decode finished, at most one tick late, and a clip's state changes only inside a call the controlling thread makes: a caller that asks `state()`, then `view()`, then plays within a tick sees one clip throughout. Until 2026-09-25 the job made the clip Ready itself with a release store, which was enough while nothing was ever taken away; eviction has to be decided where the voices' holds are known, and that is the controlling thread.

## Built clips

**A clip decoded once, by the content build, so a game's load is a copy.** `engine-content build <clip.wav> --cache` (and `build-all` over a manifest's `"clips"`; [apps](apps.md)) decodes a source with the store's own decoders into the mix format and writes a **`.clip`** ([clip_file.h](../../domain/audio/include/domain/audio/clip_file.h)) at `<ddc>/clips/<key>.clip`. The store takes one two ways: **named directly** — `load_file` of a path whose first four bytes are the `.clip` magic, whatever the file is called — or, with a derived-data root (`ClipStoreConfig::ddc_root`), **found for a source**: `load_file` or `load` of a WAV, FLAC or MP3 hashes its bytes, takes the key, and looks in the cache before it decodes anything. Under the stream threshold the whole file is read, its content hash checked, and its frames **copied**; over it the clip **streams from the `.clip` by range with no decoder at all** — the frames are one contiguous run at a 16-byte aligned offset, so a fill is a positional read straight into the ring. With no entry, or one that records another source or key, the store **decodes the source**, exactly as it would with no root; and it never writes the cache (`engine-content` fills it). `built_loads()` counts the loads a `.clip` answered. A clip loaded from a file stays evictable wherever its frames came from, and an evicted one streams from its `.clip` when it has one.

**The container** is the texture's (and the cluster container's) design, so one set of rules covers every derived artifact: a 32-byte header (magic `CLIP`, version 1, flags, section count, total bytes, a content hash over every byte after the header), a table of `{kind, element_size, element_count, offset}` records, and payloads at 16-byte aligned offsets; little-endian, no pointers. The kinds are **append-only** and a reader skips one it does not know:

| Kind | Name | Element | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `desc` | `ClipFileDesc`, 32 bytes, one | the sample rate (the mix rate, checked on read), channels (1 or 2), frames, the source's channel count and rate, flags |
| 2 | `samples` | `f32` | the clip at the mix rate, interleaved: what a voice reads, and what a stream reads by range |
| 3 | `source_hash` | `u64`, two | the source's content hash (`hash_bytes` over its encoded bytes as read) and the build key over it |

The reader refuses, each with its own sentence: a truncated file, the wrong magic or version, a section past the end, a content hash that does not match, a missing section, a rate that is not the mix rate, a channel count other than one or two, and a sample count that is not frames times channels. A **stream** opens a `.clip` by its front — the header, the table, the description and the identity, four small ranged reads (`read_clip_file_layout`) — with every check but the content hash, which would read the frames the stream exists not to read.

**The key** is `clip_cache_key(source hash)`: the source's hash mixed with a tag, `k_clip_cache_version` (1), the mix rate and the two-channel fold — nothing about the machine. A change that makes an old entry wrong (the rate, the resampler or its filter order, the fold, the layout) bumps the version, and an old entry is a miss rather than a wrong clip.

**The same bytes from every toolchain**, the rule `.tex` and `.clusters` keep ([geometry](geometry.md#the-same-bytes-from-every-toolchain)): integer PCM converts to f32 exactly, and the one C-library dependency — the resampler's filter coefficients, from libm in double, rounded to f32 — is the caveat "Determinism" already states and pins. `clip_file_tests.cpp` pins two golden files taken on MSVC: a generated 16-bit stereo WAV at 48 kHz, file `0xbdf041eca33d2c2f` (samples `0xb92b922062cf46ef`), and a generated 16-bit mono WAV at 44.1 kHz through the resampler, file `0x0c4f1a81606508d7` (samples `0x4fd32be85084006d`, the decode pin's own value). GCC and Clang must reproduce them; a deliberate change replaces them together with a bump of `k_clip_cache_version`.

**Why the store's decoders, and not a separate build decoder.** The point of the node is that the store's copy and the store's decode are the same bytes — the test holds a copy, a stream from a `.clip` and a decode of the source to that — so a game that ships built clips and one that ships sources sound the same, and a missing cache entry changes the load time and nothing else. **What it costs on disk**: the mix format is f32, twice a 16-bit source and eight to ten times a compressed one; that is the trade — decoding once at build time instead of on every load, and a stream that is a read rather than a decode — and a game short of disk ships its compressed sources and decodes at load, as before.

## Eviction

**The mixer tells the store which clips its voices hold.** `play()` retains the clip after its `Play` is pushed; the voice's ended event releases it, at the tick; and a **stolen** voice's clip is released once the audio thread has applied the `Play` that stole it — the same count of applied commands that frees a stolen voice's stream — because until then the audio thread may still be reading it. All of that happens on the controlling thread, at the phase boundary; the audio thread takes no part and does nothing new. A mixer releases whatever its voices still hold when it is destroyed. The store keeps a count per clip and, beside it, the resident bytes that eviction could free now (`evictable_bytes_`: resident, evictable, held by no voice), so "could this fit if I evicted everything unused" is a subtraction and not a scan.

**When a decode would not fit, the store evicts least recently used first**, among clips that are resident, **held by no voice**, and **loaded from a file** — the clock being the last retain, release or commit, so a clip that stopped playing a moment ago outlives one nobody has touched since it was loaded. Only a file's clip is evictable because the store can go back to the file: evicted, it **stays Ready** and a voice started on it **streams** it from the file (as a clip over the threshold does), and that play **asks the store to decode it again** (`request`) so the voices after it play it resident; with no job system the decode lands inside the play and that very voice plays it resident. A clip loaded from bytes or samples is the only copy the process has, and is never evicted. If the decode cannot fit even with every such clip evicted it is **refused**: counted (`over_budget`) and logged, and a file's clip then streams (refused residency, not refused), while anything else is `OverBudget`. A refused file clip is not decoded again on every play: `request` does nothing while it could not fit.

**Why a count at the tick, and not a snapshot from the audio thread.** The other way to know is the audio thread publishing, through a ring of its own, which clips its live voices read. It would put work — however little — on the audio thread for a question the controlling thread can already answer: every voice starts with a `Play` that thread pushed and ends with an event that thread drains, and the one gap, a steal, is closed by the applied-commands count streams needed anyway. Evictions are counted (`evictions()`), and so are the decodes an evicted clip's plays asked for (`reloads()`); an eviction is logged at debug level with the clip and its bytes, because a cache that evicts is doing its job.

## Streaming

A clip longer than a few seconds is not held decoded. A voice that plays it owns a **stream** — a ring of decoded frames and a decoder over the clip's source — and a decode job on the Efficiency pool keeps the ring filled ahead of the voice's playhead from ranged reads of the file (`io::FileHandle::read_at`, positional, so several streams read one file without a shared cursor; 64 KiB at a time, since the decoders ask for a few kilobytes per call and a read is a system call — "Performance notes" has what that was worth) or from the bytes the store kept — or, for a built `.clip`, from its frames by range with no decoder at all ("Built clips"). The decoder is the store's own — the same miniaudio decoders, the same conversion to f32 at 48 kHz with the same resampler and filter order, the same fold of more than two channels to stereo — run a piece at a time, opened through a miniaudio VFS whose reads are the source's (backend.cpp); that is what makes a streamed clip the same samples as the same clip stored.

**The protocol, three counters.** A stream is numbered in **stream frames**: frame 0 is the clip frame the voice started at, and the numbering runs on through every pass of a loop, so it only grows, and stream frame *f* lives in ring slot *f* mod *R*. The fill publishes `filled` (frames [0, filled) are written) with release after the samples; the voice publishes `consumed` (it needs no frame below it again) with release after its reads, and the fill writes a slot only for a frame below `consumed + R`; a one-shot's end is `end`, the stream frame after its last, which the fill writes as a **silent frame** so the voice's last interpolation reads zero past the end exactly as `read_tail` does on a stored one-shot. The fill always keeps one slot free for that frame. The two sides' counters sit on separate cache lines (`StreamRing`, 128 bytes, in the size table).

**The voice reads the ring as it reads a looping clip of R frames** — the same 32.32 playhead, the same runs of the same interpolation, the same formula for the frame whose right tap wraps to slot 0 — which is why a stream that never runs dry mixes to exactly the stored clip's bytes, bit for bit, at any pitch, from any start, round any number of loops. A run stops at the first of three limits: the ring's last slot, the last frame the fill has published a right tap for, and the end. The callback reads `filled` and `end` once, publishes `consumed` once, and **never waits**.

**Running dry is a gap, not a skip.** A voice whose next frame has not been decoded **holds its place and plays silence** for the rest of the block, and tries again next block; nothing of the clip is lost, it arrives late. The frames of silence are counted in `MixerStats::underrun_frames` (by the audio thread, which logs nothing) and in the stream's own counter, and the controlling thread's next `update()` logs the voice's **first** underrun once, with the clip, the slot and generation, the frames missed so far and the ring and fill-ahead sizes, and counts it in `ControlStats::stream_underruns`. Skipping ahead to where the voice "should" be was the alternative: it keeps a stream in time with a clock, but it needs the fill to seek past frames it has not decoded, which a compressed stream can only do by decoding them anyway, and it turns one sequential read into a seek — and the jump in the waveform is as audible as the gap. A stream that has not decoded its **first** frame yet is starting, not starving: the voice holds at frame 0 silently and nothing is counted, so a streamed voice's start latency is one Efficiency job and one ranged read (with no job system the first fill runs inside `play()`, and there is none).

**Who fills, and when.** `play()` of a streamed clip takes a free stream (none free: refused, `ControlStats::refused_stream`, before any voice is stolen), resets it to the clip's source from `start_frame` modulo the clip's length, and schedules the first fill. Each `Mixer::update()` — the tick — then schedules a fill for every stream with less than the fill-ahead decoded and no fill in flight; the fill tops the ring up (reads of at most 4,096 frames, each published as it lands, so a voice can play the first while the next decodes) and returns. At most one fill per stream is ever in flight, so the fill's state — the decoder, the file, the position in the clip — is touched by one job at a time and by the controlling thread only after the job's counter says it is done. With no job system the fill runs inline in `update()`, which is what tests and tools want. **A loop decides at the clip's end**: the fill reads the voice's loop flag when it gets there (a `set_params` stores it), seeks the decoder to frame 0 and carries on, so a loop turned off after the fill has gone round takes effect at the next end.

**A stream's life.** It is the voice's until the voice is gone: when the voice ends or its stop has faded, the audio thread's ended-voice event frees it; when the voice is **stolen**, the new voice's `Play` is what stops the old one reading, so the stream is freed once the audio thread has applied that command — the mixer numbers the commands it pushes and the audio thread publishes how many it has applied (`applied_`, release). A stream whose voice is gone but whose last fill is still running is freed at the first `update()` after the fill lands. `wait_streams()` waits for every fill in flight (tests and load screens); the mixer's destructor calls it, since a fill job holds a pointer to its stream.

**The numbers, and why.**

- **`audio.stream_threshold_kb`, 1875 KiB** — five seconds of stereo, ten of mono. The two kinds of sound it separates are that far apart: the effects a game triggers in bursts (footsteps, shots, doors, an explosion with its tail) are under four seconds, are played by many voices at once, and must sound on the block they are triggered in, so they are resident and one copy serves every voice; music, ambience beds and dialogue run for tens of seconds to minutes and are played by one or two voices, which is when a ring per voice costs less than the clip — a five-second clip is ten rings. At the budget of 256 MB, clips of the threshold's size would still number 140.
- **`audio.streams`, 16.** A stream is a ring and a decoder, and each streaming voice holds one. Music, two or three ambience beds and a line of dialogue are the usual load; sixteen leaves room for crossfades. The rings are allocated the first time each stream plays, on the controlling thread, and kept.
- **`audio.stream_ring_ms`, 500 ms** (188 KiB of stereo a stream, 3 MiB for sixteen), and **`audio.stream_fill_ms`, 250 ms**, the fill-ahead. The fill is scheduled by the tick, so what the ring holds must outlast the tick that sees it low plus the fill that tops it up: at 60 Hz the tick is 16.7 ms, and 250 ms of fill-ahead leaves 233 ms for the Efficiency pool's queue, the ranged read and the decode, and survives a tick hitch of 200 ms. Half the ring, so each fill decodes about half a ring — a quarter second, one ranged read of 48 KB of 16-bit stereo — rather than a stream of small reads: the test holds the default to one fill every 32 blocks at pitch 0.8. A voice pitched up reads the ring faster, so pitch times the block is bounded by the ring (a 1024-frame block at 20x pitch is most of 500 ms); a stream that needs more is a longer ring.

**What a streamed clip does not promise.** Its length (`ClipInfo::frames`) is the decoder's answer before decoding: exact for WAV and FLAC at the mix rate and for anything the content build decoded, and for a resampled clip miniaudio's arithmetic estimate, which the test measured a frame short of what the resampler then produces (32,653 against 32,654 for 30,000 frames at 44.1 kHz). The mix does not depend on it — a stream goes round when the decoder says the clip has ended, which is where the stored clip ends, and the bytes are the same — but a start frame taken modulo the length, and a caller's clock (audio_system's resumed loops), can differ by that frame.

## Devices and the null backend

`enumerate_devices()` initializes the first platform backend that will start — WASAPI on Windows; PulseAudio, then ALSA, on Linux — and lists its playback devices, opening each one unstarted to read the layout and rate its endpoint reports. **Zero devices is an answer**: the CI runners and the headless GPU server have none, and the result is an empty list with the backend's name, or `"none"` when no backend would start at all, which the test accepts as readily as the eight devices on the development desktop. The Linux backends are loaded at run time, so building needs no audio headers or libraries and a machine without them runs the same binary and finds nothing.

An `Output` opens a device (named, or the default) in the mixer's layout and lets miniaudio's device thread call `Mixer::render()` once per period (`audio.period_frames`, 480: 10 ms; miniaudio's fixed-size callback guarantees the block size). When there is no device, or none by that name, it falls back to the **null backend** — unless told not to — and says why once in the log; the game keeps running and the mix is computed and not heard. The null backend is not a device at all: nothing calls `render()` until the caller does, into its own buffer, which is what every test does and what makes the mix testable headless and deterministic.

### No test opens the speakers

(2026-11-22, S112-3; the second review of S112's N1.) The owner's rule is that no agent opens a real audio device, and engine-view's sound is on by default for a session somebody attends, so the tree keeps the two apart mechanically, not by every case remembering a flag. **The test main and the bench main** (`tests/support/test_main.cpp`, `bench_main.cpp`, linked into every `engine_<module>_tests` and `_bench` executable) **set `ENGINE_AUDIO_AUTO=null` in their own process before any case runs** (`engine::test::k_audio_auto_env`), so every engine binary a case starts inherits it, and engine-view's `--audio auto` reads it and answers the null backend however it was started — a window, `--interactive`, a game module: the world is still modelled, mixed and summarised, and nothing is played (`ENGINE_AUDIO_AUTO=off` keeps the stage closed instead; `view::k_audio_auto_env`, apps.md's table, "Sound a module plays"). An explicit `--audio <device>` is then the only way a test reaches the speakers, and only the opt-in device test may do it (`ENGINE_AUDIO_DEVICE_TEST=1`, below); no case in the tree passes one. And without a test, `auto` is the device only for a window somebody sits at: a run that ends by itself, an offscreen run, a run with no scene and one nothing ticks open nothing, so an agent's `engine-view --frames N --capture` is silent too.

### Device changes at run time

A headset connects and becomes the default; a USB DAC is unplugged; a Bluetooth device drops. The platform reports each on a thread of its own, and **all that happens there is a flag**: `Output::notify(DeviceEvent)` sets a bit in an atomic and returns. The sources of the bits are miniaudio's device notifications — `stopped` (`DeviceEvent::Stopped`) and `rerouted` (`Rerouted`) — and, on Windows, the engine's own endpoint watcher, an `IMMNotificationClient` registered with the endpoint enumerator in the module's platform file (`src/endpoint.cpp`): the default render device changing for the console role sets `DefaultChanged`, and the output's own endpoint going inactive or being removed sets `Lost`. **`Output::update()`**, called once a tick from the controlling thread, takes the bits and decides: a device that has stopped without being asked is `Lost` whether or not anything said so (miniaudio posts no `stopped` when the stop itself fails, which is what an unplugged endpoint's stream does, and ALSA has no notification at all), while a `stopped` from a device still running is PulseAudio suspending a sink and is dropped; an output opened on a *named* device ignores a new default. Anything left closes the output and opens it again with the same `OutputConfig` — on the new default, in the mixer's declared layout, which does not change (the platform converts if the new endpoint is something else) — and logs `output device changed` with the reason, the previous and new device and backend, the device's and the mix's layout, and the count of reopens. **The mixer is not touched**: its voices, their playheads, the buses and the listener are exactly where the old device's last block left them, and commands sent during the gap wait in the ring for the new device's first block. With no device at all it falls back to the null backend as `open()` does, and on Windows the watcher stays registered, so a device that later becomes the default is found at the next update.

**Why miniaudio's own rerouting is off.** miniaudio's WASAPI backend can follow the default device by itself — but it does it by reinitializing the device on the COM notification thread, holding a mutex that `ma_device_uninit` destroys, so an output closed or reopened from the controlling thread while a reroute was in flight would race it inside the library. With `wasapi.noAutoStreamRouting` set, nothing reinitializes the device behind the engine's back, which is also what makes "stopped without being asked" mean "gone". The default device is opened as *the default* (a null device id) rather than by the id it has today: a PulseAudio stream opened that way follows the default sink, the server moves it, and miniaudio reports the move as `rerouted`; opened by id, it is pinned to that sink. On Windows the same null id opens the current default and the watcher does the following.

**What the tests can and cannot reach.** A test cannot change the machine's default device, and the null backend is no miniaudio device, so miniaudio's notification callback cannot be raised headless. `realtime_tests.cpp` drives the path from the flag on: `notify()` is exactly what the callbacks call, and the test holds `update()` to reopening once per change, to its filters (a `stopped` from a running device, a new default for a named one), to the log record's fields, and the mixer to rendering the same bytes across the reopen as a mixer that never had one. The opt-in device test (`ENGINE_AUDIO_DEVICE_TEST=1`) reopens a real endpoint the same way — on the development desktop, WASAPI's default (a Sound Blaster X3 at 7.1) closed, reopened through the null id with the watcher registered, and its thread pulling the same mixer again. The watcher's callbacks themselves and miniaudio's `stopped`/`rerouted` are reached only by plugging something in.

### Headphones

A pair of headphones reports a stereo channel map, exactly as a pair of desk speakers does, so the channel map cannot choose the headphones profile. The endpoint's **form factor** can: on Windows every endpoint carries `PKEY_AudioEndpoint_FormFactor` in its property store, and enumeration and `Output::open` read it (`DeviceInfo::form_factor`, reported by `audio.devices`) through the module's platform file, mapping the platform's values onto `EndpointFormFactor` — speakers, headphones, headset (headphones with a microphone: a gaming headset, a Bluetooth hands-free device), line level, digital (S/PDIF, HDMI, digital passthrough), other (handset, microphone, network device), unknown. With `audio.layout` at `auto`, `resolve_layout()` then declares **headphones for a worn endpoint with two channels or fewer**. A worn endpoint that reports more — a gaming headset's 7.1 endpoint — keeps its own layout, because it renders those speakers to the ears in its driver, which is the decode happening at the endpoint that the direction note puts first. An explicit `audio.layout` still wins. On the development desktop the four endpoints come back as speakers (the Sound Blaster X3's 7.1 and a K3+'s stereo) and digital (two S/PDIF outputs).

**Linux is the setting.** miniaudio does not surface what PulseAudio and ALSA know about an endpoint, so `form_factor` is `Unknown` there and the headphones profile is chosen by `audio.layout`; what those APIs would offer is on the "Not yet" list.

## What miniaudio is asked to do

miniaudio 0.11.25 ([third_party/LICENSES.md](../../third_party/LICENSES.md)) is compiled as C into `engine_miniaudio` by [cmake/EngineAudio.cmake](../../cmake/EngineAudio.cmake) and linked **privately**; its header is included by exactly one engine file, `src/backend.cpp`, and no public header names a miniaudio type — the same argument [physics](physics.md#why-nothing-from-jolt-is-public) makes for Jolt. Compiled in: the device layer for WASAPI, PulseAudio and ALSA; the WAV, FLAC and MP3 decoders; the data converter and resampler the decoders use. Compiled out, each because the engine does not use it: DirectSound and WinMM (every supported Windows has WASAPI), JACK, miniaudio's own null device (it plays to nowhere on a thread of its own, and enumerating it would report a device on a machine with none), the encoders, the generators, the high-level engine, its node graph and its resource manager. The defines are public on the library target because several of them change the layout of `ma_context` and `ma_device`, which `backend.cpp` allocates. Of the device layer the engine uses the data callback, the `stopped` and `rerouted` notifications (they set a flag; see "Device changes at run time"), and not WASAPI's automatic stream routing, which is switched off for the reason given there. Of the decoders it uses `ma_decoder_init_memory` for a clip decoded whole and `ma_decoder_init_vfs` for a stream — over a VFS of the engine's own whose one "file" is the stream's source, read through `io::FileHandle` or out of the store's bytes — because the VFS entry point is the one that gives every decoder a `tell` as the memory one does (dr_wav uses it to clamp a data chunk that claims more than the file holds), so the two read a malformed file the same way; miniaudio refuses an empty path there, so the stream opens a name with no extension and the decoder is found by trying each in turn, as the memory decoder does. The Windows endpoint watcher is the engine's own COM code in `src/endpoint.cpp`, which links `ole32`; miniaudio loads what it needs of COM at run time.

**Vorbis is not compiled.** stb_vorbis ships in the same checkout (`extras/stb_vorbis.c`) and would cost one include, so it does not bring another dependency — but nothing in the tree can produce an Ogg Vorbis file to test it with short of committing a binary fixture (AGENTS.md forbids that) or writing an encoder, an untested decoder is not a feature, and stb_vorbis's history of crashes on malformed input matters for a decoder that will read mod content. It is on the "Not yet" list with that reasoning. FLAC is tested through a writer in the tests that emits VERBATIM subframes (no predictor, no Rice coder — only the framing and its two CRCs), which is how a generated clip reaches dr_flac without a fixture on disk; MP3 is compiled and has no fixture for the same reason Vorbis has none, which is worth knowing.

## Determinism

**The claim**: for a given clip set, a given layout and decoder, a given ramp time, and a given sequence of commands and the frames they are applied at, the output is the same bytes on every run, compiler and C library — **however the frames are divided into `render()` calls**, which is to say whatever period the device runs. That last clause is new with the fixed-time ramp: while a change ramped across the block it landed in, a 160-frame period ramped three times as fast as a 480-frame one, and a command log recorded on one machine mixed to different bytes on a machine whose device asked for a different period. **Why it holds**: voices are summed in slot order into a cleared buffer, and slot allocation is a function of the command sequence; every operation on the audio thread is IEEE-754 `+ − × ÷` and `sqrt` in `f32`, which are correctly rounded everywhere, with floating-point contraction off for the whole tree ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)); the playhead is integer; and nothing on the audio thread calls a transcendental function — the pan law and the cone use `sin_quarter`/`cos_degrees`, fixed polynomials, rather than `std::sin`, which MSVC's and glibc's libraries are allowed to disagree about in the last bit. **What it does not cover**: which block a command lands in on a live device, which depends on when the device asked for the block; and the device's own conversion after the mix, when the endpoint is not in the mix's format.

**How it is tested**: `determinism_tests.cpp` runs a 48-block scripted session — every command kind, pitch through the interpolating path, loops and one-shots running out, a stereo clip, 2D sources on a 2D bus and panned by hand, 3D sources moving past a turning listener with a cone, a spread and the linear model, a bus change, the pool stealing and refusing, and the master clipping — twice in-process, compares the bytes, and pins `hash_mix(layout, samples)` for stereo and for 5.1, at the default 10 ms ramp. It then renders the same session with every 480-frame block split into three calls of 160, into 7 and 473, and into seven of 64 and one of 32, and requires the same bytes; and does the same with a 15 ms ramp, which crosses the calls. A third pin is the stereo session through the master's limiter (ceiling −1 dBFS given linearly, 100 ms release), which the session's 3× master gain drives far over its ceiling; the same splits must give the same bytes through it, and until the session's first frame over the ceiling the limited output must be the plain one 240 frames later, sample for sample. The clips are generated without the C library (`exact_sine` through `sin_quarter`, and an LCG), so the hash depends on the mixer alone. The values were taken on MSVC; the Linux container build must reproduce them with GCC 13 and Clang 18, which is the cross-toolchain half of the claim.

**The pins moved once, with the ramp.** The session's blocks are 480 frames and the ramp is 480 frames, so every ramp still starts and ends where v0's did; what changed is a ramp's last frame, which is now the target itself rather than the ramp formula's rounding of it. Measured against v0's panner run in the same test (it reproduced v0's pins, `0x8daba54729a3c2f1` and `0xb15db834d3793bf4`): two samples of 46,080 moved in stereo and two of 138,240 in 5.1, each on a block's last frame, by at most 3·10⁻⁸. The new pins are `0x4e7aa0ccb3a4fdaf` and `0xc8486cc4956e413a`. A session whose blocks were not the ramp's length would have moved much more — which is the point: before, its bytes depended on the block.

**Streaming does not move the claim.** A stream that never runs dry mixes to exactly the bytes of the same clip stored ("Streaming" says why), so the determinism test plays one generated clip four ways at once — a 3D one-shot running out, a loop started past its middle at pitch 1.37, a 2D loop stopped mid-clip and one re-pitched while it plays — through rings of 50 ms that go round nine times, stored and streamed, requires the same bytes, and pins them: `0xe614cc3f511842e7`. A stream that runs dry is a gap in the output, which is a function of when its fills landed, and nothing claims that is reproducible; it is counted instead.

**The decode is pinned too, with one caveat stated.** A 44.1 kHz clip goes through miniaudio's low-pass filter, whose coefficients come from libm's `sin` and `cos` in double and are then rounded to `f32`; a last-bit difference between C libraries survives that rounding only when a coefficient sits within 2^-29 of an `f32` rounding boundary. `clip_tests.cpp` pins the hash of a decoded 44.1 kHz clip generated without libm, so a toolchain that disagrees is caught rather than assumed away.

## Protocol

`audio.devices` (no params → `engine.audio.AudioDevicesResult`): the backend, each playback device with its layout, channel count, rate, default flag and form factor (`AudioDevice` version 2 added `form_factor`, with a default, so a version-1 reader is unaffected), and the layout a mixer would be declared with now (`resolve_layout` of the default device). `register_methods()` adds it; engine-host calls it when the capability is in its build, so `engine-cli audio.devices` is the probe — the audio counterpart of `gpu.adapters`, and like it, an empty answer is a normal one.

## Invariants

- The audio thread allocates nothing, takes no lock and logs nothing. Checked by `realtime_tests.cpp` with a replaced global `operator new` and the engine's own allocation counter, both counting on the rendering thread, and a log sink counting records with every category at trace, over a session of every command kind, voices ending, steals, clipping, and renders longer than a block.
- A command naming a voice generation the slot has moved past is counted stale and not applied; generation 0 is never issued.
- The controlling thread frees a slot only when the audio thread's event says the voice ended, except by stealing it, which is an explicit `Play`.
- The event ring never overflows (`events_dropped` stays 0), and a stop the command ring refused is re-sent.
- A clip's samples do not move while a voice holds the clip; a voice is only ever given the pointer of a Ready, resident clip, and holds it from its `play()` until its ended event (or, stolen, until the audio thread has applied the steal).
- The store evicts only a resident clip loaded from a file that no voice holds, least recently used first, and only when a decode would not otherwise fit; an evicted clip stays Ready and streams. A decode is refused only when it could not fit with every such clip evicted.
- A clip changes state only inside a call the controlling thread makes (`load`, `load_file`, `add_pcm`, `request`, `update`, `wait`).
- Bus parents precede their children; the effective gain of a bus is the product of the gains on its path to the master.
- A gain change reaches its target `ramp_frames` frames after the block it arrived in starts, and a stopped voice is freed at the end of the block in which its fade of the same length finished, however the frames are divided into `render()` calls.
- The master's output is in [−1, 1]; every sample the clip changed is counted.
- A streamed voice reads only stream frames below the fill's published `filled`, never waits for one, and holds its place when it has none; the fill writes a ring slot only for a frame below the voice's published `consumed` plus the ring's length, and always leaves one slot for a one-shot's silent end frame. At most one fill per stream is in flight.
- A stream is reused only once its voice can no longer read it: after the voice's ended event, or after the audio thread has applied the `Play` that stole the voice's slot; and only once its last fill has landed.
- A streamed clip that never runs dry mixes to the same bytes as the same clip stored.
- With the limiter on, no output sample exceeds its ceiling, and a mix that has not reached the ceiling in the last 241 frames leaves it unchanged, bit for bit, 240 frames later.
- Nothing upstream of the decode stage depends on the layout; with the null decoder the mix is silence and every voice's lifecycle is unchanged.

What the rows say:

1. **A voice costs about 1.1 µs per 10 ms block** — a little over two nanoseconds a frame for the voice loop, the source model and the decode together. The default pool of 64 is 70 µs, **0.7% of one core**; the pool's size, not the mixer, is the budget, which is why it is a tunable.
2. **The fixed-time ramp's fast path is what took a quarter off.** v0 ramped every gain across every block whether it had changed or not, so every sample paid a multiply, an add and an integer-to-float conversion for a ramp from a gain to itself. A source with no change in flight now costs the plain multiply-add. `audio.mix.3d_turning` is the other side: every voice's pan changes every block, so every sample is on a ramp, and it costs what v0 always did (91.9 µs against v0's 90–95). A real scene sits between the two — a tick moves the sources that move, and a ramp is 10 ms, so a source moved every 60 Hz tick is on a ramp for 10 ms of every 16.7.
3. **The fixed cost of a block is about 1.2 µs** — clearing the output, the master's pass over it, publishing the stats — which is why the per-voice figure falls from 2.3 µs to 1.1 µs as the pool fills.
4. **A stereo 2D voice costs 40% more than a mono 3D one** despite skipping the source model: it moves twice the samples through both passes. The source model's square root and two polynomials per voice per block do not show next to 480 frames.
5. **5.1 costs 4% more than stereo** for the same voices: the stereo panner still writes two channels, the stride is wider, and the master's pass covers three times the samples. A VBAP decoder writing every speaker will cost more, and the bench row is where that will be seen.
6. **The limiter costs about 1 µs a block at rest and 7 µs working.** At rest it only swaps the block through its delay line. Working, it is about 15 ns a frame — the peak across the channels, a division, the hold's queue, the box filter's integer sum, the release and the delayed multiply and clamp — which is six voices' worth, and only while the mix is over its ceiling.
7. **Where the time goes**: the voice loop is a serial chain (the 32.32 playhead's add feeds the next frame's index) with two dependent loads a frame, and the decode is a second pass over the scratch signal. Fusing the two passes for the stereo panner and a vectorized pitch-1 path are the obvious next steps; at under 1% of a core for the default pool, neither is needed yet.

1. **Stored voices did not move.** The mix loop's one addition for them is a test of the voice's stream flag once per voice per block; every stored row is within 1% of the parent's, in both directions, which is the run-to-run spread.
2. **A streamed voice costs what a voice with a clip of its own costs**: 72.8 µs for 64 against 72.4 µs for 64 distinct stored clips — 3–4% over 64 voices sharing one clip, whose 192 KB stays in L2. The ring read is the stored loop's own code (the disassembly of the two inner loops is instruction for instruction the same); what a stream adds is memory that is its own.
3. **The fills must not run on the mixing thread.** The first version of `audio.mix.stream` ran its fills inline, between blocks, with the clock stopped — and still measured 28% over `audio.mix.3d` (90 µs for 64, and 2.9 µs for one voice against 2.25), the same with a 60 ms ring whose fills came every few blocks. The fill's cost was not in the number; its footprint was: the decoder's code and tables passing through the core's caches between two blocks cost the next block its warm instruction and data caches. With the fills on the Efficiency pool, on other cores, as a game runs them, the difference is the 3% above. That is the thread model's rule (a stream's fill is never the audio thread's) measured, and it is why the fill is a job even where an inline fill would have been simpler.
4. **The decode is small next to the read, and the reads were the cost.** A stream costs its Efficiency worker 0.2 µs per 10 ms of audio from a WAV the store holds, 8 µs for FLAC and 20 µs when it resamples — sixteen streams resampling from files are under 0.4 ms of one efficiency core per 10 ms. From a file it first cost 4.5 µs: the decoders ask their VFS for a few kilobytes at a time, and each of those was its own positional `ReadFile` with an event to wait on, a dozen a fill. The stream decoder now reads its file **64 KiB ahead** (a whole fill of 16-bit stereo in one read, and a window a hundredth of the ring's size), which took the file row from 112 to 14 µs a fill, 0.57 µs per 10 ms. A built `.clip` was read in the fill's 4,096-frame chunks — three reads a fill — and is now read in one piece to the ring's end, since there is no decoder whose output a voice could start on sooner: 36 to 24 µs. It moves twice the bytes of the 16-bit WAV, so from a WAV it costs more than the WAV does; what it saves is the decoder, which is the whole cost of a compressed or resampled source (200 and 508 µs a fill against 24). Measured 2026-09-25, the parent's executable and this one's back to back, seven repeats (others at 6.5% and 8.7% of the CPU at the start of each, and 19.8% by the end of the second, when another agent took the GPU lock — upper bounds).

**Eviction costs the audio thread nothing**, because it asks nothing of it: the holds are counted where plays are pushed and events drained. Measured the same day with the streaming build's executable and eviction's back to back (others at 1.7–9.1% of the CPU, the GPU lock free): `audio.mix.3d` at 1 to 256 voices, `_51`, `_limited` and `_limiter_idle` within 0.1% of each other (`_turning` and `_distinct` moved within their own run-to-run spread, 8–11% and 1–3% that day), and `audio_system.tick` — the tick that plays, stops and drains, now retaining and releasing — at 30.7 and 174.9 µs moving, 25.1 and 168.0 µs still, for 1,000 and 10,000 emitters, inside the ranges [audio_system](audio_system.md) measured before it (30.5–32.0 and 170–193, 24.3–27.2 and 162–180).

### Two kinds of source

- **Synthesised from the world's state**: the wind at the listener's ears, the wind in the crowns, a stream's stretches, a fall, a stretch of surf, a lake's lap. Each is continuous, is everywhere, and is a function of state the picture already reads (the scene's wind and its gusts, the trees standing near, the flow solve, the sea), so a recording would loop and would disagree with what is seen: the gust that bends a crown must be the gust heard, and a wave must sound when it breaks. The grounding gives each a level in closed form and a spectrum's shape.
- **Clips**: whatever is discrete and has an identity a game owns: a bird's call, a door, a voice, music. A game declares them with its scene and plays them through the game service.

The engine ships no recorded ambience, and none of its scenes uses a clip where a synthesised source exists.

### The shaped-noise voice

One new kind of voice in this module's mixer, used by nothing until a command asks for it (a voice's kind is tested once a voice a block, as its stream flag is):

- a seeded integer generator, white, with no call into the C library;
- **a fixed bank of eight octave band-pass filters, 63 Hz to 8 kHz**, their coefficients literals in the source, and **one movable band** (a conifer's needle tone, whose centre follows the wind) whose coefficients the controlling thread computes with this module's fixed polynomials and sends;
- nine gains as the voice's parameters, moved by a fixed-time ramp of their own (`audio.field_ramp_ms`, 50: three ticks, since a field is re-described every tick and a 10 ms ramp would stair-step it);
- from S113, up to four damped resonators a voice struck by impulses a command schedules: a trickle's separate bubbles. **Built (S113)**: `noise_strike`, `Mixer::strike_noise` (`CommandKind::StrikeNoise`, a 20-byte payload). A resonator is the two-pole recursion y[n] = a y[n−1] − b y[n−2] with a = 2 r cos w and b = r², r = e^(−d / fs) by five terms of its series and the sine and cosine `sin_quarter`'s, so the controlling thread makes the same coefficients on every toolchain; a strike sets the delays so it rings A r^n sin(w (n + 1)) from the next block's first frame, replacing what that resonator rang, for as many frames as r^n takes to reach 10⁻⁴ (`ln 10⁴ / (d / fs)`, a division). Its samples are added to the voice's signal before the voice's gain and spatial block. `NoiseState` grew to 384 bytes (24 KB at 64 voices); a voice never struck pays a test of four words a block. The struck session (two bubbles a tick on the four resonators in turn, 1.1 to 7.4 kHz, rendered in even and uneven calls to the same bytes) is pinned on MSVC as `0x0730cf117e1b9046`; the S112 session's pin did not move. The rise in pitch as a bubble nears the surface is not built.

Its parameters are one command of the size a `SetSource` is. The output goes through the same source model and decode stage as a clip's, so it is an object with a position like any other. The determinism claim above holds for it for the same reasons (integer state, `+ − × ÷` in `f32`, literal coefficients) and its bytes are pinned beside the session's. **Why a voice kind and not band-limited noise clips played by the existing voices**: a source with a spectrum would then be eight voices, and the 27 sources below 216 of a pool of 64.

**Built (S112; `noise_voice.h`).** The generator is xorshift32, its words over 2³¹ uniform on [−1, 1). Each band is the audio cookbook's band-pass with a constant 0 dB peak at Q √2 (an octave between its −3 dB points), centres 62.5 × 2^k Hz; such a filter passes white noise of variance s² at s² b0 exactly (its squared impulse response sums to b0, held by a case), so each band's numerator is scaled to `c = √(3 b0)` and **every band passes the generator's noise at an RMS of one: a band's gain is its RMS in full scale**, which is what a level in dB SPL maps onto (`audio.world.reference_db`, [world_sound](world_sound.md#the-wind-in-the-trees-and-at-the-ears-s112)). Neighbouring bands' skirts overlap, so eight gains are the bands' powers to within that overlap; moving every gain by one factor moves the voice's level by exactly it. The fixed bands' three coefficients are f32 literals computed once in double; the movable band's (`noise_band`, held to 40 Hz – 12 kHz) are computed on the controlling thread with `sin_quarter` and a correctly rounded square root. `Mixer::play_noise` pushes the Play (flag `k_voice_noise`, no samples) and its first `SetNoise` together, both or neither, so the audio thread applies them before the voice's first block, which ramps up to its description from nothing over the same ramp (S112-2: noise has no authored attack to keep, and a step from silence to broadband noise is a click); `set_noise` ramps the nine gains over `audio.field_ramp_ms` (50 ms: a world field is described again every tick, and a 10 ms ramp would stair-step it) and moves the movable band at once. A noise voice loops until stopped and is stolen as a loop is. Its state (`NoiseState`, 320 bytes, a parallel array of the pool: 20 KB at 64 voices) is sized at construction; nothing on the audio thread allocates. The scripted session's bytes (three voices, a ramp, a band moved, a stop, rendered in even and in uneven calls to the same bytes) are pinned on MSVC as `0x39115f278d92b677` (S112-2's fresh ramp moved it from `0x4a2e2b356bc6e533`); GCC and Clang must reproduce it. Its cost is `audio.mix.noise` below ("Performance notes").

### The capability: `systems/world_sound`

`ENGINE_WITH_WORLD_SOUND`, `engine_capability_requires(world_sound audio)`, off in the minimal build. It owns a **`SoundStage`**: the clip store, the mixer, the output or the null backend, the listener, a game's clips and voices, and the fields that turn the world into shaped-noise voices. A host has one object to create, hand a listener and tick; the rendering rule's counterpart holds, **sound code lives in the module, not in an app**.

**The wind that drives it is the frame's, the one every reader is given**: the scene's wind, or the live state a host's keys or a game set while the session runs (`gfx::WindLive`, handed to every reader as `FrameDesc::wind`; roadmap W151, built the night this was designed and not on this row's base). The host hands the stage the same value each tick; the stage takes the mean and the forced gust from it and lays the turbulence at each source's place over it with `wind_gust`. A game changes the wind through the wind's own service (`wind_set`, `wind_gust`) and the sound follows; no sound call sets a wind, and the stage holds none of its own.

It links `gfx` for the wind's header (`WindLive`, `wind_at`, `wind_gust`, `sea_state`: the model is not a capability; linked since S112, the first stage to read a wind) and **no capability but `audio`**. Trees and water reach it through **source tables a host fills**, a function pointer and a context each, as scene generators and derived steps are tables:

| Table | What it visits | Filled from |
|---|---|---|
| `CanopySource` | crowns in a box: position, height, **leaf area**, **class** (blade, needle with its width, bare) | the scene's instances and the active placement tiles |
| `FlowSource` | a stream's stretches: two ends, discharge, drop, wet width, regime; falls with their height and what they land on | the solved channel description and the plunge records |
| `SurfSource` | stretches of shore: position, the power dissipated in breaking, the surf similarity, the train's phase | each body's sea state, flux field and arrival field |

**The engine stays general purpose.** A crown's leaf area is measured from any mesh, as the area of its two-sided foliage parts; its class is a property a mesh entry declares (`blade` when it says nothing), which the flora recipes fill and an imported tree may write by hand. Nothing keys on which generator made a tree. A scene with no trees, no water and no wind sounds nothing and costs nothing; with a wind alone it is the ears' two voices.

### How many sound at once

| Source | Voices | Chosen by |
|---|---|---|
| the ears' wind | 2 | always, above 0.5 m/s of relative wind |
| crowns | 6 | level at the listener |
| stream stretches | 4 | level; placed at the nearest point of the line, spread by the angle it subtends |
| falls | 4 | level |
| surf stretches | 3 | level |
| **beds** | 8, one a 45° sector of azimuth | everything else |
| | **27** of `audio.voices` (64) | each count a tunable under `audio.world.*` |

**Far sources merge by power.** Sources that are not coherent add in power band by band, so everything not chosen as a voice of its own, of every kind, is summed into its sector's bed: eight band powers, placed at the power-weighted direction with a spread. A source enters or leaves the chosen set across a 3 dB band of hysteresis, and as its own voice ramps up its share leaves the bed, so the power heard does not step. A tile past `audio.world.near_m` (96) contributes one aggregate made when it activates (leaf area by class at its centroid); nearer tiles are walked by instance. Nothing past `audio.world.reach_m` (2000) is summed. **That is the capability's LOD policy**: voices by level at the listener, beds by sector, aggregates by tile.

The chosen voices are re-described every tick (a gust is tick-fast); the choice and the beds at `audio.world.refresh_hz` (8) on the job system's Efficiency pool, taken at the next tick from fixed arrays, so the frame loop allocates nothing. World voices are loops at priorities under a game's default, individual voices lowest, then beds, then the ears' wind: a game's one-shot takes a crown before it takes a bed.

### Distance

A world voice uses the `None` distance model and carries its path in its band gains: spreading (a line near, a point far), **air absorption by band** at a humidity (the tunable `audio.world.humidity`, 70), **Hoover's forest term** over the length of path under canopy, **ISO 9613-2's ground term** from the two heights and the ground's kind (soft by default; it takes as much from a ground-level stream's middle bands at 200 m as the air takes from its top), and its **barrier term** for ground between the source and the listener, from a few samples of the height field, 20 dB a band at most. A distant fall is then a rumble for the reason a real one is. S114 read each again before pinning it ([the grounding page](../experiments/world-sound.md#read-again-by-s114)); refraction is left out. A clip voice keeps the distance laws above and, since S114, the mixer's one-pole low-pass by distance (`air_db_per_m_8k`, held at `_1k`'s fit; `SpatialParams` is the seam Steam Audio's terms arrive by, S40). Built: [world_sound](world_sound.md#distance-s114).

### Spatialization's place

Unchanged. Every world voice is a source with a spatial block and nothing is panned before the decode stage, so the world gains heights and surrounds the day VBAP or a binaural decoder lands and `world_sound` never learns the layout. Until then the stereo panner feeds the front pair, on a 7.1 endpoint too. The ears' wind is the one exception and by its nature: it is made at the head, so it is two 2D voices, hard left and right, each with its own seed, their levels set by the head's yaw against the wind.

### The mix, the device, and what a test does

- **Buses**: a `world` bus under `ambient`, so a game ducks the world with one gain. Levels are computed in dB SPL at the listener and mapped to full scale by one reference (`audio.world.reference_db`); the ears' wind has a gain of its own, since at its physical level it would bury the rest. The limiter stays off: a mix too hot is counted, as ever.
- **Who opens the device: the host.** engine-view takes `--audio auto|off|null|<device>`. `auto` opens the default device in a session with a window, whatever it records (S112: a benchmark in a window is the owner's session), and is `off` for `--offscreen`. engine-host opens none (it is a server); engine-editor is a later row.
- **`null`** runs the stage on the null backend with the host pulling 800 frames a tick (S111: 48,000 / the session's tick rate, the remainder carried; engine-view ticks 240 times a second, so 200), and `--audio-out <file.wav>` keeps them. **That is how a test and an agent listen**: the bytes' hash, and band levels read from the file, never a device. A test never opens a device; the opt-in device test stays the one it is.
- A missing device falls back to the null backend with one log line, as `Output` does today.

### Determinism: sound is presentation

`derived`, as this module is. **World state**: the wind (a closed form of the sky's clock), the placements, the flow solve, the sea, the walker's pose. **Presentation**: the fields, the voices, the seeds, the device. None of it is hashed, saved or replayed, and nothing flows back.

**Nothing a game module can observe depends on audio.** When a one-shot ends is a fact about when a sound card asked for a block, so a free voice is too: `sound_play` therefore answers `Ok` with **a handle the host counts**, never the mixer's slot, whether or not a voice sounded; `sound_stop` answers `Ok` for any handle issued; there is no call that asks whether a voice is sounding, how loud anything is, or what device there is. With `--audio off` the stage is mute and answers the same, so a session recorded with sound replays without it to the same state. Seeds are functions of a source's identity, so a null render of a scripted session is the same bytes on every run and toolchain. What an NPC hears is the simulation's (S55), not this.

### The game service

A scene gains **one block, `sound`** (`engine.scene.Sound`): `clips`, each a `name`, a `path` or a **`recipe`** (a tone, a chirp, a noise burst the store generates, so a fixture has a sound with no binary in the tree), `gain_db`, `min_m`, `max_m`; and the world's gains by kind (S111: not yet; S112 adds them with the world's first sources, additively). It is one block as `sky` is, not the list the header's comment says, and it is the root's twenty-first field: a recorded raise.

- **`sound_clip(name)`**, in `on_load`: the clip's index; `NotFound` refuses the load; `Unsupported` only in a build without the capability or on a scene with no block.
- **`sound_play(clip, position | k_sound_2d, gain)`**: a one-shot at a world position, in the mixer's frame at the listener's cell as [audio_system](audio_system.md#the-mixers-frame) does it, with the clip's declared distances. A bird's call from its perch is this call.
- **`sound_stop(voice)`**: the stop's 10 ms fade.
- **Added by a later row, at the ABI number free when it is built** (the live wind's service takes 13 on its branch): `sound_play_ex` and `sound_set` with a size-led `SoundParams` (loop, pitch, distances, priority, a position that moves), `sound_gain(bus, db)`, and `sound_clip_info` (a clip's length, a fact of the content, for a game that times itself by ticks).

## Not yet

In the order the direction note puts them, the decode stage's later implementations and the calibration that feeds them — each with the seam it plugs into:

1. **Ambisonics.** An encoder into an intermediate B-format bus and a decoder from it to the declared profile, heights included. Seam: a `Decoder` whose per-source step encodes into its own bus (its `state_floats`) and whose output step decodes the bus into the layout's channels, reading `speaker_direction()` for every row.
2. **VBAP** — vector-base amplitude panning per object over every speaker of the profile. Seam: a `Decoder` that reads `DecodeInput::spatial.direction` in full and the profile's azimuths and elevations; the stereo panner's front-pair rule is its degenerate case.
3. **Windows Spatial Sound** (`ISpatialAudioClient`): the platform's object renderer, for headphones through Windows Sonic, Dolby Atmos or DTS:X and for Atmos-capable receivers. Seam: a `Decoder` that emits each source as a platform object from `DecodeInput::source` and the listener basis instead of mixing into `out`, with 2D sources going to the bed; it needs a device path of its own beside `Output`, since the platform owns the render.
4. **Steam Audio.** Occlusion, transmission and air absorption against the RT scene geometry fill `SpatialParams::attenuation` (and the per-band terms it will grow); HRTF rendering over headphones is a `Decoder`, and the headphones profile becomes binaural with it.
5. **An object stream to an external renderer** — a sound card or an HDMI receiver that renders positional objects against its own room calibration: the direction note's second sentence, and the "fourth output" it names ([05 §5.11](../plan/05-simulation.md#511-integration-notes)). The platform's spatial path is the only such endpoint on a PC today; ADM, MPEG-H and IAMF have none. Seam: the decode stage already takes the source objects and the layout, so a stream encoder is one more `Decoder` that emits objects instead of channels, and the mixer never learns which.
6. **A calibration file**: measured speaker positions, per-channel gain, delay and EQ — the user's, read by the decode stage and nothing else, and an override of the shipped profile table's rows rather than a separate path. The table's gains and delays are not columns yet; they arrive with it, and so does the per-channel delay line that makes a delay mean something.

And the rest, by what they would change:

- **Vorbis** — stb_vorbis is in the checkout; the blocker is a fixture, and its record on malformed input (see "What miniaudio is asked to do").
- **A stream that keeps time through an underrun.** A stream that runs dry holds its place (a gap, not a skip; "Streaming" says why); a game that needs a streamed clip to stay in step with a clock — music a rhythm is played to — would want the skip, which is a seek past the frames the fill never decoded.
- **A resampled stream's exact length**: the decoder's pre-decode estimate can be a frame short ("Streaming"); counting it exactly means decoding the clip once at load, which the content build does (a `.clip` is at the mix rate).
- **Submixes and bus effects**: reverb sends, ducking, a per-bus filter. Buses are gains today and have no signal of their own.
- **Better interpolation** (cubic or windowed sinc) and anti-aliasing when a voice is pitched up; linear interpolation's error is measured in the pitch test and is inaudible for the small pitch variation effects use.
- **Headphone detection on Linux.** PulseAudio (and PipeWire through it) describes a sink's `device.form_factor` in its property list — `headphone`, `headset`, `hands-free`, `speaker`, `internal`, `hifi`, `tv`, `car` — and, since PulseAudio 14, the active port's type (`PA_DEVICE_PORT_TYPE_HEADPHONES`), which is what changes when headphones are plugged into a laptop's jack; ALSA offers jack detection as mixer controls (a `Headphone Jack` switch) and UCM's device names. miniaudio surfaces none of it, so reading it means the engine's own calls into the run-time-loaded libpulse; until then Linux is the setting.
- **The declared profile following a device change.** A mixer's layout is fixed when it is built: a headset that becomes the default mid-game plays the mix the running mixer declared, converted by the platform (a 7.1 mix onto a stereo headset is the platform's fold). Building a new mixer and moving the voices across is the way; nothing asks for it yet.
- **A device that appears on Linux after there was none**: Windows' watcher finds a new default after the output fell back to the null backend; on Linux nothing reports one (PulseAudio's `rerouted` needs a stream to move, and ALSA reports nothing), so the output stays on the null backend until it is opened again. Polling the device list would do it at the cost of opening every endpoint once a poll.
- **A wider phantom stage** for 2D music as a `ChannelMapping` (the direction note's "per user preference").
- **Capture** (microphones, voice chat).
- **`content.build` for clips.** engine-host's content build takes meshes; handing it the audio step as engine-content does is a few lines in engine-host and a field in the protocol's options.
- **A store that fills the cache.** The store reads `<ddc>/clips/` and never writes it; engine-view fills the cluster cache on a miss, and a store could write the `.clip` it has just decoded the same way, if loads ever want to be paid once per machine rather than once per build.
- **A compressed built clip.** A `.clip` is f32 in the mix format, as large as the clip resident; a lossless block format (or 16-bit where the source was) would halve it on disk at the cost of a conversion on load and in every fill.

## Why this shape, in one paragraph

The things a first audio version gets wrong are the ones that cannot be retrofitted: a mixer whose unit is a pre-panned pair cannot grow a speaker layout, a layout that is assumed cannot be read from a device, a panner spread through the mixer cannot be replaced by a platform renderer, and a mix that is not a function of its inputs cannot be tested or replayed. So v0 is small where it can be — linear interpolation, a stereo panner, a hard clip, gains for buses — and exact where it has to be: sources as objects, a declared profile, one decode stage behind a table, two wait-free queues, and a hash of the output that two compilers have to agree on.
