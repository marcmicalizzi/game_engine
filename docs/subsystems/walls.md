# walls (domain)

**Purpose.** Dry-stone walls along a line over the ground (roadmap G145; the solver-needs report's §3.9, the wall half). Two generators in one capability: the mesh generator `wall` solves one **module** — a stretch of wall a few metres long, every face stone, pinning, through-stone, cover stone and cope stone as geometry — from a recipe (`engine.walls.Module`, `<name>.wall.json`), and the placement generator `walls` reads a layer of lines (`engine.walls.Layer`, the placement entry's own parameters), plans every line once, and stands modules along it by **station**. It does not do fences, retaining walls or terraces (they need the ground's edit, W93's seam), field systems (where walls run is a village plan's), the stone's lithology from the formation, moss and lichen (the cover solver's), or a photographed stone kit (an asset set). The sources, every number chosen and where structure was traded for triangles: [dry-stone walls](../experiments/dry-stone-walls.md).

**Why this shape.** The question the brief put first is whether a placement generator can build a mesh when a tile activates. It cannot: `PlacementGeneratorDesc::meshes` is read once, when the scene is, and a `tile` call only places instances of what it named ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md#the-two-kinds)). So a wall reaches the renderer as **a small kit of solved modules placed by station**, never as a mesh a tile: 39 meshes at the default two variants (a stretch and a fallen stretch at five rising grade classes each, a head and a broken stretch at nine signed ones, and the far strip), resident whatever the world's size, each a merged mesh of every stone. A hundred metres of wall is 33 instances (one a module), against the proof of concept's 1,900 stone instances, each of which was a traced structure and a run of pairs. The other way — a merged mesh a tile — would need a tile to add a mesh to the resident scene, which no host does today; it would save the module's repetition (two variants a grade) and the overlap at its joints, and is the request under [Not yet](#not-yet).

**Owned data.** The kit's recipes it writes under the derived-data root (`<ddc>/walls/wall-<hash>.wall.json`, the cliffs' rule for where: the layer's `recipes`, else the host's `--ddc`, else the repository's `ddc/`), and per open entry its plan (`walls::Plan`): every line's vertices and stations, its slots, its far pieces and a tile index — bytes a module, so what scales with the world is the entries alone.

## Stations

A line is integer millimetres in the entry (`WallLine.points_mm`, x, z pairs; `closed` joins the last to the first). **A station is arc length from the line's start in whole centimetres**: each segment's length is one correctly rounded square root of an integer (a segment is at most 10 km, so its squared length is exact in an f64), rounded to the centimetre, and the stations are their running sum. Everything along the line is decided by station and the layer's seed, never by tile (`make_plan`, `src/stations.cpp`):

- **Runs**: the stretches between vertices and gateways. A gateway is a station in `gateways_cm` with `gateway_cm` clear (3 m by default) between two heads; a run shorter than 60 cm is left open.
- **Slots**: each run is cut into `round(length / module_cm)` modules of equal length (each 85 % to 118 % of the 3 m module), their ends the millimetres their stations interpolate to (`point_at`, one rounded division). At a vertex each module runs on past it by its half-width times the tangent of half the turn (to 120°), so the corner's outside is closed; inside, the two overlap.
- **Kinds**: the first module of a run that starts at a gateway or an open line's start is a **head**, the last before one a head turned end for end; the rest are stretches. The variant is a draw of (seed, line, start station).
- **The ruin** (`ruined`, 0 to 0.9): of a line's stretches (heads excluded), `round(ruined × count)` fall, exactly, in stretches of `gap_modules` from starts ranked by a draw of their station; a standing stretch beside a fallen one is **broken** toward it. A fallen stretch keeps its footing and a ragged course and has its stone lying at its foot on both sides.
- **Pieces**: up to four standing modules of a run under one far strip.
- **Tiles**: a slot or a piece is realised by the tile its middle's integer millimetres fall in (`tile_of`); `occupies` is a binary search of that index.

So the wall is continuous across every border and the same whichever tile comes first: two modules either side of a border meet at the same station's millimetres, and each is stood on the ground by itself.

## Standing on the ground

`stand_slot` asks the ground (`Ground::floor_mm`, what a building stands on) at the module's two ends and at both faces' feet (beyond the scarcement) at five places along it, all integer millimetres. The module's grade is the chord's; it takes the **grade class** nearest (0, 0.125 … 0.5 rising for a stretch and a fallen one, turned end for end on a falling grade; −0.5 … 0.5 for a head and a broken one, whose wall end decides which way they face) and is pitched from its class onto the chord by at most half a class (3.6°), its length stretched so its foot spans the chord. It is **sunk** by as much as the chord stands above the ground anywhere sampled beyond its footing's depth (10 cm), so the footing's bed is under the ground at every foot sampled. On a plane up to a grade of 1 in 2 along the wall and 1 in 5 across it, no foot is more than 5 mm above the ground (`stations_tests.cpp`); between samples on curved ground the gap is the ground's sag over 1.5 m, and two neighbours whose sinks differ meet with that difference in their cope's line.

**Courses level, the top following the ground.** Within a module every bed is level (TCV: "lay the stones on the true horizontal, whatever the slope"), the footing steps up the slope, and the cover and cope follow the ground: a module is solved at its class's grade, not pitched onto it. The pitch that remains is at most 3.6°, which the eye does not read as a raked course.

## The module

`solve_module` (`src/module.cpp`) lays a module as a waller does, in the module's frame (x along the wall from −L/2 to L/2, y up, its foot on y = grade × x):

- **The footing** first: the largest stones (15 to 19 cm, 32 to 60 cm long), set 10 cm below the ground and 6 cm proud of each face.
- **Sweeps** along each face, a **face that is not coursed** (G177; the sources and every number on [the grounding page](../experiments/dry-stone-walls.md#a-face-that-is-not-coursed)): the band's height is graded from 15 cm at the foot to 6 cm under the cover, and a stone is not the band's height: a walling stone is 0.85 to 1.15 of it, one in five a **thin stone** of half a band that the next sweep sets another on (two on one, a step of 50 to 75 mm at the foot, TCV's "75 mm or more"), and one in five below the top quarter a **jumper** through two bands (1.7 to 2.1, at most 28 cm), as the rule and not only where a stone ends against a step. Its **bed** lies on the highest point under it, or, where most of it lies on one sloping stone, along that stone's top (`bed_line`; never along the ground's grade, so the footing stays level and steps up the slope); its **top** is not level: it falls by up to 35 % of its height end to end and keeps at most half its bed's slope, so the tops wander and do not climb. About one standing stone in six of a band's height is a **lump**: its top rises to a ridge 25 to 45 % of its height above its higher end, its face a pentagon. The hollows under every bed, deeper than 15 mm, are packed with **pinnings** to the bed's line (a wedge-shaped hollow over a wedge, either side of a lump's ridge). A stone's face length is 1.5 to 4 times its height (a jumper's 1 to 2.2), its tail into the wall at least its face's length; its face is split with its arrises knocked back ([the stone](#the-stone)). A stone ends short of a rise in what is under it (a rise past half its height more than a 0.15 slope would give), and one that ends against a step is brought up to it (to 28 cm), its other end no more than half its height lower. A `Stone` carries its bed and top at both ends and a lump's ridge (`bed_at`, `top_at`, `is_lump` in `walls.h`), and the skyline the next sweep beds on follows them sample by sample.
- **Joints crossed**: an end is kept 4 cm or more off every joint below (moved past it, or short of it, or over the step), and a stone that starts after a gap starts off one too, as does one that starts where the last ended but on another level (G177: the last ended against a jumper's side and this one is laid on the jumper; started flush with it, the jumper's edge ran on as a seam, and the first G177 build left 2.4 % of joints through three courses until it did). The seam measure reads a stone's bed and the level below at the joint itself (`bed_at`, `top_at`), since a bed laid along a sloping top differs over the 4 cm either side by its slope; for level stones it is G145's measure. **G177's layout leaves 46 of 4,560 joints through three courses (1.01 %) and 19 through four (0.42 %)** over the same 36 modules, under the bounds below. **Seams crossed after the sweeps** (G145c, `cross_seams`): a face stone is laid in its sweep but drawn only once the face is whole (`draw_pending`), so a joint found running through three courses (the measure the test holds) is crossed by moving one of its joints 5 cm along its course, lengthening the stone whose band lies within its neighbour's and shortening the neighbour (to no less than 8 cm), kept only when the module's count of seams falls; what that leaves open above or below the lengthened stone is closed with the face. It crosses few: over the 36 modules the test solves it crossed 5 of 75, because most seams stand where a through-stone or a jumper (neither of which can move) is one of the pair, or the two stones' bands overlap rather than nest. What is left, 70 of 5,465 joints through three courses (1.28 %) and 34 through four (0.62 %), is where a course ends against a step too tall for one stone, beside a through-stone's packing or a footing stepping up a slope. `module_tests.cpp` holds it at what the solver reaches, over six seeds, three grades, a stretch and a head (G178: bounds 1.1 % and 0.5 %, from G145c's 1.4 % and 0.7 %), stated as such and not from a source; the solver's draws follow `k_wall_version` (G145b kept G145's draws with a constant of their own, on which its 2 % bound held and on the version's draws failed at 2.09 %; G145c removed it). A running joint is a fault TCV names ("even an expert waller must sometimes leave one uncrossed joint", never a seam of three), so closing the rest is under [Not yet](#not-yet). G145b's three attempts (deep hollows built up in courses, a closer where a course resumes past a joint, a too-tall step taken with two stones) raised the residue or changed nothing and were taken out.
- **Through-stones**: one row, as many as keep them under 0.80 m apart, half a spacing in from each end (0.75 m in a 3 m module, so a run stretched to 118 % keeps TCV's 1 m centres), each laid at its own height in 0.52 to 0.60 m once the faces there are within 14 cm of it, its ends moved off the joints below, proud of both faces by 3 to 10 cm.
- **The cover** across the full width on the line of the body's top (60 to 90 mm, 2 to 4 cm proud), and **the cope** as random upright coping (TCV, Types of coping): on edge, 40 to 160 mm thick along the wall and mostly thin, each 0.92 to 1.11 of the cope's 0.27 m with taller and shorter alternating seven times in ten and neighbours within 50 mm, vertical within 4° and leaning as its neighbour does, 2 to 8 mm apart, its top and its two sides bulging 3 to 8 mm (a split stone's, not a board's) and its ends drawn, since the gaps between cope stones show them; three in ten are wedges, narrower at the top by up to a fifth of their thickness, and one in fifteen leans 5 to 8° on the next, which is set past its top (G145c; tilted coping, where each stone holds down its neighbour, met in random coping; the shares are design values); at a head the first is a large square topstone. `face_tests.cpp` holds the heights, the thicknesses, the neighbours' difference and the spread (no tenth of the neighbours alike), so it does not read as books on a shelf. **A rough stone, not a slab** (G177): each corner of a cope stone's top stands at its own height, 0.86 to 1 of the stone's, and is drawn in toward its middle by up to a fifth of its width and of its thickness, so it stands on its thickest edge (TCV, Coping) and its top is a ridge of its own; its thickness is 40 to 160 mm by the 1.5th power of a draw (G145c's square left a row of thin slabs).
- **A head** is flush and vertical at its end, its courses alternating a **tie** across the whole wall with **runners** along it (TCV, Wall heads). **A broken stretch** stands for its first 30 % and falls in a ragged slope to its footing, its stone at its foot; **a fallen stretch** is its footing and a ragged course with its stone lying within 1.4 m of either face, most of it close.
- **The hearting** is a dark closed core between the faces' tails, so a joint shows shadow and not the field; at a head it stands 3 cm behind the end, behind its stones' knocked-back arrises.

## The stone

A face stone is a slab split along its bedding (the sources on [the grounding page](../experiments/dry-stone-walls.md#the-stones-own-shape)): its beds flat, its face the broken side, its outline its own. In a standing face it shows its face alone, which `face_poly` in `src/module.cpp` draws from the corners it was laid with (G177): a quadrilateral whose bed and top slope as they were laid, or a lump's pentagon. Where more of it shows (a fallen stretch, the footing, near a head's end) `body` makes it from its eight corners, its bed and top sloping the same way. The face it shows has a **face proper**, four points each drawn in from the two edges that meet at its corner independently, by a share of the face's dimension across that edge (`k_inset`: a walling stone 3 to 14 %, 3 to 40 mm, so a long stone's ends wear back further than its beds; a small face and a closing stone 6 to 18 %; a cover's edges and a fallen stone's top 4 to 12 %; 40 % of a walling stone's corners broken off (G177; G145c: 45 %), a cut 25 to 50 % of its shorter side back along each edge), so the face proper is a polygon of its own and not the rectangle the stone was laid as; it is **crowned** 0.5 to 2.5 mm off its corners at a point off its middle (four triangles sharing their vertices and smooth normals) or fanned flat (two). Round it a band of **worn arrises** runs out to the stone's own corners, which are set back 0.6 of the larger inset and 3 to 8 mm behind the face's plane (5 to 19 mm), so the band is steeper than 25° and reads dark where the face proper reads lit (eight flat triangles). A walling stone's face is 12 triangles and two more a broken corner (about 16 on average), a closing stone's 10, a pinning or a closing stone under 3 cm a plain chip of 2. In a standing face that face is all of the stone that is drawn: its beds, its tail and its ends lie on and against stone and in the hearting (a pinning's top and ends too), and a joint between two stones is still 4 mm at the stones' corners while the worn arrises either side widen it in the light to what the eye reads as the stone's outline; where the wall has fallen, on the footing (its top is the scarcement) and within 0.6 m of a head's end, the faces that can be seen are drawn too. A through-stone splits both its ends and draws them in along the wall by up to 6 % of its length a corner (its top edge worn down to 6 mm), a tie its faces and the head's end, a cover stone its two edges, a cope stone none (its top and sides bulge instead, four smooth triangles each), a fallen stone its top. `face_tests.cpp` measures it on the mesh: every face stone's face has 3 to 35 mm of relief, a share of its area tilted 25° or more off the face (the worn arrises, at least 3 %), and its face proper (what is within 25°) has its largest corner cut, from its own bounding rectangle, a quarter of its shorter side or more on average (G145c: 0.25; a design value).

## The face

Faces are **built to the line** (TCV, Placing the stones): a face stone's plane is on the batter's line at its middle within 2 mm, so only its own relief stands off it; at 1 in 4 and below no stone's high point is more than 12 mm proud and 98 % are within 8 mm, and at 1 in 2, where a level stone's ends stand off a line that climbs with the ground, 25 mm. A **pinning** is set to the line at its foot and its top, 4 mm behind it, and at a head's end it reaches the wall's middle as the runners do; a head's runners reach a centimetre past the middle, so the two faces' runners meet with no slot between them on a battered end. Joints are 0 to 8 mm (a 2 mm gap either side, corners within 2 mm along the wall). **What the sweeps leave open is closed** (`close_faces`): over each face a centimetre grid from the ground to the cover's bed, every cell covered by a stone of that face marked, and each open run of two or more cells, carried up while the cells above are open too (to 30 by 12 cm), becomes a closing stone set to the line, as a waller packs the last gaps. `face_tests.cpp` casts rays at standing modules' faces, square on a centimetre apart and slanting two centimetres apart, and at a head's end: none reaches the far side, square on the hearting shows in no hole 4 cm across, and 5 % of the rays meet it (the joints; a face of 25 × 10 cm stones with 5 mm joints is about 7 %). is a 128 × 128 palette the solver writes into the container (16 × 16 swatches of 8 × 8 mottled texels: 224 of dark, mid, blue-, brown-grey, weathered light and iron-stained slabs; the last two rows plain at the stones' own mean, which the mid form's and the outline's covers and copes take, but for the hearting's one dark swatch in the far corner), each stone taking one swatch, so no two neighbours are one grey.

## The generator

`walls` (`src/placement.cpp`) is `PlacementGeneratorDesc` whole: `open` reads `engine.walls.Layer` (an unknown field refused with its path), plans the lines and writes the kit's recipes where they go (a recipe already there is not written again); `meshes` names the kit in `kit_index` order; `tile` stands the slots whose middle falls in the tile, or in a far ring the pieces; `occupies` asks the tile index; `representation` is 1 from `far_ring` out and 0 inside it and outside a world; `expand` is every slot, in plan order, which is every tile's modules. A placement's `id` is its slot's (or piece's) number in the plan, its `tag` 1 for a fallen stretch (the ruins' rubble tag, so a host splits a pop the same way). Walls are structures at sites their entry names, so like the ruins they do not ask the exclusion ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md#exclusion)): a wall a stream crosses is drawn through it, and a crossing (a culvert, a gap) is a later row.

The near form is decimated as every mesh is and cut where its error reaches the mid form's (`build_cluster_lod_levels`; [geometry](geometry.md)), so the cut picks stones near, bands from about 20 m at the default threshold, and the outline beyond. A streamed world's rings from `far_ring` out (the third by default, past 8 tiles at the default rings) place the strips instead of the modules (`representation` 1). The sward's hand-off by distance (`Placement::handoff`) is not used: its band is the sward's own tunable (25 ± 4 m) and its thinning inside the band is the sward's parts', so a wall handed off there would switch from stones to an outline at 25 m; the DAG's levels hand over per cluster by projected error instead, and the ring hands the modules to the strips far out.

## Telling the other placements where it is

A module is a placement like any other, so a later entry reads it as an earlier footprint (`Context::earlier`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md#earlier-placements)): a circle of the module's bounds' radius in the ground's plane, about 1.6 m round its middle. Grass, litter and scatter after it in the scene's order keep clear of the wall, and the circles overlap at the joints (3 m apart), so nothing grows through it. What a **band-shaped footprint** would do better: the circle clears 1.6 m either side at a module's middle where the wall is 0.35 m half-wide, so the sward stands back from the wall instead of growing to its foot, and a fallen stretch's stone (to 1.4 m) is covered only near the middle. A footprint as a segment and a half-width would clear the wall's own band and grade the cover at its foot. It is the request below.

## Determinism

Every decision is integers (millimetres, centimetres) or draws of `hash_combine`; the solver's arithmetic is f64 sums, products, divisions and correctly rounded square roots, a sine and a cosine from `core/math/deterministic.h` for a fallen stone's turn, and no C library call in a decision. `determinism_tests.cpp` pins four modules' bytes (a stretch, a head, a broken and a fallen one: positions, normals, UVs, the three forms' indices and the palette's PNG) and a layer's placements 419 km out on curved ground. Output does not depend on the order tiles come in or the thread count (`placement_tests.cpp`). **Every draw is its own statement**: the first pins agreed on MSVC and GCC and not on Clang, because a call that took two draws among its arguments (a stone's swatch and its face's bulge, a through's thickness and its projection) drew them in the compiler's order of evaluation, right to left on the first two and left to right on Clang; the draws are now named locals in a fixed order.

## A photographed kit

What a kit of real stones would need, when one is made (the solver-needs report's §4): face stones in three course bands (15, 10 and 6 cm high) by lithology, through-stones (30 cm, the wall's width plus 20 cm), cover slabs, cope stones on edge (27 cm), pinnings, and a hearting texture; each with the face it shows and its bed's plane marked, so the solver can place a kit stone where it now lays a split block.

## Not yet

- **Running joints** (G178, not closed): 46 of 4,560 joints still run through three courses (1.01 %; G145c: 1.28 %) and 19 through four. Of the 46, 13 meet a through-stone or a tie, 4 lie at the module's ends, 3 beside a jumper, 2 beside a lump, 2 at the footing, and 21 are where a stone laid over a pack of pinnings ends within 3 cm of a joint two stones down, which an end's check (it reads the skyline just below) does not see. A check of each end and start against the joints two bands down before the stone is laid (the measure taken downward) was tried: 46 became 43 on these draws and 51 on the next version's, and a square-on hole of 4 cm opened where the slots it leaves were closed, so it was not kept; planning a band's joints over the whole run, with the pinning packs counted as the band they fill, is the next try. The slots the start rule leaves beside a jumper are closed by stacked closing stones, which read at two metres as a narrow vertical strip.
- **Relief in a normal map**: the near form is back to G145's triangles (see [Budgets](#budgets)); a face's fine relief (a split face's grain, a lichen's edge) would come cheaper from a normal atlas beside the colour palette (`assets::Material::normal_image`) than from triangles.
- **A mesh a tile**: the kit repeats two variants a grade class; a tile that builds its own merged wall would not repeat and would continue its courses across module joints (a host would have to take a mesh at activation).
- **Band footprints**: as above.
- **Collision**: walls are drawn, not walked against; the scene collision reads placements' meshes and would take them as any mesh.
- **The ground at the wall**: no wear at a gateway, no worn strip along the foot; the rubble of a fallen stretch lies on the module's foot plane, so on a slope across the wall it floats or sinks by the cross-grade times its distance (up to 0.3 m at 1 in 5).
- **Lithology**: one palette of a slabby metamorphic stone; reading the formation (`rocks.md`, "Geology") would choose the palette and the course heights by rock.
- **Moss and lichen** on the cope and the north face (the cover solver's).

## Testing

`engine_walls_tests` (`tools/dev.ps1 test -Filter walls`), about 16 s in `msvc-debug`:

- `stations_tests.cpp`: the plan is the same at two tile sizes and every station finds its slot; a gateway has a head either side and the ruin takes exactly the share asked on every line; a module's footing is under the ground on planes to 1 in 2 along and 1 in 5 across, pitched at most half a class; two modules either side of a tile's border meet within a millimetre.
- `face_tests.cpp` (G145b, G145c): a face stone is a split slab (relief and arrises measured on the mesh); its outline is its own shape (the face proper's largest corner cut a quarter of its shorter side or more on average, under a tenth in at most one stone in ten: design values); a closing stone or a pinning under 5 cm wide is no taller than twice its width and a centimetre (G145b had 2,054 such slivers over the case's modules); the near form keeps its budget (1,460 triangles and 3,500 vertices a metre over 100 m on 1 in 8); every triangle is wound the way its vertices' normals face (a stretch, a head, a broken and a fallen module, two seeds), so none is culled from the side it shows; the face is flush to the line; the cope's heights, thicknesses and neighbours inside TCV's ranges and spread; and no line of sight through a face or a head's end (rays against the mesh, hole and hearting share as on [the face](#the-face)); about 14 s in `msvc-debug`, of which the rays' case is 11 s (two seeds, two grades, a stretch and a head: some two hundred thousand rays against the mesh), the outline, sliver and budget cases under 0.5 s each.
- `module_tests.cpp`: joints crossed (the bounds above, over 36 modules, 0.8 s); height, batter, through-stones and cope inside the ranges pinned from the sources; courses graded, a head flush and tied, a fallen stretch low with its stone at both sides; the container through the mesh generator registry.
- `placement_tests.cpp`: through the registry, every tile in two orders against the whole read, the far ring's strips, the refusals; a hundred metres at every form.
- `determinism_tests.cpp`: the pins above, reproduced by Clang and GCC in the containers.
- `engine_walls_bench`: `walls.solve_module` (a kit entry's miss) and `walls.stand_tile` (a tile's activation).

**A lesson from building it (2026-11-10, MSVC).** The scaffold's empty sources were linked into the three hosts first; when the real sources replaced them, ninja relinked `engine-view` but the executable still carried none of the capability's code, so `engine_view.generators` failed with `placement:walls mesh:wall` missing and a scene naming `walls` was refused. Deleting the host executables and building again fixed it. A capability scaffolded and built empty before its first real source should do the same before trusting a host's run.
