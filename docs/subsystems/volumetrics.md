# volumetrics (systems)

**The participating medium's mid tier**: the dust the wind holds over the ground, the sand blowing off the dune crests, the far-field clouds, and fires' flames, glow and smoke ([Fire](#fire), R85), drawn by one frame pass at the renderer's `AfterResolve` registration point ([renderer](renderer.md#frame-passes)). It is the first built piece of plan 04's "one field rendered three ways" ([04 §4.2](../plan/04-renderer.md#42-frame-architecture), the 2026-09-24 direction note under step 7; [13 §13.1](../plan/13-reference-consumer-games.md#day-night-and-weather), "three media at three scales, one field"): **froxels and a ray-marched far-field layer, no brick solve** — the tier between the baseline's flipbooks and the RT tier's near-field solve. Roadmap R47 (the medium), W45 (the plumes), R83 (the far clouds), P7.

## Owned data

- `MediumDesc`: the scene's `medium` block (`engine.scene.Medium`, `schemas/volumetrics.schema`), which the renderer carries uninterpreted (`SceneData::medium`, `engine.scene.Scene` version 8) and `read_medium` reads.
- `MediumPass`: the frame pass — its pipelines (`shaders/medium.slang`), one host-visible `MediumParams` block per view per frame slot, and every view's froxel grid (device-local, four halves a cell) — and a `renderer::TerrainSampler` of the scene's terrain for the dust's ground and the dunes' field for the wind record.

## The shape

One frame pass, three compute passes under one timer zone, `medium`, after the resolve has written the frame's linear radiance and its sky:

1. **Fill** the view-aligned froxels: a 3D grid of 160 × 90 × 64 per view (`volumetrics.froxels.x`, `.y`, `.z`), each cell its extinction and the light it scatters towards the eye per metre, four halves (8 bytes) a cell, 7.4 MB a view. **The slices are exponential in the distance along the pixel's ray** from `volumetrics.froxels.near_m` (0.5 m) to `volumetrics.froxels.far_m` (1.5 km): slice k ends at near · (far/near)^(k/64), 12.6% deeper than it began, so a plume ten metres away is sliced as finely as the haze a kilometre away is coarsely. Radial rather than view depth, because a pixel's distance is what the medium integrates over and what the apply looks up.
2. **Integrate** each column front to back: a cell of extinction σ and source S over its length l adds S (1 − e^(−σl)) / σ through the transmittance in front of it — the energy-conserving form, exact for a constant cell — and then holds the light and the transmittance from the eye to its far boundary.
3. **Apply** per pixel: the pixel's distance from its visibility word's depth (the high 32 bits, reversed-Z, through the view's inverse view-projection), the column at that distance — bilinear across the four nearest columns, linear between slice boundaries — and `colour · T + L_in` into the linear image, where `L_in` was exposed by the frame's exposure (`gfx::SkyFrame`) when the fill wrote it, so the medium's light is exposed as the resolve exposed the surface's ([below](#exposed-units)). **Past the far boundary the dust is closed form**: its optical depth along the ray (below) and its unshadowed in-scatter, so a sky pixel or a dune 5 km out sees the whole column of haze, not 1.5 km of it; the horizon's haze is what the closed form gives a ray that never leaves the dust.

A pixel the pass leaves untouched (no medium in front of it) is not written. The renderer's encode quantizes the image afterwards ([renderer](renderer.md#frame-passes)).

## The scattering model

**Single scattering**, a source at each point of

    S(x, ω) = σ_s · [ E_sun(x) · (p(ω·sun) · V(x) + k_multiple / 4π) + L_ambient ]

- **The phase function** is Henyey–Greenstein: sand strongly forward, `k_dust_phase_g` = 0.75 (fine dust is 0.6–0.8 at visible wavelengths). Looking towards the sun through a storm is the bright forward lobe; looking away, the haze is the dust's colour lit from behind.
- **E_sun** is the sun's illuminance outside the air (`gfx::SkyParams::sun_illuminance`) through the air above the point (`sky_light_at`, the transmittance table at the point's altitude: the eye's altitude plus its height in the frame), so a medium at dusk is lit red and at night not at all; the stand-in's white intensity without a sky.
- **V** is the sun's visibility: the cascaded maps where the frame draws them (`--shadows csm`, or `auto` on a device without ray queries), one gather of the 2 × 2 texels round the point in the first cascade that holds it against its own depth plus two texels of bias (no receiver plane: a froxel is not a surface). Outside every cascade, and under traced shadows, the medium is unshadowed: shafts through a ray-traced frame are the RT tier's (R83).
- **k_multiple** (`k_dust_multiple`, 0.35) is the one constant term for light scattered more than once: an isotropic share of the unshadowed sun, which therefore reaches into a shadow. No multiple scattering beyond it.
- **L_ambient** is the sky's: the mean of the nine harmonics' ambient radiance facing up and facing down (`sky_ambient`), which for an isotropic share is the sky's mean radiance; the stand-in's 0.35 of its sky without one.
- **The albedo** `k_dust_albedo` (0.93, 0.83, 0.68): the sand's hue, lighter, since a fine grain scatters more of every wavelength than a dune's surface reflects.

## The dust haze

**A height fog of sand whose density at the ground is a function of the wind record at the sky's hour** (`dust_state`; the wind is `terrain::WindRecord::wind_at`, the one wind every consumer reads, [terrain](terrain.md#storms)): with s the hour's strength over the record's mean,

    σ_ground = (k_dust_clear + k_dust_haze · s² + [storm hour] k_dust_wall · (s / 2.5)²) · scale

- `k_dust_clear_per_m` = 3.9 × 10⁻⁵: a calm's floor, 100 km of visibility at the ground.
- `k_dust_haze_per_m` = 4 × 10⁻⁴ times s²: the day's wind's haze (the square: the sand a wind lifts over the speed it carries it at). The record's mean day is a faint haze of about 10 km.
- `k_dust_wall_per_m` = 0.08 at a storm's 2.5 times the mean, by its square: **a storm hour is a wall**, the hour's half-sine strength of the storm (terrain.md, "Storms") taking visibility to tens of metres at its peak. A storm hour's flag comes from the record, so a strong afternoon wind on the storm erg (s ≈ 2 at its diurnal peak) is a haze of a couple of kilometres and not a wall.
- **The scale height** is `height_m` (the block's, 15 m by default) × (0.2 + 0.8 s): 3 m in a calm, 15 m on the mean day, 33 m in a storm's peak — saltation stays within a few metres of the ground, suspension rises.
- **The ground** is the terrain under the eye (`TerrainSampler::height`, the provider the renderer drew it with), so the haze is measured from where the viewer stands and settles into the interdunes below a crest; without a terrain, the world's y = 0. A single reference height for the frame rather than the ground under each froxel: a dune 60 m above the eye's floor sits in thinner dust than it should and a basin below it in thicker, which a per-froxel ground (a height map round the eye) would fix.
- **`visibility_m`** is the distance at which a horizontal ray's transmittance at the eye's height falls to 2%: −ln 0.02 / σ(h_eye) = 3.912 / σ, capped at 1,000 km. It is the field's number, computed on the CPU from the same function the GPU fills the froxels with, handed to the renderer's `Stats::visibility_m` and engine-view's summary, because **gameplay reads visibility from the field, never from the picture** (plan 04). It counts the dust; the air's own extinction is the sky's.

| Hour | s | σ at 1.7 m | Scale height | `visibility_m` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A calm day's noon | 0 | 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ /m | 3 m | 177 km |
| The record's mean day | 1.0 | 3.9 × 10⁻⁴ /m | 15 m | 10.0 km |
| A storm's peak (2.5) | 2.5 | 0.078 /m | 33 m | 50 m |
| The storm erg's strongest storm hour | 2.99 | 0.113 /m | 39 m | 35 m |
| The erg's walk at 16:00 (a storm hour, s 1.42) | 1.42 | 0.0245 /m | 20 m | 159 m |

The closed form along a ray (`dust_optical_depth`, the same in the shader) integrates σ₀ e^(−h/H) above the dust's ground and σ₀ below it: for a ray of vertical component dy from the eye, σ₀ e^(g/H) H/dy (e^(−a dy/H) − e^(−b dy/H)) to where it crosses the ground going down, and σ₀ per metre after. The test holds it to a sum along the ray within 0.2%.

## The crest plumes

**Sparse sand blowing off the dune crests** (roadmap W45; the owner's first ask). For every dune tile whose plumes can reach within `k_plume_radius_m` (160 m) of the eye — the radius plus three decay lengths at the hour's wind (the hour of the sky's clock, so a dune time-lapse never rebuilds them every game hour; their brinks are that hour's, a lapse's few metres behind the drawn sand at most — renderer.md, "One world clock, and the day's length"), in the world's 32 m tiles — the pass reads the tile's crest lines and wind through **the terrain's own read-only query**, `terrain::tile_crests` (`domain/terrain/crests.h`, added for this: `evaluate_tile`'s crest lines and `TileWind`, bit for bit, without the tile's grid and into a scratch the caller keeps, so a frame allocates nothing; [terrain](terrain.md#what-the-effect-needs)). The tiles are the pass's own, evaluated round the eye from the base field at the hour's start (no lag, no overlay: neither moves a brink by more than the lag's centimetres), and **cached by tile and hour** (4,096 entries, least recently used out): the plumes are a function of the eye and the hour, the cache only spares the query. Each crest line of height 0.5 m or more and sharpness 0.3 or more sheds a plume from each of its four segments:

- **Its density** at the brink is `k_plume_peak_per_m` (0.03 /m) × strength × sharpness² × √(saltation / 0.55 m² a day): the tile's saltation flux (`TileWind::saltation_m2_per_day`, the hour's flux times the tile's share of loose sand) against the record's mean, so a calm sheds nothing, the mean day a faint streamer off a sharp brink, and a storm's hundredfold flux ten times as dense. The square root because the flux is the cube of the wind and what hangs in the air is the flux over the speed carrying it.
- **Its shape**: a ribbon leaving the brink downwind along the tile's wind (`plume_density`): nothing upwind of the brink; a **half-Gaussian** about a centre that leaves at the brink's height and rises 0.04 m a metre (`k_plume_lift`) — lifted a little above the slip face falling away below it — its spread 0.4 m at the brink growing 0.12 m a metre downwind (`k_plume_spread_*`), 0.35 of that below the centre (`k_plume_floor`: the sand hugs its floor), the column's density falling as the spread grows (the flux is conserved across it), **decaying over 2.5 s of the wind** (`k_plume_decay_s`: 20 m at the mean wind, 50 m in a storm) and falling off past the brink's ends with the same spread.
- **On the GPU** each plume segment is binned on the CPU into a 64 × 64 grid of 6 m cells centred on the eye (its brink and the brink three decay lengths downwind, widened by the spread there), at most 4,096 segments and 131,072 references a frame, and a froxel sums the plumes of its own cell (`plumes_at`). The scattering is the dust's: the same albedo and phase, since it is the same sand.

One sample per froxel: a plume a metre thick in a froxel 12 m deep (the slices at 100 m) is caught where the cell's centre falls in it, so far plumes alias; the near ones, where the slices are a metre or less, do not.

## The far-field clouds

**A procedural layer between the block's two altitudes, moved by the wind record** (roadmap R83, P7's far half): `cloud_density`, the same arithmetic on the CPU and in the shader. Value-noise fbm in Perlin's role (three octaves from 4,096 m cells, 0.625, 0.25, 0.125) remapped from [w − 1, 1] by inverted Worley cells of 2,048 m (the Perlin–Worley of the production cloud renderers: a cell's feature point keeps the billow), times a height profile — a rounded floor in the slab's lowest 8%, a top at 0.35 of the slab for `type` 0 rising to the whole slab for 1 — thresholded by the `coverage` (a share of the shape kept, not of the sky: measured on a grid, 0.25 covers 6% of the mid-layer, 0.5 84%), eroded at the edges by Worley cells of 512 m, at 0.04 /m in a core (`k_cloud_extinction_per_m`). Every lattice wraps at **131,072 m** on x and z, so the CPU hands the GPU the noise coordinate at the eye already reduced to the period in f64 (ADR-0053) — the eye's world position less the drift — and a float holds it to 8 mm.

**The drift** (`CloudDrift`) is closed form like the record's own flux integral: the wind aloft (`wind_at`'s direction, its strength × 8 m/s × `k_cloud_speed_factor`, 2) summed hour by hour over the record's eight-year period once when the pass is made (70,080 hours, 1.1 MB of f64), so the offset at t is the periods' whole sums, the prefix to t's hour and t's hour's velocity times the time into it: continuous (a step across an hour's start is no more than the seconds of wind either side), the clouds moving with the day's wind and the storm's, a function of the seed and the time with no history. **t is the world clock** (`FramePassContext::world_time_s`, since 2026-10-20, roadmap R88; [renderer](renderer.md#one-world-clock-and-the-days-length)): the clouds move at the world's rate, never the day's, and stand still at a world rate of 0. Because the drift is the closed-form integral of the record from the world's start, the hours whose winds it sums are the world's and not the sky's: the direction a cloud drifts in follows the record's day counted from the world's start, where the dust, the plumes and the smoke's bend take the hour of the sky. Making the drift follow the sky's hour while moving by world time would need a history (a sum of velocity × world step), which this pass has none of; it is a row for later if the two ever disagree visibly. Without a wind record, a steady wind aloft of 16 m/s towards +x.

**The march**, at **a quarter of the view's pixels a side** (`k_cloud_scale`): each texel along its own pixel's ray from the eye's altitude through the slab, 48 samples (no further than 60 km along a grazing ray), offset by the texel's **interleaved gradient noise** (Jimenez 2014; R112) — a fixed pattern, no frame in it, whose neighbours in any 3 × 3 block cover the interval nearly evenly — single scattering with a **two-lobe Henyey–Greenstein** (0.7 of g 0.8 forward, 0.3 of g −0.3 back), the sun through the air at the sample's altitude and through the cloud above it (four samples towards the sun at 120, 240, 480, 960 m of the coarse shape), **one octave of multiple scattering** (`k_cloud_multiple`, 0.3 at a quarter of that optical depth, isotropic), and the sky's ambient; then the air between the eye and the cloud from the sky's aerial table at the cloud's transmittance-weighted mean distance — what it takes out of the cloud's light, and what it scatters in where the cloud hides the sky behind it. **The upsample is a cubic B-spline over the 4 × 4 texels round the pixel, and depth-free** (R112): it filters the march's per-texel sampling error over its neighbours as it upsamples. **Why**: the owner's "very static-y" far clouds were that error — along a grazing ray the 48 samples are a kilometre apart against the detail's 512 m cells, so a texel's estimate depends on its offset, and a white hash of the texel with a bilinear upsample drew each as a 4 × 4-pixel speck the clouds then drifted through; 384 samples took it away, and so, nearly, do the gradient noise and the B-spline (the high-passed RMS against 384 samples 0.437 → 0.345 codes at the horizon, 0.212 → 0.145 in the high clouds; [the measurement](../experiments/campfire-flame.md#the-clouds-the-cause-and-the-fix)). No texel holds a surface's depth, because every texel is marched along its own direction whatever the pixels under it hold, and the layer is composited into **sky pixels only** (`colour · T + L`, L stored exposed, before the froxels, which stand in front of it). A cloud in front of a surface — the eye above the cloud base, or a mountain behind a low cloud — is not drawn: the RT tier's, R83.

**The sun's light below** is darkened by the clouds' transmittance: one march of eight samples of the coarse shape towards the sun for each texel of a 128 × 128 map over the froxels' reach round the eye (23 m texels at the default far distance), on the dust's ground plane; a froxel reads it bilinearly where the sun's ray through it crosses that plane, as one more factor of its shadow. **The clouds' shadow on the ground** — the same map multiplying the sun in the resolve — is not drawn: it needs the resolve, which this change does not edit (R83).

### Exposed units

**The march's texels and the froxels hold exposed light**, the radiance times the frame's exposure (`frame_exposure`: the sky's `SkyFrame::exposure`, one per frame and every view's, or the stand-in's), and the apply adds them as they are; the transmittance and the extinction beside them are unchanged. Both are four halves (8 bytes) a texel or a cell, and a half is the wrong store for radiance in sun units: its smallest normal number is 6.1 × 10⁻⁵ and below it the denormals step by 6 × 10⁻⁸ whatever the value. **A full moon's cloud is about 10⁻⁷ of a sun unit** (the moon's light is 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ of the sun's), a few denormal steps, and each channel stood on its own staircase; the exposure, four and a half stops above the day's, then scaled the steps into the picture. That was the owner's night (2026-10-19): the far layer drawn as concentric bands of grey stepping through pink, yellow and green while the sky beside it was smooth. The other picture, a sunrise's and a sunset's sunlit clouds as flat red, yellow and green regions with hard edges, has the same cause where a channel of the reddened sun is that small, and would have the half's other end where a value passed 65,504 and became infinity. Exposed, the light sits where a display wants it, 0 to a few hundred, with a half's ten bits of mantissa at every level — what the renderer's own linear image does for the frame's colour ([renderer](renderer.md#frame-passes)). **`pack_halves` clamps to the largest finite half and writes a NaN as 0: a guard, not a tone curve**, so a stray overflow can never again become a region of one saturated hue; the shoulder is the encode's. The clouds' shadow map is a float a texel and the transmittance only, and was never affected.

## Fire

**A campfire the game can place, lit and smoking** (2026-10-19, roadmap R85; the owner's ask for the proof-of-concept game: a light at night the player sees by, and fire as a thing that exists). The block is the scene's (`engine.scene.Fire`: a scene's `fires`, or a prop's `fire` that every placed instance of the prop carries), and a fire is split between two owners: **its light is the renderer's** — a point light at the flame's centre in the frame's light array, flickering by the world's clock and the seed, shadowed by a ray under `--shadows rt` and by nothing under `csm` ([renderer](renderer.md#fires)) — and **its flame, the light it scatters and its smoke are this module's**, drawn from the frame's fires the renderer hands every frame pass (`FramePassContext::fires`, `renderer::FrameFire`). Why not the medium alone: the froxels were most of what a fire needs — they scatter the sun's and the sky's light through dust and plumes — but they had no emission and no local light, and a froxel a metre deep at eight metres is coarser than the flame. Why not the light alone: a light with no flame is a lamp. The volumetrics' answer for each piece:

- **The flame** (`flame_density`, `flame_emission` in `medium.slang`): an emissive, weakly absorbing volume over the base radius r and height h — an envelope narrow at the very base, widest a fifth of the way up and tapering to the top (`(0.75 + 0.25 smoothstep(0, 0.2, h')) (1.12 − h')^0.8` of r), its axis swaying with height by a third octave of noise, carved by two octaves of value noise in cells of 0.45 r rising through it at 1.4 m/s (`k_flame_rise_m_s`): little carving at the base, tongues higher up. The noise's lattice wraps every 64 cells on y and the CPU hands its rise already reduced to that period in f64 (`medium_fire`), so the flame is a function of the seed and the clock, smooth years in. **Its emission is tied to its light**: the light's intensity over the flame's projected area (0.6 of 2r × h, `k_flame_fill`) is a luminance, over its mean path (0.6 of 2r) an emission per metre, of which the flame carries `k_flame_brightness` (0.3) at its densest — chosen by eye on the campfire's captures, since at the whole of it every flame was one saturated blob at any exposure that showed the sand — in the blackbody colour of the block's temperature (`renderer::blackbody_rgb`), as the density's square so the core outshines the tongues' edges, hotter at the base and redder where it is thin and towards the tips. It absorbs 0.8 /m at density 1 (`k_flame_absorption_per_m`): a flame is mostly clear.
- **The near flame**: a fire whose base is within `k_fire_near_m` (8 m) and its radius of the eye is **marched per pixel in the apply** — the same kind of pass the far clouds have, but at full resolution over the few pixels whose ray crosses the flame's bounds (a vertical cylinder 1.6 r wide from just under its base to 1.3 h; `flame_chord`), 40 steps (`k_flame_steps`) offset by a hash of the pixel, a fixed pattern — composited over the surface and under the froxels in front of it. Past 8 m the flame is the froxels' (`fire_sources`), where a slice is a metre deep and the flame a few pixels. **The volume, not flame cards**, because it is the honest answer here: the flame is the same field at every distance, lit and seen through the same medium, determined by the clock alone, and the cards would have needed the resolve to draw an emissive instance it has no notion of. The switch at 8 m is a step: a fire crossing it changes from its marched flame to its froxel one in a frame, which a blend over a metre would hide (not done).
- **Its light scattered** (`fire_light`): every fire's light at a froxel — the resolve's windowed inverse square to its reach, never closer than the flame's radius, the dust's Henyey–Greenstein phase towards the eye — times the froxel's scattering (the dust's, the plumes' and the smoke's), so the haze and the smoke round a fire glow orange at night. **Unshadowed**: the in-scatter is not occluded by the stones or the ground (R85's remainder).
- **The smoke** (`smoke_density`, the same arithmetic in `volumetrics.cpp` for the tests): a point source beside the crest ribbon — a column leaving the flame's top whose centre at a rise s has drifted `wind · s / rise` downwind, the wind the record's hour wind (`wind_now`) at half its speed (`k_smoke_wind`: the wind at a fire's height under the log profile's 10 m), Gaussian across with a radius of 0.6 r at the top (`k_smoke_start`) growing by `spread` a metre of its path, the column's density falling as that radius grows and by e over 25 m of path (`k_smoke_fade_m`), faded in over the flame's top half; its extinction at the top is `k_smoke_extinction_per_m` (0.6 /m) times the block's density, scattering with the dust's phase at an albedo of 0.42 (`k_smoke_albedo`), lit by the sun or the moon, the sky and the fires.

**Every stored radiance is exposed** (the far-cloud banding fix's rule, applied to the fire's terms the same way): the flame's emission and the fires' in-scatter are added to the froxel's light after the frame's exposure multiplies them, and `pack_halves` guards the half's range; the near flame is composited in floats and exposed before it is added. **A scene with no fire draws byte-identical**: the fill and the apply test the fire bit and the fire count before they touch anything, and the fires' block is made only when the pass draws fires (`MediumOptions::fires`, on by default; `--medium-debug fire` draws the fires alone). `MediumParams` keeps its 512 bytes: the fires' address, count and near count and the smoke's albedo took four of its five spare vectors. `MediumFire` is 128 bytes, `k_max_fires` (64) a slot, host-visible.

**What it costs** and **what the captures show**: below ([What it costs](#what-it-costs), "Fire"). **Determinism**: the flame's noise, the smoke and the light are functions of the seed, the eye, the wind record and the clocks — the flame's noise and the smoke's rise on the world clock, the wind that bends them at the sky's hour (renderer.md, "One world clock, and the day's length"); the same frame is the same bytes, another world time another flame (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`).

## What it costs

Measured on the RTX 5090 over the erg's walk path (120 frames, three repeats, 1600 × 900, `--shadows csm`, 08:00, the scene at a 1,025-sample grid with the committed medium's clouds, `msvc-debug` — the passes are the same SPIR-V in any build): **the medium's passes and the encode after them 0.284 ms a frame** (`gpu_ms` of the summary's `medium` block, the fill, integrate, clouds and apply plus the renderer's encode), and the GPU frame's median **0.609 ms against 0.291 ms with `--no-medium`**. `machine_state`: the medium's run was not quiet (other processes 12–25% of the CPU at its start and end, the GPU 6.4 of 32.6 GB in use), the bare run quiet (8%); the numbers are upper bounds. The surround (11520 × 2160) was not measured; the froxel passes are fixed per view (three views, three grids) and the apply and the cloud march grow with the pixels, so it is expected near three times the froxels' share plus nine times the apply's. The linear image adds 8 bytes a pixel and each grid 7.4 MB. **Storing exposed** (2026-10-19) moved one multiply from the apply into the march and the fill and cost nothing measurable: the same run at a 1,025-sample erg, 0.316 ms before and 0.303 and 0.314 ms in two runs after, the GPU frame's median 0.609 against 0.616 and 0.619 (not quiet: other processes 18–36% of the CPU).

**What the captures show** (the erg's walk path, frame 0, 960 × 540, at the scene's day, the debug build): at 16:00, a storm hour of strength 1.42, the dust is a wall — the far dunes gone, the near ripples fading into a tan haze within a hundred metres, the sun's side a bright forward-scattered glow (`visibility_m` 159); the same frame with `--medium-debug plumes` shows sparse streamers of sand blowing off the crests in the middle distance and nothing else; with `--medium-debug clouds` broken cumulus over the dunes, lit white on their sunward side and grey beneath, softened by the air towards the horizon; at 08:00 (strength 1.16, no storm) the whole medium is a sky of scattered heaped cloud over a faint haze that softens the far dunes (`visibility_m` 7.5 km).

**Fire** ([Fire](#fire)): measured on the RTX 5090 over the campfire's camera path (1,200 frames, two repeats, **1920 × 1080**, `--shadows rt`, `msvc-debug`, `--wait-quiet 180`) with no fire, the scene's one fire, and four fires 2.5 m apart round the origin (scratch copies of `content/test-scenes/campfire/`): **the medium 0.195, 0.269 and 0.410 ms a frame** (the summary's `medium.gpu_ms`: the fill, integrate, near flames, apply and encode) — 0.07 ms for the first fire and about 0.05 for each more, nearly all of it the per-pixel march over the near flames' few thousand pixels and the fill's loop over the fires; **the resolve 0.136, 0.202 and 0.463 ms** (the median), the lights' traced shadows, a ray per pixel per fire within its 71 m reach; **the GPU frame's median 0.562, 0.706 and 1.081 ms** (p95 0.708, 0.838, 1.531). `machine_state`: none of the three was quiet — the run without a fire waited out its 180 s at 36–42% of the CPU in other processes (two agents building), the one-fire and four-fire runs at 13–19%, the GPU 0–24% at their ends but 73% at the four-fire run's end; the numbers are upper bounds.

**What the campfire's captures show** (its camera path's markers, 960 × 540, `--shadows rt`, the debug build): from six metres east, a flame of tongues rising out of two logs inside a ring of small stones, yellow at its core and orange at its edges and its tips, the sand round it lit orange and falling to the moonlit dark within five or six metres, and a broad orange glow in the dust round it that fills the picture's middle; from the west the same, the smoke a faint column leaning off downwind above the flame (the hour's wind is strong — 11 m/s at the record, half of it at the fire — so the column lies over within a metre or two of rise and is thin by the time it leaves the light); from two and a half metres, the near flame marched per pixel, its tongues and the colour running from the base's yellow to the tips' red-orange, and **the stones' and the logs' shadows thrown long across the sand away from the fire** by the light's traced shadows, each stone lit on its face to the fire. Under `--shadows csm` the fire casts no shadow (not captured: the maps shadow the key light alone, [renderer](renderer.md#fires)).

## The scenes

**No committed scene names a medium yet**, though the brief asked the three desert scenes to: the block (`{"clouds": {"coverage": 0.3, "type": 0.5, "seed": 7}}`, the dust and plumes at their defaults) was added and taken out again in this change, because three things have to come with it. engine-host's `render.*` registers no medium, so engine_cli's `render: the dunes three years on, as engine-view draws them, byte for byte` fails once engine-view draws the erg's medium and the protocol does not; the endless desert's `scattered.json` and `scattered-bounded.json` have to carry every key of its `scene.json` (`scatter_scene_tests.cpp`); and `engine_view.frame_loop` found the dust's ground allocating every frame (17 engine allocations a frame on the erg's walk: the dune provider's height query), now fixed by asking it only for the corners of the 32 m lattice round the eye not yet seen and interpolating between them — the endless desert's second flight also allocated 13 more a frame, not traced to their source. Scratch copies of the erg with the block are what [What it costs](#what-it-costs) and the captures used.

## Determinism

**No history.** Every source is a function of the frame's state — the block, the wind record at the sky's hour, the world clock, the eye, the camera, the sun — and of no previous frame: no temporal reprojection, no accumulation, no jittered sample sequence over frames. The same frame is the same bytes, in any renderer and any number of times (`volumetrics_gpu_tests.cpp`). Not part of the simulation's replay: the medium is drawn, and the one number gameplay reads, `visibility_m`, is the CPU's function of the record and the eye.

## LOD policy

The plan's three tiers of the one field (04 §4.2): **baseline** — flipbook particles and a height fog, no froxels; **mid** — froxels and a ray-marched far-field layer (**this module**); **RT** — the near-field brick solve with the froxels around it and the far layer ray traced. Within the mid tier the grid is the tunables' and does not change with distance (the exponential slices are the distance LOD), and the dust past the grid is closed form. A view whose region the renderer resamples (a Panini view) is skipped: its region is not its picture.

## Capability contract (ADR-0027)

This is a capability: it was added without editing `core/`, `foundation/`, the render graph, the scheduler, or another capability, and it can be removed from the build the same way. The renderer gained a registration point for it (`frame_pass.h`) and carries the scene's block uninterpreted; it names nothing of this module.
