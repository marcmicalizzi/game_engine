# scene_gen (domain)

`Ground::masks(out, error)` forwards `GroundProvider::masks` through a borrowed callback, so
placement generators see the same cooked W72 level as tile sources. An invalid mask view means
none, while false propagates a cook failure. The callback shares the height view's lifetime.
The flora capability registers `sward`, a bounded patch lattice keyed by mask palette weight;
its inner ring places each patch as its near form and its card, named for the renderer's hand-off
by distance (`Placement::handoff`, `k_handoff_near`/`k_handoff_far`; G136), its next ring the card
alone, and it emits no placements past that. See [flora](flora.md#grass-sward-g90). Its entry (`engine.flora.SwardPlacements`, v2 since G137)
grew a `form` per family, a `stature` block (the rule that picks a cell's form from the mask's wetness, a
stream's nearness through `riparian_factor_mm`, the slope, a shade layer of the paint and a value noise),
and the meadow's forbs (`forbs`, `forb_share`, `forb_tile_cap`): herb meshes the lattice interleaves in
the nearest ring, after the hand-off's pair and with no role, excluded as the patches are; an entry naming none of them places what it did, to the
bit ([flora](flora.md#growth-forms-by-site-g137)).

**Purpose.** The registration point a scene generator attaches through ([ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md), [02 §2.8](../plan/02-architecture.md#28-adding-a-capability)): the two kinds a generator is — a **ground provider** and a **placement generator** — and the one table, `GeneratorRegistry::global()`, the renderer's scene reader and the world ring look a generator up in by the name a scene gives it. A capability registers a constant-initialized descriptor from its own source with a static `Registrar`; a host links the capabilities its configuration has; nothing below the host names one. A scene naming a generator the executable does not carry is refused with a sentence that names it and says what the executable has (`unknown_ground`, `unknown_placement`). A plain module, not a capability: every executable that reads a scene has at least the renderer's own ground in it.

**Why it exists.** Three generators expand content the renderer draws and the world streams — the ruins, the dunes and Island City — and before this module each reached the reader and the ring by its own route: the ruins and the dunes through an `#if` in the renderer and in the world and a dependency declared only where the capability was configured ([ADR-0037](../adr/0037-scene-reader-links-ruins-where-configured.md), the one exception to [ADR-0027](../adr/0027-additive-capabilities.md) decision 1, taken a second time), the city by no route at all but a fragment written to disk. Each new generator would have added an `#if`, a conditional dependency and a consumer file in two modules that are not capabilities. What the three have in common, seen from the reader and the ring, is what this module states: a generator is a pure function of the scene's parameters, the world seed and a tile, and its output is either **ground** (a height, a floor and a grid of itself, a function of position and time) or **placements** (instances of a few meshes, with tags).

**The fixed features.** A terrain entry's ridges and basins (`engine.scene.Terrain.ridges`, `basins`) are the scene's, added by every ground the same way — the waves multiply their dunes down over them and add them, the dunes thin their sand over them and add them — so their arithmetic is here, beside the entry's type, in one copy (`terrain_features.h`: `terrain_features`, `ridge_weight`, `basin_weight`). It was the renderer's until the dunes moved into the terrain capability, and it moved expression for expression.

**Holes** (`engine.scene.Terrain.holes`, W87): circles and polygons where the ground has no surface, part of the entry like the ridges and basins and compiled once into `TerrainHoles` (`include/domain/scene_gen/terrain_holes.h`), which the renderer's meshes and the collision both ask: a cell of any lattice is a hole when its four corners lie strictly inside one ([terrain](terrain.md#holes)).

## Owned data

The table of descriptors: `GroundProviderDesc` and `PlacementGeneratorDesc`, by name, in two lists. It owns the descriptors' *registration*, not the descriptors — each is a `constexpr` object in its capability's own source — and holds no state of any generator: a generator's state is what its `make` or `open` allocated, owned by the handle the caller holds (`GroundProvider`, a placement generator's `void*` it closes).

## The two kinds

**A ground provider** (`GroundProviderDesc { name, make, flags }`) is made from a scene's terrain entry (`engine.scene.Terrain`, as parsed) into a `GroundProvider`: a table of plain functions (`GroundOps`) over the state `make` allocated, which answers:

- `height(x, z)`: the surface at the ground's own time, metres; thread-safe.
- `floor(x, z)`: what a building stands on — the ground that does not move while the surface does (the dunes migrate over the interdune floor and bury what stands there; with the waves the two are the same). Null: the surface.
- `grid(lattice, window)`: a window of heights on a `Lattice` at its own time — the scene's grid, computed exactly as the renderer's mesh computes it, or the world's grid at a spacing in millimetres — what a mesh is built from. Null: `height` at each point, which is the same heights to the bit.
- `evaluate(t, lattice, window, blocks)`: the same window at another game time, in 64 × 64 blocks a caller spreads over as many jobs as it likes: the time-lapse's re-evaluation. Null for a ground with no time; `moves()` says which.
- `travel_m(from, to)`: how far its fastest feature travels between two times, which a moving ground's cadence is timed by.
- `wind(t)`: the wind the ground's surface detail lies across at game time t — the direction the sand moves over (x, z), a unit vector, continuous in time — which the renderer lays the sand's ripples across ([renderer](renderer.md#the-sand-close-up)) without linking the provider. The dunes answer their ripple term's rule, the day's wind turned in from yesterday's over two hours ([terrain](terrain.md#what-the-effect-needs)); the waves their one prevailing wind. Null: the ground says none, and the renderer lays them along +x. It was added to the table on 2026-09-29, after `travel_m`, so a descriptor that names neither is the one it was.
  *Design, not built*: the ripples' exposure is a function of the normal and the wind and cannot see ground a feature shelters — the level floor downwind of a brink, a ruin's lee. A provider that knows its shelter would answer it as a map channel beside the sand share, and the renderer multiply the exposure by it ([renderer](renderer.md#the-sand-close-up), "What the exposure cannot see").
- `transport(t)`: what moves the ripples at game time t — the sand the ground's wind has carried across a metre of width since its epoch, m² (a path length), and the wind's strength over its record's mean. The renderer turns the first into the ripples' travel and flattens them by the second ([renderer](renderer.md#the-sand-close-up), "Ripples that move"); the dunes answer from the wind record's integral. Null for a ground with none, whose ripples stand.
- `make_rings(extent, spacing)`: the rings round a camera (`GroundRings`, over `GroundRingsOps`: the layout rule, the re-centre rule and the chunks' cluster DAGs), when the ground has them. **A chunk's DAG is in its corner's frame** (`RingChunkRef`, 2026-10-07; ADR-0053): positions metres from the chunk's corner `(i, j) · chunk_mm` and UVs measured from it in the scale of the terrain's UV frame, so the DAG does not depend on where the chunk stands and the caller puts the corner back ([renderer](renderer.md#the-grounds-tiles-are-placed-at-their-corners)).
- `open_tiles(records, tile)`: its tiles in a world ring (`GroundTiles`, over `GroundTilesOps`), their per-tile records kept wherever the world keeps them (`TileRecords`), when it has any.

- `surface_mm(x_mm, z_mm)`, `floor_mm(x_mm, z_mm)`: the surface at its own time and the floor at a point given in whole millimetres from the world's origin — what a placement generator's ground is answered through ([Placements far from the origin](#placements-far-from-the-origin)). Null: the provider's other entries stand in.

`GroundProviderDesc::flags` (`k_ground_moves`, `k_ground_rings`, `k_ground_tiles`, and since 2026-10-07 `k_ground_millimetres`) say what every ground a provider makes can do, so a host can decide a setting before it has a scene's sampler. `k_ground_millimetres` says the ground's heights are right wherever a cell reaches: it answers `height_mm`, `surface_mm` and `floor_mm`, and its grids and re-evaluations sample it at the lattice's millimetres, never at a float32 metre of the world; the renderer draws the world's tiles only from a ground that says so ([Far from the origin](#far-from-the-origin)). `GroundProvider::view()` is the `Ground` a placement generator is handed: the surface and the floor as a plain function of whole millimetres and a context.

### Sky providers

**A third kind, added 2026-09-30** ([ADR-0048](../adr/0048-the-sky-is-a-providers-model-drawn-by-the-renderer.md), `include/domain/scene_gen/sky.h`): a **sky provider** (`SkyProviderDesc { name, make }`) is made from a scene's sky entry (`engine.scene.Sky`, as parsed; its `provider` names it, and an empty one means `k_default_sky`, "earth") into a `SkyProvider` over `SkyOps`:

- `state(time_s, SkyState&)`: where the sun and the moon are at a game time — world directions, their illuminances outside the air (the sun's in units of its mean, the moon's in the sun's), angular radii, the moon's albedo and lit fraction — the celestial frame's world axes the stars turn with, and the calendar (the day of the year counted on from the scene's, and the local hour) for a summary. A closed function of the time, thread-safe.
- `atmosphere()`: the air, the same at every time (`Atmosphere`: the planet's radii, Rayleigh, Mie and ozone coefficients and heights, the night's own glow), kilometres and inverse kilometres.
- `stars()`: the star table, brightest first (`Star`: a celestial direction, a visual magnitude, a colour of luminance 1); empty for a sky that draws none.

**Why the sky is a provider and not the renderer's.** What a sky *is* — an atmosphere's coefficients, an ephemeris on a calendar at a latitude, a table of stars — is content a studio with another planet, two moons or a real catalogue replaces, and removable with the capability that registers it (`domain/sky` registers "earth", [sky](sky.md)). Drawing pixels from it is the renderer's and `domain/gfx`'s, which need nothing from a provider but these numbers ([renderer](renderer.md#the-sky)). **The provider is a function of game time alone**, the same seconds since the world's midnight epoch the ground's `wind(t)` reads, which is what ties the sun's hour, the wind's day and the moon's month to one clock ([renderer](renderer.md#one-world-clock-and-the-days-length)). The scene reader makes the provider once to check the entry — a name the executable does not carry is refused with `unknown_sky`'s sentence, an entry the provider cannot draw with the provider's own — and the renderer makes it again for the frames. `GeneratorRegistry::find_sky`, `sky_names`, `unknown_sky`, and a third `Registrar` constructor, as for the other two kinds.

**A placement generator** (`PlacementGeneratorDesc`) runs one scene entry — its parameters as JSON (`engine.scene.PlacementEntry.params`, or a legacy field's object) — through `open` (read what the entry names and check it, once), then `expand` (the whole entry, a scene read whole) or `meshes` and `tile` (a streamed scene: the meshes it holds resident, and one tile at a time in the tile's ring), and `close`. Its output is `Placements`: instances of named meshes (a path the reader resolves through the derived-data cache like any scene mesh, a name, a content hash), each with a world position (a `WorldPos`), a rotation and a scale, and a tag, and a count of the things made. `tile` must agree with `expand`: a tile's placements are the whole expansion restricted to the tile. `occupies(tile)` and `representation(ring)` let a streaming host skip what a tile does not need: a tile with nothing on it, a ring change that draws the same.

## The tile source

**The seam between the ground and whoever draws or walks on it** ([ADR-0050](../adr/0050-the-ground-is-drawn-from-the-worlds-tiles.md), proposed; `tile_source.h`). A tile source answers one question: **the heights of a window of the world's lattice at a spacing** (`ring_lattice(spacing_mm)`, counted from the world's origin in millimetres, so a tile's window is the same integers whoever asks) **at a game time**, in the 64 × 64 blocks every grid here is cut into; and, for a source that moves, how far its fastest feature travels between two times, which is what a time-lapse times its fields by. It says nothing about how the heights were made — no provider's name, parameters, seed or gather — so the renderer's tile levels and the collision's ground ask it and cannot tell a procedural ground from a tile set built ahead of time.

- **The procedural source** is a ground provider's view of itself, `GroundProvider::tiles()`, as `view()` is the placement generators': its `evaluate` at the time asked for, or, for a ground that does not move (the waves), its `grid` at its own time whatever time is asked. Its heights are the provider's to the bit, so drawing through it changes no picture.
- **A tile set built ahead** is data under keys — the heights of tile (x, z) at a spacing, written once and read back — and answers a window from the tiles it covers. It does not move. The renderer's tests build one into a derived-data cache and read it back through the same seam ([renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles)); what an authored world still lacks around it is listed in [world](world.md#an-authored-world).

A source is a table of plain functions over a state, like everything else here (`TileSourceOps`), and `TileSource` is a non-owning handle: the provider or the tile set outlives what reads it.

**Native sources, independently of motion** (2026-10-23, W67, [ADR-0057](../adr/0057-ground-providers-expose-their-native-tile-source.md)). `GroundOps::tile_source` optionally returns the capability's own source. `GroundProvider::tiles()` takes it before the generic `evaluate`/`grid` adapter, so a still authored source preserves its supported filters and can refuse a missing tile. The callback returns a handle into provider-owned state; the owner outlives the terrain layout, collision and every worker reading it. With no callback the procedural adapters are unchanged. `k_ground_tiles` still means persistent world records through `open_tiles`, and does not decide whether a terrain can be drawn from a `TileSource`. On a refused request consumers discard the output; a partial window is never a valid field.

**Material masks, the same way** (2026-10-24, W72; `ground_masks.h`). `GroundOps::masks` optionally returns a ground's mask level — per-tile palette weights a terrain's `masks` block was built into — as a `GroundMasks` (an info call and a tile call over the provider's state), built on the first ask and a cache hit after, so the renderer uploads an authored ground's materials without naming the `tiles` capability, as it reads its heights through `tile_source`. The header owns the contract both sides read: the tile's word layout (its table, four RGBA8 weights a point at every mip, a byte of wetness), `mask_tile_of` and `mask_sample`, the CPU's reading the shader mirrors. `resolve_mask_water` copies a mask region's named scene water body's outline into it, which every host that reads a scene file calls before a provider is made, so a build and its key see shapes and never names ([tileset](tileset.md#the-mask-level)). No ADR: it is ADR-0057's seam carrying a second kind of data, optional, null for every provider that has none.

**A lattice is asked for at a spacing and a filter** (2026-10-03, [renderer](renderer.md#ground-to-the-horizon)). `TileSourceOps::heights` takes `filter_mm` beside the spacing — the width each point stands for, `Lattice::filter_mm` on the provider's side — and `travel_m` takes it too. At 0 the answer is the ground at the point, which is what every ring of the world's tiles, the collision and the walker ask for and what every source answered before (`TileSource::heights` is that call). At a far level's own spacing the answer is **what a lattice that coarse can carry**: the dunes (`terrain::DuneField::carries`) keep a band whose cell is at least two of its points (the sampling theorem's; four dropped bands a box of the spacing keeps most of, and measured worse against one) and stand the rest in with their mean height at the share the bands before them leave them, flat and still, and their gather holds none of the dropped bands' primitives — a 57 km window of 256 m points gathers no ten-metre waves; `travel_m` at a filter is the fastest band the filter keeps, since a mean does not move. A source built ahead answers a filter from a coarser lattice stored beside its finest (its **mip chain**), and refuses one it was not built with. A source with nothing finer than its lattices can carry (a test's waves, the still grid of the waves) may answer the point either way. Why the seam and not the renderer: a coarse lattice that point-samples crests 20 m apart at 64 m is any height between floor and crest, changes as they move — the shimmer — and draws the wrong silhouette, and only the source knows what its ground is made of.

### Far from the origin

**A height is asked for in integers, and the lattice is integers** ([ADR-0053](../adr/0053-world-positions-are-f64-and-the-gpu-sees-none.md), 2026-10-05). A lattice point's place is `Lattice::x_mm(i)` — `i * spacing_mm`, exact in i64 for every index — and that is what a provider samples its field at; `Lattice::x(i)`, the point's float32 metres, is what float arithmetic of metres (the fixed features, a height function of metres) is handed and is never the way to a point's place. Until 2026-10-05 the dunes went from the index to the float metres and back (`to_mm(lattice.x(i))`), which is the same millimetre only while a float holds it — 16 km at any spacing, 4,000 km at 25 cm ([ADR-0050](../adr/0050-the-ground-is-drawn-from-the-worlds-tiles.md) decision 3's limit) — and the walker's 1 mm lattice 420 km out was a neighbour's height.

**A point between lattice points** is `GroundOps::height_mm` / `GroundProvider::height_mm(time_s, x_mm, z_mm)`: the surface at a point given in whole millimetres at a game time, the same heights as `evaluate` on a 1 mm lattice there but with no index to overflow — the 1 mm lattice's i32 index ends at 2,147 km. `nearest_mm(f64)` is the rounding a caller takes a `WorldPos` coordinate to millimetres with (halves up, as the terrain's own `to_mm` rounds). A provider without the entry answers through `evaluate` on the 1 mm lattice where its index reaches, and through its float `height` at its own time past that, which is exact only while a float holds the millimetre; the dunes have the entry; the renderer's waves, which are the renderer's own, do not.

**The waves stay float32, and the line is drawn at the world's tiles** (2026-10-07, roadmap W62). The waves are the stand-in ground of a scene that names no generator: float32 sines of absolute metres, drawn as the scene's one grid round the origin, a few kilometres where a float32 metre holds the millimetre (16 km). Millimetre entries would have to compute every wave's phase in f64 from the millimetre — and their grid too, or a grounded instance and the walker would disagree with the drawn ground by the float rounding even by the origin — which moves the waves' heights by the origin, their mesh's cache key and every fixture's pinned number, for ground nothing draws far out. What does go far out is the world's tiles, which follow the camera; so a provider says whether its heights are right wherever a cell reaches (`k_ground_millimetres`), the dunes do, and the renderer draws the tiles from no other ([renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles)). Anything that asks the waves far out — a grounded instance, a camera key, a placement's ground, the walker — gets their own float32 answer there, the same for every asker, over ground the waves never draw. A game that wants a ground far out names a provider with the flag.

**Where exactness now ends.** The tile source's window (`TileSourceOps::heights`, `GroundOps::grid` and `evaluate`) is still indexed in i32, so a window reaches 2³¹ points from the origin: 2.1e9 m at a metre (the collision's default), 5.4e8 m at the renderer's finest 25 cm, 2,147 km at a millimetre — past which a caller asks `height_mm` or refuses (the collision refuses the tile, [scene_collision](scene_collision.md#far-from-the-origin)). Widening it to i64 changes the renderer's and its tests' implementations of the ops, and is left for the merge of [ADR-0053](../adr/0053-world-positions-are-f64-and-the-gpu-sees-none.md)'s batch.

### Placements far from the origin

**A placement is a `WorldPos`, and the ground it stands on is asked in whole millimetres** (2026-10-06). Until then a generator computed a prop's place in float32 world coordinates (`Placement::transform`, a `Transform3`) and asked `Ground::surface`/`floor` for its height at float32 metres, so a prop on a far tile stood up to half a float's step from where it was meant — 1.6 cm at 419 km, half a metre at 10,000 km, 4 m at 1e8 m — on a neighbour's height, and two pieces laid to touch opened or closed a gap of up to a whole step ([far from the origin](../experiments/far-from-origin-2026-10-04.md#part-3-placements)). Now:

- **`Placement`** is `position` (a `WorldPos`), `rotation`, `scale`, `mesh`, `tag`; 64 bytes, 8-aligned (it was 48, 4-aligned). The rotation and scale are the mesh's own frame and stay float32; `turn()` is them as a `Transform3` with no translation. A reader composes the mesh's fit with the placement in f64 — the fit's offset turned and scaled in float32 (the size of the mesh) and added to `position` in f64 — and the GPU holds the sum as a cell and a local: the renderer's scene reader and the world's placements consumer set `SceneInstance::origin` to `position` and its transform to `turn()` (`renderer::instance_translation`), and the collision's streamed path does the same sum for a compound child (`scene_collision.cpp`, `build_placements`). A placement lands within the local's rounding of where its generator put it, 1.9 µm, anywhere a cell reaches.
- **`Ground`** answers `surface_mm(x_mm, z_mm)` and `floor_mm(x_mm, z_mm)` in f64 through a `HeightFn` that takes whole millimetres from the world's origin, and `surface(WorldPos)`/`floor(WorldPos)` take a position's x and z to the nearest millimetre (`nearest_mm`; its y is not read). **Why millimetres and not f64 metres:** the ground's own lattice, the tile source and the walker's queries are integer millimetres (`GroundOps::height_mm`, the collision's `ground_height`), so a prop and the ground walked up to it are asked the same place; the generators that decide in integers (the ruins in centimetres, the city in centimetres) reach a millimetre exactly with no float on the way; an integer cannot drift, so a site moved by whole cells asks the field the same points moved by a whole number of millimetres; and a provider evaluating a field of metres would round an f64 metre to the millimetre anyway. What it costs is a half-millimetre of place under a generator that wants a height between millimetres: the slope times 0.5 mm, a fraction of a millimetre of height on any ground here. The heights are f64 so a provider with more than a float's worth of height can hand it over; every provider here answers a float, widened.
- **`GroundOps::surface_mm` and `floor_mm`** are the provider's side: its surface at its own time and its floor at a point in whole millimetres, which `GroundProvider::view()` answers a `Ground` through (`GroundProvider::surface_mm`/`floor_mm`). `height_mm(time_s, …)` cannot stand in for the first: it is asked at a time, and only the provider knows its own. The dunes have both (`dunes_surface_mm`, `dunes_floor_mm`: the field at the integers, the scene's ridges and basins at their float metres, as `dunes_height_mm` has them). A provider without them answers through what it has: a still ground's `height_mm`, which is its one surface at every time, and otherwise `height` and `floor` at the point's float32 metres — exact only while a float holds the millimetre (16 km). The renderer's waves are such a ground: their field is float arithmetic of absolute metres, so far out they are the waves' own approximation whatever asks them; the dunes are the ground a far world is drawn on.

**The frame each generator places in.** A generator's own arithmetic may be float32 by design (a hash, a lattice) as long as it stays in a local frame and places relative to a `WorldPos`:

| Generator | Decides in | Places |
|---|---|---|
| `city` | integer centimetres, world-anchored (the plan) | `proxy_position`: the centimetres in f64, one correctly rounded division a coordinate; no site origin, since the plan's frame is the world's |
| `ruins` | integer centimetres of the world (the building's frame, `Assembler::Frame`, its tile's corner and offsets in i64) | the centimetres divided once in f64 (`grid::metres_f64`), the member's offset turned in float32 and added in f64; its ground asked at the same integers in millimetres. No site origin: its frame is the world's ([ruins](ruins.md#far-from-the-origin)) |
| `scatter` | integer millimetres of the world (a global lattice per kind, the layer's tiles in i64 millimetres) | the millimetres divided once in f64 (`scatter::position`), the height the ground's own at the same millimetres less the kind's sink; no site origin ([scatter](scatter.md#determinism)) |
| the tests' generators (`scene_gen_tests`, the renderer's `scene_gen_scene_tests`, the world's `scene_order_tests`, the collision's `scene_collision_tests`) | f64 | their `WorldPos` directly |

The proofs: `tests/scene_gen_tests.cpp` asks a sawtooth ground of millimetres at 419 km, 10,000 km and 1e8 m through the view, which answers the millimetre meant where the float32 metre answers a neighbour's; the renderer's `scene_gen_scene_tests.cpp` reads boxes placed 16.123456789 m from each far site and 0.4 µm either side of a cell's edge, through the reader and `make_instance`, and finds each instance's cell and local within 2 µm of the generator's place composed with the fit, and two boxes laid to touch across a cell's edge at each far site with faces within 4 µm of each other in an eye's frame at the seam; and `scene_collision_tests.cpp` meets a streamed box's face 419 km, 10,000 km and 1e8 m out at the origin's distance from its site to 2 µm; and the renderer's `ruins_scene_tests.cpp` holds a ruin moved by whole cells 10,000 km out to the same GPU instance records, cells moved, every piece and block ([ruins](ruins.md#far-from-the-origin)). The city has no translation test of its own: its places are its plan's integer centimetres divided once (`proxy_position`), the same arithmetic the ruins' test holds.

## The derived-data root

**A generator that writes what it builds writes it under the host's derived-data root** (`Context::ddc`, 2026-10-18). The renderer's scene reader fills it from `SceneDesc::ddc`, which both hosts set from `--ddc` (engine-view) or `render.load`'s `ddc` (engine-host) before they read the file; it is empty when the host named none. A generator with an empty root keeps the rule it had before the field: the repository's `ddc/` found by walking up from the scene's directory (`geometry::find_ddc_root`), else a directory beside the scene. Why the field: the walk found the root from **where the scene is**, so a game repository outside the engine's tree got a `ddc/` of its own under its own root whatever `--ddc` it passed, and had to junction it out to keep the bytes out of its source. The water's meshes ([water](water.md#the-mesh)), the scatter's procedural rocks (`systems/scatter/src/rock.cpp`, `<root>/scatter/`, since G62's batch) and, since T90, the `tiles` ground provider (before its block's own `ddc`; the reader also writes the root into that block, since every sampler a host makes is made from the description) read it; a mesh generator's output (the flora's palm, the rocks capability's boulders) goes through the load's own cluster cache at `SceneDesc::ddc` and never had it. The world's placements consumer, which opens a streamed entry's tiles, hands no root yet, so a streamed entry walks.

## Earlier placements

**A generator may read the placements the entries before it made, and nothing else of another generator** (2026-10-12; [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md) decision 4 allowed it — "the placements it is handed in the declared order" — and the "As built" note there records the seam). `Context::earlier` is a read-only span of `Footprint`s: where each earlier placement stands, in whole millimetres, and how far its mesh reaches on the ground — the radius of the mesh's cluster spheres in the ground's plane from its origin (`renderer::ground_radius`), times the placement's scale. **The host measures the radius, not the generator**: a generator names its meshes by path and never loads another's, and both hosts measure the same way, the reader from the mesh as it loads it (once a mesh, and only when a later entry is there to read it) and the world's placements consumer from the resident scene's parts. **What each host hands**: the reader, every entry read whole before this one (its whole expansion); the placements consumer, the streamed entries before this one **on the same tile**. So a tile is a function of (entry, seed, tile, ground, the earlier placements of the tile), and a scene whose first entry this is — the desert's layer — reads nothing new: its bytes do not move. The two differ only where an earlier placement's footprint reaches over its own tile's border, which a whole read sees from the next tile and a streamed tile does not (a building's walls lie inside the tile its centre is in, so for the ruins that is a block's overhang); and an entry read whole is not handed to a streamed one. A footprint may be a band or a polygon as well as the bounds' circle since W98 ([Footprints as shapes](#footprints-as-shapes)); both hosts still hand discs. The scatter layer is the reader: a kind keeps its `clear` beyond each footprint ([scatter](scatter.md#exclusion-by-earlier-placements)).

### Footprints as shapes

**A footprint is a disc, a band or a polygon, in whole millimetres** (roadmap W98, 2026-11-10; the day's G145 and G142b reports: the sward stood 1.6 m back from a dry-stone wall where it should grow to its foot, because the wall reached the sward as the circle round its bounds plus half a patch's diagonal, and a ledge's tread could not clear the cover off its back). `Footprint` (`exclusion.h`, where the exclusion can hold one; `scene_gen.h` says where) is:

- a **disc**, `Footprint{x_mm, z_mm, radius}` — what every host hands today;
- a **band**, `Footprint::band(ax, az, bx, bz, half)` — a segment with a half-width, its ends round: a wall's foot, a tread, a hedge, a fence line;
- a **polygon**, `Footprint::polygon(points)` — a simple polygon of `FootprintPoint`s, either turn, the points held by whoever made it while it is read: a pad, a building's plan, a yard.

**Every shape keeps the disc that holds it whole** (`x_mm`, `z_mm`, `radius`, a millimetre over): a band's middle and half its length plus its half-width, a polygon's box centre and its furthest point. So **every reader written before W98 stays right without changing** — the sward, litter, scatter, wetland, cliffs and deposits read `x_mm`, `z_mm` and `radius` and keep clear of the whole shape, never inside it, only further off than it needs — and a reader that honours the shape asks it: `edge_m(x_mm, z_mm)` (metres to the edge, negative inside) and `covers_mm` (on or inside). The arithmetic is integer millimetres in, f64 out: products, sums, the projection's one division and one square root a shape, every step correctly rounded, so a shape answers the same on every toolchain. A polygon's inside is the even-odd rule over its edges, the one the water bodies and the holes use.

**The exclusion holds shapes, so every generator honours them through the query it already makes.** `Exclusion::add_footprints(span)` (after `build`, which clears them) copies the shapes in, a polygon's points with them, and files each on the exclusion's 16 m grid over its disc's box; a fourth `ExclusionKind`, `k_exclude_footprints`, is in `k_exclude_all`, which every generator's `Context::excluded_mm` asks by default, so **a point on or inside a shape is excluded for the sward, litter and scatter with no change to them**, and a generator asking a named subset (the wetland's holes only) is not. A query reads its own cell's shapes, so its cost is the shapes filed in that 256 m² and does not grow with the world: **474 ns** a query over 10,000 bands and 1,000 polygons in a square kilometre (`msvc-release`, the case "what a footprint query costs", a million points, best of two, the CPU at 100 % with other agents building, so an upper bound; 2.9 µs in `msvc-debug`). The shapes cost 80 bytes each, a polygon's points 16 bytes each, and one 24-byte cell record for each 16 m cell its disc's box touches (one to four for a shape under 16 m). `footprint_distance_mm(x_mm, z_mm, reach_m)` is the metres to the nearest shape's edge, 0 inside, over 1e20 when none is within the reach: what a reader that keeps a margin of its own past a shape asks (the sward's half a patch, a scatter kind's `clear`). Refused with a sentence naming the footprint by its place in the span, and nothing added: a radius that is negative or not finite, a band with a half-width under a millimetre, a polygon of fewer than three points or with no area.

**What the sward does with a wall's band** (the brief's case, held by `exclusion_tests.cpp`, "a band footprint excludes its band and nothing else"): asked at a patch's moved centre, as it asks a channel, a band 0.4 m either side of a wall's line leaves free every point a millimetre past its edge, where the same wall's disc held every patch back by its whole radius plus half a patch's diagonal. The sward's patch then reaches up to half its diagonal past its centre (0.35 m at the default 0.5 m cell), so its leaves lie against the wall's foot rather than in it, as they lie against a bank's edge. **Who hands shapes, and when**: nobody yet. The hosts build the exclusion from the scene's holes, water and channels (`systems/renderer/src/scene.cpp`) and fill `Context::earlier` with discs (the reader and `systems/world/src/placement_tiles.cpp`); both are closed to W98, so what a host and a generator need to hand a shape across is written down in the roadmap's W98 row and asked of R142's contract, which carries a generator's output at a tile's activation.

Tests (`tests/exclusion_tests.cpp`): a band's edge at five places in and out and round its ends, its disc holding every point it covers, the exclusion's answer a millimetre either side of the edge at three stations, a kind subset without the footprints, the distance within a reach and nothing past it, `build` clearing them; an L-shaped polygon's inside, its notch outside, its edge distance, the same polygon in the other turn within 1e-9 m at 400 points, the exclusion's answer equal to the shape's at 3,249 points after the shape it copied went, a disc and a zero-length band in one call, and the four refusals with nothing added.

**`Context::stand`, a moving ground.** The ground a generator decides on is `Context::ground`, the scene's time; a host whose ground has moved on (the dunes under a time-lapse) hands the surface as it is now in `stand`, and a generator stands what stands on the surface on it — its height and its normal — while its place, turn and scale stay what `ground` decided ([scatter](scatter.md#a-moving-ground), [world](world.md#the-consumers)). Absent, it is `ground`.

## Exclusion

**A scene's holes, channels and water are where no generator places anything on the ground** (roadmap G135, 2026-11-08; the owner's v35 list: "sward and litter grow over the cave's hole", "grass grows through the streams"; the forest proof of concept cleared 773 trees, 1,946 plants and 9 logs standing in beds by hand, and the engine now does it). `scene_gen::Exclusion` (`exclusion.h`) is built **once per scene read** from the scene entry by the host (`SceneDesc::terrain.exclusion`, `systems/renderer/src/scene.cpp`, before the first placement entry) and handed to every generator in the `Context` (`Context::exclusion`; null is "nothing excluded", so a host that hands none and a scene with none place what they always did). A generator asks `Context::excluded_mm(x_mm, z_mm[, kinds])` for a candidate and `Context::riparian_factor_mm(x_mm, z_mm)` for a density. The same object reaches the world's placements consumer and the scene collision (`context_for` in `systems/world/src/placement_tiles.cpp` and `systems/scene_collision/src/scene_collision.cpp`), so a streamed tile, the whole read and the collision's bodies place the same points. It lives here, below the water capability, for the reason the holes do: the generators are siblings that see the ground and nothing of each other (ADR-0046 decision 4), so what they share is read from the scene entry and passed down, and none of them names `systems/water`.

**Four kinds** (`ExclusionKind`; a generator may ask for any subset; the fourth, the **shaped footprints** a host adds with `add_footprints`, is W98's and is described under [Footprints as shapes](#footprints-as-shapes)):

- **Holes** (`Terrain.holes`, W87): the ground has no surface there. `TerrainHoles::contains_mm`, the same strict-inside test the meshes' cell rule is built from.
- **Channels** (`water_channels`, S96): a point within `half the surface width + the bank's reach` of the centreline, along a segment's length with a round cap at each end, the width interpolated along the segment. The reach is [channels](channels.md)' dressed bank, `bank_width_widths * w + bank_width_m`, **read from the channel's own description** (`WaterChannel.bank_width_widths` and `bank_width_m`, version 4), which the channels recipe writes whenever its numbers are not the defaults; at the defaults (0.25 w + 0.4 m, the recipe's and the field's) the excluded corridor is the bed, the water and the bank's berm out to the riparian edge that page names: 2.65 m either side of a 3 m stream. Until T78 (2026-11-09) the exclusion copied the defaults as two constants, so a recipe with wider banks grew plants on them; `scene_gen` may not name the channels capability, so the number travels as data on the description the two already share (`domain/channels/tests/channels_tests.cpp`, "the exclusion reaches the recipe's bank width"). A negative or non-finite number on the description is read as its default, since the water reads neither and nothing else would refuse it. Segments are filed in a 16 m grid (a sorted vector, no hash map), so a query reads the segments of one cell.
- **Water** (`water`): a point inside a body's outline whose ground is below the body's level, which is `water::depth_at > 0`, wading; an open body's sea is every point whose ground is below the level. The outline is a circle, a polygon (even-odd, either winding) or a **fill**; a fill's region is found by a flood that runs when the water is read, after the placements, so the placements see the box the flood may reach (`max_extent_m` about the seed) where the ground is below the level. That is conservative: a hollow below the level inside the box that the flood would not reach is also left bare.

**One definition of a bank's edge, asked out to any reach** (W93, 2026-11-10, for the site query, G144). `Exclusion::channel_edge_distance_mm(x_mm, z_mm, reach_m)` is the distance in metres from a world point in whole millimetres to the nearest channel's bank edge — the edge the hard exclusion leaves bare: the dressed bank's, or a flood bed's where it reaches further on the point's side — 0 inside it, and a very large number (over 1e20) when no edge is within `reach_m`. It is **the one place that says where a bank's edge is**: `channel_distance_mm` is it at the riparian 3 m, to the bit, and anything that wants "metres to the nearest stream" (the site, a placement rule) asks it and keeps no copy of the edge's formula or an index of the segments of its own. Const, with no state of its own, so any number of threads ask at once; it reads the 16 m cells within the reach of the point, so 128 m is 17 cells a side and the widest edge more. Test: `exclusion_tests.cpp`, "the distance to a bank's edge out to any reach is the riparian one's" (88,000 points about two channels, one with a flood bed: equal to `channel_distance_mm` wherever that answers, the same distance from a longer reach and nothing from a shorter one).

**Beyond the hard edge, a channel thins a density** (`riparian_factor_mm`): 1 at `k_riparian_reach_m` (3 m) from the bank's outer edge, falling on a smoothstep to `k_riparian_floor` (0.2) at the edge. It is a design choice, not a fit: the riparian zone is a gradient of disturbance and moisture across the bank, with the structure of the vegetation changing over a few metres (Naiman and Decamps 1997, "The ecology of interfaces: riparian zones", Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics 28), and the numbers are stated defaults to be tuned by eye in the proof of concept. Only the sward multiplies it in ([flora](flora.md#grass-sward-g90)); the other generators only drop.

**Who asks** (G135; the generators that choose candidate points on the ground, the filter one statement in each):

| Generator | Filter | Excludes |
|---|---|---|
| `sward` | `tile` in `domain/flora/src/sward_placement.cpp`, after the earlier-footprint test; a forb at its own point (G137) | holes, channels, water; density times the riparian factor; with forms, the stream's nearness is also the stature rule's wetness |
| `litter` | `tile` in `domain/litter/src/placement.cpp`, before the density | holes, channels, water |
| `scatter` (the trees, plants, rocks and logs of a layer) | `Placer::Impl::place_kind` in `systems/scatter/src/placer.cpp`, before the ground's height; `Site::exclusion` | holes, channels, water |
| `wetland` | `generate` in `domain/wetland/src/placement.cpp` | holes only: the water garden lives in water and on a bank |
| `deposits` | `allowed` in `domain/rocks/src/deposits_generator.cpp` | a patch whose four corners and middle are all over a hole; gravel and rubble in a bed and under water are what a bed is |

Not filtered, by design: `authored` replays explicit positions an author or a tool chose (a log laid across a stream is the author's call), `ruins`, `city`, `cliffs`, `channels` and `cave` are structures at sites their entries name, and `water` is the water. A scatter layer's *excluded layers* (`exclude.layers`) are asked without it, so a layer that excludes by another keeps its clearance from the other's points even over a hole.

**What a cut adds** (W89's channel cut into the ground; settled by W93, 2026-11-10, which folded W92 in). **The corridor is decided from the cut's geometry, and for W89's cut the code was right and this page was wrong.** The page said the corridor's edge "becomes the cut's bank top, which is at least the dressed bank's outer edge, so the margin grows ... to the cut's top width". A cut's bank tops stand at **half the width** from the line — the bankfull width *is* its top width — which is inside the corridor's `0.75 w + 0.4 m`, not outside it; and the face the cut carries on past the top where it meets higher ground runs `depth / slope` more, which at the hydraulic geometry's depth (about a ninth of the width) and any bank slope over 0.45 is inside it too. So a channel with a plain cut is excluded exactly as before, and `exclusion.cpp` needed nothing. **What does reach further is a flood bed** (W93): a cut may carry one several widths wide, and the placements must leave it bare. Its reach each side is the cut's own number, handed over on the description (`WaterChannel.flood_left_m`, `flood_right_m`, version 5, which [channels](channels.md#the-flood-bed) writes), and the corridor on each side of the line is the further of the two: the bank's edge and the bed's. The riparian factor is measured from that edge. A channel that names no flood bed, arrays that are not the points' long, and a number that is negative or not finite are all excluded as before, to the bit. The tests that would have caught the page's claim: `exclusion_tests.cpp`, "a channel's flood bed is left bare on the side it reaches" (6 m of bed on one side excluded and not on the other, the riparian distance from the bed's edge, the three no-bed cases against a plain channel), and `channels_tests.cpp`, "the flood bed's width follows the valley's confinement" (every point 0.9 of the bed's reach from the ribbon's line, either side, is excluded by the exclusion built from the written description). The rounded top (W93) lowers the ground a little beyond the bank's top, most on a point bar's side (by `0.75 depth / slope`, up to 0.4 widths at a bar's 0.2): that shoulder is ground at nearly its old height and stays plantable.

**Determinism and cost.** Integer millimetres in, f64 out, products, sums and one correctly rounded square root, so the answer is the same on every toolchain and thread count; the object is read-only once built. A scene with no holes, channels or bodies builds an empty object, which the reader does not hand over (`Context::exclusion` stays null), so **every placement of such a scene is byte-identical**; `exclusion_tests.cpp` and each generator's `*_exclusion_tests.cpp` hold it, patch for patch. A scene that has them places fewer points. **`renderer.placement.exclusion`** (a tunable, default true, read when the file is read) set false leaves the object unbuilt, so a scene places as it did before G135: the before of a before-and-after, and the way out for a scene that wants grass in a bed.

## The site

**What the ground is like at a point, as the terrain variables a placement's rules read** (roadmap G144, 2026-11-10; the [grounding](../experiments/site-and-ecotope.md)). The forest proof of concept's trees stood to the summits and its meadows were composed by hand, because nothing here derived a site from the ground; "forest on the shaded aspect", "a treeline at 2,200 m, higher on the sunny side" and "green where water reaches" are a few lines once elevation, aspect and the distance to water are fields. `scene_gen::Site` (`site.h`) is that one query, the contract every placement's rules are written against (scatter's kinds now, the meadow's community, the understory's light and the forest's edge later). It follows the [exclusion](#exclusion)'s pattern: built **once per scene read** by the host (`SceneDesc::terrain.site`, `systems/renderer/src/scene.cpp`, beside the exclusion), from the scene sky's latitude (`Sky.latitude_deg`; 30°, the schema's default, for a scene naming none) and its water bodies and channels, and handed to every generator in `Context::site`; the same object reaches the world's placements consumer and the scene collision. `Context::site_mm(x_mm, z_mm[, fields])` asks it at a point in whole millimetres, on the context's own `ground`; with no site handed it answers from `Site::fallback()` (the default latitude, no water), so a rule still reads the ground.

A `SiteSample`, at a **10 m baseline** (`k_site_step_mm`, 5 m either side: the finest of the 10–30 m terrain models the literature reads these from, so a boulder's face is not a slope and a 20 m gully is still a hollow):

| Field | What | Cost (`SiteField`) |
|---|---|---|
| `elevation` | the surface at the point, metres | `k_site_elevation`: one ground query |
| `slope` | rise over run, \|∇z\|, by central differences | `k_site_form`: five ground queries for this row and the four below |
| `down_x`, `down_z` | the downslope direction as a unit vector (north −z, east +x), (0, 0) where exactly level: **no arctangent is ever needed for aspect** | |
| `heat_load` | the ground's unit normal against a reference sun — at McCune and Keon's folded bearing (225° north of the equator, 315° south) and the equinox's noon elevation 90° − \|latitude\| — over level ground's: 1 level, more facing the sun, less away, never below 0 (1.39 and 0.34 on 30° slopes facing 225° and 45° at 46.5° N) | |
| `sunward` | the unit normal's horizontal part along the sun's bearing, sin(slope) cos(aspect − bearing), [−1, 1]: what a treeline's aspect shift is linear in | |
| `curvature` | the negative five-point Laplacian, 1/m: positive on a convex crest, negative in a hollow | |
| `channel_m`, `body_m`, `water_m()` | metres to the nearest channel's bank edge (the exclusion's edge, `0.5 w + bank_width_widths w + bank_width_m`), to the nearest body's outline (a circle or polygon; 0 inside), and the nearer of the two; `k_site_water_reach_m` (128 m) when none is within it | `k_site_water`: no ground query; the exclusion's bank-edge query out to 128 m (a binary search a row of its 16 m cells within the reach, 17 rows and the widest edge more, and the segments filed there), and the bodies' boxes |

**What it does not measure, and why.** A *fill* body's region is found by a flood when the water is read, after the placements, and an *open* body's shore is where the ground meets its level, so neither is an outline here: their distance is the reach. **There is no wetness figure**: the honest local inputs are the distance to water and the curvature, named for what they are; a topographic wetness index needs the upslope area, which W95's landform solver will give as a field, and the site gains it then. Topographic position at a hillslope's scale, the canopy's shade and soil depth are not built ([grounding](../experiments/site-and-ecotope.md#what-is-not-built)).

**Determinism and cost.** Integer millimetres in; the ground's heights, then `+ − × ÷` and correctly rounded square roots; the sun's direction once at `build` through `core/math/deterministic.h`'s sine and cosine. A sample is a function of the point and the scene, never of the tile or the window that asks, the same bits in any order on any number of threads (`tests/site_tests.cpp` asks a tile border from both sides in opposite orders, against a site built from the water listed the other way round, and pins the bytes of 576 samples for the containers). Nothing is cached per tile: a generator samples the candidates it keeps after its cheap tests, which for any kind sparser than a lattice is fewer points than a lattice would be; scatter's measured cost is on [its page](scatter.md), "Kinds by site". Resident: the channels as an exclusion files them (a segment in every 16 m cell within its bank edge and 3 m, about 4 a 5 m segment of a small stream, at 24 bytes a cell) and the bodies' outlines; nothing per tile or per world size beyond the scene's water.

**The bank edge is the exclusion's, asked** (2026-11-11, roadmap T90): the site holds the scene's channels as an `Exclusion` of its own (built from the channels alone; the site measures a body's outline itself) and answers `channel_m` with `channel_edge_distance_mm` at `k_site_water_reach_m`, the reach where that query answers "none". Until T90 it kept a copy of the edge's formula (`bank_number` and `edge` in `site.cpp`), written before W93 exposed the query. **The copy was not the edge**: it left out the flood bed, which W93 added to the exclusion's edge, so beside a channel with a flood bed the site read the dressed bank's distance where the ground stays bare to the bed's (30 m off a stream whose bed reaches 20 m on that side: 27.35 m where the edge is 10 m away). Without a flood bed the two agreed to the bit, which is why the site's pin did not move. `site_tests.cpp` holds the site to the query at 6,561 points about two channels, one with a flood bed, to the bit.

## The order rule

**Ground first, then placements in the scene's order**, in the reader and in the ring. Ground is what placements stand on, so the reader makes the terrain's provider before it opens the first placement entry and hands each its `view()`, and walks the entries in the file's order (the `ruins` entries, then `placements`); the ring registers its ground consumer before its placements consumer — one function, `world::add_scene_consumers`, so no host spells it — and the placements consumer asks the entries in the same order, so a tile's ground is built before anything on it and goes after it ([ADR-0040](../adr/0040-the-tile-ring.md)'s declared order; [world](world.md#the-consumers), "The order"). Both halves are held by tests that swap two entries and find the ground still first: the renderer's `scene_gen_scene_tests.cpp` for the reader and the world's `scene_order_tests.cpp` for the ring, each with generators of its own and no capability. **A generator sees the ground it is handed and nothing of any other** (`Context`: the world seed, the tile size, the scene's directory, the ground, a streamed world's block and ring, and a job pool made on first ask). A generator that wants another's output asks the ground, never the registry, which keeps each generator a function of (entry, seed, tile, ground) — the per-tile determinism the ruins and the city rest on.

## The registrar pattern

```cpp
// In the capability's own source, beside the functions it names:
constexpr scene_gen::PlacementGeneratorDesc k_ruins{
    .name = "ruins", .open = &open, .close = &close, .expand = &expand,
    .meshes = &meshes, .tile = &tile, .occupies = &occupies, .representation = &representation};
const scene_gen::Registrar k_ruins_registrar{k_ruins};
```

The descriptor is constant-initialized; the `Registrar` adds it to `GeneratorRegistry::global()` during static initialization (a function-local static, so a registrar in any translation unit finds the registry constructed whatever order the units initialize in). The module holding it is **`WHOLE_ARCHIVE`**, or the linker drops the object nothing references and the registration with it. A second descriptor under a name another holds stops the program at start with a message — two generators under one name would make what a scene means depend on link order — while the same descriptor twice is a library linked into two images of one process, and fine. `GeneratorRegistry::add` returns the same answer as a value, which is what the tests hold.

## Adding a generator

1. Write the functions and the descriptor in the capability's own module (`src/scene_generator.cpp` by convention), and a `Registrar` beside it; make the module `WHOLE_ARCHIVE` and give it `scene_gen` as a dependency.
2. Pick a name no other generator of the kind has — the capability's own name is the convention (`ruins`, `dunes`, `city`) — and read the entry's parameters with the schema's reader where they have a schema type, so an unknown field is refused with its path.
3. Tag the module: `GENERATOR placement:<name>` on its `engine_module()` line, one `<kind>:<name>` for every registration its sources make. That is the whole of the hosts' side: engine-view, engine-host and engine-editor link every tagged module ([Which generators a host carries](#which-generators-a-host-carries)). Nothing in `renderer` or `world` changes: a scene names it in `placements` (`{"generator": "<name>", "params": {...}}`) or as its terrain's `provider`.
4. Test the generator through the registry from the capability's own tests (a scene is not needed: make a `Context`, `open`, `expand`, `tile`), and that `tile` over every tile is `expand`.

## Which generators a host carries

**One list, kept by the build** (2026-11-09, roadmap T79; [ADR-0060](../adr/0060-generators-stay-in-domain-tagged.md), proposed). A generator — a capability that registers into this module's registry or into the mesh generators' (`assets::MeshGeneratorRegistrar`), and that nothing calls by name — says so on its `engine_module()` line, with every registration its sources make:

```cmake
engine_module(NAME rocks LAYER domain OPTIONAL WHOLE_ARCHIVE
  GENERATOR mesh:rock mesh:deposit placement:cliffs placement:deposits
  DEPS ...)
```

The kinds are `ground`, `placement`, `sky` and `mesh`. The tag is refused, in every configuration and before the capability's switch is read, on a module that is not `OPTIONAL` (a registration every executable needs belongs to the module that reads it, as the renderer's `waves` does) or not `WHOLE_ARCHIVE` (nothing references a registrar, so a static library loses it and the registration with it, silently), and for a kind it does not know. Every tagged module this configuration builds joins one list (`cmake/EngineGenerators.cmake`), and the hosts that read a scene — `ENGINE_GENERATOR_HOSTS`: engine-view, engine-host, engine-editor — link it with one call before their `engine_app()`:

```cmake
engine_host_generators(engine_view _view_deps)   # [EXCLUDE <module> "<reason>"]...
```

**Why.** Until T79 the three hosts each wrote the list out by hand, edited 17, 17 and 8 times, and the editor's had lost `caves` and `soil` under a comment saying it linked them "exactly as engine-view and engine-host link them": a document placing a `.cave.json` was refused by the editor alone, with "... which this build does not carry", and nothing failed until a reviewer read the three side by side. A dropped registrar fails as silently, so the list is checked twice:

- **At configure**, from `engine_finalize_modules()`: each host on the list links the build's generators less what it excludes in writing (`EXCLUDE <module> "<reason>"`, printed on the status line and recorded in `modules.json`), and a host that links no list, misses one or excludes one it still links stops the configure with every difference named. The configure prints the list it produced (`engine generators: 17 in this configuration: ...`).
- **At run time**, `<host>.generators` (`engine_view.generators`, `engine_host.generators`, `engine_editor.generators`; no device, under a second): `apps/tests/host_generators_tests.cpp`, compiled into each host's end-to-end test executable, which `engine_app()` links from the host's own `DEPS`, so its registries are the host's. Every tagged registration must be found, and every registered name must be tagged — except the renderer's own `waves` and a test's own generator, which by convention has `test` in its name. The second half keeps the tags true: a registrar added without its name on the tag fails here. The CTest test passes on doctest's own summary (two cases or more passed, none failed), because doctest exits 0 when its filter matches nothing. The three linkers (MSVC's, GNU ld, lld) do not drop the same things, so it runs on MSVC and in the Linux containers. The editor's own `tests/editor_generators_tests.cpp` reads a document placing a cave and a soil feature through the viewport's path; on main at 2d867dd2 it failed with the registry's sentence.

`modules.json` carries the tag (`"generator": [...]` on the module's row), the build's list (`"generators"`) and each host's exclusions (`"generator_hosts"`), so a tool asks which modules are generators without parsing CMake ([08 §8.5](../plan/08-toolchain.md#85-build-system-and-ci)). `msvc-minimal` and `linux-clang-minimal` produce an empty list and three hosts that agree on it.

**What the tag does not do yet.** ADR-0060 proposes that a tagged module may not depend on `gfx` or on a module in `systems/`; it is reported there, not checked, because `rocks -> gfx` stands today.

## Invariants

- One descriptor per name per kind; the lists are sorted by name when read (`ground_names`, `placement_names`), so a sentence and a summary are the same whatever order the registrars ran in.
- A generator is a function of its entry, the world seed, the tile and the ground it is handed; its output on any number of threads is the same.
- A ground's `grid` is its `height` at the lattice's points, to the bit; `evaluate` at its own time is its `grid`.
- A placement generator's `tile` over a set of tiles is its `expand` restricted to them.
- No module that reads a scene or streams a tile depends on a generator's capability. `engine_module()` refuses it in every configuration — a module that is not a capability listing one, or a capability listing another it has not declared with `engine_capability_requires` ([08 §8.5](../plan/08-toolchain.md#85-build-system-and-ci)) — so `modules.json` cannot show `renderer -> ruins`, `renderer -> terrain`, `world -> ruins` or `world -> terrain` again; only hosts (`engine_app`) and test targets link generators.

## Testing

`tools/dev.ps1 test -Filter scene_gen`: `tests/scene_gen_tests.cpp` registers a ground and a placement generator from its own source and finds them by name; holds the unknown-name sentence word for word; refuses a second descriptor under one name and takes the same one twice; and samples a ground with no grid of its own on a lattice, the same heights as its point function; and sees a ground as a tile source — a still one answers its grid at its own time whatever time is asked, the same bytes whole or a block at a time, and a moving one its `evaluate` and its travel. A still sky of its own (`scene-gen-test-still`) is found by name, listed, refused under a second descriptor, made from an entry, moved from handle to handle, and an entry naming no provider names "earth". `tests/size_table.cpp` pins `Placement` (64 bytes, 8-aligned, since its position became a `WorldPos`), `TileCoord` and `RingPlace`; and a ground of millimetres is asked through the view at 419 km, 10,000 km and 1e8 m ([Placements far from the origin](#placements-far-from-the-origin)).

The consumers' side is tested where they are, with generators registered from the tests' own sources, so it runs in every configuration — `msvc-minimal` and `linux-clang-minimal` included, where no generating capability is built: the renderer's `scene_gen_scene_tests.cpp` reads scenes naming a test ground and a test placement generator (instances of the generator's meshes standing on the ground's floor; the ground made before any entry is opened and the entries expanded in the file's order, swapped when the file swaps them; a streamed read opening and expanding nothing; both unknown-name sentences and a generator's own refusal after where the entry is), and the world's `scene_order_tests.cpp` streams a tile of such a scene through the ground and placements consumers ([world](world.md#the-consumers)). **The proofs** ([ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md) decision 5), as of 2026-09-27: `msvc-minimal` and `linux-clang-minimal`, every generator's capability off and `scene_gen` still built, build and pass (57 of 57 tests each), the renderer's registry tests among them; `msvc-no-ecs` builds and passes (76 of 76); `modules.json` in the all-on build shows the renderer and the world depending on `scene_gen` and on no generator, and a configure that adds `ruins` to the renderer's dependencies, or drops the world's declared `store` edge, stops with the sentence `engine_module()` now says.

`tests/site_tests.cpp` (G144) holds a sample on a plane, a ridge (z = 100 − 0.01 x²) and a paraboloid bowl equal to the analytic slope, direction and curvature within 1e-9; the heat load and sunward tilt against the unit normal and the page's sun within 1e-12, the folded aspects' ranking in both hemispheres, the deterministic sun within 1e-15 of the C library's, a latitude held at 85° and one that is not a number refused; the distances to a 3 m stream's bank (7.35 m at 10 m off its line), its cap, a circle's and a square's outline, an open body not measured; the fallback a context with none reads; a tile border sampled from both sides the same bits; and the pinned bytes. `tests/exclusion_tests.cpp` (G135) holds the query over a hole, a channel (width, caps, taper, the riparian curve), a circle, polygon (either winding), fill and open body, the kinds filter, and that the same scene built in another order answers the same; the generators' own `*_exclusion_tests.cpp` hold what each drops and that a scene with none places every point it did (`domain/flora`, `litter`, `wetland`, `rocks`, `systems/scatter`).

Each placement generator is tested through the registry in its own capability (`domain/ruins` and `domain/city`, `tests/scene_generator_tests.cpp`: `tile` over the entry's tiles is `expand`, and the refusals); the dunes through the renderer's `terrain_generator_tests.cpp` (a terrain naming its provider, the enum reading as one, sampler, provider and mesh to the bit) and the world's `ground_tiles_tests.cpp`, each linking the terrain capability as a host does, and in the terrain capability's own `far_tests.cpp`, where the dunes as a tile source and through `height_mm` are the field's own height to the bit at 419 km, 10,000 km and 1e8 m on the 25 cm and the 1 mm lattices ([Far from the origin](#far-from-the-origin)).

## Performance notes

A lookup by name is a walk of a handful of descriptors, once per scene read or world made. A generator's per-point height is a call through a pointer, which is why every grid is one call a block: the mesh's grid, the time-lapse's 64 × 64 blocks, a ring's chunk. Nothing here runs per frame.

## Mask presence at load

`GroundMaskOps::present(state, tx, tz)` (W76, 2026-10-26) answers manifest presence without
payload I/O. Providers that expose masks supply it alongside `info` and `tile`; the tileset
adapter delegates to `MaskLevel::present`. The renderer uses presence for its resident-budget
count and reads each present tile only in the copy pass. A failed read of a present tile is an
error, not absence. This is a cold callback table (one extra function pointer), not frame state;
the frame's GPU material layout and hot-type sizes do not change.

The [wetland generator](wetland.md) registers patch placements with explicit depth bands, slope, optional masks, earlier-footprint clearance and stable finite-window IDs.
