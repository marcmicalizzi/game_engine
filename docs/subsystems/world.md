# world (systems, capability)

**Purpose.** World tile streaming: the tile ring of [03 §3.7](../plan/03-data-model.md#37-spatial-partition) and [05 §5.4–5.5](../plan/05-simulation.md#54-lod-tier-assignment) that ties the pieces Phase 3 already has into a world that activates and deactivates tiles as its observers move. It owns the **tile grid** (a tile size, 32 m today, a parameter), the **observer set** it is updated from (engine-view's camera is one observer, a headless run's are declared), and the **tile ring**: which tiles are active, and in which ring, as a function of the observers, with hysteresis bands and a per-update budget. What a tile *is* belongs to the **consumers** registered with it, called in a declared order when a tile activates, moves between rings or goes: what a scene's placement generators put on a tile — its ruined building, a district's proxies, each generator found in the scene-generator registry by name — as instances the renderer adds and removes between frames (`placement_tiles.h`), a tile of the scene's ground from the ground provider the terrain names, with the tile's record in the store (`ground_tiles.h`), the tile of a partitioned document through the materialization driver (`document_tiles.h`), and the tile's projections and snapshot in the persistent store, which reconciliation reads when it comes back (`tile_store.h`, `store_tiles.h`). It builds no geometry and draws nothing, and links no generator ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)): where the terrain capability is in the executable, the ground consumer holds a tile of the dune generator's field for every held tile and keeps the tile's deformation overlay in the store ([terrain](terrain.md), [ADR-0043](../adr/0043-dunes-as-a-function-of-time.md)) — the endless terrain this page used to list as out of scope — and engine-view hands the ring's tiles to the renderer, which draws the ground from them ([The drawn ground](#the-consumers), from the provider's heights and not yet from the ground consumer's held tiles). [E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md) is its first measurement; [ADR-0040](../adr/0040-the-tile-ring.md) (proposed) records the rules and defaults below. It is also where a running world is **saved, loaded and replayed** ([below](#save-and-load), [ADR-0042](../adr/0042-a-save-is-the-store-the-document-and-the-drivers.md), proposed): the persistent-state hash two runs compare, a save of the document, the store and the drivers of the run, and a load that continues it — because what a save has to hold beyond the document and the store is the ring's: its observers, its tiles and the player steering one of them.

## Why this shape

- **A ring is a tier, and the ring is `sim::TierAssignment`.** A tile's ring is the minimum over observers of the distance from the observer to the tile's centre on the ground plane, divided by the observer's weight, banded by one radius per ring — which is exactly the score and the banding an entity's simulation tier is ([05 §5.4](../plan/05-simulation.md#54-lod-tier-assignment); [sim](sim.md#why-tiers-are-a-function-over-the-observer-set)). So the ring calls `TierAssignment::assign_tiers` over the tiles it tracks, with one tier per ring and one more for "inactive", and inherits its hysteresis, its rate limits (nearest promoted first, farthest demoted first, the rest deferred), and its determinism (scores from the inputs alone, changes in index order). Two players, a camera and a quest marker are observers alike; nothing here knows which one is "the player". The alternative — a radius round a camera, in square tiles — would have been a second LOD policy beside the simulation's, disagreeing with it at every boundary, and unpicked the day a second observer arrived.
- **Tiles are columns.** Observers are projected onto the ground before they are scored, so a camera 300 m up activates the tiles under it, not none ([03 §3.7](../plan/03-data-model.md#37-spatial-partition): tiles have the full vertical extent).
- **An update costs the tiles within reach, not the world.** It enumerates the tiles whose centres are within the outermost radius (one tile wider, times the observer's weight) of some observer, merges them with the tiles still active — both sorted by tile key, so the merge is tile order — scores that set and bands it. Nothing ticks per entity; a tile the ring let go of is forgotten until it comes within reach again.
- **Consumers are a table, not a base class.** A row is a name, a context, the rings the consumer acts in, and four function pointers (`activate`, `change_ring`, `deactivate`, `commit`), for the reason `sim::MaterializationHooks` is a table: a world with no placements has no placements row and costs nothing for it, and nothing in the ring names a consumer's type. **Activation and a move between rings call the consumers in registration order; deactivation calls them in reverse** — the mirror of construction, so what a later consumer built on a tile (the store's reconciliation resolves the entities the document consumer made) lets go before what it was built on. For one event, the consumers it deactivates run before the ones it activates or moves: letting go before taking. A consumer names the rings it acts in, and a tile leaving them is a deactivation *to that consumer* while it stays active in the ring — the simulation runs in the inner rings, the ruins are drawn in all of them. `commit` is called once at the end of an update in which any of the consumer's tiles changed: it is where the placements consumer hands the renderer a whole frame's worth of changes at once.
- **Between ticks, never inside one.** A tile's activation materializes and its deactivation dematerializes, and the materialization driver must be called by whoever owns the tick, outside it ([sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented), "Between ticks": the entity store's hook refuses a call while its world is deferred). So the world is not a system in the scheduler's table: the host calls `World::update` between two `SimScheduler::step()`s — engine-host's headless run once a tick, engine-view once a frame.
- **Events in tile order.** The tracked tiles are kept sorted by `tile_key` (x, then z, signed), so `TierAssignment`'s index-ordered changes are tile-ordered events: activation, move between rings or deactivation, one per tile at most per update — which is why the ring an event names orders nothing further. The same sequence of observer sets gives the same events, byte for byte ([05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay)); the tests fly a path that turns, speeds up and doubles back with a second observer coming and going, twice, and compare the bytes.

## The ring's rules and defaults

| Parameter | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| tile size | 32 m | The scene's own (its ruins' `tile_size`), E34's; the plan's 64–128 m is a game's choice. A parameter of the scene's `world` block and of a headless run |
| inner ring | 1.5 tiles (48 m), blocks | The 3 × 3 tiles round the observer's own. E34: blocks near, and **bounded by the tiles round the camera** — nine tiles of mid blocks are about 700,000 pairs, 4% of the visibility id's 2^24 — and at 48 m a 0.3 m course is still five or six pixels high at 1080p, where the block representation is worth its instances. One tile (the observer's own and its four neighbours at a tile's centre) flips a building to blocks at under 32 m, which is a pop at arm's length |
| middle ring | 8 tiles (256 m), sections with debris | Where the ruins capability's own LOD policy drops a tile's debris (`ruins::detail_for_distance`); within it a building is the kit's members and its fallen stones |
| outer ring | 24 tiles (768 m), sections without debris (`Detail::walls`) | Where E34's far building had fallen to 73 visible pairs in sections — tens of pixels. Past it the right answer is a far-tier slab ([E33](../experiments/e33-hard-surface-kits.md#what-it-decides)'s open question), not more rings |
| hysteresis | 0.15 | `TierAssignment`'s: a tile leaves a ring only 15% past its radius (7 m at the inner ring), so an observer walking along a boundary crosses it once. The tests walk one across the inner radius a metre either side forty times: 40 events with no band, 2 with it |
| budget | 8 activations, 16 deactivations an update | Tunables (`world.ring.max_activations`, `max_deactivations`, 0 unlimited), read when a host builds its ring. E35 measured a building at 17–22 µs assembled in sections and 0.24 ms laid in blocks (the section assembler it runs first and the scene instances included), and an update's activations are nearly all at the outer ring's edge, so eight are well under a millisecond of ruins on the frame that takes them (the consumer's calls were 0.05 ms at the median and 1.8 ms at the worst); a deactivation drops what it held, so twice as many. A teleport activates the nearest eight first, and the whole inner ring is among them |
| simulated rings | the inner two (256 m) | Where the document is materialized and the store reconciled: the rings a player's actions reach. A headless run says it per call (`simulated`) |

**The window an observer walks is bounded** (T191): an update walks (2 × reach + 1)² tiles round each observer, reach its outer radius plus one times its weight, and a save's `ring` (or a client's `stream`) names both, so radius 3 at weight 10^8 walked about 10^17 tiles. `valid_ring_params` refuses a radius past `k_max_reach_tiles`, 1,024 tiles (43 times the default outer radius, a window of about 3.3 million tiles), and an observer whose reach passes it observes nothing and is counted in `unreached`, as one past the world's last tile is.

A first fill — engine-view's first frame at a camera the ring has not followed, a flythrough's repeat, a headless call's start — is an update with the budget lifted (`unlimited`); a flythrough's repeat also drops every tile first (`World::clear`), because with hysteresis a ring's state depends on how it got there, and two repeats must fly the same world.

### Where an observer is

**An observer is a `WorldPos`, and a tile is chosen from it in f64** ([ADR-0053](../adr/0053-world-positions-are-f64-and-the-gpu-sees-none.md); 2026-10-06). `sim::ObserverSet` holds f64 positions ([sim](sim.md#positions-are-f64)); `tile_at` floors the f64 coordinate over the tile size, `tile_center` is an f64 point (exact: an integer and a half times the size), and the ring's enumeration window is the observer's place in tiles in f64, with each centre's offset from it — a few tiles — the only float32. Until then the ring took float32 observers, which step by a metre at 10,000 km and by 8 m at 1e8 m: its window and its scores moved with the float's grid and not with the observer. The division by the size is exact for a power of two (32 m, 64 m); for another size it rounds once, at the size of the quotient, so a point within that rounding of a tile's edge may land on either side — the same side on every machine. The world's test walks two observers along the same path, in 1024ths of a metre, from the origin and from 419 km, 10,000 km, 1e8 m and the owner's site less a tile, crossing a cell's and a tile's edge in the first seconds, and requires the same events tile for tile, moved by the site.

**Where the tiles end** (2026-10-07, roadmap W64). A tile's index is an i32 on each axis — `TileCoord`, and the same cell `ruins::TileCoord`, `scene_gen::TileCoord` and a partitioned layer's tile name, and the store's ids pack as two 32-bit halves (`store_tile`) — so the tiles end 2³¹ tiles from the origin: 6.87e10 m at 32 m tiles, half of where a 64 m cell's index ends (`world_cell_valid`, 1.37e11 m), and nearer at a smaller tile (2.1e9 m at a metre). Until now `tile_at` and the ring's window cast `floor(x / size)` to i32 unchecked, so a position `world_cell_valid` let through past that was an out-of-range conversion, undefined behaviour that x86 turns into the first tile. **The world refuses such a position where positions enter**: `TileRing::update` lets an observer through only when its whole window of tiles is i32s (`tile_reachable(position, tile_size, reach)`, the reach the window walks); one that is not observes nothing — no candidates, no score — and is counted (`RingStats::unreached`, `UpdateStats::unreached`), and `World::update` logs it once when it starts (`an observer is past the world's last tile and observes nothing`, with the last tile's distance). `tile_at` asserts the same in debug. The window's counters are i64, so a window ending on the last tile does not wrap. **Why refuse and not widen**: every format a tile is written in holds its index in 32 bits — the store's ids, a layer's tile files, the world log's events (`TileEvent` is pinned at 12 bytes), the ruins' seeds — and an i64 index would buy tiles past half a cell's reach, where ADR-0053 already says a game needs a frame above `WorldPos`. **The test** (`world: the tiles end at the i32 index, and an observer past them observes nothing`): a position half a metre inside the last tile and the first is a tile and one half a metre past each is not, each one a cell names; an observer whose window ends on the last tile holds the tiles the origin's observer holds, moved by 2³¹ − 26 tiles, event for event; one tile further it observes nothing and is counted, and beside an observer by the origin it changes nothing that one holds; ADR-0053's far sites and a cell's edge 10,000 km out are well inside at 32 m tiles and at 1 m.

**What a tile keeps of where its records are is the document's f64.** `record_position` reads the record's position property as the doubles the document holds (`worldpos`), where it read float32s, and refuses one no cell can name; `TileProjection.position` is a `worldpos` (version 2), so a tile that goes inactive 420 km out keeps its records where they are rather than on a 3.1 cm grid, and reconciliation promotes them from there. A projection written at version 1 reads as the same position (its float32 was written as the double it widens to). `SaveObserver.position` is a `worldpos` too (version 2), and engine-host moves its observers in f64 (`position += DVec3{velocity} * step`, as `kinematics::integrate` moves a cart). Both changed the migration corpus only where the store's bytes changed: the hash a second after loading `v1`, `v2` and `v3` moved, and it was the projections — with the projection's position rounded back to float32 in one experiment build, all three continued to the hashes they had before ([the corpus README](../../content/migration-corpus/README.md)).

## The consumers

**Finite placement storage survives tile changes** (2026-10-26, W73). The authored scene reader
hands over exact counts per occupied tile in `StreamedPlacements::tile_instances`, summed across
entries sharing a tile. `PlacementTiles::create` reserves that tile's instance/tag arrays, each
entry's largest tile output, the combined tail and admission scratch. A kept pool keyed by tile
exchanges these arrays with the active map on arrival and departure; a ring change or re-standing
reuses the held arrays. Storage is proportional to the finite entry's instance count, not its
largest tile multiplied by every tile. Generators without finite counts reuse returned blocks and
their entry output at their warm-up high-water marks. This removes the fresh `Placements` and
`TileBlock` vectors previously constructed at every tile build; admission order, withheld tiles,
motion keys and placement bytes are unchanged. The CPU regression checks an unseen tile's first
arrival, rebuilds, departures and reuse without allocations; the renderer's streamed-tree flight
holds the complete host/renderer path to zero in its second flight. The
[allocation hunt](../experiments/streamed-tree-allocations-2026-10-26.md) records attribution and runs.

**What that room keeps resident** (2026-10-27, W80). Every occupied tile of a finite entry holds its instance and tag arrays for the whole run, in the pool or on the active tile, so the world keeps about a second copy of the authored instances' room: `sizeof(SceneInstance)` plus one tag byte per instance, summed across entries sharing a tile, and nothing per empty tile. The scale rule is proportional to the authored instance count and independent of the ring, the tile size and how many tiles are active: 100,334 instances at the forest cost that count times the pair, a city's cost its own count times it. `PlacementTiles::room_bytes()` is the number (the pool, the active tiles and the free list; the tail and the admission scratch, which are sized by the most one commit hands on, are not in it), and engine-view's world summary reports it as `placement_room_bytes` (`TileWorldSummary` version 3). engine-host has no streamed world behind `render.*`, so it has nothing to report. `scene_order_tests.cpp` pins the number to the load's counts, unchanged across arrivals, rebuilds and departures.
Since R100 (2026-10-26), placements receive the renderer's full fixed reservation, including
terrain replacement slots, in `PlacementTilesConfig::fixed_pairs`, and its visibility ceiling in
`max_pairs`. Each admitted tile pays `renderer::reserved_tile_pairs`, the layout's rounded block
size, rather than its live pairs. Thus the consumer and the layout agree even near the ceiling:
casters may be disabled first by settings, tiles are withheld next, and a missing tile's floor
never refuses an otherwise drawable prefix. See [the accounting](renderer.md#the-pair-accounting). **The streamed trees have a budget of their own**, which the renderer applies to the tail this consumer hands it (R137): a commit whose swaying trees' records would grow their room past `renderer.flora.tail_budget_mib` (a sixteenth of the device by default) places its tiles nearest the eye first and withholds the farthest, as this budget does, counted in engine-view's `flora` summary (`tail_withheld_tiles`) rather than here; the consumer's next commit names them again and the renderer tries them again, and meanwhile the far forest draws them ([renderer](renderer.md#a-commit-past-the-tails-budget-withholds-its-farthest-tiles)).

**The placements** (`PlacementTiles`, `placement_tiles.h`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)). What a scene's placement entries — its `ruins`, its `placements` — put on a tile, whatever generators they name: `create` opens each streamed entry's generator (`SceneData::streamed`) again, found in the scene-generator registry by the name the scene gave it, and nothing here names one — the ruins are the generator `ruins`, Island City the generator `city` (a district's proxies, each tile the plan's per-tile query at the ring's detail: [city](city.md#where-it-attaches)), and the world links no capability for either (the host does). **One consumer for every entry, in the scene's order**: a tile's placements are every entry's, entry by entry as the reader walks them, and the renderer takes one tail, so the pair budget below is decided over all of it. Activation asks each entry's generator for the tile (`tile`, in the representation the tile's ring draws: the scene's `engine.scene.WorldRing::ruins` word, which the generator reads from `scene_gen::Context::world`) — for the ruins, `ruins::Assembler` for sections and walls, `ruins::BlockAssembler` for blocks, from the entry's own seed, tile, wind and ground, which is exactly what the scene reader assembles at load, so a tile streamed in is the building a whole read has on that tile (a test holds them equal, instance for instance) — maps each placement's mesh through the scene meshes the reader left resident for the entry (`StreamedPlacements::meshes`), and keeps the tile's instances, with each placement's tag (the ruins': rubble), as a block keyed by the tile. The ground is the terrain's ground provider's floor (`TerrainSampler::provider().view()`), as the scene reader's is: the surface with the waves, and with the dune generator the interdune floor the dunes migrate over ([renderer](renderer.md#scenes-camera-paths-and-flythroughs)), so a building is the same whenever its tile comes in. A move between rings asks again only when some entry on the tile draws the two rings differently (`representation`: the ruins' blocks, sections and walls). Deactivation drops the block. Its stats count the tiles each generator built by the representation it drew (`built`, `build_ns`: the ruins' 0 blocks, 1 sections, 2 walls), which engine-view's world summary reports as the ruins' `laid` and `assembled`. It replaced `RuinsTiles`, which was compiled under `ENGINE_WORLD_RUINS` with a conditional link to the ruins capability; the calls moved into the generator and the rules below did not change. `commit` hands the drawn tiles **in tile order** to a sink. **A run sink** (`set_run_sink`; engine-view's, since 2026-10-31, roadmap W81) is handed each tile's run — its key, its place in the tail, its count, its ring, **its own instances where the consumer holds them and a version**, a number every build of the tile changes and no other tile ever had — and nothing is copied; the sink makes each a `renderer::DynamicBlock` for `SceneRenderer::set_dynamic_instances` ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)), which keeps a tile whose version it holds without reading it, so a commit costs the renderer the tiles that changed and the consumer its tiles' bookkeeping. `tail()` and `tail_tags()` write the tail out of the drawn tiles when first asked after such a commit (engine-view's handover pass and a game's props ask). An **instance sink** (`set_sink`, the tests' and any host that wants one span) is handed the tail, concatenated, as before. Until W81 every commit wrote the whole tail and the renderer compared every tile of it: along the forest proof of concept's flight that was 6.3 s and 2.7 s of a 40 s path, the largest of the placements' costs ([the measurement](../experiments/forest-load-in-2026-10-31.md)). Tile order is the order a compaction lays the tiles out in; between compactions a tile's pairs are wherever its block landed, so a depth tie between two tiles' coincident surfaces goes to the one placed later (the renderer states it as the one exception to a picture that does not depend on how its instances arrived). **The pair budget is decided here too, at the commit, over every tile held**: the tail takes tiles by ring, innermost first, then by the ring's score (`TileRing::score_of`: the nearest first), then in tile order, while the scene stays within its budget (the visibility id's 2^24 by default), and none after the first that does not fit — so every tile drawn outranks every tile withheld, and the renderer is never asked for more than it can name. A withheld tile stays held, its building assembled, and is drawn at the first commit with room for it; `withheld(tile)` says which, and the stats count them. The first version decided per event instead, and events come in tile order: E35's run of the ashlar kit under the default rings then refused 132 tiles of the inner ring and 315 of the middle one while tiles at the horizon held the budget. Blocks near and sections far is E34's handover, and both sides of it are the same ruin — the block layer lays the section assembler's own footprint, ruin and openings — but nothing cross-fades two instance sets, so a building **pops** from sections to blocks when its tile crosses the inner radius: E35 measured a median 12,000–14,000 pixels of a 1080p frame at 47 m, up to 3.9%, most of it rubble the two forms laid by rules of their own; since both lay one rubble field ([ruins](ruins.md#the-rubble-rule)) it is 7,800–9,100, up to 1.5%, three quarters of it wall tops that stand a course or a height variant apart ([E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md#after-the-rubble-rule)).

**A placement's hand-off role travels with it** (G136, 2026-11-08). A generator that places one thing as two forms near the camera names them (`scene_gen::Placement::handoff`: the sward's inner ring is each patch's near form and then its card, [flora](flora.md#grass-sward-g90)), and the consumer copies the role onto the `renderer::SceneInstance` it hands on (`SceneInstance::handoff`), as it copies the tag. The ring lists themselves are unchanged — the sward's inner ring is twice its patches, its next ring its cards, nothing past it — and the hand-off is the renderer's, by distance from the eye and not by ring ([renderer](renderer.md#the-hand-off-by-distance)). A tile that moves inward keeps its block key and changes its version, which is what the renderer's load-in stamp is kept by. **The inner ring has to reach past the hand-off band's end**: a ring holds a tile by its centre's distance, so a ring of `r` tiles of 64 m reaches at least `64 r − 45.3` m from the observer, and the v36 scene's 0.75-tile inner ring reaches 2.7 m at a tile's corner and 16 m along an axis — short of the band's 29 m, where the near form still ends at the ring's edge.

**The build budget** (W82, 2026-11-01; `PlacementTiles::begin_update`/`end_update`, `world.placements.max_builds`, default 4). A camera that crosses a kilometre in under a second — the owner's v24 dash — moves every ring at once, and the ring holds no activation budget where the world draws its tiles ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)), so one update activated 577 tiles and the commit built them all on the frame's thread: 1.5 s of one frame. With a budget, a tile that activates, or moves to a ring that draws it differently, is held **due** instead of built: a new tile draws nothing (it is neither drawn nor withheld, and takes nothing of the pair budget) and a moved one keeps drawing its old form; each commit then builds at most the budget's due tiles in the pair budget's order — innermost ring, nearest, tile order — before it admits the tail, and `end_update` commits the next ones on an update whose events did not touch the consumer (a camera that stopped after a burst). **Which tiles a commit builds is a function of the tiles held, their rings, the observers and the budget, never of timing**, so the tail and its layout are too, and a flight's repeats agree; once nothing is due the tail is the unbudgeted consumer's, tile for tile (the test). A tile no entry occupies is never built at all. engine-view sets the tunable's budget for a moving camera's update and none (every tile in its event, as before) for a restart, a complete fill and the handover pass, which measures each tile's pop in its event.

**The document** (`DocumentTiles`). Activation is `sim::Materializer::materialize(document, MaterializeScope::of_tile(t))`: the records the partitioned layers file under that tile, through the scheduler's hooks, parents before children — taken from the document's tile index, so an activation costs the tile's records and not the document ([sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented)). Deactivation flushes the write-back — what systems changed goes back to the document before the entities that changed it go — and calls `Materializer::dematerialize(MaterializeScope::of_tile(t))`, the one hook that was missing: the mirror of a tile pass, added to `domain/sim` as a registration point beside `held(scope)`, which says what a tile holds ([sim](sim.md#the-driver-a-document-through-the-hooks)). A ring tile and a document tile must be one cell: a partitioned layer on another grid is refused once, with a sentence, rather than materialized by a tile that means a different square. Records in no tile are the host's to materialize once, whole (`MaterializeScope::untiled()`).

**The store** (`StoreTiles` over `WorldStore`). `WorldStore` is the first `sim::TileStore` that is not a test's fake — the seam [ADR-0028](../adr/0028-ecs-and-persistent-store.md) chose, and the gap [sim](sim.md#tile-reconciliation) recorded: `tile_state` is the tile's snapshot in `foundation/store` (known when there is one, last active at its game time; the seed is `hash_combine(world seed, tile id)`, a function of the world and the tile like everything else a tile is), and `load_records` is the tile's projections, one `EntityRecord` each, carrying no source, so a hook resolves the entity the document consumer made and creates none. Activation runs `SimScheduler::reconcile_tile` over it — step 1 from the store, step 2's gap from the snapshot, step 3's summarizers from the tile's seed, step 4 resolving, step 5 promoting by distance to the world's observers. It registers **after** the document consumer for exactly that: on activation the records are already entities when reconciliation resolves and promotes them, and on deactivation, which runs backwards, it writes the tile while they are still held. Deactivation flushes the write-back, then writes one projection per record the tile held (`engine.world.TileProjection` as canonical JSON: the record, its type, where the document says it is, its importance) and the tile's snapshot at the tick and game time it went. The projections and the snapshot are the store's own tables (`EventLog`); the world adds no schema of its own to the file. It lives in this capability rather than in `foundation/store` because what a tile is — its id, what a projection of a document record carries, when a snapshot is taken — is the world's, and the store stays the thin SQLite layer [store](store.md) says it is.

**The world seed** is the document's (W11, 2026-11-18): one `engine.world.WorldSettings` record (`world_settings.schema`, `seed: u64 = 1`) at the fixed id `world::k_world_settings` (`776f…0001`, "world.settings" and a one), which `StoreTiles` gives the store when it is bound (`document_world_seed`), before any tile is reconciled. None, or a record of another type at that id, leaves the store's seed as it was, 1. A record rather than a manifest field because the seed is content — applied, journaled, undone, merged and permitted like any — and a new type migrates nothing, where a manifest field would have moved every save in the [corpus](../../content/migration-corpus/README.md) a step; a fixed id because the document has no index by type. Read once a run; a save carries the run's (`SaveManifest.world_seed`). It holds one `u64`.

**The ground** (`GroundTiles`, `ground_tiles.h`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)). A tile of the scene's ground for every tile the ring holds, whatever ground provider the terrain names: `create` asks the provider for its tiles (`scene_gen::GroundOps::open_tiles`) and hands it the store's side — `load`, `save` and `erase` of one record a tile, under the provider's record type (`record`) — so the consumer names no ground and links none. A ground with no tiles a world could hold (the renderer's waves) is refused with a sentence. It was `TerrainTiles`, compiled under `ENGINE_WORLD_TERRAIN` with a conditional link to the terrain capability, until 2026-09-27: the store's half stayed here, the ground's half moved into the capability (`terrain::DuneTiles`, `domain/terrain/ground_tiles.h`, reached from a host that links it through `terrain::dune_tiles(consumer.tiles())`), and a record's key did not change — the projection's entity is the record type's hash and the tile, its kind the type's stable id, so an overlay a save holds from before reads now (the test pins both). **Register it after the store consumer and before the placements consumer**, so a tile's ground is built before anything stands on it. Today's one ground with tiles is the dunes', and the rest of this paragraph is theirs ([terrain](terrain.md)): the endless desert's ground, tile by tile. Activation reads the tile's **deformation overlay** from the store — one projection of kind `engine.terrain.OverlayTile` filed under the tile, keyed by an entity that names the overlay and the tile, its blob the overlay's own record of at most 33,848 bytes — and **catches it up** to the host's game time in one step (decay is a closed form, so a month away is one advance and the same bytes as a month of ticks: the test walks across two tiles, leaves for three hours, comes back five hours later and compares the overlay with one that never left), puts the tile's **lag** and its neighbours' into the lag field (a held neighbour's from its overlay, another's from its stored record's header, plus whatever its declared drifts derive), and evaluates the tile's field at that time at **the ring's resolution**: 128 cells a side in the inner ring (the overlay's 25 cm grid, where the player is), 64 in the middle and 32 beyond, because a 25 cm tile is 2.6 ms and eight of them would be a frame ([E36](../experiments/e36-desert-generator.md)). A move between rings rebuilds only when the resolution changes; deactivation advances the overlay to now and writes it back, or **erases its projection when it is empty** — a tile whose footprints are buried stores nothing — and drops the tile. Between updates the host stamps (`deform`: into every held tile the footprint covers; nothing far from everyone is kept) and advances (`advance`: every held overlay baked and decayed, and when a tile's lag moved — a pit's nudge, a drift's day — the tile and its held neighbours are rebuilt, so shared edges still agree; the test digs a pit and compares the rebuilt tiles' shared edge). The host says what time it is (`set_time`), because the world's update carries a tick and not a game time. After the store consumer because deactivation runs backwards, so the overlay's projection is written before the store consumer snapshots the tile, and the snapshot carries it. `commit` hands the tiles built since the last commit to a sink — nobody's yet: the renderer draws a world's ground from the drawn-ground consumer below, whose heights are the provider's and not these tiles' (Not yet). The drifts a tile declares come from a callback, which a host with the ruins fills from the ruins' `Drift`s; none does yet.

**The drawn ground** (`DrawnTiles`, `drawn_tiles.h`; [renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles), [ADR-0050](../adr/0050-the-ground-is-drawn-from-the-worlds-tiles.md), proposed; 2026-09-30). The tiles the renderer draws the ground from: for every tile the ring holds, its ring, and at `commit` **what changed** since the last commit — the tiles that entered or changed ring, and the ones let go (`renderer::k_tile_gone`), in the order the ring made the events — handed to the renderer's tile set (`renderer::TerrainTileSet::change_tiles`), which rebuilds only the tiles whose key changed and swaps them in one frame. Until 2026-10-03 it handed the whole set, about 14,000 tiles a commit on the endless desert, which the renderer sorted and compared and then derived every key of: 1.5 ms of the frame's thread a commit and 7.5 ms of the worker's a rebuild, for a flight that changes about 33 tiles a frame ([renderer](renderer.md#what-a-frame-waits-for)). The world's `clear` reports every tile let go, so a restart reaches the renderer as changes too. It builds nothing itself and holds nothing but the set: the heights come from the renderer's tile source (the scene's ground provider seen as tiles, `scene_gen::GroundProvider::tiles()`), the same one the walker's collision reads, so what is drawn is what is walked on. It acts in every ring, and is registered after the ground consumer where a world has one, so a tile's record is read before it is drawn, and before the placements. engine-view registers it when its scene's `world` block asks for ground tiles (`"ground": true`); the renderer's first layout, built before the world's first update, is the ring's own first-fill rule **round where the session starts** — an interactive session's `--start` or recorded start, a path's first camera, else the orbit's — so that update hands over the same set and nothing is rebuilt. (Until 2026-10-03 an interactive session's first layout was built round the orbit camera whatever `--start` said; on the endless desert that is the old erg's centre, and the owner's first flight, started 2 km from it at 100 m/s, began with a whole ring to rebuild behind its frames and outran each catch-up until he walked: `walk_view_tests.cpp`'s high start holds it.)

**The collision** (`scene_collision::SceneCollision`, a capability of its own: [scene_collision](scene_collision.md), 2026-09-28). The static bodies a walker stands on and is stopped by, for every tile its ring holds: the scene's ground as a heightfield a tile from the ground provider at a collision spacing, and what the scene's placement generators put on the tile — each streamed entry's generator asked for the tile in the inner ring's representation, or a scene read whole's instances that reach into it — as one static compound of each piece's LOD-cut proxy, in a `physics::World`. A consumer of this table like the others, and **the last of the scene's**: registered after the ground and the placements where a world has them, so a tile's collision is made from what is on it after it is there and let go before it goes. It lives in a capability of its own, requiring this one and physics, because the world must stay buildable without physics (a module that is not a capability may not link one, and a capability may link another only by declaring the edge). engine-view keeps it in **a ring of its own round the walker's feet** — one ring of 1.5 tiles — updated once a tick rather than in the camera's drawing ring once a frame, because a character's contacts are sorted by body id and body ids follow creation order, so bodies made on a frame's schedule would walk a replay somewhere else ([apps](apps.md#walking)).

**The store's tile id** is the packing engine-host's write-back events already use: x in the high 32 bits and z in the low, each as its two's complement. So a tile's events in the log and its snapshot share one id.

**A scene's own instances are an entry too** (2026-10-24, roadmap W73): a streamed scene's file instances reach the consumer as the `authored` generator's entry, the last in the scene's order, so they are budgeted with the generators' placements and none of the consumer's rules changed ([authored](authored.md)). **What the tail carries for the sway** (W74): for an entry whose ids name the whole read's instances (`StreamedPlacements::motion_key_base`, the `authored` entry's), a placement's instance carries `motion_key = base + id − 1`, the key the renderer hashes a swaying tree's phase from, so a tree sways as it would loaded whenever its tile arrives; every other entry's placements carry none and the renderer hashes their position ([renderer](renderer.md#streamed-trees-sway)). The consumer's stats now count, per ring, the tiles drawn, their instances and pairs, and the tiles withheld (`PlacementTilesStats::ring_*`), which engine-view's world summary reports with the renderer's pair accounting ([renderer](renderer.md#the-pair-accounting)).

**The placements consumer's inputs and rebuilds** (2026-10-12, roadmap G59). Besides the entry, the seed, the tile, the ring and the ground, each entry is handed **the footprints of the entries before it on the same tile** (`scene_gen::Context::earlier`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md#earlier-placements)): where each placement stands and how far its mesh reaches on the ground, measured once a mesh from the resident scene (`renderer::ground_radius`) — so a scatter layer after a ruin keeps clear of its blocks. Its pair budget is the renderer's `GpuScene::pair_limit()` in engine-view (with the ray tracing chain, what the device's geometry indices name), and it now runs beside the drawn ground: a world drawing its ground from tiles takes streamed placements since the renderer's tail has the pool and the chain ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)), and its ring takes no activation budget either way, because a layer's `far` makes an outer ring's tile cost a mask. **A moving ground**: `restand(surface_time, budget)` builds again up to `budget` held tiles still standing on another time — innermost ring first, then nearest — with the surface at that time as `Context::stand`, and hands the tail on; a generator keeps every placement's place, turn and scale and stands it on the sand where it is now ([scatter](scatter.md#a-moving-ground)). engine-view calls it every update with the renderer's ground time, four tiles an update (`k_restand_tiles`), so a full pass over the held tiles takes their count over four updates; the dunes' own levels move by fields, not by rebuilding tiles, so there is no per-tile rebuild of the ground to follow — the placements follow the surface's time instead. What a re-stood tile costs is a tile of its generators again (`PlacementTilesStats::restood`, `restand_ns`; the scatter tests measure one on the dunes).

**Placements answered from the scene** (2026-10-17, roadmap P44; `PlacementTiles::query_tile`, `entries_for`, `trim_query_tiles`). A `--game` module's `nearest_placement` ([apps](apps.md#the-game-contract)) used to search the tiles the consumer held, so past the innermost ring its answer depended on what had streamed in — a ruin found in the recording and missed in the replay. It now asks the consumer's **query cache**: a tile's placements as a function of the scene alone — the first `entries` streamed entries in the scene's order (through the last entry of the kind asked, since a later entry is handed the earlier ones' footprints and none after), in the **innermost ring's representation** (ring 0, what the player would see standing there), standing on the scene's own ground at its own time — generated on the first ask (`build` with `for_query`, the stream's stats untouched: `queried`, `query_ns`) into a `FlatMap` keyed by the tile, and kept until `trim_query_tiles` drops the tiles farther than a reach from the walker's, which bounds it at (2·reach+1)² tiles; engine-view trims to the runner's cap, 512 m, so the fixture's 6 m look every tick and 400 m look every second share one cache. **Never the held block**, even in ring 0: a held tile is re-stood on the moving sand a few tiles an update, so its heights depend on when it was built, where the query's do not. engine-view's search goes out one square of tiles at a time and stops at the first square whose nearest possible point is farther than the best found (or the radius), so a query with a ruin 50 m away generates the tiles within about 80 m and no more. A query tile is never drawn, so it skips the pair count.

**The order** (`add_scene_consumers`, `scene_consumers.h`; [ADR-0040](../adr/0040-the-tile-ring.md) decision 2, [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md) decision 3). A host registers the document consumer, then the store consumer, then the scene's ground, then its placements; activation and a move between rings call them in that order, deactivation in reverse. The document before the store is ADR-0040's: reconciliation resolves the entities the materialization made, and a tile's projections are written while they are still held. The ground after the store, so a tile's ground record is written on deactivation before the store snapshots the tile. **The placements after the ground**, so a tile's ground is built before anything stands on it and outlives what stood on it — the reader's order too, which makes the terrain's provider before it opens the first entry ([renderer](renderer.md#scenes-camera-paths-and-flythroughs)). The last two are one function, `add_scene_consumers(world, ground, placements)`, so no host spells that half of the order; either may be null (a ground with no tiles, a scene with no streamed entry), and engine-view, whose world keeps no store, passes no ground. **The drawn ground** goes after the ground and before the placements where a world has them: a tile's record is read before the tile is drawn, and it is drawn before anything is placed on it (engine-view's world registers it alone, since a scene of ground tiles has no streamed placements yet). Within the placements consumer the entries are asked in the scene's order, the `ruins` entries and then `placements`, as the reader walks them. ADR-0040 as written registered the ruins consumer first, before the document; nothing rested on it — the ruins touched neither the document nor the store — and the ground's two rules put the placements last. **A collision consumer** ([scene_collision](scene_collision.md)) goes after all of them, for the placements' own reason: it is made from what the ground and the placements put on the tile, and it goes before they do. engine-view's walker registers it alone, in a ring of its own ([The consumers](#the-consumers)). `scene_order_tests.cpp` registers a ground and a placement generator of its own, streams one tile of a scene with two entries, then the same scene with the entries swapped, and holds the log each time: the ground, then the entries in the scene's order, and on the way out the tile's placements gone before its ground.

## A tile's meshes: residency by reference

**A mesh comes in with the first tile that names it and leaves after the last** (R142, 2026-11-10; [ADR-0061](../adr/0061-a-tiles-meshes-arrive-with-the-tile.md)). A placement generator's `tile` hands, with its placements, the meshes only that tile names ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md#a-tiles-meshes)): each a recipe for the mesh generator its path names, or a file. `TileMeshes` (`tile_meshes.h`) is the table of them the placements consumer keeps, one entry per distinct mesh (`renderer::placement_mesh_identity`: the generator and the recipe's bytes, so two tiles — or two paths — handing one recipe share one entry):

- **Built when first named.** `acquire` starts the build — `renderer::load_placement_mesh`, which is the load's own per-mesh pass: the mesh generator registry, the derived-data cache under the load's options — on the job system's efficiency pool when the host offers one (`connect_scene_meshes(placements, renderer, jobs)`; engine-view makes a pool of two for a scene with a mesh room), and where the tile is built when it offers none. **Nothing is built when the scene is opened**: a region nobody is near costs its entry.
- **Resident while a held tile names it.** A commit admits a built mesh into the renderer's mesh room ([renderer](renderer.md#meshes-that-come-and-go)), the nearest tile's first, and the host's copy of its streams goes. A mesh wanted again after it was let go comes back out of the cache: a read, not a solve.
- **Let go after the last.** A mesh no held tile names lingers for `world.meshes.linger` commits (120: two seconds of a moving camera at 60 Hz) and is retired after the tail that no longer names it has been handed on; with 0 it goes in the commit that let its last tile go. A lingering mesh never holds room against one a tile wants: it is the first thing evicted.

**An entry is a mesh slot.** The scene keeps a fixed number of slots after its own meshes, and an entry takes one when it is first named and keeps it until it goes, so a tile's instance names its scene mesh — `first_mesh + slot` — from the moment it is built, before the mesh is, and nothing is renumbered when the mesh arrives. Which slot a mesh gets depends on the order tiles came in; nothing drawn does ([renderer](renderer.md#meshes-that-come-and-go), the order rule).

**A tile waits for its meshes, and draws what it drew.** A build that names a mesh not yet resident is kept beside what the tile draws now (`TileBlock::held_*`): the tile goes on drawing its last ring's representation — nothing, for a new tile — with the pairs and the version of that, until a commit has every mesh in, and from that commit it draws the build. So no frame is handed an instance of a mesh that is not there (the renderer refuses such a tail by name, and `tile_meshes_tests.cpp` counts none over a walk), and a ring change opens no hole. A tile whose generators hand no mesh never waits; a scene with no mesh room does not stage at all, and builds in place as it always did.

**Why a hysteresis, and which.** The ring already keeps a tile through a band of its radius (`WorldRings.hysteresis`, 15 %), which is what stops a *tile* flipping when a walker paces a border. The linger is for the turns wider than that band: without it every mesh behind a walker who turns back is read and uploaded again. It is counted in commits, not seconds, so what is resident is a function of the updates a world has had and never of a clock — and it cannot change the picture, since an unnamed mesh draws nothing.

**The budget, and what is withheld.** The room's cluster arena is the budget (`renderer.meshes.budget_mib`, 256 MiB by default; `TileMeshConfig::clusters`). A commit asks for the waiting tiles' meshes in the pair budget's order — innermost ring, nearest, tile order. When the room has no run for one, room is made first from what nobody names (the lingering meshes, longest unnamed first) and then from **the farthest tile past the one asking that holds a mesh in the room** — drawn, or waiting with some of its build's meshes in or the meshes of what it drew before — which gives every mesh it names up, draws nothing, is built again at a later commit and waits in its turn (`PlacementTilesStats::mesh_yielded`). **Until W118** (T131, 2026-11-13) only a drawn tile that was not waiting gave its meshes up. A ring step rebuilds every tile it moves into a ring that draws it differently, and each of those waits — its new form's meshes partly in, its old form's held for what it draws — so after the first step of a walk at the room's size nothing past the first fill had anything left to give: G191's walk at the forest's deposit density, `far_rings` 3 in the default room, drew no deposit after its first fill ([rockfall deposits](../experiments/rockfall-deposits.md#what-a-walker-sees-in-a-streamed-host)). A waiting tile with nothing in the room (its meshes still building, or built and not admitted) is passed over, since it would be built again for nothing. `tile_admission_tests.cpp` holds it: a room of three meshes, tiles of two whose recipes are their ring's, a ring step, and the observer's tile drawn in its new ring's form within a commit — where on the base it stayed empty for as long as the walker stood there. On G191's scene walked at 4.5 m/s the base drew 88 deposit instances after its first fill and none from the fifteenth second on; the branch draws 88 to 96 to the end ([the pair](../experiments/tile-work-off-the-frame-2026-11-13.md)). When neither is left the asking tile is the first withheld, and every waiting tile after it in the order is withheld with it, so **every tile drawn outranks every tile the room withholds**, whatever order they arrived in (`mesh_starved`). It is said once per change in the log, with the bound: `the mesh room withholds tiles' meshes withheld=… bound=renderer.meshes.budget_mib resident_meshes=… resident_clusters=… room_clusters=… resident_mib=…`. A mesh larger than `renderer.meshes.max_clusters` is refused alone, by name, and its tile draws the rest.

**The frame's allowance.** A budgeted commit (`begin_update` with a budget) admits at most `world.meshes.admissions` meshes (2) and `world.meshes.admission_kib` (4,096) of staged bytes — a mesh larger than all of it goes alone — and collects only the builds that have finished; the rest wait a commit, their tiles drawn as before. A whole commit (budget 0: a restart, a capture's fill) waits for every build and admits everything the room has, so an offscreen capture is of the settled scene. `end_update` commits while a tile waits, a build is out or a mesh lingers, as it does for due tiles (W82), because a camera that stopped touches no consumer.

**A mesh waits for its textures, and they are admitted inside the same allowance** (R147, 2026-11-11; [texture](texture.md#textures-that-arrive-after-the-load)). For a host that draws (`TileMeshSink::texture` set, which `connect_scene_meshes` does), a mesh's build also finds, builds and reads the `.tex` of every image its materials sample — skipping the read of one the scene says it holds — and `admit` puts them in before the mesh: one at a time, in the mesh's order, each inside the commit's bytes (the textures' and the meshes' are one count, and a texture larger than all of it goes alone), so at the default a 2K set is three commits and its mesh the fourth. The table holds a reference on each texture from the commit that put it in until its mesh is admitted — the mesh then holds its own, and the scene gives those back when the mesh is retired, so the mesh's linger is its textures' — and gives them back if the mesh is let go while it waits. A texture already in the scene (the load's, or another tile's mesh's) costs nothing, and such a mesh arrives in one commit. **Why it waits**: a textured material's constants are white, so a rock admitted first would be a white rock that turns brown. A refused texture — the scene's pairs or bytes, `renderer.textures.slots` and `renderer.textures.budget_mib` — ends the wait: what the mesh's earlier textures held goes back, the mesh is admitted with its materials' constants, and the log says so once per change with the bound's name (`TileMeshStats::texture_refusals`, beside `textures_uploaded`, `texture_bytes` and `textures_shared`). `tile_mesh_textures_tests.cpp` holds the pace, the references and the refusal with no device (0.5 s).

**What it costs** (`TileMeshStats`): the builds and their time on whichever thread ran them, the admissions and the sink's time on the committing thread, the resident meshes, clusters and reserved bytes, the host bytes of built meshes not yet admitted, and the counts of what was evicted, refused, deferred and failed.

Measured in `msvc-debug` beside other agents' builds, so upper bounds: `tile_meshes_tests.cpp`'s five cases, which build or read some ninety fixture rocks of 200 to 512 triangles and run about a hundred commits, take 1.2 s together, 0.5 s the longest.

**The collision** builds a tile's compound from every placement the generator makes, the ones of a mesh the tile handed included (R145): a proxy keyed by the mesh's identity, built from the recipe through `renderer::load_placement_mesh` — no device, the cache entry this table reads — and resident by the collision's own ring, released after the last of its tiles ([scene_collision](scene_collision.md#a-tiles-meshes)). The two residencies share nothing but the cache: a walker's ring is a tile and a half, the drawn rings are hundreds of metres, and neither waits for the other.

## A tile's trees are seated

G201 stage 2, 2026-11-12 ([renderer](renderer.md#a-tree-seated-by-its-footprint)). `PlacementTiles::build` seats every placement of a streamed entry whose resident mesh carries a seat (`SourceMesh::seat`, no fit) on the scene's own ground at its own time (`ground_`, the sampler the generators stand on), with the scene's holes left out, by `scene_gen::seat_on_ground` — the function `load_scene` seats a whole read's with — so a tree hangs over no tread's edge, bank or cutting whichever ring builds its tile, and stands where the whole read stood it whatever order the tiles come in. The `authored` entry's placements are skipped (`StreamedPlacements::seated`): the load seated the file's grounded instances before it handed them on. A tile's own meshes are not seated (their seat is not known before they are built). The stats count `seated` and `lowered` over the run and the seating's own `seat_ns`, inside `tiles_ns`. Test: `tests/seat_tiles_tests.cpp`, four 8 m tiles of 256 trees each over a bank of the test's own, every tree to the bit and the slope the whole read's.

## A placement's form by ring

G197, 2026-11-13 ([renderer](renderer.md#a-placements-form-by-ring)). `PlacementTiles::generate` draws a placement of an entry's resident mesh as what the load's table says its tile's ring draws it as (`renderer::pick_ring_form` over `SceneData::ring_forms`): the mesh itself, a cut of its DAG for the ring's floor, a form it offers, or — under `renderer.forms.min_px` at the ring's floor — nothing. **`generate` may run on the build job** ([below](#builds-off-the-frames-thread)), and the pick is within what it may do there: a pure read of a table the load filled and nothing writes after, so it answers alike on the job and on the committing thread, as `occupies` and `representation` do. It is asked for a resident mesh alone; a tile's own mesh, which carries `k_handed | k` until `install`, is passed through. A placement drawn as nothing keeps its footprint in `earlier`, so the entries after it place the same in every ring. A tile's own meshes and the `authored` entry (`StreamedPlacements::own_forms`) are untouched.

**A ring change redraws only where the forms differ** (`redraws`; rewritten in place, [below](#a-ring-change-rewrites-the-forms-in-place)): beside the generator's own `representation`, `renderer::same_ring_forms` compares the two rings' entries of every resident mesh of every entry on the tile, for every scale. **The hold-off is the ring's own band** (`WorldRings.hysteresis`, 15% of a ring's radius): a tile leaves a ring only past its radius times 1.15, so a walker pacing a ring's edge moves a tile across it once, and forms are only ever chosen at a ring change; no second band is kept here, since a second band would mean a tile in ring *r* drawing a form cut for another ring's floor. A move outward that is not built at once (a build budget, a due tile) draws the finer form farther, which costs pairs and never the picture; a move inward not yet built draws the coarser form up to a quarter tile nearer than its floor, the margin the floor is cut with.

Until G202 a form change called the tile's generators again (on T131's job, the tile drawing its old forms until the build was installed), because the block kept only what it drew; on the engine's litter scene that took a walk's builds from 34 tiles to 73, and it read every instance of every such tile in the renderer, since a rebuilt block of the same count is compared instance by instance (`engine_view`'s "a streamed world's hand-over reads only the tiles it changed" came out 440 where it wants 0). Measured with it ([renderer](renderer.md#a-placements-form-by-ring)): on the forest, where every tile already holds an entry that draws its rings differently, both binaries built the same 193 tiles.

### A ring change rewrites the forms in place

G202, 2026-11-14. **A ring change between two rings every entry on the tile draws as the same representation is never a build** (`rebuilds`: some entry's `representation` differs, or it has none; `redraws` is that or a form differing). The placements are the same in both rings — that is what one representation promises — so `change_ring` rewrites what the tile keeps (`reform`), on the committing thread, with no generator asked:

- **The block keeps what its placements were picked from** (`TileBlock::forms`): beside each drawn instance the scene mesh it was picked from (4 bytes a drawn placement — its scale is its own `transform.scale`, which is the placement's), and every placement its ring draws as nothing, whole — the instance with its source mesh, its tag and its place among the drawn ones (`sizeof(SceneInstance)` 152 + 5 bytes). A village of 64 tiles of 1,000 placements, all drawn, keeps 256 KB more; one whose far ring draws half of them as nothing keeps its 32,000 unseen at about 5 MB, which is what a tile that could be drawn again without its generators costs. `generate` fills it (on the job or in place), `install` and a refused mesh's drop keep it parallel, and `room_bytes` counts it; the finite entries' room reserves the 4 bytes a placement at load, so an arrival allocates nothing. **A tile whose ring draws every placement as nothing is kept** (it draws nothing, holds no pairs and is not handed), so a move back inward rewrites it too.
- **The rewrite is `generate`'s answer for the new ring**, instance for instance: every kept placement in the tile's order, asked `pick_ring_form` with its own scale; a tile's own mesh and a mesh with no table draw themselves, and so does **a placement with a hand-off role** (`SceneInstance::handoff`, the sward's near form and its card) in every ring, in `generate` and in the rewrite alike, since its thinning by distance is the pool pass's (G204's ask; roles occur only in ring 0 today). For that to hold, **a placement is seated by its source mesh's seat in every ring** (a cut carries its source's seat and fit, so nothing seated moves; a placement drawn as nothing is now seated too, on the job).
- **What changed is said, not found.** A rewrite that changes nothing keeps the version, so the renderer reads none of it. One that changes something takes a new version and records, for the next commit, the version the run sink was last handed (`TileBlock::handed`) and the indices whose instance is not what was handed — the ones whose form moved, and every index from the first placement that came into or left the drawn ones (two rewrites before a hand-over add up; a tile built since its hand-over is all changed). `TileRange::from_version`, `changed` and `changed_count` carry it, and `renderer::DynamicBlock` takes it ([renderer](renderer.md#a-placements-form-by-ring)): a block held at that version with the same count is not compared, and is rewritten in place from its first changed instance, the slots before it untouched.
- **On which thread**: the rewrite is a table lookup and a copy a placement, on the committing thread where the ring change arrives, as `install`'s hand-over is; no generator is asked, so T131's rule — generators on the job — is kept. A tile due for a build keeps its ask (the serial does not move): built later, it is built for the ring it is in then; built by a job in flight for the ring it left, the commit that collects it rewrites it for the ring it is in (`collect`). A tile whose build waits for its meshes rewrites the build and draws what it held meanwhile. `restand` still builds, since the ground moved.

Counted in `PlacementTilesStats::reformed`, `reformed_instances` and `reform_ns`, and in engine-view's world log (`TilePlacementsFrame` version 3: `reformed`, `reformed_instances`, `reform_ms`, and the renderer's `told`). **What an imported or hand-authored world gets**: the same, for any entry whose generator declares one representation for two rings (the `authored` entry draws its meshes' own forms and is untouched); a scene with no ring-form table keeps 4 bytes a placement and rewrites nothing.

Test: `tests/ring_forms_tests.cpp` — the rule over a synthetic mesh (cuts, the scale bound, `min_px`, an offered form), and a streamed tile of a generator of the test's own drawn in ring 0 and ring 2: the same sixteen places, twelve of them the ring's cut and the four scaled past 1.5 the mesh itself, fewer pairs. G202's case walks one tile through rings 0, 1, 2, 3 and back to 2 with one `tile` call: 0 → 1 keeps the version and names nothing; 1 → 2 names the version handed and exactly the twelve indices that changed, and is what a build for ring 2 makes, instance for instance; ring 3 draws all sixteen as nothing and keeps the tile; 3 → 2 draws ring 2's sixteen again. `apps/engine_view/tests/ring_forms_view_tests.cpp` flies the litter scene with the forms on and off: the same tiles built, rewrites in place, the renderer told and comparing none.

## Builds off the frame's thread

T131, 2026-11-13. The owner's walk over the forest at 2.5 to 4.5 m/s had 226 frames over 50 ms in two minutes, and a spike frame's record is a backlog of due tiles drained four a commit on the frame's thread — `world.tiles_ms` 18 to 40 ms a frame for seconds at a time while the due count falls from 750 to none, several times a minute (`frames.jsonl` of the walk, read as text). So **with a job system (`PlacementTiles::set_build_jobs`) and a budgeted update, no generator is asked for a tile on the committing thread**:

- **A tile's build is two halves.** `generate` asks the entries' generators for the tile and writes its instances, tags and the meshes it handed; it reads only what `create` made and the entries' own state — no stat, no mesh table, no member scratch — so it is one function wherever it runs. `install` names the handed meshes in the table, in the order handed (so their slots and the tile's references are what a build in place gave), gives their instances those slots, and decides what the tile draws or whether it waits, on the committing thread. A build in place calls the two one after the other.
- **One job at a time** on the job system's efficiency pool builds the first `world.placements.max_builds` due tiles (four) in the pair budget's order, one after another; each slot carries the ring and the serial its tile was due with. **An entry's generator is asked for one tile at a time**, as on the frame's thread — an entry's state is its own scratch (`Placements`, plans held for reuse), and `PlacementGeneratorDesc` promises nothing about two calls at once — and `occupies` and `representation`, which take a `const void*` state and read what `open` made, may be asked on the committing thread while the job runs `tile`. The job copies the meshes each tile handed out of its entry's scratch before the next tile overwrites it.
- **A commit hands over what the last job finished and starts the next.** Each tile still held and still due with the serial the job answered is staged, given the job's instances — copied into its own room, so what the world keeps resident is the tiles' and never the job's — and installed; a tile let go, or made due again (a ring change, a yield, a re-stand) while it was built, is not, and its next job builds it (`discarded`). This is the frame's whole share of a tile's building: a copy and a few table lookups a tile, four tiles at most a commit, counted as `collect_ns` and inside `tiles_ns` (`world.tiles_ms` in the frame record), with the generators' own time on the worker in `worker_ns`.
- **A whole update builds where it is** (`begin_update(0)`: a restart, a complete fill, the handover pass): it waits for a job in flight, takes what it built, and builds the rest in place, so a capture and a restart see the same tail either way and a whole update's tail is what it always was. So does anything else that asks a generator on this thread — a query's tile (`query_tile`, which waits for the job), a build in an event with no budget, `create`, the destructor. `restand` with a job system makes its tiles due instead of building them, standing on the surface at its time, and keeps the surface a job in flight stands on until it is collected.
- **Which tiles a budgeted commit hands over now depends on timing**, as which meshes it admits already did ([A tile's meshes](#a-tiles-meshes-residency-by-reference)): a slow job's tiles arrive a commit or several later. What a tile *is* does not — its instances are `generate`'s, bit for bit, whichever thread ran it — and a whole update is unchanged. `tile_admission_tests.cpp` holds both: a budgeted walk across a field of tiles on a pool asks no generator on the walking thread, and settles on the tail of the same walk built in place, instance for instance and byte for byte.
- **engine-view's window fills its first frame the same way** (`ViewWorld::Mode::Fill`, `ViewWorld::window_mode`): the ring is filled round the camera with no budget, as before, but its placements are a budgeted update's, built off the frame's thread and drawn as they arrive, nearest first; a run that captures (`--capture`, a marker's capture) still fills whole, since its picture is of the settled scene. Until 2026-11-13 the first frame waited for every tile: 16 to 64 s on G191's deposits walk, 1.9 s on the engine's cap scene warm. engine-view makes a pool of its own for any scene with streamed placements (one performance worker, one efficiency worker, three with a mesh room) and hands the admission's tunables to the consumer. Its run sink gives back the tail's reserve, which `create` makes for every authored instance (64.6 MiB committed and 0.1 touched on the forest staging): a run sink's tail is written only when `tail()` is asked, and grows to what the drawn tiles hold.
- **One behaviour moved with it**: a placement whose mesh the table refused (no slot left) still hands the entries after it its footprint. Until now it left none, so what a later entry placed depended on what was resident; a tile is now a function of the scene and the tile alone. A scene with room for its meshes is unchanged.

**Measured** ([the pair](../experiments/tile-work-off-the-frame-2026-11-13.md), three engine scenes at the 2^24 cap and the default mesh room, a window walking 4.5 m/s, `msvc-release`, a shared machine): a window's first frame 1,946 ms → 55 ms; the tile work left on the frame's thread at the 99th percentile 1.59 → 0.50 ms and 5.32 → 0.86 ms; the most one commit's hand-over took 1.4 and 2.8 ms; no frame with 10 ms of tile builds, where the base had one each. What stays is the renderer's: the tight compactions at the cap, and one relayout after a fill that arrives over frames.

**What an imported or hand-authored world gets** (the engine stays general purpose): all of it, keyed on nothing a generator writes. A scene of imported static meshes is the `authored` entry ([authored](authored.md)), a placement generator like any other, so its tiles are built on the job and admitted by the rule below; a scene with no streamed entry makes no pool and no job and costs nothing. What a job costs is the scene's own: a tile of the authored entry is a copy of the file's instances for that tile.

## No trading at the pair cap

T131, 2026-11-13. On the same walk the pair budget sat at its cap with a tile let go and another drawn in its place every few frames — 1,608 withholding lines in the log, and 33 of the 60 worst frames rebuilding the scene's tables (`cpu.tables` 17 ms) — because the rule ranked every held tile on every commit and admitted the ranked prefix that fit: a departure behind the walker freed room before the arrival ahead was built, a tile at the horizon took it, and the arrival took it back a few commits later; two tiles of one ring a metre apart in score swapped places whenever the walker moved. **The rule now**, over the same order (ring, then score, then tile order):

- **A drawn tile keeps its place against one less than a tile nearer** (`world.placements.admit_bias`, 1 tile): it is ranked as if it stood `admit_bias × tile_size` nearer than it does, so a small move reorders nothing, and a tile must be a whole tile nearer to displace it.
- **A tile ranked before the farthest drawn tile is admitted while it fits**, as before: an arrival beside the observer displaces the farthest drawn tile at once.
- **A tile ranked past every drawn tile grows the drawn set, and only with room to spare**: the budget must still have `world.placements.admit_spare_pairs` free after it (0, the default: a sixty-fourth of what the tiles may hold, the cap less the fixed prefix — 246,519 pairs of the 2^24 with a prefix of a million), so room a departure frees is not filled from the horizon only to be taken back.
- **A tile the budget let go waits before it grows the set again** (`world.placements.admit_hold_off`, 120 commits: two seconds at 60 Hz).
- None is admitted after the first that is not, so **no withheld tile outranks a drawn one by more than the bias**, which is the invariant `placement_tiles_tests.cpp` holds (it held "none at all" until this change).

The stats count the drawn tiles the budget let go (`released`) and of them the ones drawn again while still held (`returned`: a trade), and engine-view's world log has both per update and over the run (`TilePlacementsFrame` version 2, `TileWorldSummary` version 4). `tile_admission_tests.cpp` walks a field of tiles east at the cap, an eighth of a tile an update with a quarter tile's sway either side and two builds a commit, and counts the trades: the rule it replaced, run on the same walk (no bias, no hold-off, no spare), traded 172 times and let 206 tiles go; this one trades none and lets 29 go. On the engine's cap scenes the budget let 223 and 297 tiles go along a walk, and 68 and 39 with the rule, none of them drawn again while held ([the pair](../experiments/tile-work-off-the-frame-2026-11-13.md)). None of it moves the cap: the budget is the owner's, and the rule decides only which tiles stand under it.

## A hand-off placement is held by its band

G205, 2026-11-15. **A placement with the near hand-off role holds pairs only while the band it is drawn in can reach it.** A generator that places one thing as two forms near the camera names them ([above](#the-consumers), G136), and the renderer draws the near form only short of the band's end, a distance from the eye ([renderer](renderer.md#the-hand-off-by-distance): 29 m by default). But a tile is drawn over a whole ring. On the forest staging at the owner's eye the inner ring's five tiles held 5,181,536 pairs, 5,083,393 of them the sward's near forms: each one-metre patch placed as its near form and its card over 20,480 m², of which the band covers 2,640. The rest were placed, numbered and dispatched to the cull pass, which dropped every one of them whole. No far form reaches them ([G204](renderer.md#a-swaying-meshs-form-by-ring)), and they are what held the scene at the 2^24 cap.

**Why a patch is 277 pairs.** A pair is an instance and a cluster of its mesh's whole DAG. The engine's tall-sod patch is 3,840 triangles in 86 leaf clusters of about 45 — blades share no vertices, so a cluster fills its 64 vertices long before its 124 triangles — under ten coarser levels that hold 191 more, because disconnected blades simplify badly (a connected mesh's DAG is about twice its leaves; this one is 3.2 times). The forest's patches are 180 to 277 clusters for 2,560 to 3,840 triangles, 245 a patch on average.

**What was weighed**, on the forest's inner ring (5.08 M pairs of near forms):

| | pairs | the picture | the ray tracing chain, the pool pass, the other readers |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) near placements handed by a cell finer than a tile, only where the band reaches | about a fifth (measured below) | unchanged: every instance left out is one the cull pass dropped whole | the chain's demand is the visible entries and does not move; its top-level records, and the estimate its first capacities are sized by, fall with the instances. The pool pass thins by part inside the band, where every instance still is. The collision and a game's queries ask the generators themselves and see no difference |
| (b) a near patch of fewer pairs: clusters of up to 255 vertices | 86 leaves become about 30 and a patch about 100 pairs: 1.9 M, still over the whole ring | every cut inside the band changes, so it is a picture change to measure | the chain builds a third as many, larger cluster structures for the same blades, which is a gain of its own where the sward fills the chain's budget; the pool's blocks are per visible entry, so fewer and larger; the thinning is by part and does not change |
| (b') a near patch without the DAG levels the band never selects | 10 to 20% fewer, by the level sizes | unchanged at the session's projection | none |
| (c) larger patches at the same density | the same: a 2 m patch is four times the clusters on a quarter of the instances | unchanged if the blades are | a quarter of the instances, top-level records and bottom-level structures; no help at the cap |

So (a) is built here, and (b) is a row of its own that multiplies with it; it lives in the cluster builder and flora's tables.

**The rule** (`handoff_cells.cpp`; `PlacementTilesConfig::handoff_*`, the tunables `world.placements.handoff_cell_m` 4, `handoff_margin_m` 1, `handoff_hold_m` 2, `handoff_lead_updates` 2). When a build becomes what its tile draws (`finalize`), its placements with the near role and a resident mesh are set apart (`split_near`): the tile's instances become its other placements in the tile's order and then the near ones, grouped by the cell each stands in. Each cell keeps the box of its placements' origins grown by each one's **reach** — how far from its origin the cull pass can find it: the farthest any cluster's sphere of its mesh reaches, in three dimensions, plus the padding a swaying mesh's spheres are tested with, times its scale — and its pairs. At every commit, and at `end_update` when no event committed, each cell is asked (`refresh_cells`): it is **in** when its box is within the band's end, the margin and the lead of an eye on the ground plane, and stays in until it is the hold farther. The eyes are the ring's observers (`TileRing::observers`) or the ones a host names (`set_handoff_eyes`, for a camera that is not an observer); with neither, every cell is in. The lead is what the eyes moved in the last update times `handoff_lead_updates`, at most the band's end: slack for a host whose world update runs a frame behind its camera.

**Why the picture is the one it was.** The cull pass drops a near form's pair whose sphere lies wholly at or past the band's end, measured from the eye in three dimensions. The ground distance to a cell's box is never more than that distance to any sphere of any placement in it, so a cell that is out holds only pairs the cull would drop. A cell that is in is drawn as it always was. The pair numbers move — a pair is a place in the tail's layout — and with them the one thing the renderer already says depends on how instances arrive: a depth tie between two coincident surfaces of different blocks goes to the one placed later, and a tile's near placements are now blocks of their own.

**What a cell is to the renderer.** A run of its own under a key of its own (`TileRange`, `make_runs`): the tile's own run first, under the tile's key, then each of its cells that is in, so a cell that comes or goes writes its own slots and nothing else, and the tile's own run keeps its version for as long as the tile is not built again — the renderer reads none of it. **A cell is as old as its tile** (`TileRange::part_of`, `renderer::DynamicBlock::age_of`): the hand-off's load-in fades a block's blades in over thirty frames from the change that wrote it, and a cell that comes into reach is not new — its tile held those placements all along — so its block takes the stamp its tile's block holds. Without that a cell arriving while the walker accelerates would still be fading in when the band reached it. The pair budget counts what the layout reserves: a tile's own run and each cell's (`admit`), so a withheld tile hands no cell and the cap is decided over what is handed.

**The keys.** A cell's run is named from the world's first column of tiles, x = −2^31 (`cell_key_reserved`), numbered as cells come; the consumer holds no tile of that column while the cells are on, so no key is ever both. The column is 1.4 × 10^11 m west of the origin at 64 m tiles.

**On which thread, and what it costs.** The split is a copy of the build's instances on the committing thread, where `install` already copies them, once a build: nothing for a tile with no near role but a read of each instance's role. The cells' look is a box test a cell, each update. A commit is made only when a cell came or went. No generator is asked, on any thread, for a cell that comes: the tile kept it.

**What an imported or hand-authored world gets** (the engine stays general purpose): the same, keyed on the role and nothing else — any placement entry whose placements carry `scene_gen::k_handoff_near`, in any ring. **A placement with a hand-off role keeps its mesh** (`generate`, `reform`): the ring-form rule passes it by, since the two forms hand over by distance inside one ring and the pool pass that thins them does not run for a form. A scene with no role keeps no cell and hands each tile as one run.

**What it does not do.** A near placement of a tile's own mesh stays with its tile (its reach is not known before the mesh is built). A finite entry's cells are not reserved at load, so a tile of one that carries the role allocates them at its first arrival. A tile whose near placements span more than 4,096 cells at the configured size is grouped by a cell twice as large, and again, until they fit. The split could move to the build's job with `generate`; it waits for the block's layout to settle (G202). `world.placements.handoff_cell_m=0` hands a tile's near placements with the tile, whole, as before.

## The hosts

**engine-view** (`--world`, or a scene file's `world` block; `apps/engine_view/world_view.cpp`). The scene reader leaves the file's placement entries to the world — each generator's resident meshes are loaded (for the ruins, both kits'), so the renderer has every mesh a tile can ask for resident, and no building is assembled — and engine-view updates the ring from the camera before every frame: in a flythrough from that frame's own camera (`renderer::FlightOptions::before_frame`), in the windowed loop from the previous frame's, because the loop knows a frame's camera only after it has begun it and the tail changes only between frames. The tail goes to the renderer **tile by tile**: `ViewWorld::hand_over` names each tile's run of it (`PlacementTiles::tile_ranges`) as a `renderer::DynamicBlock` keyed by the tile, so the renderer keeps each tile in a block of its own and a change costs it the tiles that changed ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)); the hand-over after a restart asks the renderer to lay the tiles out from scratch, so every repeat of a flythrough starts from the layout the first fill had. A `--game` module's props go over as **one more block after the tiles'**, keyed `view::k_game_block_key` (`ViewWorld::set_extra_block`; [apps](apps.md#props-the-objects-a-game-places)): every hand-over carries the live props as they stand, and a frame on which a prop changed and no tile did hands the same tail over again (`hand_over_now`), so the tiles' blocks are untouched either way. `--world-log <f>` writes one `engine.world.TileFrame` per update and the run's `engine.world.TileWorldSummary` last; `--world-handover <f>` flies the path again after a benchmark, untimed, and for every tile an update moves to another representation draws the frame twice — as the update left it, and with only that tile's instances put back as they were (`PlacementTiles::tile_ranges` says where a tile's instances sit in the tail, and a capture's instance slots are turned back into places in the tail with `GpuScene::tail_indices` before a tile's pixels are counted, since the renderer keeps a tile wherever its block is) — and writes what differs (`engine.world.TileHandover`): pixels by how far their depth moved, and how many of them are the tile's rubble rather than its walls (`PlacementTiles::tail_tags` marks each instance of the tail). Everything else the update did is in both pictures, so the difference is that tile's pop alone. A streamed world is rigid and rasterized in v0: `--raster rt`, `--shadows rt`, `--deform`, `--animate`, `--morph-animate`, `--reference` and `--verify-occlusion` are refused with `--world` ([apps](apps.md#engine-view---world-a-streamed-scene)). **The drawn ground** (a world block's `"ground": true`, or `--terrain-tiles`): `ViewWorld` registers `DrawnTiles` before the placements, with the renderer's tile set as its sink and the ring's radii and cells taken from the tile set's own description (`DrawnTiles::ring_params`), so the ring the world keeps and the rings the tile set was sized for are one, and with nothing else to stream it lifts the ring's budget, since the drawn ground's events are a hash map's writes and the renderer batches the rebuilds they cause on its own worker (the default eight activations an update fell behind a camera at 100 m/s: [renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles), "Which tiles"); the update runs before the time-lapse's frame, so the tiles a frame asks the tile set to rebuild are the ones the ring holds after that frame's update. A build without this capability follows the camera by the ring's first-fill rule instead (`renderer::terrain_tiles_round`): no hysteresis and no budget, the tiles a first fill holds. Since 2026-10-05 that rule takes the camera's `WorldPos` and measures each tile's centre from it in f64, so it is the same rule 10,000 km out; since 2026-10-06 the ring does the same ("Where an observer is", below), so the rule and the ring agree on every tile but one whose centre is within half a float step of a ring's radius, and the rule's float32 overload that the ring's tests used to compare with is gone. The tiles it draws are instances at their corners ([renderer](renderer.md#the-grounds-tiles-are-placed-at-their-corners)).

**engine-host** (`session.run_headless` with `stream`; [protocol](protocol.md#sessionrun_headless-the-materialized-world)). The ring round declared observers — a position (a `WorldPos`, moved in f64), a velocity in metres a game second, a weight — made by the first call that asks and kept with the session's runtime world. A call materializes the document's untiled records and its live tiles' records (the driver's live scope, below), fills the ring with no budget, and then, between every two ticks, moves the observers and updates the ring within its budget. The document consumer acts in the `simulated` rings, the store consumer too when `store` is set (the document's `world.db`, created when absent). The result's `streamed` block says what the ring did, which tiles are active, and what each consumer did and cost. **A call that declares no observers — or names no `stream` at all, on a world that is streamed — continues**: the observers go on from where they are and nothing is filled, so a run split over several calls, or over a save and a load, is the run that never stopped; a call that declares observers puts them where it says, a jump, and fills the ring round them.

**Edits between calls** (roadmap D20, 2026-11-17). An agent's `doc.apply` between two calls may edit, create or delete a record in a tile the ring holds live. Before this, a streamed call's first pass was over the untiled records only, so such an edit reached the world when its tile next came in — and a save taken while it was pending loaded with it applied where the run that never stopped had not seen it, the one way a load and the continuous run could differ that the replay test does not exercise. The call's first pass is now `sim::MaterializeScope::live()` ([sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented)): the untiled records and every tile the driver holds live, following the document's change feed from the last such pass, so it costs the records that changed — an edit in a live tile is materialized again, a deletion there or an edit that moves a record out of every live tile lets it go, an edit that moves one into a live tile brings it in, and an edit in a tile nobody simulates is left for that tile's own pass. The first call of a world, and a call the feed cannot answer, is a full pass over the same scope. What is materialized at a call's start is therefore the document's live tiles' records, as a load's would be (`ops_tests.cpp`, "run_headless streams…"; `materialize_tests.cpp`, "the live scope…"). One observer may be **the player**, steered by an input log (below). `session.save_game`, `session.load_game` and `session.state_hash` are this capability's too (below; [protocol](protocol.md#sessionsave_game-sessionload_game-sessionstate_hash)).

## The player

A headless world has one thing a player's input can move without a game to interpret it: an observer, which decides what streams in and so what is materialized, reconciled and written back — the least a player is and still changes the persistent state, which is what a replay has to be asserted over ([05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay): "input log + event log ⇒ replay"). `world::InputObserver` (`input_observer.h`) is that observer's steering: an `input::InputLog` and the `input::ActionMap` it was recorded against ([input](input.md)), an `Axis2` action, a speed. Between two ticks, where the ring runs, `step(t)` feeds tick t's events and returns the action's value times the speed — x along world x, y forward along world −z — and the host moves the observer by it over the fixed step instead of by its declared velocity. A log recorded against another map is refused, naming both hashes, as `InputLog::replay` refuses one.

**The input state at tick T is a fold of the log's events up to T** — `input::InputState` reads no clock and holds only what its events left it — so it is derived, never saved. Whoever hands the world a log hands it the whole log, and `catch_up` feeds every tick the world has already run, through `input::feed_ticks`, the loop `InputLog::replay` itself runs. A world that ran from tick 0 and one loaded at tick K therefore hold the same state at K (a key held across the save is still held), and the same velocity comes out of every tick after it; the test binds one log at tick 0 and another copy at tick 25 and compares every velocity after and the states' `state_hash`. What a save keeps is the log itself up to its tick (`recorded`), and the map: the input that happened, not the input still to come, which a later call hands over again.

## The door in the store

The player above steers by a log handed over whole; a game's player, a client's call and an external source reach the world as they happen, and **every one of them comes through the door** ([sim](sim.md#the-door), roadmap S18): stamped with the tick it reaches the world at, recorded, and in a replay handed back at that tick from the record alone. `door_events.h` writes the door's entries into the store's event log, one row an entry, with the entry's origin (`Player`, `Agent`, `Llm`, `Debug` — never `Deterministic`), its tick and game time and its kind as the row's type, filed under one tile no world reaches, `k_door_tile` ((−2^31, −2^31) under the write-back's packing), so their sequence is the door's own order and nothing written in the world's tiles interleaves with it. **What the store's log then guarantees**: every input the world took that it did not compute is a row, in the order the world took it, beside the write-backs the world computed from them; `state_hash` covers those rows, so two runs whose inputs differ never hash the same however their state came out, and the same rows written in one batch or in four hash the same; and `read_door_events` gives a replay its door back from the log alone (`door_events_tests.cpp`). [03 §3.10](../plan/03-data-model.md#310-how-deterministic-and-agent-generated-state-coexist)'s "player input … logged as events and replayed from the log" and "logged as `Agent`-origin events" are these rows.

**What a host must call** (engine-host, engine-view's walker, a game's host; none is changed by S18, and these are the calls that make one replayable):

1. Make a `sim::Door` with the world and `install` it in the scheduler before anything else of the `Input` phase, and before an event bus; `set_input_state` with the player's `input::InputState`, and a `DoorBinding` when there is a bus.
2. Hand every window or device event to `submit_input` as it arrives, never to the state directly, and read the input in the tick (a system in the `Input` phase reading the state) rather than when a frame begins. engine-view's `PlayerBody` reads its axes per frame today; that is the frame-time sampling the cadence case plants and the bisector finds.
3. End a call by flushing and recording it: `flush_writeback` and `settle` as now, then `door.record_between(DoorOrigin::Agent, sim::door_flush_kind())`; register a `door_flush_kind` handler that does the same two at `entry.tick - 1`; and in a replay (`door.replaying()`) do not flush at a call's end — the door does it where the recorded run did, and `replay_pending()` before the host hashes, saves or returns. An agent's `doc.apply` between calls is recorded the same way, its commands as the payload and a handler that applies them and runs the live scope's pass.
4. Write the new entries to the store where the host commits (`append_door_events` with a cursor it keeps: the persist hook, a call's end, before `state_hash` or a save), in a replay as in the run, so both logs hold the same rows.

## Records that move

A tile's records are materialized when the tile comes in, filed under it, and let go when it goes. A system that moves a record — a cart that drives east — and a write-back that tells the document puts the record in another tile of the document's while the materialization driver still has it filed under the first. Before this, it stayed filed there until that tile went, wherever the cart had got to: what was materialized depended on when each record had last been materialized, which no save records, and a world loaded from a save — which materializes each record where the document says it is — held a different set of entities than the run that never stopped. The replay test found it the first time it ran with carts: the continuous run and the loaded one agreed at the save and parted at the first tile to go after it. With the fix disabled the test fails the same way (measured while its pinned number was `d94f6796ccfc856f`: `0139e8a936def8b4` continuous against `d94f6796ccfc856f` loaded).

So **records follow their tile.** The driver reports the held records a write-back put in another tile (`sim::Materializer::take_moved`, [sim](sim.md#records-that-move)), and the document consumer settles them between ticks (`DocumentTiles::settle`): a record whose new tile the ring holds in the consumer's rings is filed under it (`refile`), one whose new tile is not live is let go (`dematerialize(ids)`) — its state is in the document, which is where the new tile's activation will find it. engine-host settles before every ring update and when a call ends after its last flush, and a deactivation settles after its own flush and before it writes or drops the tile (`DocumentTiles::flush`, which the store consumer calls through `StoreTilesBinding::document_tiles`), so a cart that leaves a tile as the tile goes is written under the tile it is in, not the one it left. What is materialized is then a function of the document and the live tiles, not of the way the world came to be as it is — [03 §3.4](../plan/03-data-model.md#34-the-runtime-world)'s definition of the runtime world, literally, and what makes a load the run that never stopped. A record that walks out of the simulated rings stops there, as everything past them has: it resumes when its tile is simulated again.

**And records their owners move while nothing holds them come in with the tile they are in** (2026-09-25). The rule above is for records the world holds. A resident let go at work in a tile the rings do not simulate is not held, and its routine walks it home to a live tile while its record still says work, so going by the document alone it waited there until its record's tile came in. The npc capability's schedule index is a tile source registered with the driver ([sim](sim.md#records-the-document-has-not-caught-up-with), [npc](npc.md#the-schedule-index)): a tile's activation also brings in the residents whose routines have them in it now, wherever their records are, and `settle` first calls the driver's `materialize_arrivals`, which brings in the residents a watch found walking into a live tile since the last settle (`DocumentTilesStats::arrived`, and `arrived` in `session.run_headless`'s `streamed`). That call is the one line the schedule index needed from this capability, and settling is where it goes because it is the one call every host already makes between ticks, before the ring updates. What is materialized is then a function of the document, the live tiles and the time — a resident is held when the tile its routine has it in is live — and a load, whose tile passes ask the same index at the same time, holds the residents the run that never stopped holds ([npc](npc.md#the-schedule-index) has the rule and its tests).

## The persistent-state hash

`world::state_hash` (`state_hash.h`) is [05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay)'s "identical persistent-state hashes", defined once: one 64-bit number, and its parts, that two runs of a world agree on exactly when they left the same persistent state. engine-host's `session.state_hash`, a save's manifest, the replay test and an agent all read it the same way.

| Part | What | In what order |
|---|---|---|
| clock | the tick and the game time | |
| document | every live record composed across the layers: its id, type, parent, and every property as canonical JSON | id order (`Document::objects()`), properties by name |
| events | every row of the store's `events`, payload included | (tile, sequence) |
| projections | every row of `projections`, blob included | (tile, entity, kind) |
| snapshots | every row of `snapshots`, blob included | tile |

Each row is hashed as its little-endian bytes, length-prefixed, with `core/hash`'s `hash_bytes` and the parts chained with `hash_combine` — both the same on every platform — and each table's hash takes its row count. **What it leaves out, and why**: the journal (a patch's attribution carries the wall-clock time it was committed at, so no two runs write the same journal, and it is the document's history, not the world's state); which layer a property sits in and which files a layer is stored as (the composed value is the state); the runtime world (entities, components, flecs ids: rebuilt from the document and the store); and the ring, its observers and the player's input, which drive a run rather than result from it and which a save keeps beside the hash. `session.state_hash` returns each part too, so a mismatch says where it is.

**The same number on every compiler.** The replay test pins the number its world reaches at tick 600 (`ebbd622d15c54250` where the host has the kinematics capability; `15b4cd29460682f8` before engine-host's write-back kept a flush's projections current on 2026-11-19 (S11); `034b0fdc9cda96f0` before a tile's projections kept its records' f64 positions on 2026-10-06 (`TileProjection` 2, "Where an observer is" above); `c2168e9307bca23b` before the carts integrated in f64 on 2026-10-05, ADR-0053; `d94f6796ccfc856f` before the store's table version 2 put each event's payload version in its row) and checks it on MSVC and on Clang 18 and GCC 13 in the Linux container. It holds because the world's arithmetic is IEEE `f32` and `f64` with contraction off ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)) and no library transcendental (kinematics integrates with products, sums and a square root), every number reaches the document and the store through the JSON writer's shortest round-trip form, and nothing in a store row or a composed record is a pointer, a hash-table order or a thread's.

## Save and load

[03 §3.5](../plan/03-data-model.md#35-persistent-world-state)'s save game and [ADR-0042](../adr/0042-a-save-is-the-store-the-document-and-the-drivers.md) (proposed): **a save is one directory of files and a manifest that names and hashes every one** (`save_game.h`).

```
<save>/save.json          engine.world.SaveManifest, written last
<save>/document/...       the session's document directory: manifest, layers, journal, side files
<save>/world.db           the store, backed up (store.md, "Backups")
<save>/input.jsonl        the player's input log up to the save's tick, when there is a player
<save>/input-map.json     and the action map it was recorded against
```

The manifest (`systems/world/schemas/world_save.schema`) carries the run's clock (tick, game time, rate, write-back cadence), the world seed, the ring — its parameters and budget, its observers exactly (their positions f64, `worldpos`, and their velocities float32, which widen to an `f64` and back through the JSON writer bit for bit), the active tiles with their rings, the player's observer, action and speed — the random streams a system advances by drawing (none today: every stream in the engine is a hash of the world seed, a tile, an id and a tick), the state hash when it was taken, every format its pieces are written in with the version they were written at (the manifest's own types, `engine.world.TileProjection` and `engine.world.WriteBack` in the store, `store.tables`, `engine.input.log`, the document's file types and every record type it holds), and every file with its size and `hash_bytes`, sorted by path so the manifest's bytes are a function of what it holds.

**What is state and what is derived, and where the line is.** State is what nothing else can rebuild: the document — its layers hold everything the world wrote back, and its journal is its history; the store's three tables — the event log, the projections a tile wrote when it went (written directly by the store consumer, not folded from the log, so not derivable from it today) and the snapshots; the clock; the world seed; the observers and the tiles the ring holds, which hysteresis and the budget make a function of the observers' history rather than of where they are (the test runs a ring under a budget that defers changes, restores a second from its tiles and observers at step 49, and compares their events byte for byte for forty steps after); and the input log so far. Derived is everything a load rebuilds from those: the runtime world's entities and components — a materialization of the document at the saved tiles, which "records follow their tile" makes exact — each tile's seed (the world seed hashed with the tile), what reconciliation did when a tile came in (read again from the same snapshot and projections, which a tile writes only when it goes), the input state, and the document's composed index. **The plan's "latest snapshot plus events since" is the store's own business inside `world.db`**: a save carries the whole database rather than trimming its log to the last snapshot, because the projections are not a fold of the log yet (so the log cannot rebuild them) and `session.events` reads the log's history.

**Nothing is pending when a save is taken.** Every `session.run_headless` ends with the write-back's flush and a settle, so between two calls the document already says where the world is and what is materialized is the document's live tiles'. A save therefore changes nothing — it is read-only in the protocol's sense — and its state hash is `session.state_hash`'s.

**A load continues the run** (`session.load_game`). It reads the manifest and checks it before it touches anything (below), copies the document into a new directory and the store beside it, migrates the store's tables to this build's (`EventLog::open`, a step per version), opens a session there, makes the world **at the save's clock** (`SimSchedulerConfig::start_tick` and `epoch`, [sim](sim.md#the-tick)) and cadence, materializes what no tile holds, and — for a streamed world — makes the ring with the saved parameters and budget, restores its tiles at their rings (`World::restore`: one activation per tile in tile order, so the document consumer materializes each and the store consumer reconciles each from what the store kept, and nothing is written), puts the observers where they were, and binds the player to the saved log, caught up to the tick. A load that migrated nothing must reproduce the state hash the save recorded, or it is refused and the session closed. The next `session.run_headless` continues: declare no observers (or name no `stream`), hand the player's log over again, and the world runs on as the one that never stopped.

**Refused, and migrated.** `read_save` refuses, naming what is wrong: a directory with no `save.json` (a save that did not finish has none — the manifest is written last); a manifest whose format is not `engine.save`; a format this build lacks (`engine.cloth.Drape`, from a capability switched off); a version newer than this build's ("the save's store.tables is version 99, newer than this build's 2"); a file missing, or of another size or hash than the manifest says ("the save's file document/manifest.json hashes … where the manifest says …"); a document, store or input log the manifest names that is not among its own files — a load copies only from inside the save; a field the manifest's type does not have. A save written at an **older** version migrates on read: the manifest's own types follow the schema language's `@since` rule (`schemas/README.md`, "Evolution rules") — a field introduced after the version the save names for its type is refused if present and takes its default if absent — the store's tables migrate when the store is opened, and a record type that has gained fields materializes with their defaults, which the driver already does. The result lists every step as `"<format> <from> -> <to>"`.

**A save is the files its manifest lists, read where it names them** (T191, from [T13's and T190's reading](../experiments/fuzzing-design.md#what-the-reading-found); `session.load_game` restores any save a client names). The restore copied `document/` as a tree, which followed a link and copied files the manifest does not list, so a save could carry any file the host can read into the restored world; and `read_save` hashed each listed file through any link on its path, which told a client the size of a file anywhere ("is N bytes where the manifest says …"). Now `restore_save_files` copies exactly the files the manifest lists under the document, each checked again against its size and hash, and **a file under `document/` the manifest does not list is left behind, not refused**: refusing would be as safe, but a copy tool or the platform may leave a file of its own in a save someone moved (`Thumbs.db`, `.DS_Store`, a sync client's marker) and that save is still whole. **Every listed file is reached from the save's directory through no link** (`SaveRoot::in_place`): no component below the save's directory may be a symbolic link or a junction, a dangling one included, and what exists must resolve to the save's resolved directory with the manifest's path appended. The rule is stricter than the document store's, which follows a link that stays inside, because a save is files `write_save` wrote and never a link; the save's own directory is resolved, so a save reached through a link (a mounted drive, a linked saves folder) loads. The check comes before "missing", so whether a link's target exists is not told either, and **every path the manifest names is held to one lexical rule** (`save_path_admissible`, used by `read_save` and the restore, the document's and the store's paths included): every `/`-separated component non-empty and neither `.` nor `..`, and no `\`, `:` or control character. The restore then checks each destination for itself (`restore_destination`, under its target), and `SaveRoot::in_place` refuses an empty component rather than skipping it: before the stack's mend, `document//<an absolute path>` passed the text checks and the walk, hashed as a file inside the save, and `join_path` handed the restore its absolute half to write. Every save this engine wrote lists every file of its document, so the migration corpus's saves restore whole. Cases: `systems/world/tests/hostile_save_tests.cpp`.

**The migration corpus.** `content/migration-corpus/saves/` holds one save per version of the format that has existed ([03 §3.8](../plan/03-data-model.md#38-schema-evolution); `content/migration-corpus/README.md` says what each is and what loading it must produce), and `apps/engine_cli/tests/save_tests.cpp` loads every one on every build: the tick, the hash its manifest recorded, the hash once loaded, every migration step, and the hash after one more game second. There are three. `v1` is the first format — `SaveManifest` 1, `store.tables` 1 — and `v2` the current: version 2 of the manifest added `saved_by` (the engine that wrote the save, `"engine-host 0.0.1"`), which a version 1 manifest lacks and reads as empty, and table version 2 added `events.payload_version` ([store](store.md), "Migrations"), which a version 1 store gains with every old event at 1. Both saves hold the replay test's world at tick 300. **Loaded, they are the same world**: `v1` records `8a6d7d078e00bcbf` and loads, with its two steps, to `3d528309eea2b9df` — the hash `v2` records, and the replay test's own at tick 300 — and both run on to `25aef5f85a9efe56` (`37645e758e706598` before the carts integrated in f64). `v3` is the same world written after `Node` and `Mover` went to version 2 (their positions `worldpos`, ADR-0053), which `v1` and `v2` name as two more steps that rewrite nothing. That the migrated old save and the new one agree to the bit is the corpus's strongest claim: the migration produces exactly what the current version writes, not something that merely loads.

**Byte for byte.** A save loaded and saved again is the same files: the document is copied, not rewritten (a document's load never writes, [doc](doc.md)), the store's backup is a function of its content ([store](store.md), "Backups"), the player's log is cut at the tick, and the manifest is sorted. The world test writes a save, restores it into a fresh directory, saves again from there and compares every file; the end-to-end test does the same through two hosts.

## A camera that stands

**Once the place has settled, a camera that does not move costs no tile work** (W149): no tile event, placements tile, table slot, ground chunk built or uploaded, page upload or allocation, whatever the world clock does. It holds by construction: the ring is a function of the observers alone (`World::update` reads no clock), and the drawn ground's layout of the camera and the tiles held (`TerrainTileSet::next_layout` moves a window only past its margin or for a held tile outside it); only `--time-rate` rebuilds ground. Cases: `drawn_tiles_tests.cpp` (a 200 m walk with two far levels, then 600 standing frames: 0 events, layouts, chunks), and `engine_view.frame_loop`'s standing desert (tiles, two far levels, `--time-rate 0 --world-rate 1 --terrain-async`: zero after the warm-up, the last picture the first's byte for byte) and swaying grove.

**An offscreen camera stands only on a path of one place.** With no `--camera-path`, engine-view offscreen flies `renderer::orbit_camera` (0.006 rad a frame; `--orbit` fixes the distance, not the turn), and a scene's own `camera_path` is read only by a window. On the forest that is hundreds of metres a frame: W51-2's "standing" load (2026-11-18) toggled ~185 tiles an update and re-centred the ground every 46 to 82 frames, its layout 46.7 km behind the camera.

## The world log

`engine.world.TileFrame` (`systems/world/schemas/world_tiles.schema`), one per update: the tiles tracked and active, per ring; the events by kind, the refusals and the changes the budget deferred; the ring's microseconds and every consumer's calls and milliseconds for *this* update (`WorldLog` takes a snapshot of the consumers' totals before the update and reports the difference); and what the renderer holds after it — the scene's instances and pairs, the buildings the ruins hold and their instances. Since version 2 (2026-10-31, W81) engine-view adds `placements` (`TilePlacementsFrame`): what the update's placements cost on the frame's thread — the tiles built and their milliseconds, the commit's ranking and tail, and the renderer taking the tiles split the way `renderer::TileStats` splits it (blocks compared and the instances read, the layout, the slots written, what follows the tables, a relayout's wait; the blocks kept, rewritten, placed and freed, the compactions and the tight ones) — which is what attributes a slow frame of a streamed flight ([the measurement](../experiments/forest-load-in-2026-10-31.md)). The last line is `engine.world.TileWorldSummary`: the rings, the updates and events, the ring's microseconds (median, p99, max), each consumer's totals and longest call, and the placements consumer's building (the ruins' assembly and block laying) and the renderer taking the tail. engine-view's summary line carries the same object as `world`.

## An authored world

The owner's assumption (2026-09-30): "the tile streamer will also work with world tiles for games that don't procedurally generate anything". **What stands**: the renderer and the collision ask a tile source for a tile's heights and nothing about how they were made ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md#the-tile-source)), and the renderer's tests draw a world from a tile set built ahead of time, written into a derived-data cache and read back, pixel for pixel what the procedural source draws ([renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles)). So the drawing and the walking half works for a world nobody generates. **Since 2026-10-17 the tile-set capability ([tileset](tileset.md), roadmap W23, W24) is the authoring half's first three steps**, and the list below is what was missing, what it built, and what still is:

1. **A way for a scene to name a tile set — built.** The ground provider `tiles` (`domain/tileset`, registered like any other) opens a set from the terrain's `tiles` block (`engine.scene.TerrainTileSet`, `Terrain` version 7): a built set's manifest, or a heightfield it builds one from through the derived-data cache on a miss ([tileset](tileset.md#the-provider-tiles)). It has no time function: a set that does not move is a level whose fields never change. **What still stands between a scene file and it** is the renderer's: its terrain description does not carry the `tiles` block into the entry a provider is made from, and it draws the world's tiles only from a ground that moves, so a host hands the set's own source as the renderer's tests do ([tileset](tileset.md#what-the-seam-lacks)).
2. **A content-build step that writes one, and its mip chain — built.** `engine-content terrain-tiles` reads an authored heightfield (`engine.terrain.heightfield/1`, a JSON header beside a raw float32 grid; EXR and 16-bit PNG sources are G9's) and writes the set — each tile's heights at the heightfield's spacing with a one-point apron, in whole millimetres, keyed by the source's bytes, the options and `k_tileset_version`, a hit the second time — and for every far spacing the renderer's far levels ask (`far_ratio` times the outermost ring's spacing and each doubling up to the last), the surface **box-filtered over each point's own cell**, exactly, in integers; the manifest records the extent, the spacings, the hashes and the chain, so a renderer whose far levels the set lacks is refused a filter rather than drawing holes, and says before it builds which far level and which `ring_spacing_mm` (W77, [renderer](renderer.md#ground-to-the-horizon)). Past the data the edge is held, at every level, so a far square reaching past the map draws a plain ([tileset](tileset.md#the-set)). The renderer handed such a set draws its heightfield pixel for pixel ([tileset](tileset.md#the-pixel-test)), and the collision walks on it.
3. **Maps — still missing** (W25). A tile source serves heights. A tile's colour, roughness and sand share are the scene's baked maps over its grid's UV frame, clamped at the grid's edge; an authored world needs its maps per tile — a level a set writes beside its heights, keyed as they are — and the renderer a material (or a texture array) per slot to draw them. **A rock material where the ground is too steep for sand or soil is one of those maps**, the slope's own (2026-10-19, G71): the cliff faces the `cliffs` generator stands on a mountain's steep ground are the geometry above it ([rocks](rocks.md#the-cliffs-generator)), and until the map exists the ground shows sand in their deepest recesses ([the escarpment](rocks.md#the-escarpment)).
4. **Point heights for placement and cameras — built.** The `tiles` provider answers `height`, `height_mm`, `surface_mm` and `floor_mm` with the finest tiles' bilinear surface at whole millimetres, the edge held past them ([tileset](tileset.md#the-reader)).
5. **Holes and overhangs — cliffs built, caves and arches missing.** A heightfield is a function of (x, z); cliffs, caves and arches are placements (meshes on a tile), which the placements consumer streams already — and since 2026-10-19 the `cliffs` generator finds an authored ground's steep slopes from its heights and places faces with ledges and overhangs along them, tile-stable ([rocks](rocks.md#the-cliffs-generator)), which is the cliffs' half — and **since 2026-10-24 an authored world's placements stream per tile** (roadmap W73): a scene file's own `instances` and `scatters` become the entry of the placement generator `authored` when the world streams ([authored](authored.md)), each instance filed under the tile its origin is on and drawn in the distance form its tile's ring allows ([renderer](renderer.md#authored-instances-per-tile)), so the full v8 forest's trees come and go with their tiles instead of sitting in the prefix. What it does not do yet: read a tile set's *own* placement layer — a set that carries per-tile placements beside its heights, which a content build would write — and stream the landmarks, which stay in the prefix by their reach.
6. **The world's persistent layers — still missing.** The overlay and the tile lag (the ground consumer's, [The consumers](#the-consumers)) are the procedural dunes'; an authored world would take the overlay's mechanism unchanged and has no lag.

**In a document** (roadmap W66): an `engine.terrain.Heightfield` record names the heightfield, its origin and vertical scale, and `systems/doc_scene` maps the one live record to the scene's terrain, building the set on a miss ([doc_scene](doc_scene.md#the-mapping), [tileset](tileset.md#the-record)); sculpting it in the editor is that row's second half.

What it does **not** lack: the tile ring, its consumers and their order, the store, the save, the renderer's slots and seams, the time-lapse (a set that does not move is a level whose fields never change), and the collision.

## Performance notes

Measured in `msvc-release` on the development machine (i9-10980XE, RTX 5090); the flythrough's numbers are [E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md)'s.

- **The ring** (`world.ring.*`, `--repeats=5`, beside another agent's test suite at 12% of the CPU, so upper bounds): an update of the default rings round one observer moving 1.5 m an update, **77 µs**; round two observers 173 µs and four 367 µs, since each observer's window is enumerated and merged; a first fill from nothing, 1,810 tiles at once, 303 µs. Along E35's flythrough the same update was 82 µs at the median (p99 258 µs) over 2,036 active tiles, and 21 µs under rings of 1.5, 6 and 10 tiles (354 active).
- **A tile through the store** (`world.store.tile`): 64 records' projections and a snapshot written, and read back, in an in-memory store, **0.51 ms** (8 µs a record).
- **The placements consumer over the ruins** (E35, when it was `RuinsTiles`): a building assembled in sections 17 µs (the kit of boxes) and 22 µs (E33's ashlar kit); laid in blocks 235–238 µs, the section assembler it runs first included; a deactivation nothing measurable. Since both forms lay one rubble field, the handover runs measured 25 and 34 µs in sections (more debris members, each on its own terrain query) and 140 µs laid in blocks (fewer fallen blocks), on a machine a third busy ([E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md#after-the-rubble-rule)). The consumer's calls are 0.05 ms at the median on a frame with events and 1.3–1.8 ms at the worst.
- **The renderer taking the tail** was the cost that mattered, and is not any more: E35 measured 3.2 ms a change for a thousand buildings of the kit of boxes (21,000 instances), 15–17 ms for the ashlar kit at 5–9 million pairs and 34 ms at the median at the 2^24 budget, each waiting for the device, because every change rewrote the whole tail; with each tile a block of its own in the renderer, the pair entries expanded on the GPU and a table set per frame in flight, [E37](../experiments/e37-tile-instance-blocks.md) measured 0.36 ms for the boxes and 0.17–0.18 ms for the ashlar kit on the same path, with no wait at all. What is left is the tail itself: the ruins consumer concatenates every drawn tile on each commit and the renderer compares every tile it is handed against what it holds, which is why the boxes, with more and smaller instances, cost twice what the heavier kit does.
- **The budget's ranking** (a sort of the tiles held on every commit) does not show beside the tail.
- **A fast flight over the forest proof of concept** (W81, 2026-10-31, [the measurement](../experiments/forest-load-in-2026-10-31.md); v23, 64 m tiles in rings of 0.75 to 20 tiles, 1600×900, 40 s at up to 99 m/s and then walking): the placements cost the frame's thread 19.4 s of the path before the hand-over named its tiles by version and 7.3 s after, the renderer's part 9.3 → 2.6 s and a commit's part that read every held tile 10.8 → 0.42 ms at the median. What is left is the tiles' own building (4.6 s, 1.3 ms a tile, on the frame's thread), the tight compactions of a tail at the pair ceiling (60 a flight, 30–56 ms each) and the stride's growth (twice, 80–145 ms).
- **The owner's v24 dash** (W82, 2026-11-01, [the measurement](../experiments/forest-load-in-2026-10-31.md#w82-the-turn-between-frames-named); 1920×1080, `--raster rt --shadows rt`, the owner's recorded poses as a camera path): two dashes of about a kilometre in under a second each activated up to 577 tiles in one update, and building them on the frame's thread took 1.5–2.9 s of one frame (about 2.6 ms a tile) — the frame record's new `world` section named it, where its `cpu` sections had summed to under 10 ms. With the build budget at four a commit the dash's frames went from 1.0–1.6 s to 19–43 ms in a window's terms, the frames over 100 ms from 18 to 3 (the stride's growth, still waited for) and over 33 ms from 44 to 29, while the backlog of up to 984 due tiles drains at four a frame; the before and after are on the page.

## Not yet

- **A tile's mesh brings its textures only when built** (R147; walked on since R145: [scene_collision](scene_collision.md#a-tiles-meshes)): with `--no-cache`, or one image with no built `.tex`, the whole mesh draws its materials' constants ([renderer](renderer.md#meshes-that-come-and-go)). Entries open under the whole read's root (T119, `StreamedPlacements::ddc`).
- **A dynamic scene is rigid and rasterized.** The renderer's tail has no ray tracing chain and no deformed-vertex pool ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)): the chain's top-level records and the pool's table are laid out once per instance. A streamed world therefore draws its shadows with the cascaded maps or none.
- **The handover pops.** Nothing cross-fades or dithers between a tile's two instance sets. E35 measured the pop (above); the two forms now agree on the ground, which halved it, and what is left is mostly the walls' tops. A dither over both sets for a few frames would hide it, and is renderer work across every rasterizer and the ray path (E35 lists what it needs); walls whose section variants follow the blocks' courses would shrink it first. Demotions happen behind a camera that moves forward and were never on screen along the flythrough.
- **No far tier of the world's own.** Past the outer ring, and in the tiles the pair cap withholds, the world streams nothing; the renderer draws the far forest there, each world tile's trees as one rigid prefix mesh of their coarsest forms built at load ([renderer](renderer.md), "The far forest"; R111, ADR-0059), over its own far ground levels (ADR-0051). E33's slab is not built.
- **A commit still visits every held tile, and the renderer's bookkeeping after a change every slot.** Since W81 (2026-10-31) the hand-over names each tile with its version and nothing is copied or compared for a tile no update built ([The consumers](#the-consumers)); what is left per commit is the pair budget's ranking of the held tiles and the runs' list — a few hundred entries — and in the renderer what follows the tables of a change (the ray tracing chain's drop order, the policy counts over the instance table, the poser's chunks), which the [measurement](../experiments/forest-load-in-2026-10-31.md) lists with what each costs. The blocks leave holes in the renderer's pair space that its cull dispatches — a median 14–18% of it along E35's path, bounded at 25% by a compaction — and a hole costs the cull what a pair it culls does.
- **One job builds the tiles, one tile after another** ([Builds off the frame's thread](#builds-off-the-frames-thread), T131). Since 2026-11-13 a budgeted update's tiles are built off the frame's thread, but by one job at a time, because `scene_gen::PlacementGeneratorDesc` promises nothing about two `tile` calls on one state at once: a burst drains at the speed of one worker (a forest tile at 15 to 60 ms is 16 to 60 tiles a second), where several would need each generator's `tile` audited and the contract changed to say it may be called concurrently, or an entry opened once a worker. A whole update (a first fill, a restart) still builds in place on the frame's thread.
- **The ring does not promote or demote the document's records.** The document consumer materializes at the driver's one tier; the ring's rings map to representations, not yet to the simulation's tiers, and reconciliation's promotion is its own. Residents are the exception, and not the ring's doing: the npc capability scores the residents it holds against the ring's observers and drives their `promote`/`demote` itself ([npc](npc.md)).
- **The store's projections outlive a record deleted while its tile is not live**, until that tile comes in and is written again with it gone; `upsert` cannot tell a deletion from a record that moved away. Harmless to the world (reconciliation resolves a projection whose record is gone to nothing), but the store's size and hash carry it until then. A record deleted in a live tile is let go at the next call ("Edits between calls", above), so its tile's next write leaves it out.
- **A run without a store reads no world seed** (W11): engine-host's headless run with `keep_store` off draws its residents' variations from 1; `world::document_world_seed` where it makes the stream would close it.
- **A run split over calls is the unsplit run only when it splits on the write-back cadence.** Every call ends with a flush, so a call that ends off the cadence commits once more than an unsplit run would; a save taken there, loaded and run on, matches the split run, not the unsplit one. The replay test splits at 300 with a cadence of 60. **Such a run now replays** (S18): the call's flush is a door entry, and a replay — in one call or split another way — flushes where the recorded run did ([The door in the store](#the-door-in-the-store), step 3; sim's and npc's cases). What is left is engine-host making the three calls, which is the host's row.
- **A save is the whole store**, not the latest snapshot and the events since: the projections are written directly rather than folded from the log, so the log cannot rebuild them yet, and nothing trims the log. A save is therefore as large as the store and the document together — the replay test's at tick 300 is 107 KB: a 40 KB store of five 8 KiB pages, a 37 KB journal of write-backs, and the document's tile files — and there is no incremental save.
- **engine-view does not save.** Its world streams the ruins for the renderer and has no document or store to save; `--save-at` would save nothing a load could continue.
- **The drawn ground is not the ground consumer's.** Since 2026-09-30 the renderer draws a world's ground from its tiles ([The drawn ground](#the-consumers)), but from the scene's ground provider seen as tiles, not from the ground consumer's held tiles: the overlay (footprints, pits, the sand a drift is short of) and the tile lag are kept in the store and drawn by nobody, and the collision reads the same source the renderer does, so neither sees them. Drawing them is the tile source taking a tile's overlay and lag, or the pool reading a deformation map (05 §5.13). No host stamps footprints yet, and no host fills the drift callback from the ruins.
- **Ground tiles and streamed placements are not drawn together.** A world's streamed placements make the scene dynamic, and a dynamic scene has no deformed-vertex pool, which every drawn tile is drawn through ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)): a scene asking for both is refused with a sentence.

## Capability contract (ADR-0027)

This is a capability: it was added without editing `core/`, `foundation/`, the render graph, the scheduler, or another capability, and it can be removed from the build the same way.
