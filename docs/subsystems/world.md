# world (systems, capability)

**Purpose.** World tile streaming: the tile ring of [03 §3.7](../plan/03-data-model.md#37-spatial-partition) and [05 §5.4–5.5](../plan/05-simulation.md#54-lod-tier-assignment) that ties the pieces Phase 3 already has into a world that activates and deactivates tiles as its observers move. It owns the **tile grid** (a tile size, 32 m today, a parameter), the **observer set** it is updated from (engine-view's camera is one observer, a headless run's are declared), and the **tile ring**: which tiles are active, and in which ring, as a function of the observers, with hysteresis bands and a per-update budget. What a tile *is* belongs to the **consumers** registered with it, called in a declared order when a tile activates, moves between rings or goes: what a scene's placement generators put on a tile — its ruined building, a district's proxies, each generator found in the scene-generator registry by name — as instances the renderer adds and removes between frames (`placement_tiles.h`), a tile of the scene's ground from the ground provider the terrain names, with the tile's record in the store (`ground_tiles.h`), the tile of a partitioned document through the materialization driver (`document_tiles.h`), and the tile's projections and snapshot in the persistent store, which reconciliation reads when it comes back (`tile_store.h`, `store_tiles.h`). It builds no geometry and draws nothing, and links no generator ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)): where the terrain capability is in the executable, the ground consumer holds a tile of the dune generator's field for every held tile and keeps the tile's deformation overlay in the store ([terrain](terrain.md), [ADR-0043](../adr/0043-dunes-as-a-function-of-time.md)) — the endless terrain this page used to list as out of scope — and engine-view hands the ring's tiles to the renderer, which draws the ground from them ([The drawn ground](#the-consumers), from the provider's heights and not yet from the ground consumer's held tiles). [E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md) is its first measurement; [ADR-0040](../adr/0040-the-tile-ring.md) (proposed) records the rules and defaults below. It is also where a running world is **saved, loaded and replayed** ([below](#save-and-load), [ADR-0042](../adr/0042-a-save-is-the-store-the-document-and-the-drivers.md), proposed): the persistent-state hash two runs compare, a save of the document, the store and the drivers of the run, and a load that continues it — because what a save has to hold beyond the document and the store is the ring's: its observers, its tiles and the player steering one of them.

## Why this shape

- **A ring is a tier, and the ring is `sim::TierAssignment`.** A tile's ring is the minimum over observers of the distance from the observer to the tile's centre on the ground plane, divided by the observer's weight, banded by one radius per ring — which is exactly the score and the banding an entity's simulation tier is ([05 §5.4](../plan/05-simulation.md#54-lod-tier-assignment); [sim](sim.md#why-tiers-are-a-function-over-the-observer-set)). So the ring calls `TierAssignment::assign_tiers` over the tiles it tracks, with one tier per ring and one more for "inactive", and inherits its hysteresis, its rate limits (nearest promoted first, farthest demoted first, the rest deferred), and its determinism (scores from the inputs alone, changes in index order). Two players, a camera and a quest marker are observers alike; nothing here knows which one is "the player". The alternative — a radius round a camera, in square tiles — would have been a second LOD policy beside the simulation's, disagreeing with it at every boundary, and unpicked the day a second observer arrived.
- **Tiles are columns.** Observers are projected onto the ground before they are scored, so a camera 300 m up activates the tiles under it, not none ([03 §3.7](../plan/03-data-model.md#37-spatial-partition): tiles have the full vertical extent).
- **An update costs the tiles within reach, not the world.** It enumerates the tiles whose centres are within the outermost radius (one tile wider, times the observer's weight) of some observer, merges them with the tiles still active — both sorted by tile key, so the merge is tile order — scores that set and bands it. Nothing ticks per entity; a tile the ring let go of is forgotten until it comes within reach again.
- **Consumers are a table, not a base class.** A row is a name, a context, the rings the consumer acts in, and four function pointers (`activate`, `change_ring`, `deactivate`, `commit`), for the reason `sim::MaterializationHooks` is a table: a world with no placements has no placements row and costs nothing for it, and nothing in the ring names a consumer's type. **Activation and a move between rings call the consumers in registration order; deactivation calls them in reverse** — the mirror of construction, so what a later consumer built on a tile (the store's reconciliation resolves the entities the document consumer made) lets go before what it was built on. For one event, the consumers it deactivates run before the ones it activates or moves: letting go before taking. A consumer names the rings it acts in, and a tile leaving them is a deactivation *to that consumer* while it stays active in the ring — the simulation runs in the inner rings, the ruins are drawn in all of them. `commit` is called once at the end of an update in which any of the consumer's tiles changed: it is where the placements consumer hands the renderer a whole frame's worth of changes at once.
- **Between ticks, never inside one.** A tile's activation materializes and its deactivation dematerializes, and the materialization driver must be called by whoever owns the tick, outside it ([sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented), "Between ticks": the entity store's hook refuses a call while its world is deferred). So the world is not a system in the scheduler's table: the host calls `World::update` between two `SimScheduler::step()`s — engine-host's headless run once a tick, engine-view once a frame.
- **Events in tile order.** The tracked tiles are kept sorted by `tile_key` (x, then z, signed), so `TierAssignment`'s index-ordered changes are tile-ordered events: activation, move between rings or deactivation, one per tile at most per update — which is why the ring an event names orders nothing further. The same sequence of observer sets gives the same events, byte for byte ([05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay)); the tests fly a path that turns, speeds up and doubles back with a second observer coming and going, twice, and compare the bytes.

## The ring's rules and defaults

| Parameter | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| tile size | 32 m | The scene's own (its ruins' `tile_size`), E34's; the plan's 64–128 m is a game's choice. A parameter of the scene's `world` block and of a headless run |
| inner ring | 1.5 tiles (48 m), blocks | The 3 × 3 tiles round the observer's own. E34: blocks near, and **bounded by the tiles round the camera** — nine tiles of mid blocks are about 700,000 pairs, 4% of the visibility id's 2^24 — and at 48 m a 0.3 m course is still five or six pixels high at 1080p, where the block representation is worth its instances. One tile (the observer's own and its four neighbours at a tile's centre) flips a building to blocks at under 32 m, which is a pop at arm's length |
| middle ring | 8 tiles (256 m), sections with debris | Where the ruins capability's own LOD policy drops a tile's debris (`ruins::detail_for_distance`); within it a building is the kit's members and its fallen stones |
| outer ring | 24 tiles (768 m), sections without debris (`Detail::walls`) | Where E34's far building had fallen to 73 visible pairs in sections — tens of pixels. Past it the right answer is a far-tier slab ([E33](../experiments/e33-hard-surface-kits.md#what-it-decides)'s open question), not more rings |
| hysteresis | 0.15 | `TierAssignment`'s: a tile leaves a ring only 15% past its radius (7 m at the inner ring), so an observer walking along a boundary crosses it once. The tests walk one across the inner radius a metre either side forty times: 40 events with no band, 2 with it |
| budget | 8 activations, 16 deactivations an update | Tunables (`world.ring.max_activations`, `max_deactivations`, 0 unlimited), read when a host builds its ring. E35 measured a building at 17–22 µs assembled in sections and 0.24 ms laid in blocks (the section assembler it runs first and the scene instances included), and an update's activations are nearly all at the outer ring's edge, so eight are well under a millisecond of ruins on the frame that takes them (the consumer's calls were 0.05 ms at the median and 1.8 ms at the worst); a deactivation drops what it held, so twice as many. A teleport activates the nearest eight first, and the whole inner ring is among them |
| simulated rings | the inner two (256 m) | Where the document is materialized and the store reconciled: the rings a player's actions reach. A headless run says it per call (`simulated`) |

A first fill — engine-view's first frame at a camera the ring has not followed, a flythrough's repeat, a headless call's start — is an update with the budget lifted (`unlimited`); a flythrough's repeat also drops every tile first (`World::clear`), because with hysteresis a ring's state depends on how it got there, and two repeats must fly the same world.

### Where an observer is

**An observer is a `WorldPos`, and a tile is chosen from it in f64** ([ADR-0053](../adr/0053-world-positions-are-f64-and-the-gpu-sees-none.md); 2026-10-06). `sim::ObserverSet` holds f64 positions ([sim](sim.md#positions-are-f64)); `tile_at` floors the f64 coordinate over the tile size, `tile_center` is an f64 point (exact: an integer and a half times the size), and the ring's enumeration window is the observer's place in tiles in f64, with each centre's offset from it — a few tiles — the only float32. Until then the ring took float32 observers, which step by a metre at 10,000 km and by 8 m at 1e8 m: its window and its scores moved with the float's grid and not with the observer. The division by the size is exact for a power of two (32 m, 64 m); for another size it rounds once, at the size of the quotient, so a point within that rounding of a tile's edge may land on either side — the same side on every machine. The world's test walks two observers along the same path, in 1024ths of a metre, from the origin and from 419 km, 10,000 km, 1e8 m and the owner's site less a tile, crossing a cell's and a tile's edge in the first seconds, and requires the same events tile for tile, moved by the site.

**Where the tiles end** (2026-10-07, roadmap W64). A tile's index is an i32 on each axis — `TileCoord`, and the same cell `ruins::TileCoord`, `scene_gen::TileCoord` and a partitioned layer's tile name, and the store's ids pack as two 32-bit halves (`store_tile`) — so the tiles end 2³¹ tiles from the origin: 6.87e10 m at 32 m tiles, half of where a 64 m cell's index ends (`world_cell_valid`, 1.37e11 m), and nearer at a smaller tile (2.1e9 m at a metre). Until now `tile_at` and the ring's window cast `floor(x / size)` to i32 unchecked, so a position `world_cell_valid` let through past that was an out-of-range conversion, undefined behaviour that x86 turns into the first tile. **The world refuses such a position where positions enter**: `TileRing::update` lets an observer through only when its whole window of tiles is i32s (`tile_reachable(position, tile_size, reach)`, the reach the window walks); one that is not observes nothing — no candidates, no score — and is counted (`RingStats::unreached`, `UpdateStats::unreached`), and `World::update` logs it once when it starts (`an observer is past the world's last tile and observes nothing`, with the last tile's distance). `tile_at` asserts the same in debug. The window's counters are i64, so a window ending on the last tile does not wrap. **Why refuse and not widen**: every format a tile is written in holds its index in 32 bits — the store's ids, a layer's tile files, the world log's events (`TileEvent` is pinned at 12 bytes), the ruins' seeds — and an i64 index would buy tiles past half a cell's reach, where ADR-0053 already says a game needs a frame above `WorldPos`. **The test** (`world: the tiles end at the i32 index, and an observer past them observes nothing`): a position half a metre inside the last tile and the first is a tile and one half a metre past each is not, each one a cell names; an observer whose window ends on the last tile holds the tiles the origin's observer holds, moved by 2³¹ − 26 tiles, event for event; one tile further it observes nothing and is counted, and beside an observer by the origin it changes nothing that one holds; ADR-0053's far sites and a cell's edge 10,000 km out are well inside at 32 m tiles and at 1 m.

**What a tile keeps of where its records are is the document's f64.** `record_position` reads the record's position property as the doubles the document holds (`worldpos`), where it read float32s, and refuses one no cell can name; `TileProjection.position` is a `worldpos` (version 2), so a tile that goes inactive 420 km out keeps its records where they are rather than on a 3.1 cm grid, and reconciliation promotes them from there. A projection written at version 1 reads as the same position (its float32 was written as the double it widens to). `SaveObserver.position` is a `worldpos` too (version 2), and engine-host moves its observers in f64 (`position += DVec3{velocity} * step`, as `kinematics::integrate` moves a cart). Both changed the migration corpus only where the store's bytes changed: the hash a second after loading `v1`, `v2` and `v3` moved, and it was the projections — with the projection's position rounded back to float32 in one experiment build, all three continued to the hashes they had before ([the corpus README](../../content/migration-corpus/README.md)).

## The consumers

**The placements** (`PlacementTiles`, `placement_tiles.h`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)). What a scene's placement entries — its `ruins`, its `placements` — put on a tile, whatever generators they name: `create` opens each streamed entry's generator (`SceneData::streamed`) again, found in the scene-generator registry by the name the scene gave it, and nothing here names one — the ruins are the generator `ruins`, Island City the generator `city` (a district's proxies, each tile the plan's per-tile query at the ring's detail: [city](city.md#where-it-attaches)), and the world links no capability for either (the host does). **One consumer for every entry, in the scene's order**: a tile's placements are every entry's, entry by entry as the reader walks them, and the renderer takes one tail, so the pair budget below is decided over all of it. Activation asks each entry's generator for the tile (`tile`, in the representation the tile's ring draws: the scene's `engine.scene.WorldRing::ruins` word, which the generator reads from `scene_gen::Context::world`) — for the ruins, `ruins::Assembler` for sections and walls, `ruins::BlockAssembler` for blocks, from the entry's own seed, tile, wind and ground, which is exactly what the scene reader assembles at load, so a tile streamed in is the building a whole read has on that tile (a test holds them equal, instance for instance) — maps each placement's mesh through the scene meshes the reader left resident for the entry (`StreamedPlacements::meshes`), and keeps the tile's instances, with each placement's tag (the ruins': rubble), as a block keyed by the tile. The ground is the terrain's ground provider's floor (`TerrainSampler::provider().view()`), as the scene reader's is: the surface with the waves, and with the dune generator the interdune floor the dunes migrate over ([renderer](renderer.md#scenes-camera-paths-and-flythroughs)), so a building is the same whenever its tile comes in. A move between rings asks again only when some entry on the tile draws the two rings differently (`representation`: the ruins' blocks, sections and walls). Deactivation drops the block. Its stats count the tiles each generator built by the representation it drew (`built`, `build_ns`: the ruins' 0 blocks, 1 sections, 2 walls), which engine-view's world summary reports as the ruins' `laid` and `assembled`. It replaced `RuinsTiles`, which was compiled under `ENGINE_WORLD_RUINS` with a conditional link to the ruins capability; the calls moved into the generator and the rules below did not change. `commit` concatenates every tile's block **in tile order** and hands the tail to a sink — engine-view's is `SceneRenderer::set_dynamic_instances` ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)), with each tile's run named as a block of its own, so the renderer keeps every tile where it put it and a change costs it the tiles that changed. Tile order is the order a compaction lays the tiles out in; between compactions a tile's pairs are wherever its block landed, so a depth tie between two tiles' coincident surfaces goes to the one placed later (the renderer states it as the one exception to a picture that does not depend on how its instances arrived). **The pair budget is decided here too, at the commit, over every tile held**: the tail takes tiles by ring, innermost first, then by the ring's score (`TileRing::score_of`: the nearest first), then in tile order, while the scene stays within its budget (the visibility id's 2^24 by default), and none after the first that does not fit — so every tile drawn outranks every tile withheld, and the renderer is never asked for more than it can name. A withheld tile stays held, its building assembled, and is drawn at the first commit with room for it; `withheld(tile)` says which, and the stats count them. The first version decided per event instead, and events come in tile order: E35's run of the ashlar kit under the default rings then refused 132 tiles of the inner ring and 315 of the middle one while tiles at the horizon held the budget. Blocks near and sections far is E34's handover, and both sides of it are the same ruin — the block layer lays the section assembler's own footprint, ruin and openings — but nothing cross-fades two instance sets, so a building **pops** from sections to blocks when its tile crosses the inner radius: E35 measured a median 12,000–14,000 pixels of a 1080p frame at 47 m, up to 3.9%, most of it rubble the two forms laid by rules of their own; since both lay one rubble field ([ruins](ruins.md#the-rubble-rule)) it is 7,800–9,100, up to 1.5%, three quarters of it wall tops that stand a course or a height variant apart ([E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md#after-the-rubble-rule)).

**The document** (`DocumentTiles`). Activation is `sim::Materializer::materialize(document, MaterializeScope::of_tile(t))`: the records the partitioned layers file under that tile, through the scheduler's hooks, parents before children — taken from the document's tile index, so an activation costs the tile's records and not the document ([sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented)). Deactivation flushes the write-back — what systems changed goes back to the document before the entities that changed it go — and calls `Materializer::dematerialize(MaterializeScope::of_tile(t))`, the one hook that was missing: the mirror of a tile pass, added to `domain/sim` as a registration point beside `held(scope)`, which says what a tile holds ([sim](sim.md#the-driver-a-document-through-the-hooks)). A ring tile and a document tile must be one cell: a partitioned layer on another grid is refused once, with a sentence, rather than materialized by a tile that means a different square. Records in no tile are the host's to materialize once, whole (`MaterializeScope::untiled()`).

**The store** (`StoreTiles` over `WorldStore`). `WorldStore` is the first `sim::TileStore` that is not a test's fake — the seam [ADR-0028](../adr/0028-ecs-and-persistent-store.md) chose, and the gap [sim](sim.md#tile-reconciliation) recorded: `tile_state` is the tile's snapshot in `foundation/store` (known when there is one, last active at its game time; the seed is `hash_combine(world seed, tile id)`, a function of the world and the tile like everything else a tile is), and `load_records` is the tile's projections, one `EntityRecord` each, carrying no source, so a hook resolves the entity the document consumer made and creates none. Activation runs `SimScheduler::reconcile_tile` over it — step 1 from the store, step 2's gap from the snapshot, step 3's summarizers from the tile's seed, step 4 resolving, step 5 promoting by distance to the world's observers. It registers **after** the document consumer for exactly that: on activation the records are already entities when reconciliation resolves and promotes them, and on deactivation, which runs backwards, it writes the tile while they are still held. Deactivation flushes the write-back, then writes one projection per record the tile held (`engine.world.TileProjection` as canonical JSON: the record, its type, where the document says it is, its importance) and the tile's snapshot at the tick and game time it went. The projections and the snapshot are the store's own tables (`EventLog`); the world adds no schema of its own to the file. It lives in this capability rather than in `foundation/store` because what a tile is — its id, what a projection of a document record carries, when a snapshot is taken — is the world's, and the store stays the thin SQLite layer [store](store.md) says it is.

**The ground** (`GroundTiles`, `ground_tiles.h`; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)). A tile of the scene's ground for every tile the ring holds, whatever ground provider the terrain names: `create` asks the provider for its tiles (`scene_gen::GroundOps::open_tiles`) and hands it the store's side — `load`, `save` and `erase` of one record a tile, under the provider's record type (`record`) — so the consumer names no ground and links none. A ground with no tiles a world could hold (the renderer's waves) is refused with a sentence. It was `TerrainTiles`, compiled under `ENGINE_WORLD_TERRAIN` with a conditional link to the terrain capability, until 2026-09-27: the store's half stayed here, the ground's half moved into the capability (`terrain::DuneTiles`, `domain/terrain/ground_tiles.h`, reached from a host that links it through `terrain::dune_tiles(consumer.tiles())`), and a record's key did not change — the projection's entity is the record type's hash and the tile, its kind the type's stable id, so an overlay a save holds from before reads now (the test pins both). **Register it after the store consumer and before the placements consumer**, so a tile's ground is built before anything stands on it. Today's one ground with tiles is the dunes', and the rest of this paragraph is theirs ([terrain](terrain.md)): the endless desert's ground, tile by tile. Activation reads the tile's **deformation overlay** from the store — one projection of kind `engine.terrain.OverlayTile` filed under the tile, keyed by an entity that names the overlay and the tile, its blob the overlay's own record of at most 33,848 bytes — and **catches it up** to the host's game time in one step (decay is a closed form, so a month away is one advance and the same bytes as a month of ticks: the test walks across two tiles, leaves for three hours, comes back five hours later and compares the overlay with one that never left), puts the tile's **lag** and its neighbours' into the lag field (a held neighbour's from its overlay, another's from its stored record's header, plus whatever its declared drifts derive), and evaluates the tile's field at that time at **the ring's resolution**: 128 cells a side in the inner ring (the overlay's 25 cm grid, where the player is), 64 in the middle and 32 beyond, because a 25 cm tile is 2.6 ms and eight of them would be a frame ([E36](../experiments/e36-desert-generator.md)). A move between rings rebuilds only when the resolution changes; deactivation advances the overlay to now and writes it back, or **erases its projection when it is empty** — a tile whose footprints are buried stores nothing — and drops the tile. Between updates the host stamps (`deform`: into every held tile the footprint covers; nothing far from everyone is kept) and advances (`advance`: every held overlay baked and decayed, and when a tile's lag moved — a pit's nudge, a drift's day — the tile and its held neighbours are rebuilt, so shared edges still agree; the test digs a pit and compares the rebuilt tiles' shared edge). The host says what time it is (`set_time`), because the world's update carries a tick and not a game time. After the store consumer because deactivation runs backwards, so the overlay's projection is written before the store consumer snapshots the tile, and the snapshot carries it. `commit` hands the tiles built since the last commit to a sink — nobody's yet: the renderer draws a world's ground from the drawn-ground consumer below, whose heights are the provider's and not these tiles' (Not yet). The drifts a tile declares come from a callback, which a host with the ruins fills from the ruins' `Drift`s; none does yet.

**The drawn ground** (`DrawnTiles`, `drawn_tiles.h`; [renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles), [ADR-0050](../adr/0050-the-ground-is-drawn-from-the-worlds-tiles.md), proposed; 2026-09-30). The tiles the renderer draws the ground from: for every tile the ring holds, its ring, and at `commit` **what changed** since the last commit — the tiles that entered or changed ring, and the ones let go (`renderer::k_tile_gone`), in the order the ring made the events — handed to the renderer's tile set (`renderer::TerrainTileSet::change_tiles`), which rebuilds only the tiles whose key changed and swaps them in one frame. Until 2026-10-03 it handed the whole set, about 14,000 tiles a commit on the endless desert, which the renderer sorted and compared and then derived every key of: 1.5 ms of the frame's thread a commit and 7.5 ms of the worker's a rebuild, for a flight that changes about 33 tiles a frame ([renderer](renderer.md#what-a-frame-waits-for)). The world's `clear` reports every tile let go, so a restart reaches the renderer as changes too. It builds nothing itself and holds nothing but the set: the heights come from the renderer's tile source (the scene's ground provider seen as tiles, `scene_gen::GroundProvider::tiles()`), the same one the walker's collision reads, so what is drawn is what is walked on. It acts in every ring, and is registered after the ground consumer where a world has one, so a tile's record is read before it is drawn, and before the placements. engine-view registers it when its scene's `world` block asks for ground tiles (`"ground": true`); the renderer's first layout, built before the world's first update, is the ring's own first-fill rule **round where the session starts** — an interactive session's `--start` or recorded start, a path's first camera, else the orbit's — so that update hands over the same set and nothing is rebuilt. (Until 2026-10-03 an interactive session's first layout was built round the orbit camera whatever `--start` said; on the endless desert that is the old erg's centre, and the owner's first flight, started 2 km from it at 100 m/s, began with a whole ring to rebuild behind its frames and outran each catch-up until he walked: `walk_view_tests.cpp`'s high start holds it.)

**The collision** (`scene_collision::SceneCollision`, a capability of its own: [scene_collision](scene_collision.md), 2026-09-28). The static bodies a walker stands on and is stopped by, for every tile its ring holds: the scene's ground as a heightfield a tile from the ground provider at a collision spacing, and what the scene's placement generators put on the tile — each streamed entry's generator asked for the tile in the inner ring's representation, or a scene read whole's instances that reach into it — as one static compound of each piece's LOD-cut proxy, in a `physics::World`. A consumer of this table like the others, and **the last of the scene's**: registered after the ground and the placements where a world has them, so a tile's collision is made from what is on it after it is there and let go before it goes. It lives in a capability of its own, requiring this one and physics, because the world must stay buildable without physics (a module that is not a capability may not link one, and a capability may link another only by declaring the edge). engine-view keeps it in **a ring of its own round the walker's feet** — one ring of 1.5 tiles — updated once a tick rather than in the camera's drawing ring once a frame, because a character's contacts are sorted by body id and body ids follow creation order, so bodies made on a frame's schedule would walk a replay somewhere else ([apps](apps.md#walking)).

**The store's tile id** is the packing engine-host's write-back events already use: x in the high 32 bits and z in the low, each as its two's complement. So a tile's events in the log and its snapshot share one id.

**The order** (`add_scene_consumers`, `scene_consumers.h`; [ADR-0040](../adr/0040-the-tile-ring.md) decision 2, [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md) decision 3). A host registers the document consumer, then the store consumer, then the scene's ground, then its placements; activation and a move between rings call them in that order, deactivation in reverse. The document before the store is ADR-0040's: reconciliation resolves the entities the materialization made, and a tile's projections are written while they are still held. The ground after the store, so a tile's ground record is written on deactivation before the store snapshots the tile. **The placements after the ground**, so a tile's ground is built before anything stands on it and outlives what stood on it — the reader's order too, which makes the terrain's provider before it opens the first entry ([renderer](renderer.md#scenes-camera-paths-and-flythroughs)). The last two are one function, `add_scene_consumers(world, ground, placements)`, so no host spells that half of the order; either may be null (a ground with no tiles, a scene with no streamed entry), and engine-view, whose world keeps no store, passes no ground. **The drawn ground** goes after the ground and before the placements where a world has them: a tile's record is read before the tile is drawn, and it is drawn before anything is placed on it (engine-view's world registers it alone, since a scene of ground tiles has no streamed placements yet). Within the placements consumer the entries are asked in the scene's order, the `ruins` entries and then `placements`, as the reader walks them. ADR-0040 as written registered the ruins consumer first, before the document; nothing rested on it — the ruins touched neither the document nor the store — and the ground's two rules put the placements last. **A collision consumer** ([scene_collision](scene_collision.md)) goes after all of them, for the placements' own reason: it is made from what the ground and the placements put on the tile, and it goes before they do. engine-view's walker registers it alone, in a ring of its own ([The consumers](#the-consumers)). `scene_order_tests.cpp` registers a ground and a placement generator of its own, streams one tile of a scene with two entries, then the same scene with the entries swapped, and holds the log each time: the ground, then the entries in the scene's order, and on the way out the tile's placements gone before its ground.

## The hosts

**engine-view** (`--world`, or a scene file's `world` block; `apps/engine_view/world_view.cpp`). The scene reader leaves the file's placement entries to the world — each generator's resident meshes are loaded (for the ruins, both kits'), so the renderer has every mesh a tile can ask for resident, and no building is assembled — and engine-view updates the ring from the camera before every frame: in a flythrough from that frame's own camera (`renderer::FlightOptions::before_frame`), in the windowed loop from the previous frame's, because the loop knows a frame's camera only after it has begun it and the tail changes only between frames. The tail goes to the renderer **tile by tile**: `ViewWorld::hand_over` names each tile's run of it (`PlacementTiles::tile_ranges`) as a `renderer::DynamicBlock` keyed by the tile, so the renderer keeps each tile in a block of its own and a change costs it the tiles that changed ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)); the hand-over after a restart asks the renderer to lay the tiles out from scratch, so every repeat of a flythrough starts from the layout the first fill had. `--world-log <f>` writes one `engine.world.TileFrame` per update and the run's `engine.world.TileWorldSummary` last; `--world-handover <f>` flies the path again after a benchmark, untimed, and for every tile an update moves to another representation draws the frame twice — as the update left it, and with only that tile's instances put back as they were (`PlacementTiles::tile_ranges` says where a tile's instances sit in the tail, and a capture's instance slots are turned back into places in the tail with `GpuScene::tail_indices` before a tile's pixels are counted, since the renderer keeps a tile wherever its block is) — and writes what differs (`engine.world.TileHandover`): pixels by how far their depth moved, and how many of them are the tile's rubble rather than its walls (`PlacementTiles::tail_tags` marks each instance of the tail). Everything else the update did is in both pictures, so the difference is that tile's pop alone. A streamed world is rigid and rasterized in v0: `--raster rt`, `--shadows rt`, `--deform`, `--animate`, `--morph-animate`, `--reference` and `--verify-occlusion` are refused with `--world` ([apps](apps.md#engine-view---world-a-streamed-scene)). **The drawn ground** (a world block's `"ground": true`, or `--terrain-tiles`): `ViewWorld` registers `DrawnTiles` before the placements, with the renderer's tile set as its sink and the ring's radii and cells taken from the tile set's own description (`DrawnTiles::ring_params`), so the ring the world keeps and the rings the tile set was sized for are one, and with nothing else to stream it lifts the ring's budget, since the drawn ground's events are a hash map's writes and the renderer batches the rebuilds they cause on its own worker (the default eight activations an update fell behind a camera at 100 m/s: [renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles), "Which tiles"); the update runs before the time-lapse's frame, so the tiles a frame asks the tile set to rebuild are the ones the ring holds after that frame's update. A build without this capability follows the camera by the ring's first-fill rule instead (`renderer::terrain_tiles_round`): no hysteresis and no budget, the tiles a first fill holds. Since 2026-10-05 that rule takes the camera's `WorldPos` and measures each tile's centre from it in f64, so it is the same rule 10,000 km out; since 2026-10-06 the ring does the same ("Where an observer is", below), so the rule and the ring agree on every tile but one whose centre is within half a float step of a ring's radius, and the rule's float32 overload that the ring's tests used to compare with is gone. The tiles it draws are instances at their corners ([renderer](renderer.md#the-grounds-tiles-are-placed-at-their-corners)).

**engine-host** (`session.run_headless` with `stream`; [protocol](protocol.md#sessionrun_headless-the-materialized-world)). The ring round declared observers — a position (a `WorldPos`, moved in f64), a velocity in metres a game second, a weight — made by the first call that asks and kept with the session's runtime world. A call materializes the document's untiled records whole, fills the ring with no budget, and then, between every two ticks, moves the observers and updates the ring within its budget. The document consumer acts in the `simulated` rings, the store consumer too when `store` is set (the document's `world.db`, created when absent). The result's `streamed` block says what the ring did, which tiles are active, and what each consumer did and cost. **A call that declares no observers — or names no `stream` at all, on a world that is streamed — continues**: the observers go on from where they are and nothing is filled, so a run split over several calls, or over a save and a load, is the run that never stopped; a call that declares observers puts them where it says, a jump, and fills the ring round them. One observer may be **the player**, steered by an input log (below). `session.save_game`, `session.load_game` and `session.state_hash` are this capability's too (below; [protocol](protocol.md#sessionsave_game-sessionload_game-sessionstate_hash)).

## The player

A headless world has one thing a player's input can move without a game to interpret it: an observer, which decides what streams in and so what is materialized, reconciled and written back — the least a player is and still changes the persistent state, which is what a replay has to be asserted over ([05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay): "input log + event log ⇒ replay"). `world::InputObserver` (`input_observer.h`) is that observer's steering: an `input::InputLog` and the `input::ActionMap` it was recorded against ([input](input.md)), an `Axis2` action, a speed. Between two ticks, where the ring runs, `step(t)` feeds tick t's events and returns the action's value times the speed — x along world x, y forward along world −z — and the host moves the observer by it over the fixed step instead of by its declared velocity. A log recorded against another map is refused, naming both hashes, as `InputLog::replay` refuses one.

**The input state at tick T is a fold of the log's events up to T** — `input::InputState` reads no clock and holds only what its events left it — so it is derived, never saved. Whoever hands the world a log hands it the whole log, and `catch_up` feeds every tick the world has already run, through `input::feed_ticks`, the loop `InputLog::replay` itself runs. A world that ran from tick 0 and one loaded at tick K therefore hold the same state at K (a key held across the save is still held), and the same velocity comes out of every tick after it; the test binds one log at tick 0 and another copy at tick 25 and compares every velocity after and the states' `state_hash`. What a save keeps is the log itself up to its tick (`recorded`), and the map: the input that happened, not the input still to come, which a later call hands over again.

## Records that move

A tile's records are materialized when the tile comes in, filed under it, and let go when it goes. A system that moves a record — a cart that drives east — and a write-back that tells the document puts the record in another tile of the document's while the materialization driver still has it filed under the first. Before this, it stayed filed there until that tile went, wherever the cart had got to: what was materialized depended on when each record had last been materialized, which no save records, and a world loaded from a save — which materializes each record where the document says it is — held a different set of entities than the run that never stopped. The replay test found it the first time it ran with carts: the continuous run and the loaded one agreed at the save and parted at the first tile to go after it. With the fix disabled the test fails the same way (measured while its pinned number was `d94f6796ccfc856f`: `0139e8a936def8b4` continuous against `d94f6796ccfc856f` loaded).

So **records follow their tile.** The driver reports the held records a write-back put in another tile (`sim::Materializer::take_moved`, [sim](sim.md#records-that-move)), and the document consumer settles them between ticks (`DocumentTiles::settle`): a record whose new tile the ring holds in the consumer's rings is filed under it (`refile`), one whose new tile is not live is let go (`dematerialize(ids)`) — its state is in the document, which is where the new tile's activation will find it. engine-host settles before every ring update and when a call ends after its last flush, and a deactivation settles after its own flush and before it writes or drops the tile (`DocumentTiles::flush`, which the store consumer calls through `StoreTilesBinding::document_tiles`), so a cart that leaves a tile as the tile goes is written under the tile it is in, not the one it left. What is materialized is then a function of the document and the live tiles, not of the way the world came to be as it is — [03 §3.4](../plan/03-data-model.md#34-the-runtime-world)'s definition of the runtime world, literally, and what makes a load the run that never stopped. A record that walks out of the simulated rings stops there, as everything past them has: it resumes when its tile is simulated again.

**And records their owners move while nothing holds them come in with the tile they are in** (2026-09-25). The rule above is for records the world holds. A resident let go at work in a tile the rings do not simulate is not held, and its routine walks it home to a live tile while its record still says work, so going by the document alone it waited there until its record's tile came in. The npc capability's schedule index is a tile source registered with the driver ([sim](sim.md#records-the-document-has-not-caught-up-with), [npc](npc.md#the-schedule-index)): a tile's activation also brings in the residents whose routines have them in it now, wherever their records are, and `settle` first calls the driver's `materialize_arrivals`, which brings in the residents a watch found walking into a live tile since the last settle (`DocumentTilesStats::arrived`, and `arrived` in `session.run_headless`'s `streamed`). That call is the one line the schedule index needed from this capability, and settling is where it goes because it is the one call every host already makes between ticks, before the ring updates. What is materialized is then a function of the document, the live tiles and the time — a resident is held when the tile its routine has it in is live — and a load, whose tile passes ask the same index at the same time, holds the residents the run that never stopped holds ([npc](npc.md#the-schedule-index) has the rule and its tests).

## The persistent-state hash

`world::state_hash` (`state_hash.h`) is [05 §5.10](../plan/05-simulation.md#510-determinism-and-replay)'s "identical persistent-state hashes", defined once: one 64-bit number, and its parts, that two runs of a world agree on exactly when they left the same persistent state. engine-host's `session.state_hash`, a save's manifest, the replay test and an agent all read it the same way.

| Part | What | In what order |
|---|---|---|
| clock | the tick and the game time | |
| document | every live record composed across the layers: its id, type, parent, and every property as canonical JSON | id order (`Document::objects()`), properties by name |
| events | every row of the store's `events`, payload included | (tile, sequence) |
| projections | every row of `projections`, blob included | (tile, entity, kind) |
| snapshots | every row of `snapshots`, blob included | tile |

Each row is hashed as its little-endian bytes, length-prefixed, with `core/hash`'s `hash_bytes` and the parts chained with `hash_combine` — both the same on every platform — and each table's hash takes its row count. **What it leaves out, and why**: the journal (a patch's attribution carries the wall-clock time it was committed at, so no two runs write the same journal, and it is the document's history, not the world's state); which layer a property sits in and which files a layer is stored as (the composed value is the state); the runtime world (entities, components, flecs ids: rebuilt from the document and the store); and the ring, its observers and the player's input, which drive a run rather than result from it and which a save keeps beside the hash. `session.state_hash` returns each part too, so a mismatch says where it is.

**The same number on every compiler.** The replay test pins the number its world reaches at tick 600 (`15b4cd29460682f8` where the host has the kinematics capability; `034b0fdc9cda96f0` before a tile's projections kept its records' f64 positions on 2026-10-06 (`TileProjection` 2, "Where an observer is" above); `c2168e9307bca23b` before the carts integrated in f64 on 2026-10-05, ADR-0053; `d94f6796ccfc856f` before the store's table version 2 put each event's payload version in its row) and checks it on MSVC and on Clang 18 and GCC 13 in the Linux container. It holds because the world's arithmetic is IEEE `f32` and `f64` with contraction off ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)) and no library transcendental (kinematics integrates with products, sums and a square root), every number reaches the document and the store through the JSON writer's shortest round-trip form, and nothing in a store row or a composed record is a pointer, a hash-table order or a thread's.

## Save and load

[03 §3.5](../plan/03-data-model.md#35-persistent-world-state)'s save game and [ADR-0042](../adr/0042-a-save-is-the-store-the-document-and-the-drivers.md) (proposed): **a save is one directory of files and a manifest that names and hashes every one** (`save_game.h`).

```
<save>/save.json          engine.world.SaveManifest, written last
<save>/document/...       the session's document directory: manifest, layers, journal, side files
<save>/world.db           the store, backed up (store.md, "Backups")
<save>/input.jsonl        the player's input log up to the save's tick, when there is a player
<save>/input-map.json     and the action map it was recorded against
```

The manifest (`systems/world/schemas/world_save.schema`) carries the run's clock (tick, game time, rate, write-back cadence), the world seed, the ring — its parameters and budget, its observers exactly (their positions f64, `worldpos`, and their velocities float32, which widen to an `f64` and back through the JSON writer bit for bit), the active tiles with their rings, the player's observer, action and speed — the random streams a system advances by drawing (none today: every stream in the engine is a hash of the world seed, a tile, an id and a tick), the state hash when it was taken, every format its pieces are written in with the version they were written at (the manifest's own types, `engine.world.TileProjection` and `engine.world.WriteBack` in the store, `store.tables`, `engine.input.log`, the document's file types and every record type it holds), and every file with its size and `hash_bytes`, sorted by path so the manifest's bytes are a function of what it holds.

**What is state and what is derived, and where the line is.** State is what nothing else can rebuild: the document — its layers hold everything the world wrote back, and its journal is its history; the store's three tables — the event log, the projections a tile wrote when it went (written directly by the store consumer, not folded from the log, so not derivable from it today) and the snapshots; the clock; the world seed; the observers and the tiles the ring holds, which hysteresis and the budget make a function of the observers' history rather than of where they are (the test runs a ring under a budget that defers changes, restores a second from its tiles and observers at step 49, and compares their events byte for byte for forty steps after); and the input log so far. Derived is everything a load rebuilds from those: the runtime world's entities and components — a materialization of the document at the saved tiles, which "records follow their tile" makes exact — each tile's seed (the world seed hashed with the tile), what reconciliation did when a tile came in (read again from the same snapshot and projections, which a tile writes only when it goes), the input state, and the document's composed index. **The plan's "latest snapshot plus events since" is the store's own business inside `world.db`**: a save carries the whole database rather than trimming its log to the last snapshot, because the projections are not a fold of the log yet (so the log cannot rebuild them) and `session.events` reads the log's history.

**Nothing is pending when a save is taken.** Every `session.run_headless` ends with the write-back's flush and a settle, so between two calls the document already says where the world is and what is materialized is the document's live tiles'. A save therefore changes nothing — it is read-only in the protocol's sense — and its state hash is `session.state_hash`'s.

**A load continues the run** (`session.load_game`). It reads the manifest and checks it before it touches anything (below), copies the document into a new directory and the store beside it, migrates the store's tables to this build's (`EventLog::open`, a step per version), opens a session there, makes the world **at the save's clock** (`SimSchedulerConfig::start_tick` and `epoch`, [sim](sim.md#the-tick)) and cadence, materializes what no tile holds, and — for a streamed world — makes the ring with the saved parameters and budget, restores its tiles at their rings (`World::restore`: one activation per tile in tile order, so the document consumer materializes each and the store consumer reconciles each from what the store kept, and nothing is written), puts the observers where they were, and binds the player to the saved log, caught up to the tick. A load that migrated nothing must reproduce the state hash the save recorded, or it is refused and the session closed. The next `session.run_headless` continues: declare no observers (or name no `stream`), hand the player's log over again, and the world runs on as the one that never stopped.

**Refused, and migrated.** `read_save` refuses, naming what is wrong: a directory with no `save.json` (a save that did not finish has none — the manifest is written last); a manifest whose format is not `engine.save`; a format this build lacks (`engine.cloth.Drape`, from a capability switched off); a version newer than this build's ("the save's store.tables is version 99, newer than this build's 2"); a file missing, or of another size or hash than the manifest says ("the save's file document/manifest.json hashes … where the manifest says …"); a document, store or input log the manifest names that is not among its own files — a load copies only from inside the save; a field the manifest's type does not have. A save written at an **older** version migrates on read: the manifest's own types follow the schema language's `@since` rule (`schemas/README.md`, "Evolution rules") — a field introduced after the version the save names for its type is refused if present and takes its default if absent — the store's tables migrate when the store is opened, and a record type that has gained fields materializes with their defaults, which the driver already does. The result lists every step as `"<format> <from> -> <to>"`.

**The migration corpus.** `content/migration-corpus/saves/` holds one save per version of the format that has existed ([03 §3.8](../plan/03-data-model.md#38-schema-evolution); `content/migration-corpus/README.md` says what each is and what loading it must produce), and `apps/engine_cli/tests/save_tests.cpp` loads every one on every build: the tick, the hash its manifest recorded, the hash once loaded, every migration step, and the hash after one more game second. There are three. `v1` is the first format — `SaveManifest` 1, `store.tables` 1 — and `v2` the current: version 2 of the manifest added `saved_by` (the engine that wrote the save, `"engine-host 0.0.1"`), which a version 1 manifest lacks and reads as empty, and table version 2 added `events.payload_version` ([store](store.md), "Migrations"), which a version 1 store gains with every old event at 1. Both saves hold the replay test's world at tick 300. **Loaded, they are the same world**: `v1` records `8a6d7d078e00bcbf` and loads, with its two steps, to `3d528309eea2b9df` — the hash `v2` records, and the replay test's own at tick 300 — and both run on to `25aef5f85a9efe56` (`37645e758e706598` before the carts integrated in f64). `v3` is the same world written after `Node` and `Mover` went to version 2 (their positions `worldpos`, ADR-0053), which `v1` and `v2` name as two more steps that rewrite nothing. That the migrated old save and the new one agree to the bit is the corpus's strongest claim: the migration produces exactly what the current version writes, not something that merely loads.

**Byte for byte.** A save loaded and saved again is the same files: the document is copied, not rewritten (a document's load never writes, [doc](doc.md)), the store's backup is a function of its content ([store](store.md), "Backups"), the player's log is cut at the tick, and the manifest is sorted. The world test writes a save, restores it into a fresh directory, saves again from there and compares every file; the end-to-end test does the same through two hosts.

## The world log

`engine.world.TileFrame` (`systems/world/schemas/world_tiles.schema`), one per update: the tiles tracked and active, per ring; the events by kind, the refusals and the changes the budget deferred; the ring's microseconds and every consumer's calls and milliseconds for *this* update (`WorldLog` takes a snapshot of the consumers' totals before the update and reports the difference); and what the renderer holds after it — the scene's instances and pairs, the buildings the ruins hold and their instances. The last line is `engine.world.TileWorldSummary`: the rings, the updates and events, the ring's microseconds (median, p99, max), each consumer's totals and longest call, and the placements consumer's building (the ruins' assembly and block laying) and the renderer taking the tail. engine-view's summary line carries the same object as `world`.

## An authored world

The owner's assumption (2026-09-30): "the tile streamer will also work with world tiles for games that don't procedurally generate anything". **What stands**: the renderer and the collision ask a tile source for a tile's heights and nothing about how they were made ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md#the-tile-source)), and the renderer's tests draw a world from a tile set built ahead of time, written into a derived-data cache and read back, pixel for pixel what the procedural source draws ([renderer](renderer.md#the-ground-from-the-worlds-tiles)). So the drawing and the walking half works for a world nobody generates. **What an authored world still lacks**, exactly:

1. **A way for a scene to name a tile set.** A tile source is handed to the renderer by its host (a test, today); `engine.scene.Terrain` names a ground provider, and there is no provider — or scene field — that opens a tile set from the derived-data cache. The smallest step is a ground provider (`tiles`, registered like any other) whose parameters name the set's manifest and whose `tiles()` is the set, so the registry and every host serve it unchanged.
2. **A content-build step that writes one**: `engine-content` builds meshes, textures and the ruins' fragments; nothing reads an authored heightfield (a DEM, an editor's sculpted tiles) and writes the set — each tile's heights at the finest level's spacing with a one-point apron, keyed by content, plus a manifest of its extent and spacings — as a `derived` node with a cache key the way a `.clusters` file has one. **And its mip chain** (2026-10-03, [renderer](renderer.md#ground-to-the-horizon), [ADR-0051](../adr/0051-the-far-ground-is-the-renderers.md)): the renderer's far levels ask the source for a lattice *filtered to* each far spacing (`TileSourceOps::heights`'s `filter_mm`), and a set built ahead answers that from a coarser lattice it stores beside the finest, refusing a filter it was not built with. So the build writes, for every far spacing the renderer may ask (the first far level's — `far_ratio` times the outermost ring's spacing — and each doubling of it up to the last), the authored surface **box-filtered over each point's own cell** — the mean of the finest heights over the square one coarse spacing wide round the point, which a heightfield with no generator behind it can only compute from the finest — in tiles of the same points a side with a point of apron, keyed by the spacing as a filter, and records the chain's spacings in the manifest, so a renderer whose far levels the set does not cover refuses it at load rather than drawing holes. Each level is a quarter of the one below it, so the whole chain is a third more than the finest. The renderer's test set (`terrain_tiles_gpu_tests.cpp`) has that chain, written from the procedural source's own filtered answer, and draws the procedural picture from it pixel for pixel; it is also the case where the chain's extent matters — a far level's window is kilometres to tens of kilometres, so the chain covers far more ground than the finest.
3. **Maps.** A tile source serves heights. A tile's colour, roughness and sand share are the scene's baked maps over its grid's UV frame, clamped at the grid's edge; an authored world needs its maps per tile, and the renderer a material (or a texture array) per slot to draw them.
4. **Point heights for placement and cameras.** A scene reader grounds an instance and a camera key on `GroundProvider::height`; a set would answer it by interpolating its tiles (item 1's provider).
5. **Holes and overhangs.** A heightfield is a function of (x, z); cliffs, caves and arches are placements (meshes on a tile), which the placements consumer streams already — but an authored world's tile set would need its placements authored per tile too, and that is a placement generator reading its own set.
6. **The world's persistent layers.** The overlay and the tile lag (the ground consumer's, [The consumers](#the-consumers)) are the procedural dunes'; an authored world would take the overlay's mechanism unchanged and has no lag.

What it does **not** lack: the tile ring, its consumers and their order, the store, the save, the renderer's slots and seams, the time-lapse (a set that does not move is a level whose fields never change), and the collision.

## Invariants

Each is a test in `tests/world_tests.cpp`, `tile_store_tests.cpp`, `consumer_tests.cpp` or `placement_tiles_tests.cpp` unless it says otherwise.

- Tile order is x then z, signed, and `tile_key` sorts in it; `store_tile` is the write-back packing.
- A still observer's rings are exactly the tiles whose centres are within each radius, and each tile's ring is `TierAssignment::tier_of` of its score. A second update from the same place changes nothing.
- The budget spreads a first fill over updates, the nearest first (the default rings fill 1,804 tiles in 227 updates of eight); a teleport activates the whole inner ring among its first eight and deactivates the old place farthest first.
- The same observer path gives the same events, byte for byte (`TileEvent`'s padding is a named, zeroed byte, sim.md's rule for a type whose bytes are compared).
- Hysteresis: a boundary walk crosses once where no band crosses forty times.
- Two observers take the nearer; a heavier one reaches farther.
- Consumers activate in registration order and deactivate in reverse; within one event the deactivations run first; a consumer outside a ring sees the tile come and go at the ring's edge; a refusal is counted and the tile stays active for the others.
- The store round-trips a tile's projections and snapshot, and reconciliation over it reads the gap from the snapshot, the seed from the world and the tile, and the records from the projections, resolving and promoting them — across a closed and reopened file.
- A tile of a partitioned document materializes when it activates, is written to the store and dematerialized when it goes, and reconciles from what it wrote when it comes back; a document on another grid is refused, once, with a sentence.
- The placements consumer's tile holds exactly the ruins generator's building — the assembler's — for that tile in the ring's representation — blocks, sections, walls — the tail is every drawn tile's instances in tile order; and under a pair budget that cannot draw every tile, every tile drawn outranks every tile withheld (by ring, then by the ring's score), the scene stays within the budget, and a withheld tile is drawn again once the ring holds less, with no event of its own.
- The scene reader streams a world's ruins entries instead of assembling them: the generator's resident meshes (both kits'), no instance; a whole read is unchanged; `--world` on a file whose entries are on another grid is refused (`placement_tiles_tests.cpp`).
- End to end: engine-view streaming a scene's four buildings draws the same picture a whole read draws, 0 of 57,600 pixels different (`apps/engine_view/tests/ruins_view_tests.cpp`); `session.run_headless` with `stream` materializes a partitioned document's tiles round a walking observer and reconciles them from the store (`apps/engine_cli/tests/ops_tests.cpp`). A walk replayed offscreen over the erg drawn from the world's tiles is the same walk, tick for tick and to the walker's state chain, as over its grid, and its feet stay within 10 cm of the finest tile level as drawn, off the grid's edge as on it, still and at a day a second (`apps/engine_view/tests/walk_view_tests.cpp`, "a walk on the world's tiles").
- A ring restored from the tiles another held, at their rings, with its observers, goes on to the same events byte for byte; a restore onto a ring that holds tiles, of tiles out of order, or into a ring it does not have is refused and changes nothing (`save_tests.cpp`).
- The ground consumer over the dunes, made through the registry as a scene reader makes them, holds the generator's tile at the ring's resolution; a footprint goes to every held tile it covers and to none when nobody holds it; an overlay written when its tile went and read when it came back is the overlay that never left, byte for byte; a month away buries it and its projection is erased; a pit's lag rebuilds the tiles round it and their shared edge agrees; a record's key in the store is the one `TerrainTiles` wrote; the waves, which have no tiles, are refused (`ground_tiles_tests.cpp`, where the terrain capability is). A ruin's debris lies on the generator's floor (`ruins_on_dunes_tests.cpp`, where both are).
- The placements consumer over a streamed city holds, for each tile, the plan's per-tile query at the ring's detail — rooms in the inner ring, floors in the next — as instances of the twelve resident proxy boxes; a whole read is the whole city; a city on another grid than the world's is refused when the scene is read (`city_tiles_tests.cpp`, where the city capability is).
- The scene's consumers are registered ground first and placements after it: a tile's ground is built before any placement entry is asked for the tile, the entries are asked in the scene's order — swapped in the scene, swapped in the log, the ground still first — and a tile's placements are dropped before its ground goes; a ground with no tiles is not registered (`scene_order_tests.cpp`, with a ground and a generator of the test's own and no capability).
- A record a write-back moved into a live tile of the consumer's rings is filed under it; one moved where nothing is live is let go; one a deactivation's own flush moves is settled before the tile is written, so the tile it left writes no projection of it (`consumer_tests.cpp`).
- The state hash is the persistent state's however it was written: the same records made in two orders, one of them in a journaled transaction, hash the same; two stores holding the same rows written in two orders hash the same; a written-back property, the clock and an event each change it (`save_tests.cpp`).
- A player's input state is a fold of its log: bound at tick 25 and caught up, it steers exactly as one bound at tick 0; a log recorded against another map, an action that is not an `Axis2`, and a negative speed are refused (`save_tests.cpp`).
- A save reads back as written; restored and saved again it is the same files byte for byte, manifest included; a changed file, a missing one, a newer version, a format the build lacks, an unknown field, a document path outside the save and a directory that is not a save are refused naming what is wrong, as is a save into a directory that is not empty or inside the document; a version 1 manifest reads with `saved_by` empty and one migration step, and a version 1 manifest carrying `saved_by` is refused naming the field (`save_tests.cpp`).
- The migration corpus: every save under `content/migration-corpus/saves/` loads to the tick, hashes and migration steps its README records and runs on one game second to the recorded hash; `v1`, migrated, and `v2` load to the same state and run on to the same state; a build without the kinematics capability refuses both naming `engine.kinematics.Mover` (`apps/engine_cli/tests/save_tests.cpp`).
- End to end, the replay assertion: a streamed world with a scripted observer, a player steered by an input log and carts driving across tile edges, run 300 ticks, saved, and run 300 more; the save loaded in a fresh host and run the same 300 with the log handed over again; and the starting document run 600 ticks in one call — the three state hashes and all their parts are one, the loaded world saved again is the same files, and the number is the one pinned for every compiler (`apps/engine_cli/tests/save_tests.cpp`).

## Performance notes

Measured in `msvc-release` on the development machine (i9-10980XE, RTX 5090); the flythrough's numbers are [E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md)'s.

- **The ring** (`world.ring.*`, `--repeats=5`, beside another agent's test suite at 12% of the CPU, so upper bounds): an update of the default rings round one observer moving 1.5 m an update, **77 µs**; round two observers 173 µs and four 367 µs, since each observer's window is enumerated and merged; a first fill from nothing, 1,810 tiles at once, 303 µs. Along E35's flythrough the same update was 82 µs at the median (p99 258 µs) over 2,036 active tiles, and 21 µs under rings of 1.5, 6 and 10 tiles (354 active).
- **A tile through the store** (`world.store.tile`): 64 records' projections and a snapshot written, and read back, in an in-memory store, **0.51 ms** (8 µs a record).
- **The placements consumer over the ruins** (E35, when it was `RuinsTiles`): a building assembled in sections 17 µs (the kit of boxes) and 22 µs (E33's ashlar kit); laid in blocks 235–238 µs, the section assembler it runs first included; a deactivation nothing measurable. Since both forms lay one rubble field, the handover runs measured 25 and 34 µs in sections (more debris members, each on its own terrain query) and 140 µs laid in blocks (fewer fallen blocks), on a machine a third busy ([E35](../experiments/e35-world-streaming.md#after-the-rubble-rule)). The consumer's calls are 0.05 ms at the median on a frame with events and 1.3–1.8 ms at the worst.
- **The renderer taking the tail** was the cost that mattered, and is not any more: E35 measured 3.2 ms a change for a thousand buildings of the kit of boxes (21,000 instances), 15–17 ms for the ashlar kit at 5–9 million pairs and 34 ms at the median at the 2^24 budget, each waiting for the device, because every change rewrote the whole tail; with each tile a block of its own in the renderer, the pair entries expanded on the GPU and a table set per frame in flight, [E37](../experiments/e37-tile-instance-blocks.md) measured 0.36 ms for the boxes and 0.17–0.18 ms for the ashlar kit on the same path, with no wait at all. What is left is the tail itself: the ruins consumer concatenates every drawn tile on each commit and the renderer compares every tile it is handed against what it holds, which is why the boxes, with more and smaller instances, cost twice what the heavier kit does.
- **The budget's ranking** (a sort of the tiles held on every commit) does not show beside the tail.

## Not yet

- **A dynamic scene is rigid and rasterized.** The renderer's tail has no ray tracing chain and no deformed-vertex pool ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)): the chain's top-level records and the pool's table are laid out once per instance. A streamed world therefore draws its shadows with the cascaded maps or none.
- **The handover pops.** Nothing cross-fades or dithers between a tile's two instance sets. E35 measured the pop (above); the two forms now agree on the ground, which halved it, and what is left is mostly the walls' tops. A dither over both sets for a few frames would hide it, and is renderer work across every rasterizer and the ray path (E35 lists what it needs); walls whose section variants follow the blocks' courses would shrink it first. Demotions happen behind a camera that moves forward and were never on screen along the flythrough.
- **No far tier.** Past the outer ring nothing is drawn; E33's slab is the answer and does not exist.
- **Every change hands over the whole tail.** The renderer keeps each tile in a block of its own and writes only what changed ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go), [E37](../experiments/e37-tile-instance-blocks.md): 0.17–0.36 ms a change, no wait for the device), but the sink is still one span of every drawn tile, so the consumer concatenates the tail and the renderer compares every tile against what it holds to find the ones that moved. A sink that named the tiles that changed would cost the change and nothing else. The blocks leave holes in the renderer's pair space that its cull dispatches — a median 14–18% of it along E35's path, bounded at 25% by a compaction — and a hole costs the cull what a pair it culls does.
- **The ring does not promote or demote the document's records.** The document consumer materializes at the driver's one tier; the ring's rings map to representations, not yet to the simulation's tiers, and reconciliation's promotion is its own. Residents are the exception, and not the ring's doing: the npc capability scores the residents it holds against the ring's observers and drives their `promote`/`demote` itself ([npc](npc.md)).
- **The store's projections outlive a deleted record** until its tile is written again with it gone; `upsert` cannot tell a deletion from a record that moved away.
- **The world seed** of a headless run's store is 1; a document has no world seed to give it yet. A save carries it, so the day a document has one a load gives it back.
- **An edit between calls to a record in a live tile reaches the world when its tile next comes in**, not on the next call: a streamed call's pass is over the untiled records only. So a save taken while such an edit is pending loads with the edit applied, where the run that never stopped would not see it until the tile came back — the one way a load and the continuous run can differ that the replay test does not exercise, since its only inputs are the observers and the input log. The fix is a change-feed pass over the live tiles at the start of a call; a tile pass now costs its tile (the document's tile index, [sim](sim.md#the-contract-as-implemented)), so that pass would cost the records that changed.
- **A run split over calls is the unsplit run only when it splits on the write-back cadence.** Every call ends with a flush, so a call that ends off the cadence commits once more than an unsplit run would; a save taken there, loaded and run on, matches the split run, not the unsplit one. The replay test splits at 300 with a cadence of 60.
- **A save is the whole store**, not the latest snapshot and the events since: the projections are written directly rather than folded from the log, so the log cannot rebuild them yet, and nothing trims the log. A save is therefore as large as the store and the document together — the replay test's at tick 300 is 107 KB: a 40 KB store of five 8 KiB pages, a 37 KB journal of write-backs, and the document's tile files — and there is no incremental save.
- **engine-view does not save.** Its world streams the ruins for the renderer and has no document or store to save; `--save-at` would save nothing a load could continue.
- **The drawn ground is not the ground consumer's.** Since 2026-09-30 the renderer draws a world's ground from its tiles ([The drawn ground](#the-consumers)), but from the scene's ground provider seen as tiles, not from the ground consumer's held tiles: the overlay (footprints, pits, the sand a drift is short of) and the tile lag are kept in the store and drawn by nobody, and the collision reads the same source the renderer does, so neither sees them. Drawing them is the tile source taking a tile's overlay and lag, or the pool reading a deformation map (05 §5.13). No host stamps footprints yet, and no host fills the drift callback from the ruins.
- **Ground tiles and streamed placements are not drawn together.** A world's streamed placements make the scene dynamic, and a dynamic scene has no deformed-vertex pool, which every drawn tile is drawn through ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)): a scene asking for both is refused with a sentence.

## Capability contract (ADR-0027)

This is a capability: it was added without editing `core/`, `foundation/`, the render graph, the scheduler, or another capability, and it can be removed from the build the same way. Outside its directory it added a registration point to `domain/sim` (`Materializer::dematerialize(scope)` and `held(scope)`, the missing mirror of a tile pass), used another (`settle` calls the driver's `materialize_arrivals`, for the npc capability's schedule index — [above](#records-that-move)), the renderer's instance tail and the scene reader's streamed entries ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)), and the hosts' glue.

| Registration point | This capability | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Capabilities it requires | `engine_capability_requires(world store)`; `ruins` and `terrain` where configured | declared; off when store is off |
| Component and event types | `systems/world/schemas/world_tiles.schema`: the store's projection and the world log's lines; `world_save.schema`: a save's manifest | done |
| Tick scheduler entry | none: the update runs between ticks, where materialization must | not needed |
| Render-graph passes | none: the placements consumer hands the renderer instances it already draws | not needed |
| Content-build derived step | none: a tile's building is assembled at activation, from its seed | not needed |
| Protocol methods | `session.run_headless` takes the ring (`stream`) and its player; `session.save_game`, `session.load_game` and `session.state_hash` are this capability's, registered by engine-host, and answer 1006 without it | done |
| Tunables | `world.ring.max_activations`, `world.ring.max_deactivations` | done |
| LOD policy | the ring itself: `sim::TierAssignment` over tiles, with hysteresis and a budget | done |
| Determinism | `world::k_determinism` = `hashed`: the active set is a function of the observer sequence and decides what is materialized; the ruins it draws are `derived`; `world::state_hash` is the persistent-state hash a replay compares, pinned across compilers | done |
| Zero cost when unused | no linked code (`ENGINE_WITH_WORLD=OFF`); a scene with no `world` block and no `--world` runs no ring, and a headless run with no `stream` none either | done |
| Tests and size table | `tests/world_tests.cpp`, `tile_store_tests.cpp`, `consumer_tests.cpp`, `placement_tiles_tests.cpp` (where the ruins are), `ruins_on_dunes_tests.cpp` (where the terrain is too), `tests/size_table.cpp` (`TileCoord` 8 bytes, `TileEvent` 12) | done |
| Bench | `bench/world_bench.cpp`: `world.ring.update`, `world.ring.fill`, `world.store.tile` | done |
| Removal proof | `ENGINE_WITH_WORLD`, off in the minimal build | works |

**Removing it.** `cmake --preset msvc-minimal` (or `-DENGINE_WITH_WORLD=OFF`) drops the module, its tests, and its bench; the module disappears from `build/<preset>/modules.json` and is listed there under `disabled_capabilities`. engine-view then refuses `--world` with a sentence, and engine-host answers a `stream`, `session.save_game`, `session.load_game` and `session.state_hash` with 1006 Unavailable.
