# window (foundation)

**Purpose.** OS windows, input events (keyboard, mouse, gamepads, raw joysticks), force feedback and rumble, and the two pieces of Vulkan glue a window system owns — the instance extensions a surface needs and surface creation — in a backend header of their own ([below](#the-vulkan-surface)). SDL3 (ADR-0012, zlib) sits underneath, statically linked with video, events, and gamepads only; nothing outside this module includes SDL. Headless machines keep working: `init()` reports failure and callers run without a window, which is how the CI runners and `engine-host` behave. This module stays raw — the binding table and the per-tick state a game reads are `foundation/input`'s, and it deliberately does not depend on this one.

**API.** `window::init()` starts the video subsystem (false with the reason on a machine without a display) and then the gamepad and joystick subsystems, whose failure is logged rather than returned: a machine with no joystick driver still gets a window. `Window::create(WindowDesc)` opens a resizable, high-DPI-aware window; `pixel_width()/pixel_height()` are the framebuffer size, refreshed on every `Resized` event; `logical_size()` is the window's size in its own coordinates — the ones a pointer event's `x`/`y` are in — asked of SDL each call, which on a display that scales is smaller than the framebuffer (a 1280 × 720 window at 150 % is 1920 × 1080 pixels), so a host that turns the pointer into a pixel of its picture multiplies by the ratio of the two (engine-editor's picking and gizmo, [apps](apps.md#engine-editor-the-engines-window-on-a-document); added 2026-10-10 because a click landed beside its target under scaling). `poll(Event&)` pulls one event at a time: `Quit`, `CloseRequested`, `Resized`, focus, `KeyDown/KeyUp` (a small `Key` enum for the common keys — `Delete` last, after the F keys, so no earlier value was renumbered — plus the raw scancode), mouse move, buttons, and wheel. `window.h` names no graphics API; the surface glue is in [the Vulkan surface](#the-vulkan-surface) below. **A Vulkan window takes the GPU lock before it appears**: with `WindowDesc::vulkan` (the default) and the test environment's switch on, `create()` joins the process's hold on the machine-wide GPU lock before `SDL_CreateWindow` (or, for a window created hidden, `show()` does, before it shows it — a hidden window is on nobody's screen and takes nothing), and `destroy()` leaves it after the window is gone ([gpu_lock](gpu_lock.md#windows)). A window is for a GPU device, and a process that has to wait for the lock must wait with nothing on screen: on 2026-09-30 two test engine-views that had opened their windows and then waited in `Device::create`, which pumps no messages, sat "Not responding" on the owner's desktop for a quarter of an hour behind a merge gate's hold. With the switch off — every session a person starts — it takes nothing.

## Borderless windows and presentation

`WindowDesc::borderless` (engine-view's `--borderless`) opens a window with no title bar or border at the primary display's top-left corner (`SDL_WINDOW_BORDERLESS`, then `SDL_GetDisplayBounds` of `SDL_GetPrimaryDisplay`), so a window of the display's size covers it exactly — the "borderless fullscreen" a game offers. It is **not** an exclusive fullscreen: the display mode never changes and other windows can come over it. Sizes are in pixels on Windows here (a `--width 1280` window has a 1280-pixel swapchain on the 150 %-scaled desktop), so `--width` and `--height` of the display are what cover it.

**What covering the display changes, and what it does not** (2026-09-25, [the second owner session](../experiments/second-interactive-session-2026-09-25.md#present-pacing)). The owner's three 4K monitors are **one display to Windows**: NVIDIA Surround presents them as a single 11520×2160 display at 82 Hz (`\\.\DISPLAY1`, 12.2063 ms a refresh by the driver's own report), with one refresh clock, so a window spanning them is not a window spanning three displays and there is no clock disagreement between monitors to pace against; a 1920×1080 60 Hz monitor above them is `\\.\DISPLAY2`. What decides how a window paces is whether it **covers that display**: a window that does is flipped straight to it by the compositor (its present-to-display interval is exactly the refresh, frame after frame), and one that does not is composed by DWM (its frames reach the display at the refresh on average, but one in six or so a few milliseconds late and the next early, and 50–60 ms after they were sampled with the default queue). **Decorated or borderless made no difference**: the owner's decorated 11520×2160 window covers the display with its title bar pushed off the top, and it and a borderless one paced identically in every configuration measured (the pairs of frames 0.6 ms and 23.7 ms apart included), so `--borderless` is a convenience, not a fix; the fix is the display pacer ([apps](apps.md#pacing)). A window that spans several displays with separate clocks — two monitors without Surround — was not measured; it is composed by DWM on the primary display's clock, and the pacer's miss detection is what would notice it.

## Child windows

**A window may be a child of another, at a rectangle, movable** (2026-10-11, roadmap A37). `WindowDesc::parent` makes the window a rectangle inside the parent's client area — at (`x`, `y`) in the parent's coordinates, `width` by `height`, no border, clipped by the parent and moved with it — and `Window::set_rect` moves and sizes it inside the parent, raising it above its siblings, with its pixel size following at once; `set_visible` shows or hides it (taking the GPU-lock hold on first show, as `show()` does for a Vulkan top-level window); `focus()` gives it the keyboard; `is_child()` says which it is. It carries a Vulkan surface exactly as a top-level window does (`backend/vulkan/surface.h` is unchanged), which is the point: engine-editor keeps its swapchain in a child placed over the web shell's viewport cell while the parent's other child is an OS web view ([webview](webview.md), [apps](apps.md#engine-editor-the-engines-window-on-a-document)). `Window::os_handle()` gives the platform's own handle (an HWND) for a module that embeds a platform view in a window without naming SDL.

**Why not SDL's own.** SDL3's `SDL_CreateWindowWithProperties` takes a parent, but what it makes is an *owned top-level* window — a popup, a tooltip, a modal — that the platform does not clip to the parent's client area and does not move inside it, and whose keyboard focus SDL routes to the parent. So on Windows the child is a plain `WS_CHILD | WS_CLIPSIBLINGS` window of the module's own class (black background, so a child that has just grown shows the dark of the cell rather than white for the frame before it is drawn), which SDL then **adopts** as an external window (`SDL_PROP_WINDOW_CREATE_WIN32_HWND_POINTER` with `SDL_PROP_WINDOW_CREATE_VULKAN_BOOLEAN`): its pointer events and its Vulkan surface are SDL's, as a top-level window's are, and nothing above this module sees the difference. `destroy()` lets SDL go first and then destroys the window, which SDL does not do for one it adopted. Elsewhere `create` refuses a parent with the reason (the only user, the editor's embedded shell, is Windows-only too).

**Events arrive through the parent's `poll`**, marked `Event::child` (a `bool` in what was padding): SDL has one event queue per process, so polling the child alone would drop the parent's, and a small module table says whose child a window id is. A child's pointer coordinates are its own; its `Resized` is its own size and leaves the parent's alone. **Keyboard focus is the parent's in SDL's eyes**: SDL gives a window keyboard focus only when it is the foreground window, which a child never is, so keys typed while the child has the platform's focus come through the parent's `poll` *unmarked* — a host that embeds other platform views (a web view handles its own keys and never reaches SDL) can treat every key SDL reports as the child's. A child is not activated by a click as a top-level window is, so a host calls `focus()` when the pointer presses in it. A child's `logical_size` is its client size in pixels, the coordinates SDL reports its pointer in (SDL's own idea of an adopted window's size can lag a move it did not make).

**Relative mode on an adopted child** (2026-10-12, roadmap A39). `set_relative_mouse(true)` on a child is **this module's own mode, not SDL's**, because SDL's cannot work there, and how that was found is worth keeping. The owner's report: in engine-editor the right-button look "moves a tiny bit, then stops". The scripted `--look` test passed, because it pushes the gesture into SDL's queue as the child's own events and never takes the path a pointer takes. engine-editor's `--trace-input` (one line per pointer event, with `Window::pointer_report()`: SDL's keyboard focus and mouse focus, whether SDL's mode was asked and in effect, the cursor in the child's coordinates, SDL's and the platform's idea of the child's rectangle, the cursor clip) over a real pointer driven by `SendInput` showed it at once: between the right button's press and its release not one motion event reached the editor, under any window id; SDL's flag sat on the child (id 4) while SDL's keyboard focus was the top-level window (id 3), the mode was never in effect, the cursor walked out of the child and the camera turned 0°. The cause is two rules of SDL's (3.4): **its relative mode belongs to its keyboard focus** — the mode is on only while the keyboard-focus window carries the flag, and SDL gives keyboard focus only to a window that is the platform's foreground window (`WIN_UpdateFocus` compares with `GetForegroundWindow`), which a `WS_CHILD` never is — and **it drops the absolute motion of a window that carries the flag** while the mode is off (`SDL_PrivateSendMouseMotion`: absolute motion for a flagged mouse-focus window is discarded unless warp motion is asked for). So the motion the frame before the flag went on got through — the "tiny bit" — and nothing after. SDL's rectangle for the child matched the platform's to the pixel (hypothesis (a) of the brief, a stale rectangle, was not it), asking only on a change moved nothing (SDL returns early for a mode a window already has), and no motion was dropped by `route` for its window id: there was none. Asking SDL's mode of the **top-level parent** instead (which does hold the keyboard focus) put the mode in effect, and then SDL's raw-input thread delivered nothing at all — no motion, and not the release — beside the web view's cross-process child window; that was measured too and abandoned. What a child gets now: on `set_relative_mouse(true)`, if the cursor is over the child and the child's window is the platform's foreground window (a real pointer's gesture), the cursor is hidden (`SDL_HideCursor`) and confined to the child's rectangle (`ClipCursor`); each `MouseMove` of the child then reports `dx`/`dy` as its distance from the point where the gesture began, at which `x`/`y` stay, and the cursor is put back there at once (`SDL_WarpMouseInWindow`, whose own motion message SDL ignores) — so the platform's next motion message, which Windows makes from the cursor's position when it is read, runs from there too; on `set_relative_mouse(false)` (or the window losing focus) the clip goes, the cursor is shown where the gesture began. A gesture posted to a window that is not in front (a test) leaves the desktop's cursor alone and reports the child's motion as it comes, which is the same delta. SDL's flag never goes on a child. The deltas are the platform's cursor pixels, after the system's pointer acceleration, where a top-level window's SDL mode gives raw device counts; measured on the owner's desktop with the same 300 counts of `SendInput` motion: 478 pixels, 30 events, the camera turned 60.25°. `relative_mouse()` on a child says whether the cursor is held; `relative_mouse_asked()` whether the mode was asked for (they differ for a posted gesture). One child at a time holds it; asking again for the mode a window has does nothing.

**What the child's messages say** (2026-10-14, roadmap A40). engine-editor's two cases that drive the viewport through the platform's queue (`--test-look-os`, `--test-keys`; [apps](apps.md#engine-editor-the-engines-window-on-a-document)) passed or failed by what the owner's desktop was doing: the look failed whenever another process held the foreground, the keys too whenever the session was locked, and both beside other suites' windows. `--trace-input` under exactly that condition (another process in front, the cursor elsewhere) said why the look's background branch turned the camera **0°** and not by the posted amount: the press arrived, then **a release nobody posted**, then the three moves, each followed by a motion to the desktop's cursor. SDL arms `TrackMouseEvent` on the first motion after each `WM_MOUSELEAVE`, and the platform posts the leave at once when the desktop's cursor is not over the window; on the next motion SDL, finding itself untracked, reads the buttons from the device (`WIN_CheckAsyncMouseRelease`, `GetAsyncKeyState`) and releases every one the device says is up — and a posted press has no device behind it. The leave's own handler then reports a motion to wherever the desktop's cursor is (`GetMessagePos`), which moved SDL's idea of the pointer, so the next delta was hundreds of pixels. So a posted gesture turned the camera only in the one state nobody arranges: the cursor over the child. The keys' failure is the other SDL rule above: a key goes to SDL's keyboard focus, which is only ever the platform's foreground window, so under the lock (and anywhere the editor is not in front) SDL gave the posted keys window id 0 and `poll` dropped them. Neither is a matter of posting activation first: a posted `WM_ACTIVATE` or `WM_SETFOCUS` moves nothing in SDL, because `WIN_UpdateFocus` compares the window with `GetForegroundWindow()` whatever the message says.

So on Windows a child's procedure is **this module's, in front of SDL's** (which it calls), and an SDL event filter reads what SDL makes while the child's procedure handles a message. Three corrections, each where SDL consults the device or the desktop instead of the message it was handed: **a release SDL reads from the device during a motion is not reported** while the child's own button messages hold that button and the child holds the capture (under which the platform delivers every release to it as a message; the release message, when it comes, is reported by the module if SDL, having let go already, makes none); **a motion SDL makes up during `WM_MOUSELEAVE` is not reported** while it has let go of a button that way, a motion's `dx`/`dy` is from the child's previous pointer message (not SDL's last position, which that made-up motion moved), and a button event's position is its message's; and **a key the child's procedure handles that SDL gives no window** (no keyboard focus) is the child's parent's, unmarked, as a key SDL gives its focus is. Touch and pen events, whose mouse ids are SDL's own, and everything SDL makes outside the child's procedure (a pushed event, a warp) pass untouched. For a person's pointer nothing changes: with the button physically down the device agrees with the messages, a release under capture is always a message, and the corrections never fire; held mode is decided as before (A39 is not reopened). `window: a child's pointer and keys are what its messages say` posts a right-button drag and Ctrl+Z to a hidden child, a step at a time as a host's frames do, where the cursor can never be over it and it is never in front: before the change it saw the press and the release together and no key at all; after, the press, three moves of 10 pixels and the release, and all four keys. `window::desktop_state()` reports the desktop this thread is on, the executable whose window the platform has in front (none off the input desktop) and whether the session is locked (`WTSQuerySessionInformationW`), for a report that has to say what the desktop was doing.

**A desktop that is not the input desktop**, measured on 2026-10-14 with engine-editor started by `CreateProcessW` with `STARTUPINFOW::lpDesktop` on a desktop the test made with `CreateDesktopW` (what `tests/support/test_desktop.h` does for every editor end-to-end case): the Vulkan swapchain presents (14 and 34 frames drawn, exit 0) and WebView2 makes its child and loads the page (the page's layout placed the viewport); **`GetForegroundWindow()` is NULL** throughout, so no window there is ever in front and SDL gives none of them its keyboard focus; `GetCursorPos` fails with error 5 (access denied), `SetCursorPos` returns false, `GetAsyncKeyState` reads every button up, and `ClipCursor` returns true (whether it confines the input desktop's cursor was deliberately not tried; the module calls it only for a window in front, which never happens there). Before the corrections above both cases failed there exactly as under the lock — yaw 0 and no undo — which is what made the private desktop a test of them rather than a way around them.

**Dropped files.** `EventKind::DropFile` (2026-10-12, A39) reports a file dropped on a window from the platform's file manager — SDL's `SDL_EVENT_DROP_FILE`, routed like a pointer event, so a child's drop arrives marked `child` with the drop point in its coordinates — with the path in `Event::text` (UTF-8), which points at the module's own copy and is valid until the next `poll`: `Event` stays a plain value, 64 bytes now (`text` is the pointer). One event per file; a drag's progress and dropped text are not reported. `push_event` takes one too (the caller keeps the string alive until it is polled), which is how a test reaches the editor's drop path without a file manager.

## The display's scale, and events through the platform's queue

**`EventKind::ScaleChanged`** (2026-10-11, roadmap A38) reports that the display's scale under a top-level window changed — the window was dragged to a monitor of another scale, or the scale was changed in the system's settings — with the new scale in `Event::value` (1.5 at 150 %); `Window::display_scale()` reads it at any time (SDL's `SDL_GetWindowDisplayScale`; 1 without a window). SDL sends one or two when a window is first shown, at the scale it already has, so a host compares with the scale it last saw before acting. On Windows the window's coordinates are pixels at every scale, so a scale change moves nothing a host computed from them by itself; what changes is how many pixels a web page's CSS pixel is, which is why engine-editor re-places its viewport and asks its page to lay out again ([apps](apps.md#engine-editor-the-engines-window-on-a-document)). `Window::set_size(w, h)` resizes a top-level window (the platform answers with `Resized`); a child is placed by its parent with `set_rect`.

**`Window::post_os_event`** posts a key, a mouse button or a motion to the **platform's** queue for the window (`PostMessageW` to its HWND on Windows; keys carry their scancode in the message as a real one does, which is what SDL reads), so it takes the path a real event takes from there — the window procedure, the platform's keyboard focus, SDL's translation — where `push_event` starts after all of it; `Window::post_to_focus` posts to whichever window has this thread's keyboard focus, which may be another module's (a web view's); `has_os_focus()` says whether this window has it. They are how engine-editor's `--test-keys` proves that a click moves the keyboard into the viewport and that a key typed while the page has it never reaches SDL, without handing a keystroke to whatever window the desktop has in front. A posted key is then SDL's like a typed one, so it goes to SDL's keyboard focus, which SDL gives only to the foreground window (above): on a desktop where the test's window is not in front, a key posted to a top-level window reaches SDL with no window and is dropped, as a real one could not be sent; one posted to a child is its parent's ([what the child's messages say](#child-windows), A40).

## The Vulkan surface

`foundation/window/backend/vulkan/surface.h` is a **backend header**, under the convention gfx's are ([gfx](gfx.md#the-rhi-surface-and-the-backend-surface)): `window::vulkan::instance_extensions()` feeds `gfx::DeviceOptions::instance_extensions`, and `window::vulkan::create_surface(window, instance, out)` and `destroy_surface(instance, surface)` bracket a `gfx::Swapchain`. Until 2026-09-25 these were members of `Window` in `window.h`, which put `VkInstance` and `VkSurfaceKHR` in the public header everything that opens a window compiles against — `engine-input`, which only reads devices, and engine-view's input conversion among them — for the benefit of the one caller that presents. Now `window.h` names no graphics API, and only what presents includes the backend header: `apps/engine_view` and gfx's swapchain test. `tools/lint.ps1` keeps both halves (`vulkan-in-public-header` allows Vulkan names only under `backend/vulkan/`; `vulkan-backend-include` allows including this directory only from `domain/gfx`, `foundation/window` and `apps/engine_view`). A second graphics backend brings its own glue beside it; a D3D12 swapchain wants the window's HWND, which `Window::native()` already hands out.

The two handle types are still forward-declared the way SDL does it (`typedef struct VkInstance_T* VkInstance`), so the header pulls in neither SDL nor the Vulkan headers and `foundation/window` (layer 1) stays below `domain/gfx` (layer 2); on 64-bit targets the typedefs are exactly vulkan_core.h's, and a translation unit that includes both sees the same typedef twice, which C++ allows. The three functions are implemented in `window.cpp` beside the rest, where SDL is. SDL's Vulkan entry points exist whatever display backends SDL was built with, so the header compiles and links under `ENGINE_WINDOW_BACKENDS=none` as well, where `init()` fails first and nothing calls it.

**Which display a window is on** (2026-10-09, E39): `Window::desktop_center(x, y)` is the window's centre in desktop coordinates (`SDL_GetWindowPosition` plus half its size), false without a window or where the platform does not say. engine-view hands it to `gfx::output_at` to find the output its window is on, whose peak and SDR white level an HDR picture is drawn for ([renderer](renderer.md#hdr-output)). The colour spaces a surface offers past sRGB non-linear need `VK_EXT_swapchain_colorspace`, which the presenting device enables on the instance beside this module's surface extensions where the loader has it ([gfx](gfx.md#the-hdr-branches-and-the-surfaces-offers)); this module adds nothing for it.

**Gamepads.** `poll()` also reports `GamepadConnected`, `GamepadDisconnected`, `GamepadButtonDown`, `GamepadButtonUp`, and `GamepadAxis`. `Event::gamepad` is this module's own slot id, `0..k_max_gamepads-1` (8), handed out lowest free first and released on disconnect — not SDL's instance id, which grows for the life of the process and would make a replay depend on the order pads were plugged in. `GamepadButton` is the standard layout named by position rather than by label (`South` is Xbox A, PlayStation cross, Nintendo B), plus the dpad, shoulders, sticks, `Back`/`Guide`/`Start`, and `LeftTrigger`/`RightTrigger` for the pads whose triggers click digitally (SDL routes the GameCube's there); anything else maps to `Unknown` and is dropped. `GamepadAxis` is the two sticks and the two triggers, normalized to -1..1 and 0..1 in `Event::value`, keeping SDL's sign convention (+x right, +y **down**, so a stick pushed forward reads negative). `gamepad_button_name`, `gamepad_axis_name`, `gamepad_name(slot)`, and `gamepad_connected(slot)` are for logs and tools. Gamepad events carry no window, so they go to whichever window is polling; the slot table is process-wide and `shutdown()` closes every pad. **The enumerator values of `GamepadButton` and `GamepadAxis` are part of the input log format** (docs/subsystems/input.md): append to them, never reorder or renumber.

**Raw joysticks.** SDL announces every device as a joystick, and the subset it has a mapping for a second time as a gamepad. This module opens a joystick only when SDL does *not* consider it a gamepad, so the two tables never hold the same device: everything with a known layout is reported by name through the gamepad events above, and everything else — wheels, pedal boxes, sequential and H-pattern shifters, flight sticks, button panels — arrives as `JoystickConnected`, `JoystickDisconnected`, `JoystickAxis`, `JoystickButtonDown`, `JoystickButtonUp`, and `JoystickHat`. There is nothing to name them by, so they carry bare indices: `Event::index` is the axis, button, or hat number the device itself uses, `Event::value` is the axis position normalized to -1..1 (no trigger convention — a pedal that rests at one end of its travel reads -1 at rest, which is the binding's `scale` to deal with, not the module's), and `Event::hat` is a `HatDirection`.

**The two slot spaces are separate.** `Event::gamepad` indexes the gamepad table, `Event::joystick` the joystick table; both start at 0, both are handed out lowest free first, both are released on disconnect, and **gamepad 0 and joystick 0 are two different devices**. Whoever converts these events into `input::RawEvent`s keeps them apart by *source*, never by the slot number: a `RawEvent` whose source is `GamepadButton` has a gamepad slot in `device`, one whose source is `JoystickButton` has a joystick slot. `k_max_joysticks` is 8, like `k_max_gamepads`.

**Hats.** A hat (a "POV hat", the d-pad on a wheel rim or a flight stick) reports a *position*, not a press: `HatDirection` is a bitmask of `Up` 1, `Right` 2, `Down` 4, `Left` 8, with the four diagonals as two bits or-ed and `Centered` as none — SDL's own numbering, and part of the log format (`input::k_hat_up` and friends mirror it). `hat_direction_name` names the nine positions and answers "Unknown" for anything else. Because a binding names a direction rather than a hat, the conversion to `input::RawEvent` turns one hat event into four digital signals, one per cardinal direction, coded `input::hat_code(hat_index, direction)`.

**Names.** Drivers name a device badly or not at all, so this module keeps a small table and prefers it over what the driver said. A Thrustmaster T300 RS arrives from the Windows driver as `44F B66E` — its USB ids in hex, because nothing wrote an OEM name into the registry. A Logitech F710 with its switch on XInput arrives as `XInput Controller #1`, because that is all XInput knows. A T500 RS gear shift reports `Thustmaster T500 RS Gear Shift`, a typo in the hardware's own product string. `resolve_device_name(vendor, product, raw_name)` answers the table's entry where it has one, the driver's name where it does not, the ids in hex (`"044F B66E"`) where nothing named it, and `"Unknown device"` where there is not even that; `known_device_name` is the table lookup alone, `nullptr` for a miss. The table's rows are keyed on vendor and product — every id in it read out of SDL's own database in the vendored source, so **0x044F 0xB65D is SDL's generic "Thrustmaster Wheel FFB" and not the T.16000M, which is 0x044F 0xB10A** — except the gear shift's, which is keyed on its exact misspelled name under its vendor, because the probe that found it recorded events and not ids. `gamepad_name(slot)`, `joystick_name(slot)`, the names in the connected events' log records, and `InputDeviceInfo::name` are all the resolved name; `InputDeviceInfo::raw_name` is what the driver said, unedited. Adding a device is a row in `k_device_names` in `src/window.cpp` and a line here.

**Describing a device without waiting for an event.** `input_devices(Vector<InputDeviceInfo>&)` lists every attached gamepad and joystick with its resolved `name` and the driver's `raw_name`, SDL's 32-character `guid` (bus, vendor, product, version — stable across sessions and machines), `vendor`, `product`, the number of `axes`, `buttons`, and `hats`, whether SDL calls it a gamepad, and the slot it holds. Reading the counts needs the device open, which SDL reference-counts, so listing does not disturb the event stream or the slot tables. `slot` is `k_invalid_slot` (0xFF) for a device whose arrival event has not been polled yet — SDL announces what is already plugged in as an arrival, so a caller polls for a moment first — or one that refused to open or found every slot of its kind taken.

**Rumble.** `rumble(gamepad, low, high, ms)` runs the two motors of a gamepad slot at 0..1 for a duration; it returns false for an empty slot or a pad with no motors, which is most of them, so false means "nothing happened" rather than "something went wrong". Joysticks have none: a force-feedback wheel is a haptics device, not two motors.

**Force feedback.** A wheel is driven with *forces*, over the raw joystick slot space, through SDL's haptics API. `haptics_open(joystick)` takes the device (false for an empty slot, a device with no haptics, or a machine with no haptics subsystem — `init()` asks for `SDL_INIT_HAPTIC` as optionally as it asks for the others), `haptics_info(joystick, HapticInfo&)` reports which of `constant`, `spring`, `damper`, `friction`, and `sine` the device supports and how many `axes` it can be pushed along, and `haptics_close` stops everything and hands it back. `set_constant_force(joystick, level)` is a fixed push along the first axis, -1..1; `set_spring(joystick, strength, center)` pulls towards a point, strength 0..1 and centre -1..1; `set_damper(joystick, strength)` resists velocity; `stop_forces(joystick)` stops and destroys all three. Each effect is created on first use, run with an infinite length, and *updated in place* afterwards, so a game that writes a new spring strength every frame is not rebuilding an effect every frame. Everything returns false where it is unsupported: false means "no force was applied", never "something went wrong". `shutdown()` and a disconnect close a slot's haptics before its joystick.

**Opening a wheel stops its own centring**, and a game has to take that over. The driver applies an autocentre spring until an application takes the device; SDL's open *is* that takeover, so a wheel that felt sprung goes limp the moment `haptics_open` succeeds — which is exactly what happened to the T300 RS during the probe that produced `content/input-logs/t300.jsonl`. So: a game that opens a wheel sets a spring or a damper itself and keeps it running for as long as it holds the device. `set_spring(slot, 0.5f, 0.0f)` is a reasonable resting force. `engine-input ffb` (docs/subsystems/apps.md) is how to feel one without writing a game.

**Relative mouse.** `Window::set_relative_mouse(true)` hides the cursor, holds it inside the window, and keeps `MouseMove`'s `dx`/`dy` reporting motion where an absolute pointer would have stopped at the screen's edge — SDL's `SDL_SetWindowRelativeMouseMode`, which is what a first-person camera needs; `relative_mouse()` reads back what SDL says is in effect. False for no window or a platform that refused (with the reason logged once as a warning), so a caller that tracks what it asked for does not ask again every frame. SDL keeps the mode per window and suspends it while the window has no focus, so switching away gives the cursor back without the caller doing anything; engine-view also drops its own capture on `FocusLost`, and takes the pointer on `FocusGained` and on a click in the window — switching to its window is switching to fly it — while Escape gives it back ([apps](apps.md#--interactive-a-camera-somebody-flies)). It is a mode of the window and not an input binding on purpose: *whether* the pointer is looking is a window's decision, and the conversion to `input::RawEvent` reads it — motion while the window does not hold the pointer is pointing, and engine-view's edge drops it before it becomes input. **Every event carries the platform's timestamp** (`Event::timestamp_ns`, SDL's `SDL_GetTicksNS` clock), and `relative_mouse_since_ns()` says when the mode last came on, on the same clock (0 while it is off): motion queued before that moment is the absolute pointer's, whose delta runs from wherever the cursor last was and can be a screen's width, and a caller that asks for the mode when it decides to capture — a frame after the click or the focus — tells the two apart by the stamps (engine-view's `PointerGate`, [apps](apps.md#--interactive-a-camera-somebody-flies), 2026-10-03).

**Synthetic events.** `Window::push_event(const Event&)` puts a key (by `scancode`), a mouse button, pointer motion or a wheel event on the window's own queue, and the next `poll()` returns it through the same switch a platform event takes — `SDL_PushEvent` with the window's id. Gamepad and joystick events are refused, because their slots are this module's names for real devices. It exists so a window can be driven with nobody at it: `engine-view --inject-input` feeds a recorded session through it for the end-to-end test and for smoke runs on a shared desktop, where synthesizing keystrokes at the operating system would type into whatever window has focus instead.

**Background events.** `set_background_input(true)` clears SDL's rule that device events are dropped while a process owns windows and none of them holds keyboard focus — right for a game, wrong for a tool started from a terminal whose user then has both hands on a wheel, which is why `engine-input` turns it on and `engine-view` does not.

**Build.** `cmake/EngineGraphics.cmake` fetches SDL `release-3.4.16` (`ENGINE_SDL_TAG`) and builds it static with audio, render, GPU, camera, and sensor subsystems off; joystick and haptics stay on, and `init()` calls `SDL_InitSubSystem(SDL_INIT_GAMEPAD)`, then `SDL_INIT_JOYSTICK`, then `SDL_INIT_HAPTIC` after the video subsystem (the first implies the second; each is asked for separately so a machine whose gamepad mappings fail to load still reports its wheels and sticks, and one with no force-feedback driver still reads them). On Linux SDL needs the X11 or Wayland development headers at build time (the libraries themselves are loaded at run time), and refuses to configure without them; the CI workflow installs them. A headless server build can set `SDL_UNIX_CONSOLE_BUILD=ON` to skip that check, and `init()` then fails cleanly at run time.

**Depends on.** `base`, `containers`, `log`, `gpu_lock`; SDL3 (third_party/LICENSES.md).

**Testing.** `tools/dev.ps1 test -Filter window`: creates a hidden window, checks the pixel size and that the logical size is no larger than it, the surface extension list, event draining, rename, destroy, and shutdown; records a skip on machines without a display. The drain also covers the gamepad and joystick paths, since SDL reports every device already plugged in as an arrival during `init()`: each one must come through with a slot below its table's size and an open handle behind it, every gamepad button must map to something other than `Unknown`, every axis value must be in range, every hat must be one of the nine named positions, untouched slots of both kinds must read as empty, and `shutdown()` must close them all. The test then lists the devices, prints each one's resolved name, the driver's raw name, GUID, vendor, product, and counts as a doctest message — which is how a machine reports what it has — checks that every listed device holds the slot its arrival claimed and that its `name` is what `resolve_device_name` makes of its ids, and calls `rumble` on an empty slot and on each pad. On the same hidden window it turns relative mouse mode on and off and reads back what SDL says each time (a hidden window has no focus, so SDL records the mode without taking the pointer from whoever is at the machine), pushes a synthetic `W`, a synthetic Delete and a synthetic motion and finds all three coming back out of `poll()` with their scancode, `Key`, `dx` and `dy`, and checks that a gamepad event is refused and that all three calls, and `logical_size`, answer false once the window is destroyed. Force feedback is the same shape: no test can feel one, so every empty joystick slot must answer false to `haptics_open`, `haptics_info`, the three `set_*` calls and `stop_forces`, and `haptics_close` on a slot that was never opened must do nothing; a slot that *does* have a device is opened, its `HapticInfo` printed as a message, stopped, and closed again without ever setting a force, since opening alone already takes a wheel's centring away. A machine with no device simply records that and checks the empty slots. A borderless window is created hidden at the size asked, so the flag is exercised without covering anybody's desktop. On Windows a Vulkan window is created against a scratch GPU lock left by a holder whose lease runs out three seconds in, while a second thread counts the process's visible top-level windows: none may appear while the lock is still the other holder's, and the lock is this process's once `create()` returns and gone once `destroy()` does (with the hold taken after `SDL_CreateWindow` instead, 185 of the thread's looks saw a window during the wait); then a window created hidden takes nothing until `show()`, which takes the lock before it shows it. The child window case also checks that the platform's queue (`post_os_event`) refuses a gamepad event and a key it has no virtual key for, and anything once the window is gone, that the display's scale is a positive number (1 without a window), and that `set_size` resizes the parent and refuses the child; the platform's queue carrying a click and keys into a visible child is engine-editor's `--test-keys` case. A second case needs neither a display nor a device: it pins the numbering of `GamepadButton`, `GamepadAxis`, and `HatDirection` and checks that every enumerator has a name of its own. A third pins the name table against the exact strings the drivers of this project's own devices produced — `"44F B66E"`, `"XInput Controller #1"`, `"Thustmaster T500 RS Gear Shift"` — including that the typo row matches under its vendor and nobody else's, and that an unknown device keeps the driver's name or, failing that, gets its ids. `apps/engine_input` is the end-to-end probe for devices nobody here owns (docs/subsystems/apps.md); `domain/gfx`'s swapchain test and `engine-view`'s end-to-end test open real windows.

**Not yet.** Haptics beyond a constant force, a spring, and a damper: no periodic effect (the rumble strip and the engine vibration a wheel's `sine` would give), no ramp, no envelope, no per-axis direction, no gain or autocentre control, no trigger rumble, and no LED or touchpad state. The three forces are also unverified on hardware — nobody here has a wheel to feel one, so what is tested is the plumbing and `content/input-logs/README.md` records what the T300 rig did. Devices beyond `k_max_gamepads`/`k_max_joysticks`, joystick trackballs (`SDL_EVENT_JOYSTICK_BALL_MOTION`), battery level, and the non-standard gamepad buttons — paddles, the touchpad, `Misc1` — which are dropped rather than reported. Text input and IME, clipboard, and multiple windows and per-output presentation for surround (docs/plan/04-renderer.md §4.8). Relative mouse mode has no raw-input or acceleration setting of its own (SDL's defaults: the platform's relative motion, unscaled), and synthetic gamepad events do not exist.

**Performance notes.** Event polling is a few microseconds per frame. SDL's static library adds about a minute to a cold CI build.
