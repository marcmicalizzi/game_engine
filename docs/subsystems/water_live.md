# water_live (systems)

**Purpose.** The water's shallow-water step on the card (roadmap W175, under W173): the scheme of [water_state](water_state.md) — the CPU reference in double precision — written as a compute shader in single precision, the smallest host code that runs it on a device, device cases that hold it to the reference's own cases, and a bench that says what it costs a frame. It is the capability that will later run the water's state in a scene. As of 2026-11-25 it holds no scene, draws nothing, adds no pass to a frame and has no scheduler entry: those are a later row's, and the measurements this row exists for are on [water-step-on-the-card](../experiments/water-step-on-the-card.md).

**Why this shape.** The design of the simulated water (W173 and its decision record) leans on a number nobody had: what a well-balanced finite-volume step costs a frame at the grids a lake, a stream's reach and a beach need. A step that is not the reference's scheme would measure something else, so the shader follows the reference's step operation for operation — the same expressions in the same operand order — and is held to the reference's cases rather than to cases of its own. **It is not yet the reference's whole step**: it carries no carried quantities (it ignores `Config::carried`), and it computes none of the reference's per-cell outputs (the speed, the bed stress in x and y, the dissipation), which the foam and the bed will read; writing them would add 12 to 16 bytes a cell a step and their arithmetic, so the 32 bytes a cell measured here is the cost of depth and momenta, not of the full state. It is a capability of its own in `systems/` (ADR-0027) because the state's later home is a scene, which the renderer and the world own, and nothing in `domain/water_state` may depend on a device.

- **Each cell gathers.** A thread is a cell: it reads its own state and its four neighbours', computes the flux through each of its four faces, and writes its new state into the second copy. No scatter, no atomic, one fixed order, so the same device gives the same bytes twice. A face is computed twice, once by each of its cells, from the same arguments in the same order; a second form computes every face once into a buffer and the update reads it (`StepOptions::stored_faces`), and a case holds the two to the same bytes, which is the proof that a face's flux is one value on both sides ("Two forms of the faces", below).
- **The bed is a height from the grid's own datum.** `set_bed` takes world heights and a datum in double and gives the card `bed - datum`, rounded once. A grid's positions are its own (cell (x, y) at index y·nx + x; [ADR-0053](../adr/0053-world-positions-are-f64-and-the-gpu-sees-none.md) puts no world position on the card), and a lake at rest at 2,000 m is the same bytes as at zero. In world heights it is not: single precision's ulp at 2,000 m is 2⁻¹³ m, and the surface's rounding becomes a force (the inexact lake's case measures it). The bed is a buffer the step reads at every step and may find changed.
- **The step's length is chosen on the card.** Each update writes, per workgroup of 16 × 16 cells, the largest |u| + c and |v| + c of the state it wrote and the volumes its cells added to the ledger; a one-workgroup pass reduces those records in a fixed tree, takes dt = cfl·dx / (max(|u| + c) + max(|v| + c)) as the reference does, and folds the volumes into the ledger. So a run of steps needs nothing from the host, and the frame does not wait for a readback to know its step.
- **Single precision throughout.** Every field that changes is a 32-bit float; nothing is 16 or 8 bits.

**The state.** Two copies, each `k_state_planes` (4) planes of nx·ny floats: h (m), hu and hv (m²/s), and the source's remainder (m, kept only when `set_source(..., compensate)` asks). The planes the design adds come after these, in the same layout: the ground's store, the carried quantities (one plane each, up to the reference's `k_max_carried`), the bed's mobile layer; the bed, Manning's n and the sources are buffers of their own that the host may rewrite between steps. The workgroups' records (`WaterPartial`, 32 bytes) and the ledger (`WaterLedger`, 48 bytes: each sum a pair hi + lo kept by TwoSum, so 10⁵ steps add in about twice single precision) are mirrored structs, pinned with the push constants (`StepParams`, 120 bytes) in `tests/size_table.cpp`.

**The step.** `shaders/water_step.slang`, five entries in one module: `prime_main` (the records of a state the host just wrote), `dt_main` (the step's length and the ledger), `update_main` (the step), and `faces_x_main`/`faces_y_main` (the faces, stored). The scheme is the reference's at its first milestone: first order; the hydrostatic reconstruction (Audusse, Bouchut, Bristeau and Sainte-Marie 2016, (3.1)–(3.5)) over the central-upwind flux (Bollermann, Chen, Kurganov and Noelle 2014, (2.8), (2.13)–(2.14)); the face's bed max(z_L, z_R); identical states given their exact flux, which keeps a lake at rest to the bit; a velocity of zero below the dry depth; Manning's friction implicit, (hu)' = (hu)* / (1 + dt g n² |u*| / h^(4/3)); sources and sinks; closed edges as mirrored ghosts and open ones as copied ghosts; a depth rounded below zero clamped and counted. A step of zero length (an advance that has landed) copies the state unchanged.

**The arithmetic.** The module is compiled with `-fp-mode precise`, which is what decorates every float operation with SPIR-V's NoContraction; the `precise` on each line emits nothing by itself (Slang 2026.17.1 writing SPIR-V directly; the experiment page, "Contraction"). So no driver may fuse a multiply and an add, and addition, subtraction and multiplication are correctly rounded on every conformant device. Division and the square root are not: Vulkan allows them 2.5 ulp and more, so two devices may differ in a last bit. With `Ops::fixed` (specialization constant 0) they and the friction's cube root are built from the first three alone: an estimate from the operand's bits, which integer arithmetic makes exactly, and a fixed number of Newton steps (four for 1/x, three for 1/√x, four for ∛x). What each form costs and gives is on the experiment page.

**An exchange under the last bit.** A slow source or sink — a loam's take, 3.7e-6 m/s, is 6.2e-8 m in a 60 Hz step — is about the last bit of a depth near a metre (2⁻²⁴ m below 1 m), so a plain step rounds each step's take to a whole ulp and the pond's loss comes out wrong by a few percent. `set_source(values, every, compensate)` offers the two remedies the design weighed: applying the source on a coarser clock (on one step in `every`, `every` times the amount), and keeping each cell's remainder (plane 3): the target is what the step owes plus what earlier steps' rounding left out, what reaches the depth is h' − h (exact by Sterbenz's lemma while the step is small against the depth), and the rest waits for the next step. The case and the numbers are below and on the experiment page.

**Two forms of the faces.** `StepOptions::stored_faces = false` (the default): one pass, each cell computing its four faces. `true`: a faces pass computes each of the (nx + 1)·ny x faces and nx·(ny + 1) y faces once into a buffer of four floats a face, and the update reads them. The two are the same bytes on this card (a case), so the gather's two computations of a face agree to the bit; the default is the gather because it moves fewer bytes (the experiment page has both costs).

**Running it.** `DeviceStep::create(device, water_state::Config, StepOptions)` makes the buffers and pipelines; `set_bed`, `set_manning`, `set_source` and `write_state` upload; `run_steps(count, max_dt)` and `advance(duration)` submit and wait; `read_state`, `read_ledger` and `state_hash` read back. `record(graph, count, max_dt, timer)` puts steps into a render graph the caller owns — every pass declares what it reads and writes and the graph derives the barriers — and with a `gfx::GpuTimer` each pass is a zone (`water.dt`, `water.faces`, `water.update`). An advance keeps its time left on the card in float: the last step lands on it, and the steps after it in the same batch choose a length of zero. `advance` refuses a source on a coarser clock (`set_source(..., every > 1)`): the clock counts steps on the host, it would count the zero-length steps that pad an advance's last batch, and `every` times one step's dt is the sum of the skipped steps' only while dt is constant, so the coarser clock holds with `run_steps` at a fixed `max_dt` only.

**Owned data.** Nothing outside a `DeviceStep`: the state, its inputs and its ledger on the device. The reference (`water_state::Solver`) stays the source of truth for the scheme; the card's form follows it.

**Invariants.** Checked by `tests/water_live_device_tests.cpp` on the device and `tests/water_live_tests.cpp` without one. Each bound comes from one argument: single precision's unit roundoff is 2²⁹ times double's, and a first-order monotone step under its CFL limit does not amplify a perturbation, so the card may differ from the reference by about one ulp of the water involved a step, S × 2⁻²³ after S steps; a reference bound that is a count of double's ulps is scaled by 2²⁹.
- A lake at rest over a bed of binary fractions with dry islands stays exactly at rest for 20 s (no bit of depth or momentum moves, no round-off is clamped), at a datum of zero and at 2,000 m with the datum there, the two the same bytes; in both forms of the arithmetic.
- A lake at rest over an inexact bed stays within 5.4e-5 m of its surface and under 5.4e-3 m/s (the reference's 1e-13 m and 1e-11 m/s times 2²⁹); at 2,000 m with the datum there it is the same bytes as at zero; handed world heights at 2,000 m its cells move (the case pins it, since the bounds alone cannot tell the two apart).
- Stoker's and Ritter's dam breaks at 200, 400 and 800 cells: L1 orders at least 0.5 and the finest grid within the reference's own bound (0.0045, 0.0058); the card within S × 2⁻²³ of the reference in L1 and its volume within S × 2⁻²⁴.
- The reference's count of water (wetting, drying, a spring, a drain, an open edge, friction): the card's own balance within S × 2⁻²⁴ of the water involved, its volume and outflow within S × 2⁻²³ of the reference's.
- The same bytes twice on one device, and the gather and the stored faces the same bytes.
- A closed basin keeps its water to the update's own rounding: the volume moves by no more than S × cells × 2⁻²⁴ × h_max × area.
- A still pond's slow sink kept by the compensated sum loses what it owes to two ulps of the depth.
- The SPIR-V carries NoContraction and no GLSL.std.450 Fma; the mirrored structs keep the shader's offsets and sizes.

**Where the two cards part** (2026-11-26). A ladder case ("a ladder of the count's flow", in `tests/water_live_device_tests.cpp`) runs the same 400 steps and prints, after steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 and 400 (so a first difference at steps 1 to 4 is exact, and one at a later rung lies somewhere after the rung before it), the step's length as the card chose it (its bits), a hash of each plane apart (h, hu, hv, the compensated sum's remainder), the workgroups' records and the ledger, each plane's row hashes, and every cell's bits before the first step and after the first two. It reads them through three diagnosis calls. Two of them, `read_partials_bits` and `read_ledger_bits`, are read-only copies. The third, `run_length_pass`, is not read-only: it runs the length pass alone, which writes the ledger's length and sums, folds the records' volumes into the ledger and zeroes them. Run between steps it would change the ledger's bits and the order its sums are added in, so a diagnosis runs it on a step it then discards, never between the steps of a run it means to compare; the case does that. The case asserts only what holds on every device: the same ladder twice on one device, and the laddered run's final hash equal to a run of the same 400 steps in one batch. Both cards ran the tree committed as b8a574920. The two logs' line numbers differ by a line or two because MSVC and GCC give a macro that spans lines a different `__LINE__`. ## Where a step runs in a frame

(2026-11-26, [ADR-0081](../adr/0081-a-frame-records-the-ticks-a-simulation-owes.md), proposed, under [ADR-0080](../adr/0080-water-carries-a-state.md). This is the design W191 builds, on a registration point of the renderer's that the row R252 builds first; **nothing of it exists on 2026-11-26**: today the step runs only in a graph a caller owns, through `DeviceStep::record`, and in the waiting forms `run_steps` and `advance`.)

**The state steps on the world's ticks, and a frame carries the ticks it owes.** The tick is the world's fixed step, 60 Hz in every host: engine-host's runtime world and engine-view's animated world step at it, and engine-view's window, whose session ticks at 240 Hz, falls due on it at session tick k·tick_hz/60, as the animated world does. A frame never counts time for the state: not from `dt_s`, not from the world clock, not from how many frames have passed. The scheduler does, and the frame records what the scheduler's ticks left to do.

**Who counts the ticks.** The capability installs, with `sim::SimScheduler::add_system`, one `Derived` system at `TickPhase::PostPhysics`, `water_live.ticks`. On each tick it decides which tiles wake at that tick, from the events that touch a tile and from the simulation's observers by distance with hysteresis (never the render camera, never a read-back), and appends a **tick record** to the host's list of owed ticks: the tick, its length in the state's time (1/60 s times the world rate that tick ran at, [ADR-0056](../adr/0056-one-world-clock-and-the-days-length.md)), its wakes and its events in their fixed order — (tick, the event kind's declared order, the source's index in its domain, the order of submission). The world rate of a 60 Hz tick is the one the host holds where that tick falls due; engine-view acts on a rate key after a frame's session ticks have run, so a key takes effect from the next frame's ticks, its tick depends on where the frames fell, and a recording must log the tick at which the change took effect, or its replay is a different input. engine-host's `render.*` session and engine-view each make a `sim::SimScheduler` for this when a scene with a live domain is loaded, and none otherwise; it has no executor and needs no ECS (`domain/sim` is not optional and links no flecs; W191 makes engine-view link it in every configuration), so the no-ECS presets keep it and the minimal build, where this capability is off, never makes one. In a window it is stepped from a tick observer that chains whichever observer was set before it — the animated world's, or with `ENGINE_WITH_ECS=OFF`, where there is no animated world, the game runner's or none; offscreen, once for each new frame index; in a flight, once a path frame, from the flight's `before_frame` hook (engine-host's render session already passes one, `ground_before_frame`, where a scene's ground asks, and the new hook chains it). Beside the animated world's own `SimWorld::step`, the scheduler is a second counter in engine-view, kept in step by construction — both at 60 Hz, both stepped by the same rule from the same observer chain or frame index, sharing no state — and a case holds the two counts equal after every frame where both step. A host with no device — engine-host's runtime world — installs no `water_live.ticks` and no list: it runs baked, or W194's coarse state.

**Where the step is recorded: a frame step, at the top of the frame.** The capability registers one frame step (`SceneRenderer::add_frame_step`, `systems/renderer/frame_step.h`, [renderer](renderer.md)) with a function, a commit and a timer zone, `water step`, with one renderer only (registered with two it would step twice). The renderer calls it in **every** recorded frame, before the frame's first pass and so before the cull, the deformed-vertex pool, the rasterizers, the traced chain and the resolve, whatever the frame draws: every rasterizer, the ray path, `--raster direct`, every debug view, a capture, a flight's warm-ups. Its context holds the graph, the timer, the device, the bindless set, the slot and the frame index, and nothing of the picture: no image, no visibility buffer, no view, no clock. The step's passes read and write the domain's own buffers only. Registering it makes no linear image and changes nothing the resolve does. Nothing in this module names the renderer: the step is `LiveWater::record(graph, timer, slot, ...)`, and the host adapts the renderer's context to it in a few lines of its own, so a device case or a host with a device and no picture records the same step into a graph of its own.

**Once per frame handed over, whatever the frame is recorded for.** The renderer records one frame several times when it has to — the measured runs' redraws, `render_offscreen`'s growth, a capture's accumulation cycle and its normals frame. So the host calls `LiveWater::prepare` once in its turn before `begin_frame`, which arms one recording; the armed step records into the next recording, and the renderer's commit, called after the queue has accepted it, disarms the step and marks what it staged as on the card. Redraws and a capture's later frames record no step and draw the state the first recording left; a failed recording is staged and stepped again by the next.

**The card walks the queue of ticks.** Tick records live on the card across frames, in a queue of 64 records of 16 bytes (the tick's low 32 bits — `SimTick` is wider, and the card compares ticks only within its own queue — a length, the first and count of its entries), and their events and wakes in an event arena sized at attach for the domain's largest event — at least one tile's patch of bed heights, 16 KiB at 64² cells — and a quarter second of small events. The armed step copies the owed records it has room for, whole and in order, from the frame slot's own host-visible block; a record goes onto the card only with all of its events, so a changed bed's patch too large for one frame's block is staged over several, and its tick and the ticks after it wait on the host's list. Then the step records a run of **sub-step slots shared by every queued tick**, each six passes in this order, so that no sub-step's length is chosen from speeds older than the state it steps: a one-workgroup **begin** pass reads the card's cursor and, when the current tick has landed and another is queued, begins it, writing the next two passes' indirect arguments; an **apply** pass writes the tick's events and wakes into the inputs, the tile table and the woken tiles' starting state at the tick's own boundary; a **prime** pass (today's `prime_main`) writes the workgroups' records of what the apply changed — both indirect, and zero groups when no tick began or it began with no event and no wake; the one-workgroup **length and ledger** pass (today's `dt_main`) chooses the sub-step's length by the reference's rule from those records, lands the last one on the tick's end, folds the ledger and writes the last passes' indirect arguments, the domain's groups or zero when nothing is left; then the **faces**, where they are stored, and the **update**, both indirect. A tick that has not landed when a frame's slots run out continues in the next frame's first slots. Which copy of the state is current is a word on the card that every reader reads, so the host-side copy flag `DeviceStep::record` keeps today, and the copies' addresses it puts in the push constants, go: every pass declares both copies, read and write, and the shaders choose by the word. A slot recorded past the work costs its two one-workgroup passes and three empty indirect dispatches (four where faces are stored). A frame records slots whenever the card may hold work — what the last read-back showed queued, and what the frames committed since have staged — and none only when both are empty, so a paused session still drains its queue.

**The same bytes at any frame rate, while no tick was dropped and no wake was refused.** Every tick is stepped as a unit from the state the tick before left, its events applied and primed at its own boundary, every sub-step chosen by the card from the state alone; how many slots a frame recorded and when a read-back arrived change only how far the drawn state lags, never the state. So a window at any frame rate, an offscreen run and a replay of the same ticks give the same bytes on one device, so long as no tick was dropped and no wake was refused — whether a tile's pages can be grown depends on the device's memory at that moment, not on the ticks — and a flight's repeats each start from a cleared queue. A dropped tick and a refused wake are each counted and reported, and every case that holds the card to the reference or one run to another asserts that neither happened. The count of slots a frame records is twice the sub-steps the frames needed over the last second, by the read-backs, and at least twice the ticks newly staged, up to `water_live.frame.max_slots` (read once at attach, 16 to begin).

**What holds the records, and what bounds the lag.** A record's place and its events' bytes are reclaimed when a read-back shows the cursor past them, which changes no state. The queue's 64 records are a size chosen so a domain keeping up never waits for room, not a correctness bound: the records held are the lag at a read-back's frame plus what `frames_in_flight` frames have staged since — about 60 with the renderer's default two frames in flight after a hitch on a lag near the threshold, about 75 with three — and a record with no room waits on the host's list, at the cost of lag only. The host's list is made at attach with 128 records, the queue's and about a second of ticks beside it. When the lag a read-back reports for its own frame passes a quarter second of ticks, the domain freezes as it stands when that read-back is folded, `frames_in_flight` frames later, said once; the ticks it does not step are dropped and counted, their events carried in order by one events-only record a frame, until the queue drains (ADR-0081, the owner's decision A, as recommended). If the host's list fills before that read-back arrives, the drop starts there; events that find neither the arena nor the list with room are dropped too, counted and said once. Which tick is dropped first depends on read-back latency and pacing, which is why every comparison asserts that none was.

**What a frame can carry.** `max_slots` (16) is a ceiling on a frame's work, so it is also where following the world rate stops: a domain that takes two sub-steps a 1/60 s tick takes eight at four times the world rate, and a frame at 15 frames a second owes four such ticks, 32 sub-steps against 16 slots, so it lags and freezes. "Up to four times" (ADR-0080's seventh decision for the owner) holds for such a domain only above about 30 frames a second.

**What wakes a tile.** The simulation's observers by distance with hysteresis, and events — never the camera, never a read-back. In engine-view the only simulation observer is the walker's body in a walking session with its physics (the observer `walk.cpp` gives the collision's tile ring); a flying session's observers are the views' eyes, which are the camera, and a flight and an offscreen run have none; engine-host's runtime world has its streamed ring's observers and a bound player, its `render.*` session none. Where there is none, only events wake tiles — a source, an edit, and the scene's authored wakes at tick 0 — and water flowing towards an asleep tile meets that tile's closed edge, until the rows that give engine-view the simulation's observers and the wake by need are built. A pond meant to fill beyond the tiles its scene wakes stops at their edge; W191's live scene wakes its whole footprint by authored wakes.

**The clocks, case by case.** The remainder of real time below a tick stays in the host's clock; a frame that kept up draws the last tick it stepped, a frame that lags draws where the card stood, which may be inside a tick, and nothing interpolates a state by the clock's fraction. At a hitch the host's clock runs at most a quarter second of ticks and drops the rest of the stall, and the frame after stages every owed tick the queue has room for. A session that runs no ticks stages nothing new and drains what the card still holds; at a world rate of 0 a tick has no length and carries its events alone. Past four times the world rate the records carry their events with no length, so the inputs stay current, and the domain runs baked for as long as the rate lasts, starting again as a waking tile does when it falls back; a domain whose far field is stale is frozen as it stands instead (ADR-0080's seventh decision for the owner; staleness is W194's). A clock set by hand — a `render.*` request's, `--time-of-day`, a frame drawn again — steps nothing: the state stands at its last tick, and the summary says which. In a flight path frame f owes frame f's own tick (of `world_rate / 60` seconds), warm-ups and the drain owe none, and every repeat starts the state again from its load at warm-up frame 0, or at path frame 0 with no warm-up, clearing with it the host's list, the card's queue, arena, cursor and ledger, and counting a restart that each read-back carries — so a repeat that lagged leaves no unlanded tick in front of the next, and each repeat measures the same water.

**Sources.** A source on the coarser clock (`set_source(..., every > 1)`) cannot follow sub-steps the card chooses, as `advance` already refuses it; the frame step takes the compensated sum only.

**Read-backs come back a slot later, labelled with their tick.** The step's last pass copies the cursor and, for each tick landed in the frame, its number, its sub-steps, its shortest and longest sub-step, the ledger's sums and the wet cells — and a state hash or a window of cells when a case asks — into the slot's own host-visible block; the block is folded when the slot is recorded again, after `begin_frame` has waited for it, and only if its recording was committed (the texture feedback's shape, `TextureFeedback::fold`). The latency is `frames_in_flight` frames, so each record says which tick it is. Nothing waits for one, nothing `hashed` reads one, and none changes the state: a read-back feeds this module's statistics, the reclaiming of the queue and the arena, the next frames' slot counts, the lag and the cases. **No read-back decides which tiles are awake**; whether a tile has settled and may retire is ADR-0080 decision 11's hand-off, to be designed with the tiers (W194) and the tiles by distance (W195).

**The coarse state inside the tick shares the ticks and never reads the card.** Where a game asks for live water it can feel (ADR-0080's first decision, W194), a `Hashed` system of this module at `TickPhase::Physics`, `water_live.coarse`, steps a coarse state by the reference's arithmetic on the same tick from the same events, before `water_live.ticks` writes the tick's record. The card's state and the coarse one are two functions of one sequence of ticks, held together by a bound on the cell-averaged level that a case checks; nothing `hashed` reads the host's list, the queue, a read-back or the card.

**A steady frame allocates nothing and compiles nothing.** The host's list, the slots' blocks, the card's queue and arena, the read-back blocks, the pipelines and their specializations are made at attach; a tile's pages, made when it first wakes, are grown in `LiveWater::prepare`, never inside a frame's recording or its commit, and the record of the tick that wakes it is staged only once they exist. `engine_view.frame_loop` flies a live scene among its path scenes and holds its second flight to zero allocations and zero device buffers. The frame step's zone reaches `Stats::gpu_frame_steps`, the benchmark record's `PassMs::frame_steps` and the summary's `PassPercentiles::frame_steps`; the step's sub-zones (`water.dt`, `water.faces`, `water.update`) are inside it. Its line in the frame budgets is W195's.

**Absent, refused, off or lost.** A scene with no live domain, or one whose tier is baked — every scene that says nothing (ADR-0080 decision 6) — makes no `LiveWater`, registers no frame step, installs no system and makes no scheduler; the renderer loops over no step. A domain the device cannot step, or whose worst-case tiles (printed at load) pass the memory this module may hold, runs baked: one line at load naming the domain and the reason, and the summary's `water_live` block says `baked` and why. A tile whose pages cannot be grown when it wakes stays asleep, counted and said once per domain: `prepare` tries once, before the waking tick is staged, and on a refusal the tick is staged without that wake, its other wakes and events intact, rather than held, since holding it would wait on memory that may never come and freeze the whole domain for one tile. A lost device takes the card's state with it: the host reloads it with the scene, and nothing hashed is affected. Nothing is retried a frame and nothing is said a frame.
