# scatter (systems)

**Purpose.** The scatter generator ([07 §7.6](../plan/07-content-pipeline.md#76-procedural-generation-volume-content), roadmap G37): a capability that places instances of a few meshes over the ground **deterministically, by rule** — rocks, dry scrub and debris on the desert now, and the placer the vegetation rows (G54's tree solver, G55's ecology) will stand on. A **scatter layer** is data (`engine.scatter.Layer`): a seed, a region, a density field, rules per placed kind, exclusions and a budget. It registers as the placement generator `scatter` in the scene-generator registry ([scene_gen](scene_gen.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md)), so a scene names a layer the way it names the ruins or the city — `{"generator": "scatter", "params": <layer>}` in its `placements` — and nothing below a host names it. It does not author meshes (beyond a procedural rock to look at), grow anything, simulate, or write documents.

**Why this shape.** Three choices carry it, each against an alternative that was on the table.

- **A placement generator, not a system with a tick.** A layer's output is a pure function of (layer, seed, tile, ground), which is exactly the contract a placement generator already has (`expand`, `tile`, `meshes`, `occupies`, `representation`), and the renderer's scene reader and the world's placements consumer already turn `scene_gen::Placements` into instances that cull and LOD like everything else. So the capability adds no render pass, no scheduler entry and no instance path of its own; what it owns is where things go.
- **A lattice and two rounds of local maxima, not Bridson's queue.** Bridson's algorithm grows a pattern from a seed point, so a point's fate depends on the order the pattern reached it: run per tile, two tiles disagree at their border; run over the union, a tile depends on which neighbours were present. Streaming needs the opposite — **a tile's placements a function of the layer, the seed and the tile alone**, so streaming a tile in never changes a placement already visible. So every cell of a global lattice (side r / √2 for a kind of spacing r, integer millimetres) holds one candidate at a hashed place with a hashed priority, and a candidate is kept by two rounds of Luby's rule: in round one, a candidate with no higher-priority candidate within r; in round two, among the candidates round one neither kept nor shadowed (none kept within r), the same rule again. A candidate's fate depends on the candidates within 3 r, which any rectangle computes from the lattice over its own cells and a six-cell apron. That is the **border apron**: a point near a tile border is decided by the tile its position is in, with the neighbour's candidates in view, and the same point is decided the same way by any request that contains it. No two kept points of a kind are within r, by construction; the pattern is blue noise, a little less dense than Bridson's maximal one (0.48 / r² against about 0.7 / r²), which a sparse desert never reaches.
- **Integers and a hash where it decides.** Places are integer millimetres of the world (ADR-0053's lattice), the priorities and every draw a 64-bit hash, distances compared in i64; the density field, the slope, the scale and the rotation are f64 `+ - * /` and `sqrt`, which IEEE rounds the same on every toolchain with floating-point contraction off ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-no-floating-point-contraction.md)). Nothing calls the C library inside a decision: `sin_cos` and the uphill yaw's atan2 are the module's own polynomials. That is what lets a hash pinned on MSVC hold on GCC and Clang, as geometry's does ([geometry](geometry.md#the-same-bytes-from-every-toolchain)).

## What a layer is

`systems/scatter/schemas/scatter.schema`, the capability's own types (`engine.scatter.*`, version 1):

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `seed` | the layer's; 0 takes the world's (the scene terrain's) |
| `tile_size` | the layer's tile edge, 32 m by default; a streamed world's must be the same |
| `region` | `Scene` (every tile a streamed world asks for, without end), `Tiles` (`tile_min`..`tile_max`, both included) or `Polygon` (`[x, z]` world metres, even-odd) |
| `density` | placements a square metre, shared by the kinds by `weight`: `Constant` (`per_m2`), `Noise` (`per_m2` times a value-noise fBm of world position: `octaves`, `wavelength`, `gain`, `lacunarity`, mapped from [`low`, `high`] to [0, 1]) or `Mask` (`per_m2` times the red channel of a `.tex` file over `mask_min`..`mask_max`, zero outside) |
| `kinds[]` | per placed kind: `mesh` (a glTF, GLB or `.clusters` path relative to the scene, as `engine.scene.MeshPlacement` names one) or `rock` (the procedural rock, below); `weight`; `spacing` (no two of the kind closer); `clearance` (none closer to an earlier kind's placement); `clear` (none closer than this beyond an earlier entry's footprint: [below](#exclusion-by-earlier-placements)); `slope_min_deg`/`slope_max_deg`; `height_min`/`height_max`; `stand` (`Surface`, the ground at its own time, or `Floor`, the ground that does not move under it); `yaw` (`Random`, `Uphill` — the mesh's +z up the slope — or `Fixed`, plus `yaw_deg`); `scale_min`/`scale_max`; `align` (the mesh's +y to the ground's normal); `sink` (metres below the ground at scale 1); `far` (the budget, below) |
| `exclude` | `circles` (a `worldpos` and a radius: a landmark, an editor's placement), a `mask` (`.tex`, nothing where its red is at least `mask_threshold`), `layers` (another layer's file and a clearance: nothing within it of that layer's placements) |
| `budget` | `per_tile`: the most placements one tile keeps, of every kind |
| `meshes` | where the procedural rocks' GLBs are written, relative to the scene; empty is `scatter/` under the host's derived-data root (`--ddc`, `scene_gen::Context::ddc`, as the water's meshes are: [scene_gen](scene_gen.md)), else the checkout's `ddc/scatter` found by walking up from the scene, else `scatter` beside it |

**A kind's `mesh` may name a rock the rock solver makes** — a `.rock.json` recipe ([rocks](rocks.md)), which the scene reader builds through the mesh-generator registry like any generated mesh, so a layer places solved granite boulders and scree with no change here (`content/test-scenes/rock-field/` does). The procedural rock stays the stand-in for a kind that names nothing, and needs no capability. **A cliff is not a kind**: a face along steep ground is a line hundreds of metres long and a mesh of its own, which the rocks capability's `cliffs` generator traces from the ground's heights and places ([rocks](rocks.md#the-cliffs-generator)); a layer after it in the scene sees each face as one earlier footprint, the circle round its whole bounds, which for a long face clears far more than the face — so boulders at a face's foot are the generator's own (`foot_mesh`) until footprints are shapes (below, "Not yet").

A layer is **scene data, not a document record**: it has no `materialize` declaration and its output is never written back as records. What an editor edits one by one is `engine.scene.MeshPlacement` ([doc_scene](doc_scene.md)); a layer's output becoming records an editor can override is a row of its own when it is wanted (below, "Not yet").

## The pipeline, and the order it runs in

For each kind, in the layer's order, over a rectangle of the world:

1. **The Poisson-disc**: the lattice candidates of the rectangle's cells and their apron, the two rounds, the kept points whose place is in the rectangle.
2. **The density**: a point is kept with probability `density(x) · share / (k_packing / r²)` — the field at the point, the kind's share of the weights, over what the lattice packs at that spacing — by a hash of its own candidate, so the density is met in expectation wherever it is under the cap (the tests hold it within 5% over 512 m squares, constant, by weight, noise and mask) and a kind past the cap is as dense as its spacing allows.
3. **The region**.
4. **The exclusions that need no ground**: circles, the mask, the earlier entries' footprints that reach the rectangle (each one's radius plus the kind's `clear`; [below](#exclusion-by-earlier-placements)), the other layers (each evaluated here from its own file, seed and rules — never asked of another entry, ADR-0046 decision 4 — over the rectangle grown by its clearance; a layer an exclusion names may not exclude by layers itself, so the evaluation is one level deep), and the clearance from the earlier kinds, which are placed over the rectangle grown by that clearance so a point sees every earlier point near it whichever rectangle asked.
5. **The ground** (`scene_gen::Ground`, the view every placement generator is handed: the surface or the floor in whole millimetres): the height, then the slope by central differences half a metre either side where a rule needs it (a slope limit, an uphill yaw, alignment).
6. **The placement**: the scale, the yaw, the alignment and the height less the sink — the height and the alignment's normal from `Site::stand` when the host hands a surface that moved ([below](#a-moving-ground)) — as a `Point` (integer millimetres in x and z, f64 height, a float32 quaternion, the yaw, the scale, the kind, and the budget's rank).

Then, per tile, the **budget's cap** keeps the `per_tile` placements of lowest rank (a hash of each one's candidate, not the order they were made in), and the **ring's mask** leaves out the kinds a far ring does not place. A tile's output is sorted by (kind, z, x), so two runs give the same bytes.

**Where exactness ends.** The lattice's cells are i64 millimetres, so a layer is exact wherever a cell reaches; the density's noise reads f64 metres, exact to a nanometre at 10,000 km. A rectangle's apron is six cells, so a kind's work per tile grows as (tile / spacing + 12)²: a 32 m tile at a 1 m spacing is about 2,700 candidates.

## Determinism

`k_determinism` is **"derived"**: a layer is rebuilt wherever it is asked for and is never part of the simulation's state. **The tile rule**: a tile's placements are a function of the layer, the seed, the tile and the ground alone — the same asked alone or beside its neighbours, in any order, on any thread — and the tiles of a block are the block, point for point, with no point at a border lost or doubled. **The bytes**: `hash_points` over the pinned layer's 16 tiles is `1ab4c8d051dbb2cb` (516 placements) and the procedural rock's GLB `7a6687b85300637a`, taken on MSVC; `tests/scatter_tests.cpp` holds both, so GCC and Clang must reproduce them, and a change that moves them moves every scattered scene and says so in its commit.

## The budget is the LOD policy

A capability declares its LOD policy (ADR-0027); this one's is a budget, because what costs is **pairs** (an instance times its clusters, every one visited by the cull pass every frame), and a smaller mesh is not the answer until the tree solver's cards exist:

- **`far` per kind**: in a streamed world, a ring whose nearest tile is farther from the camera than `far` places none of that kind (`Placer::ring_kind_mask`; the ring's inner radius in tiles times the tile edge). The set a ring keeps only shrinks outwards, so the generator's `representation` is the number of kinds it leaves out, and a tile moving between two rings of one tier is not built again. **Far tiers place fewer kinds, not smaller meshes.**
- **`per_tile`**: the most placements a tile keeps, decided before the ring's mask, so a far ring draws a subset of a near ring's placements and a ring change never moves or adds one.

A whole read has no camera, so it places every kind and only `per_tile` applies; the cull pass and the cluster LOD do the rest.

## Where it enters the renderer

**A whole read** (no `world`, or a `placements` entry with `"stream": false`, `engine.scene.PlacementEntry` version 2): the scene reader opens the layer, expands its region (refusing a region of the whole scene, which has no whole), and its placements become plain `SceneInstance`s, each at its `WorldPos` with its rotation and scale ([renderer](renderer.md#scenes-camera-paths-and-flythroughs)). The kinds' meshes are appended once and shared by every instance, so a thousand rocks are one mesh's clusters loaded once through the derived-data cache. More than 32 tiles go to the job system's performance pool in contiguous runs joined in tile order, so the instances are the ones one thread makes.

**A streamed world** (`world`, `stream` true): the reader asks for the meshes, and the world's placements consumer (`world::PlacementTiles`) calls `tile` for each tile its ring activates, in that ring's tier, and hands the tail to `set_dynamic_instances` ([world](world.md#the-consumers), [renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go)). **On tile ground too, since 2026-10-12** (roadmap G59): a world that draws its ground from tiles (`world.ground`, desert-endless) refused streamed placements until then, because a scene whose instances came and went had neither the deformed-vertex pool the tiles are drawn through nor the ray tracing chain; the renderer's tail now has both — each block writes its own slots of the chain's per-instance tables, and the pool's per-frame blocks follow the visible list ([renderer](renderer.md#instances-that-come-and-go), "The pool and the chain over the tail"). `stream: false` stays for a **bounded** layer read whole with the scene (`scattered-bounded.json`).

**`far` in practice.** A ring places a kind when its nearest tile is within the kind's `far` of the camera: with the desert's rings of 1.5, 8, 24 and 64 tiles of 32 m, the inner radii are 0, 48, 256 and 768 m, so a `far` of 200 m places in the first two rings and 40 m in the first alone. A ring that places no kind costs a mask, not a tile: `place_tile` returns before the lattice when the mask is empty, which is what lets the world's ring that draws the ground to the horizon (12,900 tiles) run with no activation budget beside a layer that places on the 200 near ones.

## Exclusion by earlier placements

**A kind keeps its `clear` beyond every earlier entry's footprint** (2026-10-12; [scene_gen](scene_gen.md#earlier-placements), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-scene-generators-register-themselves.md) decision 4 and its "As built" note on earlier placements). The host hands `Context::earlier`: where each placement of the scene's earlier entries stands, in whole millimetres, and its mesh's reach on the ground (`renderer::ground_radius`, times its scale). In the pipeline it is an exclusion that needs no ground (step 4): a candidate within a footprint's radius plus the kind's `clear` — in i64 millimetres, as the spacing is — is not placed, so a rock is never inside a ruin's block or its `clear`. **Determinism**: the footprints are part of what a tile is a function of, and they only take placements away — every placement kept is one the layer makes alone — so a layer whose entry is first (the desert's) gives the bytes it gave before the seam, and the pinned hash does not move. A whole read hands `expand` every earlier entry's placements at once; the generator sorts them by the tile they stand on once and gathers each tile's neighbourhood by binary search (`Nearby`), so the tiles' jobs share it and the bytes are the one thread's. A streamed tile is handed its own tile's: the two differ only where an earlier footprint reaches over its tile's border. Footprints are circles; shapes are not built (below).

## A moving ground

**The dunes move under a time-lapse; a placement standing on them follows** (2026-10-12). A kind with `stand` `Surface` decides on the scene's ground (`Site::ground`: the slope and height limits, the uphill yaw) and stands on `Site::stand`, the surface as the host's ground has drawn it now — its height, and its normal for the alignment — so the sand moving under a rock raises, lowers and tilts it and never moves it across, turns it about +y, rescales it or takes it away. A `Floor` kind stands on the floor, which does not move. The world's placements consumer re-stands its held tiles a few an update, nearest first, whenever the surface's time is not the one they stood on (`world::PlacementTiles::restand`; engine-view hands it the renderer's ground time, four tiles an update); the dunes' levels move by evaluated fields rather than by rebuilding tiles, so the placements follow the surface's time rather than a tile rebuild of the ground. **What it costs**: a re-stood tile is the generator's tile again plus one surface query a placement (five where it aligns) — 16.9 ms a tile in a `msvc-debug` build on the seam test's 64 m erg of dunes with two layers and 791 placements on 16 tiles (2026-10-12, the machine shared with other builds: an upper bound, and a debug one); on the desert, the layer's tiles are the ring activation's cost again ([the desert's numbers](../../content/test-scenes/desert-endless/README.md#the-scattered-variant)).

## The desert's layer

[`content/test-scenes/desert-endless/scattered.json`](../../content/test-scenes/desert-endless/README.md#the-scattered-variant) is desert-endless field for field plus one layer, `erg-rocks-and-scrub`, **streamed** (`stream` true) over the whole erg (`region` `Scene`) since 2026-10-12: two procedural rocks — a sandstone-coloured boulder (6 m spacing, slope under 18°, aligned, 0.6–1.8 scale, `far` 200 m: the first two rings) and a dark low tuft standing in for dry scrub (4 m spacing, 2.5 m clear of a boulder, slope under 24°, `far` 40 m: the first ring) — at a noise density of up to 0.006 a square metre, at most 12 a tile. `scattered-bounded.json` is the same layer read whole over a polygon a kilometre wide along the camera path's 12 km (13,461 tiles), which is what `scattered.json` was before the tail could carry it. **Why variants and not `scene.json` itself**: the first draft put the layer in `scene.json`, and then every reader of that scene needed the scatter capability — the renderer's tests that read it for its terrain do not link it and refused it (the Clang container found it), a build with only this capability switched off would refuse the committed scene, and a debug read of the scene would place 13,000 tiles of rocks on the dunes for tests that want the sand. `tests/scatter_scene_tests.cpp` holds both variants equal to `scene.json` in every other field, since a second copy of the erg's bands that nothing held would drift (the README's own history), and the two layers equal but for the region, `stream` and `far`. The E10 prop corpus is the owner's and outside the repository, so the scene names no mesh of it; a kind's `mesh` is where one goes. What each costs against the frame budgets is in the README.

## Owned data

The layer as read (`Placer`, immutable once opened, so any number of tiles may be placed on any number of threads), the masks it decoded, the excluded layers' placers, and the procedural rock's file. No state between calls: a tile is computed whole each time it is asked for.

## Invariants

- A tile's placements are a function of (layer, seed, tile, ground): the same twice, in any tile order, alone or beside its neighbours; the tiles of a block are the block (`scatter_tests.cpp`).
- No two placements of a kind are closer than its spacing, nor a kind's placement nearer an earlier kind's than its clearance, across every border.
- Slope, height, region and every exclusion hold for every placement.
- The density is met within 5% over a large region wherever it is under the spacing's cap.
- The budget keeps at most `per_tile` a tile, a subset of the uncapped tile; a far ring's tile is a subset of a near ring's.
- `tile` over the region's tiles is `expand`.
- The pinned hash is the same on MSVC, GCC and Clang.

## Public API

`include/systems/scatter/scatter.h`: `Site` (the ground, the moving ground's `stand`, the `earlier` footprints), `Placer` (`open`, `place_tile`, `place_rect`, `earlier_reach_mm`, `occupies`, `region_tiles`, `ring_kind_mask`, `density_at`), `Point`, `position`, `RectMm`, `Mask`, `kind_meshes`, `make_rock`, `rock_glb`, `rock_file_name`, `hash_points`, `sin_cos`, `k_packing`, `k_placement_generator`.

## Depends on

`base`, `containers`, `math`, `hash`, `json`, `schema`, `schemas`, `io` (masks, excluded layers, the rock's file), `log`, `jobs` (a whole read's pool), `time`, `scene_gen` (the registry and the ground), `texture` (a `.tex` mask), `scatter_schemas`. No renderer, no document, no other capability; the tests link the renderer and `gfx` for the scene case.

## Performance notes

**What a scatter costs a frame is its instances' shadows** ([the desert's numbers](../../content/test-scenes/desert-endless/README.md#the-scattered-variant), 2026-10-11, RTX 5090): 28,857 rocks (202,000 pairs) add 0.06 ms of cull and 0.39 ms of cascaded shadow maps at 1080p, and 0.83 ms at 11,520 × 2160 with traced shadows — a bottom-level structure and cluster structures per visible rock every frame and shadow rays through a top-level structure of tens of thousands of small instances; a third of the density costs about half of that. Both of the endless desert's budgets break at either density, which is why the layer sits in a variant the gate does not fly. The renderer-side answer, when it is wanted, is per-instance ray tracing cost for many small rigid instances (instancing one bottom-level structure per mesh and LOD rather than per instance). Not measured: the whole read's own time (it is in the `scatter placed` log line), and a streamed layer's cost per activation in a flight. A tile's cost is the lattice over its cells and apron, times the kinds, plus five ground queries for each candidate a slope rule keeps — the ground is the cost on the dunes, a few microseconds a query — and a kind with a clearance places the earlier kinds over its apron again. Nothing here runs per frame: a whole read runs once with the scene, a streamed tile once per activation. Not measured yet: a streamed layer's cost per activation in a flight.

## Not yet

- **Document records.** A layer's output becoming `MeshPlacement` records an editor can move, delete or pin, with the layer re-run around them, is the document path (`materialize` or a derived layer); this slice writes none. Placements as document records are not built.
- **Footprints as shapes.** An earlier entry's placement is a circle — its mesh's bounds on the ground — not a building's outline, a wall's line or a road's band, and a streamed tile sees its own tile's footprints only, so one that reaches over its tile's border is missed by the next tile. Exclusion by another layer's file stays the simple case beside it (its placements as circles of one clearance). An entry read whole is not handed to a streamed one.
- **The tree solver** (G54) and its cards for the far tiers, and ecology (G55): the placer is what they will stand on; until then a far tier places fewer kinds.
- **Roads and water**: distance to them (§7.6's list) waits for roads and rivers (G38).

## Capability contract (ADR-0027)

This is a capability: it was added without editing `core/`, `foundation/`, the render graph, the scheduler, or another capability, and it can be removed from the build the same way. Outside its directory it changed the hosts' generator lists (engine-view and engine-host link it for its registrar) and the scene schema's `PlacementEntry.stream` with the reader's one condition on it — the renderer's, not a capability's.

**Removing it.** `cmake --preset msvc-minimal` (or `-DENGINE_WITH_SCATTER=OFF`) drops the module, its schema library and its tests; the module disappears from `build/<preset>/modules.json` and is listed there under `disabled_capabilities`. Everything else still builds and passes, and a scene naming `scatter` is refused with the registry's sentence.
